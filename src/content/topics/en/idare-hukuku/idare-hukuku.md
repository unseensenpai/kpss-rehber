## Core idea

Administrative law governs the organisation, acts and liability of the administration that exercises public power; administrative justice (*idari yargı*) reviews whether those acts are lawful. The core logic: the administration is **bound by law**, can affect people through unilateral acts, and in return is subject to **judicial review**. The test has 6 questions; they usually ask about types of organisation, the elements of an act, types of liability and types of action.

## Administrative organisation

- **Unity of the administration:** central administration and decentralised bodies form one whole, held together by **hierarchy** and **administrative tutelage**.
- **Hierarchy** (*hiyerarşi*): within the central administration, a superior may instruct a subordinate and approve, amend or revoke their acts.
- **Administrative tutelage** (*idari vesayet*): the centre's exceptional oversight of decentralised bodies, limited by law; as a rule it does not include amending their acts.
- **Central administration:** the Presidency, ministries and provincial organisation (provinces and districts; governors and district governors). **Devolved authority** (*yetki genişliği*) lets the provincial governor take decisions locally.
- **Decentralisation** (*yerinden yönetim*): **territorial** (special provincial administration, municipality, village) and **service-based** (such as universities and public professional organisations).

## Administrative acts

- **Features:** unilateral, **executory** (*icrai*, producing legal effects by itself), benefits from a **presumption of legality** and can be enforced by the administration itself.
- **Elements:** **competence, form, reason, subject, purpose.** A defect in any of them makes the act annullable; a very grave defect leads to **nullity** (*yokluk*).
- **Purpose element:** an act must always serve the public interest; one done for private ends is a **misuse of power** (*yetki saptırması*).
- **Administrative contracts:** contracts on running a public service that give the administration superior powers (such as a concession contract).

## Liability of the administration

- **Service fault** (*hizmet kusuru*): the service not working at all, working late or working badly.
- **Strict liability** (*kusursuz sorumluluk*): the **risk** principle and **equalisation of sacrifice** (special harm borne for the public interest).
- **Personal fault and recourse:** for harm caused by an official while on duty, the claim is brought against the administration, which may then recover what it paid from the official.

## Administrative justice

- **Courts:** administrative and tax courts at first instance, **regional administrative courts** on appeal (*istinaf*), and the **Council of State** (*Danıştay*) on cassation; the Council of State also hears some cases at first instance.
- **Annulment action** (*iptal davası*): brought by a person whose **interest** is affected by an unlawful act; the act's elements are reviewed.
- **Full remedy action** (*tam yargı davası*): a person whose **right** is violated claims compensation for the harm.
- **Stay of execution** (*yürütmenin durdurulması*): requires both that the act is **clearly unlawful** and that carrying it out would cause **harm that is hard or impossible to repair**.
- **No review of expediency:** courts cannot review whether an act was expedient and cannot issue decisions that amount to administrative acts.
- **Court of Jurisdictional Disputes:** resolves conflicts of jurisdiction and judgment between the ordinary and administrative courts.

## Commonly confused

- *Hierarchy* is a general, inherent power within the central administration; *administrative tutelage* over decentralised bodies is exceptional and limited by law.
- An *annulment action* needs only an affected interest; a *full remedy action* needs a violated right and harm.
- *Devolved authority* gives a provincial body decision power; *delegation* (*yetki devri*) transfers one authority's power to another where a law allows.

## Study tip

When a question describes an act, check the five elements in order: who made it, in what form, on what grounds, with what effect and for what purpose. Always check time limits and procedural rules in the current Administrative Procedure Law (*İYUK*) on mevzuat.gov.tr.
