## Core idea

Political science studies how **power** is gained and used in society and why it is accepted. At its centre are the state, power and **legitimacy**. The Public Administration test has about **8 questions** on it. Questions mostly match thinkers with views, ask for the core principles of ideologies and test the distinguishing features of systems of government.

## Basic concepts and the state

- **Power:** the ability to make others do what you want; **authority** is power accepted as legitimate.
- **Weber's types of authority:** traditional, charismatic and legal-rational.
- **Elements of the state:** territory, population, sovereignty and political organisation.
- **Sovereignty:** the idea, systematised by Bodin, of the state's supreme and indivisible power.
- **Social contract:** **Hobbes** calls for a strong sovereign; **Locke** sees protecting natural rights (life, liberty, property) as the state's purpose; **Rousseau** stresses the general will.

## Thinkers

- **Plato:** gives rule in the ideal state to the philosopher.
- **Aristotle:** classifies forms of government by the number of rulers and their aim.
- **Machiavelli:** separates politics from morality; seen as a pioneer of realist politics.
- **Montesquieu:** defends liberty through the **separation of powers** (legislative, executive, judicial).

## Ideologies

- **Liberalism:** individual freedom, limited government, market economy.
- **Conservatism:** preserving tradition and institutions, gradual change; associated with Burke.
- **Socialism:** equality and social control over the means of production.
- **Fascism:** a one-party, authoritarian order that absorbs the individual into state and nation.

## Forms of state, systems of government and elections

- **Unitary state:** one legislature, one executive, one judiciary; in a **federal state** powers are constitutionally shared between the centre and the federated units.
- **Presidential system:** strict separation of legislature and executive; a single executive elected by the people.
- **Parliamentary system:** the executive comes from and is responsible to parliament; the executive is dual (head of state and government).
- **Electoral systems:** **majoritarian** systems favour stability, **proportional representation** favours fairness. **Duverger's law** says single-round plurality voting tends to produce a two-party system.
- **Pressure groups:** aim to influence decisions, not to take power; this sets them apart from parties.

## Commonly confused

- *Power* can rest on force; *authority* is power accepted as legitimate.
- A *federal state* is one sovereign state; a *confederation* is a union of sovereign states formed by treaty.
- A *political party* seeks power; a *pressure group* only seeks to influence decisions.

## Study tip

Make a one-line "signature idea" card for each thinker (Montesquieu: separation of powers, Hobbes: strong sovereign). Compare systems of government in a table using two questions: where does the executive come from, and to whom is it responsible?
