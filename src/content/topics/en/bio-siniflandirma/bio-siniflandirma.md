## Core idea

Classification groups organisms by **common ancestry and similarity**. The modern approach is **phylogenetic**: groups should reflect evolutionary relationships, so homologous structures and DNA sequences count, not just outward appearance. The Biology test has **4 questions** here, usually on the order of categories, binomial naming and the distinguishing features of major groups.

## Principles

- **Hierarchy:** domain → kingdom → phylum → class → order → family → genus → species. Moving down, shared features increase and the variety of individuals decreases.
- **Species:** a group of individuals that can interbreed and produce **fertile** offspring.
- **Binomial nomenclature (Linnaeus):** the first word is the genus, the second the species epithet; written in Latin and italics, with the genus capitalised (*Homo sapiens*).
- **Homologous structures** share a common origin (a human arm and a bat wing) and show kinship; **analogous structures** do the same job but have different origins (bird and insect wings).

## Major groups

- **Three domains:** Bacteria, Archaea (prokaryotes) and Eukarya.
- **Bacteria:** cell walls contain peptidoglycan; some photosynthesise or fix nitrogen. **Archaea** often live in extreme environments and lack peptidoglycan in their walls.
- **Protists:** mostly single-celled eukaryotes (amoeba, euglena, algae); eukaryotes that do not fit the other kingdoms.
- **Fungi:** heterotrophs with chitin cell walls; they secrete digestive enzymes outside and absorb the products.
- **Plants:** non-vascular (mosses) and vascular; vascular plants are seedless (ferns) or seed plants, and seed plants are gymnosperms or angiosperms.
- **Animals:** invertebrates and **chordates**; chordates share, at some stage of development, a notochord, a dorsal hollow nerve cord and pharyngeal slits. Vertebrates are fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.

## Commonly confused

- *Homologous* structures show common origin; *analogous* structures show similar function.
- *Archaea* are prokaryotes but form a domain separate from *bacteria*.

## Study tip

Memorise the order of categories with a mnemonic and pick one distinguishing feature for each major group (wall material, mode of nutrition, vascular tissue, notochord); questions usually ask about that one feature.
