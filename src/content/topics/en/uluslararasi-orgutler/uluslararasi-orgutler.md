## Core idea

International organisations are bodies created by states through a **founding treaty**, with their own organs and continuity. When you learn an organisation, think of its founding, aim, main organs, decision-making and relationship with Turkey together. The test has **4 questions** on this subject; they usually ask you to match an organisation with its aim or organ and to tell apart bodies with similar names.

## Classification

- **Membership:** universal (UN) or regional (EU, African Union).
- **Purpose:** general or functional (economic, technical, military).
- **Powers:** in **intergovernmental** organisations decisions rest on state consent; **supranational** organisations (the EU in some areas) can adopt decisions that bind member states directly.
- **Non-governmental organisations:** set up by private persons rather than states (such as Amnesty International).

## The United Nations

- Founded in **1945** by the **UN Charter**, adopted at the San Francisco Conference.
- **Main organs:** the **General Assembly** (one vote per member), the **Security Council** (primary responsibility for international peace and security; 15 members, 5 permanent members with a **veto**), the **Economic and Social Council**, the **Trusteeship Council**, the **International Court of Justice** and the **Secretariat**.
- **Permanent members:** the USA, Russia, China, the United Kingdom and France.
- **Specialised agencies:** bodies set up by separate treaties and linked to the UN, such as the World Health Organization, the International Labour Organization, UNESCO, FAO, the IMF and the World Bank.

## European and security organisations

- **NATO (1949):** a collective defence organisation; **Article 5** treats an attack on one member as an attack on all. Turkey joined in **1952**.
- **Council of Europe (1949):** human rights, democracy and the rule of law; the **European Convention on Human Rights** and the **European Court of Human Rights**. Turkey joined in 1949, the year it was founded.
- **European Union:** the EEC was created by the **Treaty of Rome (1957)** and the EU by **Maastricht (1992)**; the **Treaty of Lisbon** renewed the institutional structure. Its institutions: the European Council, the Council of the EU, the **European Commission**, the European Parliament and the Court of Justice of the EU. Turkey became a candidate country in 1999.
- **OSCE:** the CSCE process that began with the 1975 Helsinki Final Act became an organisation in 1995.

## Economic and regional organisations

- **World Trade Organization (1995):** replaced GATT; has a mechanism for settling trade disputes.
- The **Organisation of Islamic Cooperation**, the **Black Sea Economic Cooperation Organization**, the **Economic Cooperation Organization** and the **Organization of Turkic States** (formerly the Turkic Council) are regional bodies Turkey belongs to.
- **G20:** not a treaty-based organisation but an informal forum of major economies.

## Commonly confused

- The *Council of Europe* (Strasbourg, human rights) is not an EU body; the *European Council* of the EU brings together heads of state or government.
- *General Assembly* resolutions are generally recommendations; *Security Council* decisions under Chapter VII are binding.
- The *ECtHR* is the court of the Council of Europe, the *Court of Justice of the EU* that of the EU.

## Study tip

Prepare a short ID card for each organisation: year founded, headquarters, aim, main organ, Turkey's status. Memberships and institutional structures can change, so also check current sources.
