## Core idea

Each theory offers a different view of human nature, the source of problems and how change happens, and uses techniques that fit that view. The test has 10 questions, the heaviest section of the field; they usually quote a counsellor's statement and ask which theory or technique it belongs to.

## Psychodynamic approaches

- **Psychoanalytic (Freud):** id, ego, superego; the unconscious; **defence mechanisms** (repression, projection, displacement, reaction formation, rationalisation). Techniques: free association, dream analysis, interpreting **transference** and **resistance**.
- **Adlerian (individual psychology):** **inferiority feelings** and striving for superiority, **lifestyle**, **social interest**, birth order, early recollections. Techniques: encouragement, "acting as if", "spitting in the soup", catching oneself.

## Humanistic-existential approaches

- **Existential (May, Frankl, Yalom):** freedom and responsibility, the search for meaning, isolation, awareness of death. Frankl's **logotherapy**: paradoxical intention, dereflection.
- **Person-centred (Rogers):** **conditions of worth** create incongruence between self and experience. The counsellor's **genuineness (congruence)**, **unconditional positive regard** and **empathy** are sufficient conditions for change; the approach is non-directive.
- **Gestalt (Perls):** **here and now**, awareness, **unfinished business**, responsibility-taking "I" language. Techniques: **empty chair**, exaggeration, dialogue.

## Behavioural and cognitive approaches

- **Behavioural:** behaviour is learned and can be changed. Techniques: reinforcement, extinction, modelling, token economy, **systematic desensitisation** (Wolpe; relaxation with a graded fear hierarchy), assertiveness training.
- **Rational emotive behaviour therapy (Ellis):** the **ABC model**: not the activating event (A) but the belief (B) determines the consequences (C); irrational "must" and "should" beliefs are disputed (D).
- **Cognitive therapy (Beck):** **automatic thoughts**, schemas and cognitive distortions (overgeneralisation, catastrophising, all-or-nothing thinking); Socratic questioning, thought records.
- **Transactional analysis (Berne):** Parent, Adult and Child ego states; life positions.

## Brief, present-focused approaches

- **Reality therapy (Glasser):** choice theory; basic needs (survival, love and belonging, power, freedom, fun). Behaviour is chosen; Wubbolding's **WDEP** system (wants, doing, evaluation, plan).
- **Solution-focused (de Shazer, Berg):** focus on solutions rather than problems; the **miracle question**, exception questions, scaling questions.

## Basic skills

- **Interaction skills:** active listening, open and closed questions, reflecting content and feeling, paraphrasing, summarising, confrontation (pointing out discrepancies), interpretation, self-disclosure, immediacy.

## Commonly confused

- *Ellis* changes irrational beliefs by disputing them; *Beck* tests automatic thoughts through collaborative questioning.
- *Gestalt* works with awareness and the here and now, *reality therapy* with needs and responsible choice; neither focuses on the past.
- *Projection* (a defence mechanism) attributes one's own impulse to others; *reflection of feeling* (a skill) mirrors the client's feeling back to them.

## Study tip

Write one sample counsellor sentence per theory ("If a miracle happened overnight and the problem were solved, what would you notice first?" is solution-focused). Questions almost always come with a sentence like this.
