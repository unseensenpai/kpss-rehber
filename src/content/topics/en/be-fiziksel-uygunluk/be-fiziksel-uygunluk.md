## Core idea

Physical fitness (*fiziksel uygunluk*) is being able to do daily tasks **without undue fatigue** and having energy left for leisure activities. School physical education aims more at **health-related** fitness than at competitive success. The test has **3 questions**; they usually ask which component a test or activity relates to.

## Components

- **Health-related:** **cardiorespiratory endurance** (the heart, circulation and lungs working for a long time), **muscular strength** (the maximum force produced at once), **muscular endurance** (repeating without tiring), **flexibility** (range of motion at a joint), **body composition** (ratio of fat to lean tissue).
- **Skill-related (performance):** **speed**, **agility** (speed of changing direction), **balance**, **coordination**, **power** (strength combined with speed; jumping), **reaction time**.

## Application and measurement

- **FITT principle:** a programme is built by adjusting frequency, intensity, time and type.
- The **World Health Organization** recommends that children and adolescents do an average of at least 60 minutes a day of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity; check the current recommendation in the official source.
- **Tests:** the progressive shuttle run measures cardiorespiratory endurance, sit-and-reach measures flexibility, push-ups and curl-ups measure muscular endurance, and the standing long jump measures power.
- **Body mass index = body weight (kg) / height (m)²**; a rough indicator of body composition, interpreted by age and sex for children.

## Commonly confused

- *Muscular strength* is the maximum force in one effort; *muscular endurance* is the ability to repeat.
- *Speed* is moving fast in a straight line; *agility* is changing direction quickly.
- *Power* is applying force quickly; it is not the same as *strength*.

## Study tip

Write a test and a school activity next to each component. Looking at the duration and type of movement in a question's activity quickly gives you the component.
