## Core idea

Microeconomics studies how **individual decision units** (consumers, firms, markets) make choices under scarce resources. Its core logic is **marginal analysis**: keep going until the extra benefit of one more unit equals its extra cost. With **12 questions** it is the heaviest subject in the test. Questions usually involve a simple calculation (elasticity, equilibrium, profit) or a comparison of market structures.

## Supply, demand and elasticity

- **Law of demand:** when price rises, quantity demanded falls. A price change is a movement **along** the curve; a change in income or tastes **shifts** it.
- **Price elasticity:** %ΔQ / %ΔP. If the absolute value is above 1, demand is **elastic**; with elastic demand, a price cut raises total revenue.
- **Income elasticity:** positive for **normal** goods, negative for **inferior** goods, above 1 for **luxury** goods.
- **Cross elasticity:** positive for **substitutes**, negative for **complements**.
- A **price ceiling** creates shortages (black markets), a **price floor** creates surpluses. A tax falls more heavily on the **less elastic** side.

## Consumer theory

- **Cardinal approach:** equilibrium is MUx / Px = MUy / Py; **diminishing marginal utility** applies.
- **Ordinal approach:** an **indifference curve** shows bundles with equal utility; its slope is the **marginal rate of substitution** (MRS). Equilibrium is where the indifference curve is tangent to the **budget line**: MRS = Px / Py.
- **Substitution and income effects:** the substitution effect always moves against price. For an inferior good the income effect works the other way; if it outweighs the substitution effect, the good is a **Giffen good** with an upward-sloping demand curve.
- **Consumer surplus:** the gap between what a buyer is willing to pay and what they pay.

## Production and cost

- **Law of diminishing returns:** in the short run, adding a variable input to a fixed one eventually lowers marginal product. MP crosses AP at AP's **maximum**.
- **Long run:** all inputs are variable; at the tangency of the **isoquant** and isocost line, MRTS = w / r. **Returns to scale** can be increasing, constant or decreasing.
- **Costs:** TC = TFC + TVC. The **marginal cost** (MC) curve crosses AVC and AC at their **minimum** points.
- **Economic profit** includes opportunity cost, so it is smaller than accounting profit. Zero economic profit is called **normal profit**.

## Market structures

- **Perfect competition:** many firms, homogeneous goods, free entry and exit; the firm is a **price taker**, P = MR. Equilibrium is P = MC. If price falls below **minimum AVC**, the firm stops producing in the short run (**shutdown point**). In the long run P = min LAC and economic profit is zero.
- **Monopoly:** one seller, entry barriers; MR < P. Profit is maximised at **MR = MC**, but since P > MC there is a **deadweight loss**. A monopolist produces on the **elastic** part of demand. Market power is measured by the **Lerner index** (P − MC) / P. Price discrimination is first, second or third degree.
- **Monopolistic competition (Chamberlin):** differentiated products, free entry; zero profit in the long run but **excess capacity**.
- **Oligopoly:** few interdependent firms. **Cournot** firms compete in quantity, **Bertrand** firms in price (with homogeneous goods Bertrand drives P down to MC). **Stackelberg** is a leader-follower model. The **kinked demand curve** (Sweezy) explains price rigidity. A **cartel** maximises joint profit.
- **Game theory:** in a **Nash equilibrium**, no player wants to change strategy given the other's strategy. In the **prisoners' dilemma**, individual rationality leads to a jointly worse outcome.

## Factor markets and welfare

- **Factor demand:** a firm hires labour until **marginal revenue product** (MRP = MP × MR) equals the wage. Under **monopsony** (a single buyer), wages and employment are lower than under competition.
- **Pareto efficiency:** an allocation is efficient if no one can be made better off without making someone worse off.
- **Market failures:** **externalities** (a Pigouvian tax or Coase's bargaining solution), **public goods** (non-rival and non-excludable; the free-rider problem), **asymmetric information**.

## Commonly confused

- *Adverse selection* is information asymmetry **before** a contract; *moral hazard* is **after** it.
- An *inferior good* is not always a *Giffen good*; Giffen requires the income effect to outweigh the substitution effect.
- The *shutdown point* is at min AVC; the *break-even point* is at min AC.

## Study tip

For every market structure answer the same four questions: number of firms, type of good, entry conditions and the equilibrium rule (P = MC or MR = MC?). In numerical questions find MR and MC first; most answers come from that equality.
