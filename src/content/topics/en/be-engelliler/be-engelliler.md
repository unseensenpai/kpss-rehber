## Core idea

Adapted physical education (*uyarlanmış beden eğitimi*) changes activities, equipment, rules and teaching methods so that students with special needs can **take part safely and actively**. The aim is not to leave the student out but to secure **participation**. The test has **2 questions**; they usually ask for a suitable adaptation for a type of disability.

## Basic concepts

- **Inclusion** (*kaynaştırma*): a student with special needs learning in the same class as peers.
- **Least restrictive environment:** the setting that meets the student's needs while separating them from peers as little as possible.
- **IEP** (*BEP*): individual goals and adaptations prepared by a team for the student's level. Check the detailed procedures in current MEB regulations.

## Adaptations by type of disability

- **Visual impairment:** a ball with a bell or sound, verbal and tactile guidance, a guide partner, a guide rope; a fixed, obstacle-free space.
- **Hearing impairment:** visual signals (flag, light, hand signs), demonstration, positions where the student can see the teacher's face.
- **Intellectual disability:** simple, short instructions, breaking skills into small steps, plenty of repetition and positive reinforcement.
- **Orthopaedic impairment:** a smaller area, lighter or larger equipment, playing seated, relaxed rules.
- **Autism spectrum disorder:** predictable routines, visual schedules, reduced sensory stimulation.

## Sports and organisations

- **Goalball:** a team sport developed for people with visual impairments, played with a ball that makes sound.
- **Boccia:** a target sport that athletes with severe physical impairments can also play.
- The **Paralympic Games** are the elite competition for athletes with physical and visual impairments; the **Special Olympics** serve people with intellectual disabilities.

## Commonly confused

- *Inclusion* concerns the setting; *adaptation* is changing the activity.
- The *Paralympic Games* are elite competition; the *Special Olympics* are a participation-focused organisation for people with intellectual disabilities.

## Study tip

For each disability, ask "which sense or skill is limited, and what makes up for it?" The right option is usually the adaptation that keeps the student included and uses another channel in place of the limited one.
