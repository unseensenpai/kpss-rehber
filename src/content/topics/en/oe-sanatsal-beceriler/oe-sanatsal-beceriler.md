## Core idea

In special education, art activities (*sanatsal beceriler*) are a strong teaching area that supports **fine motor**, language, social and sensory development as well as self-expression. What matters is that the student **takes part in the process** and can make their own choices. There are **2 questions**; they ask about the aims of activities and about adaptations.

## Aims

- **Fine motor skills:** cutting, gluing, painting, kneading; hand-eye coordination.
- **Communication and language:** naming colours, shapes and textures; describing what they made.
- **Social skills:** sharing materials, waiting for a turn, making a group product.
- **Original expression and choice:** choosing colours, materials and subjects supports self-determination.

## Teaching

- **Process-focused approach:** experience and participation matter; everyone is not expected to make the same product.
- **Task analysis:** a complex activity (e.g. a collage) is broken into steps; prompts are given if needed and faded over time.
- **Drama and role-play:** used for recognising emotions, acting out social situations and using language.

## Adaptations

- **Motor difficulties:** thick-handled brushes and pencils, spring-loaded scissors, non-slip surfaces, taped-down paper.
- **Visual impairment:** **tactile** materials (clay, raised lines, varied textures), strong colour contrast, a work area with clear edges.
- **ASD:** visual instructions, predictable routines, materials chosen with sensory sensitivities in mind.

## Commonly confused

- *Process-focused* art emphasises participation and experience; *product-focused* art emphasises the finished work.
- *Art education* is skill and expression teaching led by the teacher; *art therapy* is a therapeutic intervention that requires specialist training.

## Study tip

Options in which everyone must make the same product, or the teacher does the work for the student, are usually wrong; go for the option that increases participation and choice.
