## Core idea

Comprehension and expression cover the **receptive** (reading, listening/viewing) and **productive** (speaking, writing) language skills. It is the heaviest part of the Turkish ÖABT: **16** of the 50 questions come from here. Every text is treated as an **act of communication**: who is telling whom, for what purpose and by what means? Questions usually give a paragraph and ask for its main idea, mode of discourse or way of developing ideas; others ask you to judge reading and writing strategies from a teacher's point of view.

## Meaning from word to paragraph

- **Word meaning:** literal, extended and figurative meaning; technical terms; synonyms, antonyms and homonyms; idioms, proverbs and *ikileme* (reduplicated pairs).
- **Sentence meaning:** relations such as cause-effect, purpose-result, condition and comparison; subjective and objective judgements, assumption and prejudice.
- **Paragraph structure:** opening, development and closing sentences; the **main idea** (the writer's core message) and **supporting ideas**; the topic and title of a paragraph.
- **Flow:** ordering sentences, inserting a sentence and spotting the one that breaks the flow.

## Modes of discourse and ways of developing ideas

- **Exposition (*açıklama*):** gives information; the basic mode of informative texts.
- **Argumentation (*tartışma*):** tries to refute one view and prove another.
- **Description (*betimleme*):** paints a picture with words and appeals to the senses.
- **Narration (*öyküleme*):** tells events along time and characters; there is movement.
- **Ways of developing ideas:** definition, exemplification, comparison, **citing authority** (*tanık gösterme*), using numerical data, analogy.

## The four basic language skills

- **Reading:** pre-reading (predicting, setting a purpose), while-reading (note-taking, questioning, monitoring meaning) and post-reading (summarising, evaluating) strategies; reading aloud, silent, critical and creative reading.
- **Listening/viewing:** active listening, note-taking, summarising and questioning what was heard.
- **Speaking:** prepared and impromptu speech; stress, intonation, body language.
- **Writing:** **process writing** with planning, drafting, revising and publishing stages; writing suited to the text type.

## Text and textuality

- **Text types:** informative (article, essay, column, news) and literary (short story, poem) texts.
- **Standards of textuality:** **cohesion** (grammatical links on the surface: reference, conjunctions, ellipsis), **coherence** (logical unity of meaning), intentionality, acceptability, informativity, situationality, intertextuality.

## Commonly confused

- The *main idea* is the writer's message; the *topic* is what the text is about.
- *Cohesion* is formal linkage; *coherence* is unity of meaning.
- *Exposition* conveys information; *argumentation* aims to refute an opposing view.

## Study tip

For every paragraph question, first ask "Why did the writer write this?" For mode-of-discourse questions, look for movement (narration), images (description) and an opposing view (argumentation).
