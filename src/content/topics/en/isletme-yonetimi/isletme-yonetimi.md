## Core idea

Management is using people and resources effectively to reach an organisation's goals through **planning, organising, directing, coordinating and controlling**. The backbone of this subject is the historical development of management thought plus the motivation and leadership theories that explain human behaviour at work. The test has **10 questions**; they mostly ask you to link a principle to the right thinker, match a theory with an example, or tell structural concepts apart.

## Management functions

- **Planning:** setting goals and the ways to reach them in advance; mission, vision, strategy, policy and budget belong here. **SWOT analysis** examines strengths and weaknesses (internal) and opportunities and threats (external).
- **Organising:** dividing and grouping work, assigning authority and responsibility.
- **Directing** (*yöneltme*): moving employees to act towards the goals.
- **Coordinating:** harmonising the efforts of different units.
- **Controlling** (*denetim*): comparing results with standards and correcting deviations.

## Development of management thought

- **Scientific management (Taylor):** finding the one best way through time and motion studies, division of labour, pay by performance; productivity at shop-floor level.
- **Administrative process (Fayol):** divides management into functions and proposes 14 principles (division of work, unity of command, unity of direction, centralisation, scalar chain and others).
- **Bureaucracy (Weber):** an ideal organisation based on written rules, hierarchy, specialisation and impersonal relations.
- **Neoclassical approach:** the **Hawthorne studies** (Mayo) showed that workers' social needs and informal groups affect output; human relations came to the fore.
- **Modern approaches:** the **systems approach** sees the organisation as an open system interacting with its environment (input-process-output-feedback). The **contingency approach** holds that there is no single best way; the right method depends on the situation.
- **Management by objectives (Drucker):** manager and employee set goals together and performance is judged against them.

## Organising

- **Authority** is the right to decide and command; **responsibility** is the duty to do the work. Authority can be delegated, ultimate responsibility cannot.
- **Span of control:** the number of people reporting directly to a manager. A narrow span gives a tall structure, a wide span a flat one.
- **Centralisation** keeps decisions at the top; **decentralisation** pushes them to lower levels.
- **Departmentalisation:** by function, product, region or customer. A **matrix organisation** combines functional and project structures; employees have two bosses.
- **Line** units serve the goal directly; **staff** units advise and have no command authority.

## Motivation and leadership

- **Maslow:** physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualisation; once a lower need is met, the next one motivates.
- **Herzberg (two-factor):** absence of **hygiene factors** (pay, working conditions, supervision) causes dissatisfaction, but their presence does not motivate; **motivators** (achievement, recognition, the work itself, responsibility) do.
- **McGregor:** **Theory X** assumes people avoid work and need tight control; **Theory Y** assumes they are willing to take responsibility.
- **McClelland:** needs for achievement, power and affiliation.
- **Vroom (expectancy):** motivation depends on the expectation that effort leads to performance and performance to reward, and on the value of the reward. **Adams (equity):** people compare their own input-output ratio with others'.
- **Leadership:** the **trait** approach looks at the leader's personal qualities; the **behavioural** approach (Ohio, Michigan, Blake and Mouton's managerial grid) at task- and people-oriented behaviour; the **situational** approach (Fiedler, Hersey-Blanchard, House's path-goal theory) at the style that works in a given situation.

## Commonly confused

- *Unity of command* means each employee takes orders from one superior; *unity of direction* means activities with the same goal fall under one plan and one manager.
- *Hygiene factors* prevent dissatisfaction; *motivators* motivate.
- *Taylor* studies work at the shop floor; *Fayol* studies management at the top.
