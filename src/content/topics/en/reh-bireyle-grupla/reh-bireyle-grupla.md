## Core idea

Counselling is a purposeful, staged process carried out within a trusting relationship between client and counsellor. In group counselling, **interaction among members** is an additional source of change. The test has 7 questions; they usually give an excerpt from a session or group and ask which stage, therapeutic factor or leadership skill it shows.

## The individual counselling process

- **Building the relationship and structuring:** a climate of trust; explaining duration, confidentiality and its limits, and roles.
- **Exploration and assessment:** understanding the client's problem and resources.
- **Goal setting:** concrete, observable goals agreed with the client.
- **Intervention and action:** using techniques suited to the theory.
- **Termination and follow-up:** reviewing gains, preparing for ending, a follow-up session if needed.

## Types and structure of groups

- **Psychoeducational groups:** teaching knowledge and skills (coping with test anxiety, communication skills); the most common type at school.
- **Counselling groups:** working on interpersonal problems and developmental issues.
- **Psychotherapy groups** and **task groups** also appear in the classification.
- An **open group** accepts new members; a **closed group** keeps the same members from start to finish.
- **Pre-group interview:** assessing members' suitability and preparing them for the group.

## Group stages and therapeutic factors

- **Initial stage:** getting acquainted, anxiety, seeking trust, setting rules.
- **Transition stage:** resistance, struggles for control, challenging the leader.
- **Working stage:** high cohesion; members take risks and work on their goals.
- **Termination stage:** summarising what was learned and carrying it into daily life.
- **Yalom's therapeutic factors:** instillation of hope, **universality** (seeing the problem is not unique to me), imparting information, altruism, corrective recapitulation of the family group, development of social skills, imitative behaviour, interpersonal learning, **group cohesiveness**, catharsis, existential factors.

## Leadership skills

- **Linking:** connecting members' similar experiences.
- **Blocking:** stopping harmful behaviour (gossip, interrogation, blaming).
- **Cutting off and drawing out:** limiting a monopolising member, involving a silent one.
- **Modelling, summarising, terminating** and explaining the limits of confidentiality in groups.

## Commonly confused

- The *transition stage* features resistance and conflict, the *working stage* cohesion and risk-taking.
- *Universality* is the feeling "I am not alone", *group cohesiveness* the sense of belonging and commitment to the group.
- A *psychoeducational group* is structured and information-focused; a *counselling group* works on personal experiences.

## Study tip

In group excerpts, watch how members treat the leader and each other: challenging the leader signals the transition stage, members giving each other feedback signals the working stage.
