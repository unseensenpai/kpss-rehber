## Core idea

History studies the activities of past human societies by giving **place and time**, **relying on sources** and tracing **cause and effect**. Events do not repeat and cannot be tested by experiment, so the historian's basic tool is the source and its criticism. The test has 4 questions; they usually ask about types of sources, auxiliary sciences, stages of criticism and schools of historiography.

## Sources and criticism

- **Primary source:** a document, inscription, coin, archival record or memoir from the time of the event.
- **Secondary source:** a later work written on the basis of primary sources.
- **External criticism:** tests whether a document is **authentic** (script, paper, seal, language).
- **Internal criticism:** tests whether its **content** is accurate and its author reliable.
- **Anachronism:** placing an event or idea in a time it does not belong to.

## Auxiliary sciences

- **Palaeography** studies old scripts, **diplomatics** the form and formulae of official documents, **epigraphy** inscriptions, **numismatics** coins and **chronology** the reckoning of time.
- Archaeology, philology, geography, anthropology and ethnography also supply evidence.

## Historiography and calendars

- **Narrative:** tells events; Herodotus is named at the start of this tradition.
- **Pragmatic (didactic):** aims to draw lessons; Thucydides stands out for his search for causes.
- **Scientific (critical):** explains events through source criticism and cause and effect.
- **Ibn Khaldun:** in the *Muqaddimah* he explained the rise and fall of societies with *asabiyya* (group solidarity) and *umran* (civilisation).
- **Ranke:** archive-based history that aims to show the past "as it actually was".
- **Annales school:** focuses on economy, society and **long-term structures** rather than political event history.
- **Calendars:** the **Hijri calendar** is lunar and starts from the Hijra (622); the **Rumi calendar** was the solar Ottoman fiscal calendar; the **Jalali calendar** was prepared under the Great Seljuks; Turkey has used the Gregorian calendar since **1926**.

## Commonly confused

- *External criticism* asks whether a document is genuine; *internal criticism* asks whether what it says is true.
- *Palaeography* reads the script itself; *diplomatics* studies a document's official form.
- The *Hijri calendar* is lunar; the *Rumi calendar* is solar.

## Study tip

Pair each auxiliary science with an object (coin, inscription, decree). When a question shows the object, you can name the science at once.
