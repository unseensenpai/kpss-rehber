## Core idea

**Sociology of religion** does not debate whether religion is true; it studies the two-way interaction between religion and society with methods of observation and research. Its central questions are how religion shapes society and how social change affects religious life. The test has 2 questions; they usually ask you to match sociologists with views or about key concepts.

## Classical theorists

- **Auguste Comte:** the **law of three stages**; thought passes through theological, metaphysical and positive stages.
- **Karl Marx:** saw religion as an ideology produced by the economic base that legitimises inequality.
- **Émile Durkheim:** *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life*; defined religion through the distinction of the **sacred** and the **profane**. He studied Aboriginal Australian **totemism** and stressed that religion produces social integration.
- **Max Weber:** *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism*; showed that religious ideas can shape economic behaviour. He distinguished **charismatic**, traditional and legal authority and used an interpretive (*verstehen*) method.

## Religious groups and change

- **Church:** a large institution broadly accommodated to society, with membership by birth. **Sect:** a small group critical of society, with voluntary membership. **Cult:** usually a new group tied to a charismatic leader.
- **Secularisation:** the decline of religion's influence on social institutions and individual life; **Peter Berger** first defended the thesis, then revised it in light of religious revival.
- **Civil religion:** symbols and ceremonies expressing a society's shared values in sacred language (Robert Bellah).
- **Turkey:** **Ziya Gökalp**, influenced by Durkheim, addressed the social functions of religion.

## Commonly confused

- *Durkheim* focuses on religion's integrating function, *Weber* on its power to drive social change.
- A *church* is accommodating and inclusive, a *sect* critical and selective.

## Study tip

Match the four classical theorists with one key concept each (Comte–three stages, Marx–ideology, Durkheim–sacred and profane, Weber–Protestant ethic); most questions are solved by this matching.
