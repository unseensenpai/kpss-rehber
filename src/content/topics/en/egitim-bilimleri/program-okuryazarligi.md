## Core idea

Curriculum literacy is a teacher's ability to **read, understand and adapt** a curriculum to the classroom. Every curriculum answers four questions: **What will students gain** (objectives/outcomes)? **What will we teach** (content)? **How will we teach** (teaching-learning process)? **How will we know they learned** (assessment)? Questions most often ask about the level of an outcome and how content is organised.

## Types of curriculum

- **Educational programme:** all planned activities in and out of school.
- **Instructional programme:** the part of the educational programme planned for teaching at school.
- **Course programme:** the programme of a single course.
- **Hidden curriculum:** attitudes and values learned through school culture though never written down.
- **Null (neglected) curriculum:** topics that could be taught but are left out.

## Taxonomies of objectives

- **Cognitive domain (Bloom):** knowledge, comprehension, application, analysis, synthesis, evaluation. In the revised taxonomy: remember, understand, apply, analyse, evaluate, **create**.
- **Affective domain (Krathwohl):** receiving, responding, valuing, organisation, characterisation.
- **Psychomotor domain:** perception, guided response, mechanism, adaptation, origination.
- Levels are **cumulative**: a higher level requires the ones below it.

## Organising content

- **Linear:** topics follow one another and each is taught once.
- **Spiral:** the same topic returns in later grades in greater depth.
- **Modular:** content is split into independent units.
- **Pyramid:** a broad base first, then specialisation.

## Curriculum development

- Process: **needs analysis → setting objectives → selecting and organising content → planning learning experiences → evaluation**.
- **Tyler model:** Which objectives should the school pursue? Which experiences serve them? How should they be organised? How do we know the objectives were reached?
- **Design approaches:** subject-centred (puts knowledge first), learner-centred (interests and needs), problem-centred (social problems).
- The context of current curricula is set by the programmes updated under the Türkiye Century Education Model; see that topic.

## Commonly confused

- *Comprehension* means explaining knowledge in your own words; *application* means transferring it to a new situation.
- *Valuing* means adopting and preferring a value; *characterisation* means turning it into a consistent way of life.
- In *spiral* organisation topics recur; in *linear* organisation they do not.

## Study tip

Look at the verb in an outcome: "lists" is knowledge, "explains" comprehension, "calculates" application, "compares" analysis, "designs" synthesis or creation, "critiques" evaluation. With this verb list you can classify outcomes in questions quickly.
