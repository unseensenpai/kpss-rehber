## Core idea

Guidance is systematic, professional help for a person to **know themselves**, solve their problems and **realise their potential**. It does not decide **for** the person; it helps them make their own decisions. Today's approach is **developmental and preventive**: instead of waiting for problems, it supports every student's development. Questions usually describe a school practice and ask which service or type it is.

## Principles

- Guidance is for **every student**, not only those with problems.
- **Voluntariness** is essential; no one is forced.
- **Confidentiality** is protected.
- The individual is valuable and **makes their own decisions**.
- Guidance is continuous, part of education and a team effort at school.

## Types and services

- **Types:** **educational** (learning, course choice, adjusting to school), **career** (exploring and choosing occupations), **personal-social** (self-knowledge, relationships).
- **Appraisal:** gathering information about students.
- **Information service:** about schools, careers and educational opportunities.
- **Counselling:** individual or group help led by a specialist.
- **Orientation and placement:** directing a person to a programme or activity suited to their abilities.
- **Follow-up:** tracking how a placed person is doing.
- **Consultation:** helping teachers and families.
- **Referral:** sending a person to a specialist institution when the school cannot help.
- **Preventive services:** acting before problems arise (addiction, bullying).

## Techniques for getting to know students

- **Non-test techniques:** observation, **anecdotal records** (a factual record of a behaviour), interviews, questionnaires, autobiography, **sociometry** (shows relationships and choices within a group), **"Guess who?"** (students name classmates who fit descriptions), rating scales.
- **Test techniques:** aptitude, achievement, interest and personality tests; administered by specialists.

## Theories

- **Rogers (client-centred):** in a climate of unconditional acceptance, empathy and genuineness, the person finds their own solution.
- **Holland:** personality types match work environments: realistic, investigative, artistic, social, enterprising, conventional.
- **Super:** career choice reflects a self-concept that develops over a lifetime.

## The teacher's role at school

- The **homeroom guidance teacher** runs class guidance activities, gets to know students and refers them to the school counsellor when needed.
- Teachers do **not** provide counselling; they refer cases that need expertise.

## Commonly confused

- *Referral* sends a person to an outside specialist; *orientation and placement* directs them to a suitable programme.
- *Sociometry* reveals relationships in a group; *"Guess who?"* shows how students see each other.
- *Preventive* guidance comes before problems arise, *remedial* guidance after.

## Study tip

Think of a school example for each service (a careers day is information, high-school choice work is placement, a meeting with parents is consultation). Matching the scene in a question to one of these examples is the fastest way to the answer.
