## Core idea

Teaching has three layers: **strategy** (the general approach to reach a goal), **method** (the way to apply it) and **technique** (the concrete classroom practice that carries out the method). Questions usually describe what a teacher does in class and ask what it is called. The deciding clue is **who produces the knowledge and how**: does the teacher present it, does the student discover it, or does the student investigate it?

## Principles of teaching

- **From concrete to abstract, known to unknown, simple to complex, near to far.**
- **Student-centredness:** teaching is arranged around students' readiness and individual differences.
- Principles of **relevance to life, activity, clarity and economy** are also often asked.

## Teaching strategies

- **Expository teaching (Ausubel):** the teacher presents knowledge in an organised way, moving from general to specific (deduction); the lesson starts with an **advance organiser**. Aims at meaningful learning.
- **Discovery learning (Bruner):** students reach generalisations from examples (induction); the teacher guides.
- **Inquiry (Dewey):** students solve a problem through the steps of the scientific method; problem-solving skills grow.

## Methods and techniques

- **Lecture:** passes on a lot of information quickly; students are passive.
- **Discussion:** builds critical thinking through exchange of views.
- **Demonstration and practice:** used for teaching skills.
- **Case study:** a real or realistic situation is examined and solutions are sought.
- **Problem solving and projects:** students work on a real problem over a longer period.
- **Techniques:** brainstorming (generating ideas without criticism), six thinking hats (different ways of thinking), fishbone (cause and effect), stations, drama and role play, simulation, question and answer, field trips.

## Instructional models

- **Mastery learning (Bloom):** under suitable conditions most students can learn. Elements: cognitive entry behaviours, affective entry characteristics, quality of instruction (cues, participation, reinforcement, feedback-correction).
- **Model of school learning (Carroll):** the level of learning depends on the ratio of time **spent** to time **needed**.
- **Gagné's events of instruction:** gain attention, inform of the objective, recall prior knowledge, present the stimulus, guide learning, elicit performance, give feedback, assess performance, enhance retention and transfer.

## Commonly confused

- *Expository teaching* moves deductively (general to specific), *discovery* inductively (specific to general).
- A *strategy* is a general approach; a *technique* is a concrete classroom practice. Brainstorming is a technique.
- A *case study* examines a situation; *problem solving* produces a solution to a problem.

## Study tip

For each strategy, answer "what do the teacher and the student do at the start of the lesson?" Once you find those two roles in the classroom scene of a question, the right option follows.
