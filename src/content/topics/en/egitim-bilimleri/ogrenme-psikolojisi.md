## Core idea

Learning is a **relatively lasting** change in behaviour that results **from experience**. Changes caused by growth, maturation, fatigue or drugs do not count. Theories explain learning from different angles: **behaviourists** look at observable behaviour and the environment, **cognitivists** at mental processes, **social learning** at observing others. Questions almost always give an example and ask which concept it fits.

## Classical conditioning (Pavlov, Watson)

- An **unconditioned stimulus (US)** causes a natural response; paired with it, a **neutral stimulus** becomes a **conditioned stimulus (CS)** that causes the response on its own.
- **Generalisation:** giving the same response to similar stimuli. **Discrimination:** responding only to a specific stimulus.
- **Extinction:** the response weakens when the CS is repeated without the US. **Spontaneous recovery:** an extinguished response reappears after a while.
- Emotional responses (fear, anxiety) are often learned this way.

## Operant conditioning (Thorndike, Skinner)

- The **consequence** of a behaviour determines how likely it is to recur (Thorndike: law of effect).
- **Positive reinforcement:** a pleasant stimulus is **given**; the behaviour increases.
- **Negative reinforcement:** an unpleasant stimulus is **removed**; the behaviour **increases**. (It is not a form of punishment.)
- **Punishment:** aims to reduce behaviour; an unpleasant stimulus is given (type 1) or a pleasant one is taken away (type 2).
- **Schedules of reinforcement:** continuous reinforcement teaches fast; intermittent reinforcement (ratio or interval; fixed or variable) makes behaviour resistant to extinction.

## Social learning (Bandura)

- People learn by **observing** others. Process: **attention → retention → reproduction → motivation**.
- **Vicarious reinforcement:** seeing a model rewarded also affects the observer.
- **Self-efficacy:** a person's belief that they can succeed at a task.

## Cognitive theories and memory

- **Information processing:** sensory register → **short-term (working) memory** (limited capacity, about 7 ± 2 items) → **long-term memory**. Attention moves information from the sensory register to short-term memory; meaningful encoding and rehearsal move it to long-term memory.
- **Gestalt:** learning can happen through grasping the whole at once (**insight**). **Tolman:** learning can happen without reinforcement and show up when needed (**latent learning**).
- **Constructivism:** learners **build** knowledge themselves on top of prior knowledge.
- **Forgetting:** decay (unused traces fade), **interference** (proactive when old learning blocks new, retroactive when new learning blocks old), motivated forgetting.

## Motivation

- **Intrinsic motivation** comes from enjoying the activity itself; **extrinsic motivation** from external factors such as rewards or grades.
- **Maslow:** physiological → safety → belonging → esteem → self-actualisation.
- **Weiner (attribution):** what people attribute success or failure to (ability, effort, luck, task difficulty) shapes future effort.

## Commonly confused

- *Negative reinforcement* **increases** behaviour; *punishment* decreases it.
- In *classical conditioning* the stimulus comes before the response; in *operant conditioning* the consequence comes after the behaviour.
- In *proactive interference* old learning blocks new; in *retroactive interference* new learning blocks old.

## Study tip

Ask two questions of every example: "Does the behaviour increase or decrease?" and "Is something given or taken away?" The answers settle the type of reinforcement or punishment.
