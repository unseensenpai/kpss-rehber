## Core idea

**Measurement** is observing a trait and expressing the result with numbers or symbols; **evaluation** is comparing that result with a **criterion** and making a decision. Without measurement there is no evaluation, but measurement alone is not a decision. Questions ask about the measurement problem in a classroom situation (reliability or validity?) or what a distribution tells us.

## Scales of measurement

- **Nominal:** only sorts into groups (sex, shirt numbers).
- **Ordinal:** shows rank, but intervals are not equal (race placings).
- **Interval:** equal intervals but no absolute zero (temperature in °C, exam scores).
- **Ratio:** has an absolute zero; ratios are meaningful (height, mass).

## Errors, reliability and validity

- **Constant error:** the same amount in every measurement. **Systematic error:** in one direction, affecting certain people (a teacher who always grades high). **Random error:** occurs by chance (fatigue, luck).
- **Reliability:** the **consistency** of results; freedom from random error. Methods: test-retest, parallel forms, split-half, internal consistency (KR-20, Cronbach's alpha), inter-rater agreement.
- **Validity:** a tool **measures what it intends to measure**. Content validity (covering the topics), construct validity, criterion-related validity (predictive, concurrent).
- A valid measurement must be reliable; a reliable one may not be valid.

## Types of evaluation

- **Diagnostic:** at the start of teaching, identifies readiness and learning difficulties.
- **Formative:** during teaching, finds and fixes gaps; not for grading.
- **Summative:** at the end, determines achievement and assigns grades.
- **Criterion-referenced (absolute)** evaluation uses a fixed standard; **norm-referenced (relative)** evaluation uses the group's performance.

## Item analysis and distributions

- **Item difficulty (p):** the share of students answering correctly; between 0 and 1, and **the higher it is, the easier the item**.
- **Discrimination (d):** how well an item separates high and low achievers; 0.40 and above is very good.
- **Central tendency:** mean, median, mode. **Spread:** range and standard deviation; a small standard deviation means a homogeneous group.
- **Right (positively) skewed distribution:** scores pile up at the low end; the test is **hard** (mode < median < mean). **Left (negatively) skewed:** the test is **easy**.

## Alternative assessment tools

- Performance tasks, projects, **portfolios**, **rubrics**, checklists, self and peer assessment. They make the process and higher-order skills visible.

## Commonly confused

- *Reliability* is consistency; *validity* is measuring the right thing.
- *Formative* assessment is during the process and not for grades; *summative* is at the end and for grades.
- The higher the difficulty index, the **easier** the item; the name is misleading.

## Study tip

In every question first ask: "Is the problem consistency, or what is being measured?" That split solves most reliability-validity questions. For skewness, think of the tail: if the tail is on the right, most students scored low.
