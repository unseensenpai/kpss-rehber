## Core idea

Educational technology is not just devices; it is the science of **using knowledge, methods, tools and people in a planned way** to make learning more effective. The right material is the one that suits the **objective**, the **students** and the **setting**. Questions usually describe a teacher's choice or design of material and ask which principle or model it fits.

## Communication

- Teaching is communication: **source** (teacher) → **message** (content) → **channel** (medium) → **receiver** (student), with **noise** that disrupts the process and **feedback** returning to the source.
- In effective teaching the message is encoded at the receiver's level and feedback is gathered constantly.

## Dale's cone of experience

- From concrete to abstract: **direct purposeful experiences** (learning by doing) → models and simulations → dramatisations → demonstrations → field trips → exhibits → films and images → pictures and recordings → visual symbols → **verbal symbols**.
- The closer to the concrete end, the more lasting the learning, but time and cost rise. Choose the level that fits the topic and the students.

## Selecting and designing materials

- **Selection criteria:** fit with the objective, student level, cost, availability, ease of use, time.
- **Design principles:** simplicity, consistency, **emphasis** (highlighting what matters), balance, legibility, use of colour and white space.
- **Multimedia principles (Mayer):** words and pictures together improve learning (**multimedia**); related text and images belong side by side (**contiguity**); extra elements should be removed (**redundancy/coherence**); with animation, narration beats on-screen text (**modality**).

## Instructional design models

- **ADDIE:** Analysis → Design → Development → Implementation → Evaluation.
- **ASSURE:** Analyse learners → State objectives → Select methods, media and materials → Utilise them → Require learner participation → Evaluate and revise.

## New learning environments

- **Distance education:** synchronous (live lessons) or asynchronous (recorded content).
- **Blended learning:** a mix of face-to-face and online learning.
- **Flipped classroom:** students learn the topic at home (videos, reading) and class time goes to practice and discussion.
- **Digital literacy and digital citizenship:** finding, evaluating and using information correctly, safely and ethically; respecting copyright.

## Commonly confused

- *Educational technology* covers the whole process, *instructional technology* the teaching process; neither is just tools.
- A *flipped classroom* swaps class and home activities; *blended learning* combines two settings.
- In Dale's cone, *verbal symbols* are the most abstract and *direct purposeful experiences* the most concrete.

## Study tip

Memorise the ADDIE and ASSURE steps by their initials, then write a classroom example for each. Questions usually describe one step a teacher takes and ask which it is.
