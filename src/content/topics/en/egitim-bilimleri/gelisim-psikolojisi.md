## Core idea

Development is **orderly and continuous** change from conception to death. For a teacher it matters because it shows **what a student is ready to learn and when**. Questions usually describe a child's behaviour ("she says the water decreased when it was poured into another glass") and ask which theory, stage or concept it fits.

## Basic concepts and principles

- **Growth:** quantitative increase in the body. **Maturation:** readiness set by heredity, independent of learning. **Readiness:** the maturity and prior knowledge needed for new learning.
- **Critical period:** the window when a trait is acquired most easily.
- **Principles:** development runs head to toe and centre outwards, from general to specific; it is continuous but not equally fast at every stage; it differs between individuals; it comes from the interaction of heredity and environment.

## Cognitive development

- **Piaget:** a child fits new information into existing schemas (**assimilation**) or changes the schema (**accommodation**); this achieves **equilibration**.
  - **Sensorimotor (0–2):** object permanence is acquired.
  - **Preoperational (2–7):** symbolic play, **egocentrism**, attributing life to objects; no conservation.
  - **Concrete operational (7–11):** **conservation**, classification, seriation and reversibility; thinking is tied to the concrete.
  - **Formal operational (11+):** hypothetical and abstract thinking, forming hypotheses.
- **Vygotsky:** development happens through **social interaction**. The gap between what a child can do alone and with help is the **zone of proximal development**; an adult's temporary support is **scaffolding**. Language is the tool of thought.

## Personality and psychosocial development

- **Erikson** (a crisis at each stage): basic trust vs mistrust → autonomy vs shame → initiative vs guilt → **industry vs inferiority** (school age) → **identity vs role confusion** (adolescence) → intimacy vs isolation → generativity vs stagnation → integrity vs despair.
- **Freud:** oral, anal, phallic, latency (school age) and genital stages.

## Moral development and ecological theory

- **Kohlberg:** **preconventional** level (punishment and reward), **conventional** level (approval of others, following rules and laws), **postconventional** level (universal principles and social contract).
- **Bronfenbrenner:** a child is shaped by nested systems: micro (family, school), meso (links between them), exo (indirect settings, a parent's workplace), macro (culture), chrono (time).

## Commonly confused

- *Assimilation* leaves the schema unchanged; *accommodation* changes it.
- *Egocentrism* belongs to the preoperational stage; *acquiring conservation* to the concrete operational stage.
- The *zone of proximal development* is a range; *scaffolding* is the support within it.

## Study tip

Put Piaget's and Erikson's stages on one timeline by age: ages 7–11 are both concrete operations and industry. Recalling both theories when you see an age in a question eliminates options quickly.
