## Core idea

Education is the process of bringing about desired changes in a person's behaviour **deliberately, through their own experience**. This topic asks the question behind every school practice: **Why do we teach this way?** The answer comes from philosophy (what is true and valuable), society (which culture is passed on), economics (why we invest in education) and politics (who gets which education). Questions usually ask you to match a practice with the related philosophy or concept.

## Basic concepts

- **Education:** a planned, deliberate process of changing behaviour; it can happen in and out of school.
- **Instruction:** learning carried out in a planned way at school.
- **Deliberate enculturation:** a society passing its culture to new generations through planned means; education itself.
- **Formal / informal education:** education in planned institutions such as schools versus learning that happens spontaneously in the family and surroundings.

## Philosophical schools and educational philosophies

- **Idealism:** reality is unchanging ideas. Teacher-centred education based on classic works and reason.
- **Realism:** reality is an objective world independent of us. Education based on science and observation.
- **Pragmatism:** what is true is what works. Learning through experience and problem solving (Dewey).
- **Existentialism:** the individual is free and responsible for their choices. Focuses on students finding their own meaning.
- **Perennialism:** unchanging universal truths and classic works should be taught.
- **Essentialism:** the core knowledge and skills of the culture should be passed on; order and discipline matter.
- **Progressivism:** student-centred; learning by doing and solving problems.
- **Reconstructionism:** school is a tool for changing and rebuilding society.

## Social, economic and political foundations

- **Socialisation:** becoming a member of society by learning its values and rules.
- **Acculturation:** different cultures interacting and borrowing from each other. **Cultural change:** a culture transforming over time.
- In the sociology of education, the **functionalist** approach links education to maintaining social order; the **conflict** approach links it to reproducing inequality.
- **Economic foundation:** education is an **investment**; a skilled workforce (**human capital**) raises productivity and growth. It is also consumption spending.
- **Political foundation:** education policy reflects the kind of person the state wants to raise; **equality of opportunity** is a core principle.

## History of Turkish education

- **Ottoman period:** *sıbyan* schools (basic education), madrasas (higher education) and the **Enderun** palace school that trained statesmen.
- **Tanzimat and after:** Western-style schools (*rüştiye*, *idadi*), the teacher-training **Darülmuallimin** and **Darülfünun**, the forerunner of the university.
- **Republic:** unity of education with the **Law on the Unification of Education** (1924); a literacy campaign with the **Alphabet Reform** and the **Nation's Schools** (1928); the **Village Institutes** (1940) that trained teachers from the villages.

## Commonly confused

- *Perennialism* stresses unchanging truths, *essentialism* the culture's core knowledge; both are teacher-centred.
- *Progressivism* aims at the individual's growth, *reconstructionism* at transforming society.
- *Socialisation* happens within one society; *acculturation* between two cultures.

## Study tip

Make a table that answers "what does the teacher do, what does the student do, what is taught?" for each philosophy. Questions usually describe a classroom scene, and one row of the table fits it exactly.
