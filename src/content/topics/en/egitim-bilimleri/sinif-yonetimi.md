## Core idea

Classroom management means setting up and maintaining an environment **suitable for learning**. Good management is not punishing after a problem appears but **preventing it in advance**. Questions usually describe a classroom incident and ask for the teacher's best response or which model a response fits. The right option is usually the one that does not humiliate the student, does not interrupt teaching and addresses the cause of the behaviour.

## Dimensions

- **Physical arrangement:** seating (traditional rows, U-shape, clusters), light, sound, temperature.
- **Planning:** lesson plans and the order of activities.
- **Time management:** allocated time, instructional time, and the **academic learning time** students actually spend learning.
- **Relationships and communication:** teacher-student and student-student relationships.
- **Behaviour management:** rules and dealing with undesirable behaviour.

## Models

- **Reactive model:** the teacher intervenes with punishment and reward after behaviour appears.
- **Preventive model:** problems are prevented before they arise; rules and routines are set from the start.
- **Developmental model:** management is shaped by students' developmental characteristics.
- **Holistic model:** combines the preventive and developmental approaches; teaching, environment and relationships are arranged together.

## Key approaches

- **Kounin:** an effective manager shows **withitness** (knows what is going on everywhere), handles several things at once (**overlapping**) and keeps the lesson flowing.
- **Canter (assertive discipline):** the teacher states rules and expectations clearly and firmly and applies consistent consequences.
- **Dreikurs (logical consequences):** undesirable behaviour meets a consequence logically linked to it; the goal behind it (attention, power...) is explored.
- **Glasser:** students choose their behaviour and take responsibility for it.
- **Gordon:** the teacher uses an **"I-message"** that describes their feelings instead of a blaming "you-message".

## Rules and undesirable behaviour

- Rules should be **few, clear and positively worded**, and set together with students where possible.
- Intervention starts with the **mildest step**: eye contact, moving closer, saying the student's name; without stopping the lesson or exposing the student.
- The **cause** of the behaviour (boredom, not understanding, seeking attention) is found and addressed.

## Commonly confused

- The *preventive* model stops problems in advance; the *reactive* model intervenes afterwards.
- An *I-message* describes the teacher's feeling ("I find it hard to teach when there's talking"); a *you-message* blames ("You're always talking").
- A *logical consequence* is related to the behaviour; a *punishment* may not be.

## Study tip

Filter the options with three questions: does it interrupt teaching, does it humiliate the student, does it address the cause? The option that passes the filter is almost always the answer.
