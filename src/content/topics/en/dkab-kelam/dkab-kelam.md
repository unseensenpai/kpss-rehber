## Core idea

*Kelam* (kalam) is the discipline that explains, grounds and defends the Islamic articles of faith with scriptural and rational arguments. The theologian first asks how knowledge is gained, then how the world, God, prophethood and the hereafter can be proven. The test has 5 questions; they usually ask about types of argument, sources of knowledge and the differences between the Maturidi, Ash'ari and Mu'tazili views.

## Emergence and periods

- The first debates arose from politically rooted questions such as **grave sin**, **leadership (*imamet*)** and **predestination**.
- The **Mu'tazila** was the first systematic school, giving reason wide scope; **Maturidism** and **Ash'arism** built Sunni kalam.
- The ***mütekaddimin*** (early theologians) had limited contact with philosophy; the ***müteahhirin*** (later theologians), usually dated from al-Ghazali and Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, brought philosophical topics into kalam.
- In the late Ottoman period the **New Kalam** (*Yeni İlm-i Kelam*) movement sought to renew kalam for modern questions.

## Knowledge and God's existence

- **Sources of knowledge (Sunni):** ***havass-ı selime*** (sound senses), ***haber-i sadık*** (truthful report: mass-transmitted reports and the Prophet's report) and **reason**. Inspiration is not a universally valid source.
- ***Hudus*** **argument:** the world came into being; what comes into being needs a creator (the theologians' main argument).
- ***İmkan*** **argument:** contingent beings depend on a necessary being (*vacibü'l-vücud*); associated with Ibn Sina.
- **Argument from design:** order and purpose in the world point to a conscious creator.

## Attributes, prophethood and human acts

- **Attributes:** Ahl al-Sunnah affirms attributes together with the essence; the Mu'tazila does not regard them as entities distinct from the essence. The Maturidis count ***tekvin*** as a separate attribute.
- **Prophethood:** the main proof is the **miracle**; prophets' qualities are truthfulness, trustworthiness, sinlessness (*ismet*), intelligence and conveying the message.
- **Human acts:** for the Mu'tazila humans create their own acts; Maturidis with ***cüz'i irade*** (partial will) and Ash'aris with ***kesb*** (acquisition) hold that God creates the act while the human chooses and is responsible.
- **Good and evil (*hüsün-kubuh*):** the Mu'tazila holds they are known and binding by reason, the Ash'aris tie them to revelation, the Maturidis accept that reason can know some of them.

## Commonly confused

- The *temporality argument* starts from the world's coming into being, the *contingency argument* from beings not being necessary.
- *Kasb* (Ash'ari) stresses acquiring the act, *partial will* (Maturidi) the human power to choose.
- A *miracle* belongs to a prophet, a *keramet* to a person regarded as a saint.

## Study tip

For each topic write the Mu'tazili, Maturidi and Ash'ari views in three columns; tell the arguments apart by asking "what does it start from?".
