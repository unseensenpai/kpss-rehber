## Core idea

Grammar describes the rules of Turkish; linguistics studies language scientifically as a **system of signs**. **12 questions** in the Turkish ÖABT come from this area. What you know from the KPSS-level [Grammar](/kpss-rehber/en/topic/turkce/dil-bilgisi/) topic still applies, but questions expect technical terms and deeper analysis. They usually ask you to analyse sound changes, suffix types, phrases and sentence elements.

## Introduction to linguistics

- **Linguistic sign:** for Saussure, a union of the **signifier** (sound image) and the **signified** (concept); the link between them is **arbitrary** (conventional).
- **Synchronic and diachronic:** studying a language at one moment or across its history.
- **Units:** **phoneme** (smallest sound that distinguishes meaning), **morpheme** (smallest unit with meaning or function), word, sentence.
- **Language types:** by structure, isolating, **agglutinative** and fusional (inflecting) languages; Turkish is agglutinative and adds suffixes **after** the root.

## Phonology

- **Vowels:** unrounded-rounded, open-close, back-front distinctions.
- **Harmony:** major and minor vowel harmony; consonant harmony (assimilation, as in *kitapçı*).
- **Sound changes:** consonant softening, vowel loss, vowel narrowing, sound insertion, buffer consonants (*kaynaştırma*), consonant loss, metathesis (*göçüşme*, sounds swapping places).

## Morphology and parts of speech

- **Root and stem:** noun roots, verb roots; stems derived with derivational suffixes.
- **Suffixes:** **derivational suffixes** form new words; **inflectional suffixes** mark a word's role in the sentence (case, possessive, plural, mood and person suffixes).
- **Parts of speech:** noun, adjective, pronoun, adverb, postposition, conjunction, interjection and verb.
- **Verbals (*eylemsi*):** verbal nouns, participles and converbs; derived from verbs but used like other parts of speech.
- **Phrases:** noun compounds, adjective phrases, postpositional phrases, verbal phrases, reduplications.

## Syntax

- **Elements:** predicate, subject, object, indirect complement, adverbial complement; elements outside the sentence.
- **Sentence types:** by predicate type (nominal or verbal), position (regular or inverted), meaning (affirmative, negative, interrogative) and structure (simple, complex, compound with or without conjunctions).

## Commonly confused

- A *phoneme* distinguishes meaning but carries none by itself; a *morpheme* carries meaning or function.
- A *derivational suffix* changes a word's meaning; an *inflectional suffix* only sets its role.
- *Synchronic* study looks at a cross-section of a language; *diachronic* study looks at its historical change.

## Study tip

In every analysis question, first break the word down to its root, then label each suffix as derivational or inflectional. For sentence elements, always find the predicate first and ask your questions from it.
