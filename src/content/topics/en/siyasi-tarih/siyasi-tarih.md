## Core idea

Political history (*siyasi tarih*) studies how the modern international system changed from **Westphalia in 1648** to the end of the Cold War. The **new order** built after each great war (Vienna, Versailles, Yalta) is the backbone of the subject. The test has **8 questions** on this subject; they usually ask about the result, period or chronological order of a treaty or development.

## From Westphalia to the 19th century

- **Peace of Westphalia (1648):** ended the Thirty Years' War; seen as the start of a state system based on **sovereign equality** and non-interference.
- **Congress of Vienna (1815):** reorganised Europe after Napoleon on the principles of **legitimacy** and **balance of power**. The great powers' settling of problems through congresses is called the **Concert of Europe**. The **Holy Alliance** aimed to protect monarchies against revolution.
- **Nationalism:** Italian and German unification were completed in the second half of the 19th century.
- **Eastern Question:** the problem of the future of Ottoman lands. The **Treaty of Paris (1856)** ended the Crimean War and admitted the Ottoman Empire to the European public law of states; the **Congress of Berlin (1878)** created new states in the Balkans.
- **Imperialism and blocs:** the **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and the **Triple Entente** (Britain, France, Russia).

## The First World War and the interwar years

- **Wilson's Fourteen Points:** an end to secret diplomacy, self-determination, a general international organisation.
- **Treaty of Versailles (1919):** imposed heavy terms on Germany; the **League of Nations** was founded. The League proved ineffective, as the USA stayed out and its sanctions were weak.
- **Efforts to keep peace:** **Locarno (1925)** and the **Kellogg-Briand Pact (1928)**, which renounced war as an instrument of national policy.
- **Collapse of the order:** the 1929 depression and the rise of revisionist states; the **Munich Conference (1938)** symbolises **appeasement**. The 1939 German-Soviet non-aggression pact opened the way to war.

## The Second World War and the Cold War

- The **Atlantic Charter (1941)** and the **Yalta** and **Potsdam (1945)** conferences shaped the post-war order; the **United Nations** was founded.
- **Truman Doctrine (1947):** **containment** of communism; aid to Turkey and Greece. The **Marshall Plan** funded Europe's economic reconstruction.
- **Blocs:** **NATO (1949)** and the **Warsaw Pact (1955)**; the Korean War and the **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)** were peaks of tension.
- **Détente:** arms-control talks in the 1970s and the **Helsinki Final Act (1975)**.
- **The end:** the **fall of the Berlin Wall (1989)** and the **dissolution of the Soviet Union (1991)**.

## Commonly confused

- The *Holy Alliance* was solidarity among monarchies; the *Concert of Europe* was great-power congress diplomacy.
- The *Treaty of Paris (1856)* brought the Ottomans into the European system; the *Congress of Berlin (1878)* confirmed Ottoman losses in the Balkans.
- The *Truman Doctrine* was military and political aid, the *Marshall Plan* economic aid.

## Study tip

Build a timeline that sums up, in one line, the order set up after each war: the war, the peace treaty or congress, the organisation founded, the main principle. In chronology questions this timeline is your fastest help.
