## Core idea

Exercise physiology studies how the body responds to exercise **immediately (acute responses)** and **lastingly with regular training (chronic adaptations)**. The energy source muscle uses directly is **ATP**; how ATP is resupplied depends on the duration and intensity of the effort. The test has **4 questions**; they mostly ask for the dominant energy system of a sport and the effect of training on the heart and breathing.

## Energy systems

- **ATP-PC (phosphagen) system:** without oxygen, very fast, very short (up to about 10 seconds); 100 m, throws, jumps.
- **Anaerobic glycolysis:** without oxygen, uses carbohydrate, lactate builds up; high-intensity effort of about 1–2 minutes (such as 400 m).
- **Aerobic (oxidative) system:** works with oxygen; uses carbohydrate and fat, slow but long-lasting; marathon, long-distance swimming.
- The systems work **together**; questions ask for the "dominant" one.

## Muscle fibres

- **Type I (slow-twitch):** fatigue-resistant, oxidative; common in endurance athletes.
- **Type II (fast-twitch):** powerful but quick to tire; common in sprinters and jumpers.

## Circulation and breathing

- **Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate.** In trained people stroke volume rises and **resting heart rate falls**.
- **Maximum heart rate** is roughly estimated in practice as "220 − age".
- **VO2max:** the highest amount of oxygen that can be used per unit time; an indicator of aerobic capacity.
- **Anaerobic threshold:** the intensity at which lactate starts to build up quickly in the blood.
- **EPOC:** oxygen consumption staying raised for a while after exercise (formerly called oxygen debt).

## Commonly confused

- An *acute response* is a temporary change in a single session; a *chronic adaptation* is a lasting change from regular training.
- *Stroke volume* is the blood pumped in one beat; *cardiac output* is the blood pumped in one minute.
- The *ATP-PC* system serves efforts of seconds; *anaerobic glycolysis* serves efforts of about one to two minutes.

## Study tip

List events by effort duration (throw, 100 m, 400 m, 1500 m, marathon) and write the dominant energy system next to each; most questions come straight from this list.
