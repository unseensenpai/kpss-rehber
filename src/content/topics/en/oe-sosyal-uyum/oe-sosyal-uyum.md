## Core idea

Social adjustment skills (*sosyal uyum becerileri*) cover interpersonal skills such as greeting, waiting for a turn and asking for help, plus self-care and independent living skills. In special education these skills may not develop **on their own**, so they are **taught in a planned way**. There are **2 questions**; they ask about types of deficit and teaching methods.

## Key concepts

- **Social skill:** showing the appropriate behaviour in a given social situation.
- **Social competence:** others judging the person's social behaviour as appropriate; the social value given to the outcome of skills.
- **Acquisition deficit:** the student does not know the skill at all; it must be taught.
- **Performance deficit:** the student knows the skill but does not show it; the setting and reinforcement are adjusted.

## Teaching methods

- **Direct instruction:** defining the skill, **modelling**, rehearsal through **role-play**, **feedback** and reinforcement.
- **Video modelling:** the student watches a short video of the skill and then performs it; video self-modelling is one form.
- **Social story:** a short text describing a social situation, what will happen and the expected behaviour from the student's point of view.
- **Peer-mediated instruction:** trained peers start and sustain interactions.
- **Self-management:** the student monitors, records and reinforces their own behaviour.

## Independent living and generalisation

- **Self-care:** dressing, eating, toileting, personal hygiene; taught with **task analysis** and chaining.
- **Community-based instruction:** teaching skills such as shopping or using public transport in real settings.
- **Generalisation:** working with different people, settings and materials; follow-up after teaching for **maintenance**.

## Commonly confused

- An *acquisition deficit* needs teaching; a *performance deficit* needs changes to motivation and setting.
- A *social skill* is the behaviour itself; *social competence* is how others judge that behaviour.

## Study tip

In a case, check whether the student shows the skill in another setting: if yes, it is a performance deficit; if never, an acquisition deficit.
