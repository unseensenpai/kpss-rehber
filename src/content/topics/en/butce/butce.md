## Core idea

A budget is the document that sets out the state's revenue and expenditure estimates for a period and through which **the legislature authorises** them. This authority is the **power of the purse** (*bütçe hakkı*); the budget is both an economic plan and a legal authorisation. This subject has 6 questions in the test; they cover budget principles, budget systems, the budget cycle and the core concepts of Law No. 5018.

## Budget principles

- **Unity** (*birlik*): all revenue and expenditure is gathered in a single document.
- **Gross budgeting** (*genellik*): revenue and expenditure are shown in full, without netting one against the other.
- **Universality** (*tümlük*): no revenue or expenditure is left outside the budget.
- **Annuality:** the budget covers a one-year period.
- **Prior authorisation:** legislative approval comes before execution.
- **Specification** (*tasrih*): appropriations are detailed and tied to specific purposes.
- **Balance, clarity and accuracy:** the classical view seeks balanced revenue and spending, readability and realistic estimates.
- **Non-earmarking** (*gelirlerin genelliği*): no specific revenue is reserved for a specific expense.

## Budget systems

- **Classical (line-item) budget:** focuses on inputs; shows what is bought and serves control.
- **Performance-based budget:** links resource use to goals and results.
- **Planning-programming-budgeting system:** combines long-term planning with the budget.
- **Zero-based budget:** every item is justified from zero each year.
- **Programme budget:** allocates resources through programmes tied to policy goals; Türkiye's central government budget has moved to this structure.

## The budget cycle

- **Preparation:** institutions draft proposals within the medium-term programme and medium-term fiscal plan.
- **Approval:** the central government budget bill is submitted to the Grand National Assembly (TBMM) **by the President**; it becomes law after debate in the Plan and Budget Committee and the General Assembly.
- **Execution:** institutions use appropriations; spending beyond appropriations is not allowed.
- **Audit:** results are reported to the TBMM through the **final accounts** (*kesin hesap*); the **Court of Accounts** (*Sayıştay*) performs external audit on the TBMM's behalf.

## Law No. 5018

- Its aim is to ensure **effective, economical and efficient** use of public resources, **fiscal transparency** and **accountability**.
- **General government:** central government (general-budget administrations, special-budget administrations, regulatory and supervisory agencies), social security institutions and local administrations.
- Resources are tied to goals through the **strategic plan** and the **performance programme**.
- **Financial management actors:** top manager, **authorising officer** (*harcama yetkilisi*), realisation officer and accounting officer.
- **Internal control** covers ex-ante financial control and internal audit; **external audit** belongs to the Court of Accounts.

## Commonly confused

- The *unity principle* stresses a single document; the *universality principle* stresses that nothing is left out.
- *General-budget administrations* are run directly from the central budget; *special-budget administrations* have their own budgets.
- *Internal audit* happens inside the institution; *external audit* is done by the Court of Accounts.

## Study tip

Study budget principles in pairs (unity and universality, gross budgeting and non-earmarking), because questions often mix up these close ideas. Summarise the scope and actors of Law No. 5018 in a table. Legislation can change, so always check the current legislation before the exam.
