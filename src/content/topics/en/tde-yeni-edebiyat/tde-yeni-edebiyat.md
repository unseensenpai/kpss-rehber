## Core idea

Modern Turkish literature is the literature that has developed **in contact with Western literature** from the Tanzimat era to today. It is the heaviest literature section of the Turkish Language and Literature ÖABT: **15 questions**. The logic is a chain of reactions: each group defines itself by breaking with the previous one on language, themes and the purpose of art. Questions cover writer-work-period matches, comparisons of periods and finding which view a passage reflects.

## The Tanzimat period

- **First generation:** "art for society"; new genres such as the newspaper, drama and the novel; a call for simpler language. **Şinasi** (*Şair Evlenmesi*; the newspaper *Tercüman-ı Ahvâl* with Agâh Efendi), **Namık Kemal** (*Vatan yahut Silistre*, *İntibah*), **Ziya Paşa**, **Ahmet Mithat Efendi**.
- **First novels:** Şemsettin Sami's *Taaşşuk-ı Talat ve Fitnat* is considered the first Turkish novel.
- **Second generation:** "art for art's sake"; individual themes. **Recaizade Mahmut Ekrem** (*Araba Sevdası*), **Abdülhak Hâmit**, **Sami Paşazade Sezai** (*Sergüzeşt*).

## Servet-i Fünun and Fecr-i Âti

- ***Servet-i Fünun*:** heavy, ornate language, pessimism, the theme of escape, adapting aruz to Turkish, Western forms such as the sonnet. **Tevfik Fikret** (*Rübab-ı Şikeste*, The Broken Lute), **Cenap Şahabettin**, **Halit Ziya** in the novel (*Mai ve Siyah*, *Aşk-ı Memnu*), **Mehmet Rauf** in the story.
- ***Fecr-i Âti*:** "art is personal and to be respected"; a short-lived group. Its most lasting name is **Ahmet Haşim** (symbolism).

## National Literature

- The **New Language** (*Yeni Lisan*) movement around the journal *Genç Kalemler* (Young Pens): Istanbul speech, plain Turkish, syllabic metre.
- **Ömer Seyfettin** (short story), **Ziya Gökalp** (ideas and poetry), **Mehmet Âkif Ersoy** (*Safahat*, plain language in aruz), **Halide Edib**, **Yakup Kadri**, **Reşat Nuri**.

## The Republican period

- **Poetry:** the **Five Syllabists** (Faruk Nafiz Çamlıbel and others); **Yahya Kemal**, who built a distinctive Istanbul poetry in aruz; **Nazım Hikmet**, who established free verse; **Necip Fazıl** (*Kaldırımlar*, Pavements); Cahit Sıtkı Tarancı.
- ***Garip*:** Orhan Veli, Oktay Rifat, Melih Cevdet; a revolt against metre, rhyme and "poetic" diction.
- **Second New (*İkinci Yeni*):** Cemal Süreya, Edip Cansever, Turgut Uyar, İlhan Berk; images, hermetic meaning, breaking ordinary language use.
- **Novel and story:** **Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar** on the psychological, individual line (*Huzur*, *Saatleri Ayarlama Enstitüsü*, The Time Regulation Institute); **Sait Faik** and **Sabahattin Ali** in the story; **Yaşar Kemal** on the **socialist realist** and **village novel** line (*İnce Memed*, Memed, My Hawk); **Oğuz Atay** with modernist narration (*Tutunamayanlar*, The Disconnected).
- **After 1980:** **postmodern** narrative comes to the fore: metafiction, intertextuality, mixing of genres and styles. **Orhan Pamuk** (*Kara Kitap*, The Black Book) is the best-known name of this line.

## Commonly confused

- *Servet-i Fünun* means heavy language and personal pessimism; *National Literature* means plain language and national themes.
- *Fecr-i Âti* holds that art is personal; the *first Tanzimat generation* sees art as a service to society.
- *Garip* poetry is plain and everyday, the *Second New* image-driven and hermetic; the latter arose partly as a reaction to the former's extreme plainness.

## Study tip

For each group, answer "what did it defend, what did it oppose" in two sentences. Try to recall works by their period before their author; period knowledge is the key to most questions.
