## Core idea

Ecology studies how organisms relate to each other and to their non-living surroundings. Two basic rules apply: **energy flows one way**, with most of it lost as heat at each trophic level, while **matter is recycled**. The Biology test has **6 questions** here, usually on food webs and pyramids, population growth graphs, interactions between species, and succession.

## Ecosystems and energy

- **Levels:** individual → population (individuals of one species) → community (populations of different species) → ecosystem (community plus the non-living environment) → biosphere.
- A **habitat** is where an organism lives; a **niche** is the species' whole role and use of resources in the ecosystem.
- **Trophic levels:** producers; primary, secondary and top consumers; decomposers. Energy passed from one level to the next is typically about 10%.
- **Ecological pyramids:** the pyramid of energy is always upright; pyramids of numbers and biomass can be inverted in some ecosystems (for example many insects living on a single tree).
- **Cycles:** carbon (photosynthesis and respiration), nitrogen (fixation, ammonification, nitrification, denitrification), water and phosphorus. The phosphorus cycle has no significant atmospheric phase.

## Populations and communities

- **Population growth:** with unlimited resources growth is **exponential** (J curve); with limited resources it is **logistic** (S curve), levelling off around the **carrying capacity** (K).
- **r and K strategies:** r-strategists produce many small offspring with little or no care; K-strategists produce few offspring with much care, and their populations stay near K.
- **Interactions:** competition (−/−), predation and parasitism (+/−), **mutualism** (+/+), **commensalism** (+/0). **Competitive exclusion principle:** two species sharing the same niche cannot coexist for long.
- **Keystone species:** a species whose effect on its community is disproportionately large relative to its abundance.
- **Succession:** primary succession starts on ground with no soil (lava, bare rock) with **pioneer species** (lichens); secondary succession happens in disturbed areas where soil remains (after a fire) and is faster. It ends in a relatively stable **climax** community.
- **Biomes:** large land communities shaped by climate (temperature and rainfall): tundra, taiga, temperate deciduous forest, grassland, desert, tropical rainforest.

## Commonly confused

- A *habitat* is an address, a *niche* is a profession: where a species lives versus how it lives.
- In *commensalism* one partner benefits and the other is unaffected; in *mutualism* both benefit.
- *Primary succession* begins without soil; *secondary succession* begins where soil is present.

## Study tip

Learn the interaction types by their (+/−) signs and find an example for each; in graph questions, first decide whether the curve is J or S and where the K line lies.
