## Core idea

Chemistry explains the structure and changes of matter **at the atomic level**: electron configuration sets the tendency to bond, bonds set a substance's properties, and reactions rearrange atoms while conserving mass. With **15 questions** it is the second-largest subject in the Science test. Questions focus on mole calculations, comparing periodic trends, identifying bond and reaction types, and interpreting acids and bases.

## Atoms and the periodic table

- **Atomic number** is the number of protons; **mass number** is protons plus neutrons. **Isotopes** have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
- **Electron configuration:** orbitals fill in order of energy (1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d …); the outer-shell (valence) electrons decide chemical behaviour.
- **Periodic trends:** across a period from left to right, atomic radius decreases while ionisation energy and electronegativity generally increase; down a group, atomic radius increases and ionisation energy decreases.
- **Groups:** 1A alkali metals, 7A halogens, 8A noble gases (stable configuration).

## Bonds and states of matter

- **Ionic bond:** electron transfer between a metal and a non-metal (NaCl).
- **Covalent bond:** electron sharing between non-metals; polar if electronegativities differ (H₂O), non-polar if not (Cl₂).
- **Metallic bond:** delocalised electrons; explains conductivity and malleability.
- **Weak interactions:** **hydrogen bonds** (between molecules where H is bonded to F, O or N), dipole-dipole, London forces. These explain differences in boiling points.
- **Mixtures:** homogeneous (solutions) and heterogeneous; separated by filtration, distillation, crystallisation or a separating funnel.

## Moles and reactions

- **Mole:** 6.02×10²³ particles (Avogadro's number). n = m/M; at standard conditions (0 °C, 1 atm) one mole of ideal gas occupies 22.4 L.
- **Conservation of mass:** in a balanced equation atoms and mass are conserved. Example: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.
- **Limiting reactant:** the substance used up first, which fixes the amount of product.
- **Reaction types:** combustion, synthesis, decomposition, displacement, neutralisation, precipitation.

## Solutions, acids, bases and equilibrium

- **Molarity:** M = n/V (mol/L). Adding a solute lowers the freezing point and raises the boiling point (colligative properties).
- **Acids and bases:** Arrhenius acids release H⁺ in water and bases release OH⁻; in Brønsted-Lowry terms, acids donate protons and bases accept them. Pure water at 25 °C has pH 7; pH < 7 is acidic, pH > 7 is basic.
- **Neutralisation:** acid + base → salt + water (HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O).
- **Rate and equilibrium:** temperature, concentration, surface area and a **catalyst** increase rate; a catalyst lowers activation energy but does not shift equilibrium. **Le Chatelier:** equilibrium shifts in the direction that reduces the imposed change.
- **Energy:** exothermic reactions release heat (ΔH < 0), endothermic reactions absorb it (ΔH > 0).

## Commonly confused

- A *physical change* keeps the substance's identity (melting); a *chemical change* makes a new substance (burning).
- A *hydrogen bond* is a weak attraction between molecules; a *covalent bond* is a strong bond within a molecule.
- A *catalyst* changes the rate but is not consumed and does not shift equilibrium; the *limiting reactant* is used up.

## Study tip

In every calculation, first convert the given amount to moles, use the coefficients of the balanced equation as a ratio, then convert back to the unit asked for. Summarising periodic trends with arrows in a single table speeds up comparison questions.
