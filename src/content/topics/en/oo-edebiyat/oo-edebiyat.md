## Core idea

Early childhood literature supports the child's language development, imagination, aesthetic sense and love of reading. A good book suits the child's **developmental level** in both content and form. The test has 3 questions; they usually ask you to recognise a genre or judge whether a book is suitable.

## Criteria for choosing books

- **Content:** close to the child's experience, short with a single plot line; free of fear, violence and **stereotypes** (gender, occupation, disability); conveys its message through events rather than direct preaching.
- **Language:** short, clear sentences, repetition, rhythm and sound patterns.
- **Pictures:** **text-picture harmony**; pictures do not merely repeat the text but complete the story. **Wordless books** tell the story only through pictures and let children build their own story.
- **Physical features:** durable, thick pages, large print, safe materials, a size that fits the child's hands.

## Genres

- **Lullaby** (*ninni*): words sung to a melody to put a child to sleep.
- **Nursery rhyme** (*tekerleme*): rhythmic word play, often with loose meaning; **counting-out rhymes** (*sayışma*) used to pick who is "it" belong here.
- **Riddle** (*bilmece*): describing something indirectly by its features so it can be guessed.
- **Folk tale** (*masal*): extraordinary events, unspecified time and place; begins with a **formulaic opening** (*döşeme*) such as "Bir varmış bir yokmuş" (once there was, once there wasn't).
- **Fable** (*fabl*): a short story in which personified animals or objects teach a lesson.
- **Finger play** (*parmak oyunu*): a short poem or rhyme acted out with hand and finger movements.
- **Story and poem:** realistic events and feelings suited to the child's world.

## Reading activities

- **Interactive reading:** while reading, the teacher asks questions, invites predictions and makes room for children's comments.
- **Acting out:** telling stories with books, puppets and picture cards and dramatising them; a **book corner** children can reach freely.

## Commonly confused

- A *folk tale* is extraordinary and set in an unspecified time; a *fable* teaches an explicit lesson through personified animals.
- A *nursery rhyme* is a sound and rhythm game, a *riddle* is about guessing something.

## Study tip

When judging a book, think through four headings in order: content, language, pictures, physical features. In an unsuitable option one of these is usually clearly flawed.
