## Core idea

Tax law governs the relationship between the state's tax claim and the taxpayer's tax debt. Its foundation is the constitutional **principle of legality** (*kanunilik ilkesi*): taxes, levies, fees and similar financial obligations can only be imposed, changed or abolished by law. The details are largely set by the **Tax Procedure Law No. 213** (*Vergi Usul Kanunu*). This subject has 6 questions in the test; they mostly ask about the stages of the taxation process, assessment and penalty types, the statute of limitations and settlement.

## Sources, application and parties

- **Sources:** the Constitution, tax laws, international treaties, presidential decisions and regulations; court decisions guide interpretation.
- **Interpretation:** tax laws apply according to their **wording and spirit**; the **real nature** of transactions is what counts.
- **Proof:** the taxable event and related transactions can be proven by any evidence **except an oath**.
- The **taxpayer** (*mükellef*) is the person on whom the tax debt falls by law; the **tax withholder** (*vergi sorumlusu*) is the person held responsible for paying the tax to the tax office.
- **Tax capacity:** does not depend on civil-law capacity; minors and persons under guardianship can be taxpayers, and their legal representatives carry out their duties.

## The taxation process

- **Taxable event** (*vergiyi doğuran olay*): the tax claim arises when the event or legal situation the law ties the tax to occurs.
- **Assessment** (*tarh*): the tax office calculates the tax claim from the base and rates in the law and fixes the amount. Types: **assessment on return**, **ex officio assessment** (*re'sen tarh*, when the base cannot be determined from returns or records), **supplementary assessment** (*ikmalen tarh*, for the missing part of an already assessed tax) and **administrative assessment**.
- **Notification** (*tebliğ*): written notice of tax matters to the taxpayer by the competent authority; by post, by an officer, by public announcement or electronically.
- **Accrual** (*tahakkuk*): the assessed and notified tax reaches the stage where it must be paid.
- **Collection** (*tahsil*): payment of the tax claim in line with the law; collection of public receivables is also governed by **Law No. 6183**.

## Time limits, limitation and settlement

- **Time limits** are set by law, by the administration or by courts.
- In cases of **force majeure** (serious illness, natural disaster, unavoidable situations beyond the person's will), time limits do not run.
- After the **assessment limitation period**, the tax can no longer be assessed; after the **collection limitation period**, the claim can no longer be pursued. Limitation is one of the ways a tax debt ends; payment and write-off (*terkin*) are others.
- **Settlement** (*uzlaşma*): agreement between taxpayer and administration on tax and penalty amounts; it can be **before assessment** or **after assessment**. No lawsuit can be filed for settled amounts.

## Tax penalties

- **Tax loss penalty** (*vergi ziyaı cezası*): imposed when tax is not accrued on time or is accrued short, causing a revenue loss.
- **Irregularity penalty** (*usulsüzlük cezası*): for breaches of form and procedure rules, divided into first and second degree. A **special irregularity penalty** applies to certain duties.
- **Smuggling offences** (*kaçakçılık suçları*): carry prison sentences and are tried in criminal courts, separate from administrative tax penalties.
- **Voluntary disclosure** (*pişmanlık ve ıslah*): a taxpayer who meets its conditions avoids the penalty.

## Commonly confused

- *Assessment* calculates the tax, *accrual* makes it payable, *collection* is the actual payment.
- *Ex officio assessment* is used when the base cannot be determined at all; *supplementary assessment* completes a missing part.
- The *taxpayer* owns the tax debt; the *tax withholder* must pay someone else's tax.

## Study tip

Repeat the process in order: taxable event, assessment, notification, accrual, collection. Build a short case for each assessment and penalty type. Time limits, rates and penalty amounts change often, so always check the current legislation before the exam.
