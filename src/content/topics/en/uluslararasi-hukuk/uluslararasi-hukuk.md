## Core idea

International law is the set of rules governing relations between subjects of international law, above all states. With no supreme legislator and no compulsory court, most rules rest on the **consent** of states. The test has **10 questions** on this subject; they ask you to define and tell apart the sources, treaty concepts, maritime zones and methods of dispute settlement.

## Sources and relation to domestic law

- **ICJ Statute, Article 38:** the main sources are **treaties**, **customary law** and **general principles of law**; subsidiary means are **judicial decisions** and **teachings** of scholars. If the parties agree, the court may decide **ex aequo et bono** (on equity).
- **Custom:** needs **state practice** (material element) and **opinio juris** (the belief that it is legally binding).
- **Jus cogens:** peremptory norms from which no treaty may derogate (such as the bans on genocide and torture).
- **Monism and dualism:** monism sees international and domestic law as one order, dualism as two separate orders.

## Treaties and subjects

- The **1969 Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties** is the core text.
- **Pacta sunt servanda:** a treaty in force binds the parties and must be performed in good faith.
- **Reservation:** a state excludes or modifies certain provisions as they apply to it.
- **Rebus sic stantibus:** a fundamental change of circumstances may, in limited cases, be a ground for ending a treaty.
- **Elements of the state:** permanent population, defined territory, effective government and capacity to enter into relations with other states.
- **Recognition:** under the *constitutive* view recognition creates the state; under the *declaratory* view it confirms an existing fact.
- **International organisations** also have limited legal personality; the individual is a subject to a limited extent.

## Law of the sea and diplomatic relations

- **1982 UN Convention on the Law of the Sea:** the **territorial sea** is up to 12 miles, the **contiguous zone** 24 miles, the **exclusive economic zone** up to 200 miles; the **continental shelf** concerns the seabed and subsoil. Turkey is not a party to this convention.
- **Innocent passage:** the right of foreign ships to pass through the territorial sea without disturbing peace.
- **1961 Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations:** mission premises are **inviolable**; diplomats enjoy immunity from jurisdiction. The receiving state may declare a diplomat **persona non grata**. Consular relations are governed by the **1963** convention.

## Use of force and disputes

- **UN Charter, Article 2(4):** the use or threat of force is prohibited. Exceptions: **self-defence under Article 51** and **Security Council action under Chapter VII**.
- **Peaceful settlement:** diplomatic means (negotiation, **good offices**, **mediation**, inquiry, conciliation) and legal means (**arbitration**, judicial settlement).
- **International Court of Justice:** in The Hague; hears disputes between states, its jurisdiction rests on state consent (including the **optional clause**), and it gives advisory opinions.
- **International Criminal Court:** created by the Rome Statute; tries individuals for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and the crime of aggression; it is **complementary** to national courts.

## Commonly confused

- *Good offices* bring the parties to the table, while *mediation* also proposes solutions.
- The *ICJ* hears disputes between states, the *ICC* deals with the criminal liability of individuals.
- The *territorial sea* is under state sovereignty, the *exclusive economic zone* gives only sovereign rights over resources.

## Study tip

Draw the maritime zones on a diagram from the coast outwards, and rank the peaceful settlement methods by the role of the third party. Treaty participation and case law can change, so check current sources.
