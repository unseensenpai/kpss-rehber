## Core idea

Applied behaviour analysis (*uygulamalı davranış analizi*, UDA) defines behaviour in **observable and measurable** terms and changes it by arranging the environment. Behaviour is studied together with what comes before and after it: **antecedent → behaviour → consequence**. There are **4 questions**; they usually present a classroom scene and ask which principle or method is being used.

## Measurement and recording

- **Event recording:** how many times the behaviour occurs (frequency).
- **Duration recording:** how long the behaviour lasts; **latency recording** is the time between a cue and the start of the behaviour.
- **Interval recording:** the observation period is split into intervals and each is marked for whether the behaviour occurred (whole interval, partial interval, momentary time sampling).
- **Inter-observer reliability:** the rate of agreement between two observers' records.

## Changing behaviour

- **Positive reinforcement:** a pleasant stimulus is added after the behaviour; **negative reinforcement:** an unpleasant stimulus is removed. Both **increase** behaviour.
- **Extinction:** no longer reinforcing a previously reinforced behaviour; a temporary burst may appear at first.
- **Differential reinforcement:** reinforcing other, alternative, incompatible or lower rates of behaviour.
- **Functional assessment:** finds the function of a problem behaviour (attention, escape from tasks, access to items, sensory stimulation); the intervention is chosen to match that function.

## Teaching skills

- **Shaping:** reinforcing responses that come closer and closer to the target behaviour.
- **Chaining:** teaching a skill broken into steps by task analysis, using forward, backward or total-task chaining.
- **Errorless teaching:** constant time delay, simultaneous prompting, least-to-most and most-to-least prompting. Prompts are **faded** over time.
- **Single-subject designs:** AB, ABAB (withdrawal), multiple baseline, multiple probe and changing criterion designs.

## Commonly confused

- *Negative reinforcement* increases behaviour; *punishment* decreases it.
- *Shaping* gradually refines the form of one behaviour; *chaining* links different steps together.
- *Extinction* withholds the reinforcer; *time-out* temporarily removes the student from a reinforcing setting.

## Study tip

For every scene ask two questions: "Was a stimulus added or removed?" and "Did the behaviour increase or decrease?". Those two answers settle the type of reinforcement or punishment.
