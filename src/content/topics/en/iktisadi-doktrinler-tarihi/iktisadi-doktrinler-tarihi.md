## Core idea

The history of economic thought follows **which answer each school gave to which question**: What is the source of wealth, where does value come from, should the state intervene in markets? Each school arises as a reaction to the previous one. The test has **2 questions**; they usually ask you to match a concept or view with the right thinker or school.

## Before the Classics: mercantilists and Physiocrats

- **Mercantilism** (*merkantilizm*): wealth is the accumulation of **precious metals** (gold, silver). So exports are encouraged, imports restricted, and the state intervenes actively.
- **Physiocrats (Quesnay):** only **agriculture** creates a "net product"; industry and trade are sterile. The **Tableau Économique** is the first table showing circulation in the economy. The slogan "laissez faire, laissez passer" is associated with this school.

## The Classical school

- **Adam Smith:** *The Wealth of Nations* (1776). The source of wealth is **labour and the division of labour**; individual self-interest becomes social benefit through the **invisible hand**. In trade he argues for **absolute advantage**.
- **David Ricardo:** **comparative advantage**, **differential rent** and the labour theory of value.
- **Thomas Malthus:** population grows geometrically, food arithmetically; wages fall to subsistence.
- **J. B. Say:** **Say's law**: supply creates its own demand, so there is no general overproduction.
- For the Classics, the state has limited duties (security, justice, some public works) and the market balances itself.

## Reactions to the Classics

- **Karl Marx:** moves from the labour theory of value to **surplus value** and exploitation; argues capitalism will collapse through its own contradictions.
- **German Historical school and Friedrich List:** reject universal laws and stress national conditions. List defends temporary protection on **infant industry** grounds.
- **Institutional economics (Veblen):** studies institutions and habits; the concept of **conspicuous consumption** is his.

## The marginalist revolution and after

- **Marginalists (Jevons, Menger, Walras):** derive value from **marginal utility**, not labour. Walras built **general equilibrium**; Menger founded the **Austrian school**.
- **Alfred Marshall:** combines supply and demand; **partial equilibrium** analysis and the concept of elasticity.
- **John Maynard Keynes:** *The General Theory* (1936). After the Great Depression he rejects Say's law; unemployment comes from **insufficient effective demand**, which government spending should support.
- **Monetarism (Friedman):** inflation is a monetary phenomenon; money supply should follow a rule.
- **New Classical school (Lucas):** under **rational expectations**, anticipated policy does not affect real variables.
- **Austrian school (Hayek):** stresses dispersed knowledge and opposes central planning.

## Commonly confused

- *Mercantilism* sees wealth as a gold stock; the *Physiocrats* see it as agriculture's net product.
- *Absolute advantage* is Smith's; *comparative advantage* is Ricardo's.
- *Say's law* says demand follows supply; *Keynes* says demand determines output.

## Study tip

For each school write three things on one line: the source of value, the role of the state and the key concept. When a question gives a slogan or a concept, this table takes you straight to the right thinker.
