## Core idea

Children's rights see the child not as an object in need of protection but as an **individual with rights**. The key text is the **Convention on the Rights of the Child**, adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 1989; Türkiye is a party to the Convention. The test has 2 questions; they usually ask you to match principles and groups of rights with example situations.

## Historical development

- **Geneva Declaration:** one of the first international documents on children's rights, from the League of Nations era.
- **UN Declaration of the Rights of the Child:** a non-binding declaration issued before the Convention.
- **The Convention:** binding on states parties; implementation is monitored by the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child.

## Core principles of the Convention

- **Definition of a child:** every human being below 18, unless majority is reached earlier under the applicable law.
- **Non-discrimination:** the rights apply to every child.
- **Best interests of the child:** a primary consideration in every decision concerning the child.
- **Right to life, survival and development.**
- **Respect for the child's views (participation):** the child may express views on matters affecting them, and these are given weight according to age and maturity.

## Groups of rights and Türkiye

- **Groups:** rights are often grouped as **survival, development, protection and participation**; education, play and rest fall under development rights.
- **Child Protection Law:** in Türkiye the main law on children in need of protection and children pushed into crime is **Law No. 5395** (*Çocuk Koruma Kanunu*).
- **Reporting:** teachers have a duty to report suspected neglect and abuse; check current legislation in official sources.

## Commonly confused

- A *declaration* is not binding; the *Convention* binds states parties.
- *Participation rights* concern hearing the child's views, *protection rights* concern protecting the child from abuse and exploitation.

## Study tip

Match the four core principles to a classroom example (setting class rules together with children is participation). Questions usually come as such a scene.
