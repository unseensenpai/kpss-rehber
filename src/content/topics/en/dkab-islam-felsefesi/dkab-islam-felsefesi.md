## Core idea

Islamic philosophy is the tradition of thought that began when ancient Greek philosophy entered the Muslim world through translation, and that seeks **harmony** between revelation and reason. The test has 2 questions; they usually ask you to match philosophers, works and views, or concern the al-Ghazali–Ibn Rushd debate.

## Emergence and the Peripatetic tradition

- **Translation movement:** under the Abbasids, especially at the **House of Wisdom**, Greek and Syriac works were translated into Arabic.
- ***Meşşailik*** (Peripatetic school): combines the Aristotelian line with Neoplatonic elements.
- **Al-Kindi:** regarded as the first Muslim Arab philosopher; argued that philosophy and religion do not conflict.
- **Al-Farabi:** the ***muallim-i sani*** (Second Teacher); in *The Virtuous City* he set out his view of the virtuous society and state; he systematised the theory of **emanation** (*sudur*).
- **Ibn Sina:** *al-Shifa*, *al-Najat*, *al-Isharat*; divided being into **necessary** and **contingent** and grounded God as the **Necessary Being** (*vacibü'l-vücud*); explained the soul's independence with the "flying man" thought experiment.

## Critique and reply

- **Al-Ghazali:** in *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* he criticised the philosophers on twenty issues; he judged three of them irreconcilable with religion: the **eternity of the world**, the claim that **God does not know particulars**, and the **denial of bodily resurrection**. He considered logic necessary for the sciences.
- **Ibn Rushd:** replied with *The Incoherence of the Incoherence*; in *The Decisive Treatise* he argued that religion and philosophy reach the same truth. His Aristotle commentaries were also influential in the Latin world.

## Other traditions

- **Ibn Tufayl:** in *Hayy ibn Yaqzan* he told, as a story, how reason alone can reach the truth.
- **Suhrawardi:** ***İşrakilik*** (Illuminationism); grounded knowledge in illumination and intuition.
- **Ibn Khaldun:** in the *Muqaddimah* he explained the rise and fall of societies with the concepts of ***asabiyet*** (group solidarity) and ***umran*** (civilisation).

## Commonly confused

- *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* is al-Ghazali's critique; *The Incoherence of the Incoherence* is Ibn Rushd's reply.
- The *Peripatetic* school relies on reason and demonstration, *Illuminationism* on intuition and illumination.

## Study tip

Match each philosopher with one work and one key concept (al-Farabi–virtuous city, Ibn Sina–Necessary Being, Ibn Khaldun–asabiyya); for a two-question subject this is the most efficient preparation.
