## Core idea

A counsellor uses basic concepts of development, learning, motivation and social interaction to understand clients. This section covers the core of psychology's main fields needed for counselling. The test has 8 questions; they usually give a short example of behaviour and ask which concept explains it. The AGS-level developmental and learning psychology topics also form the foundation.

## Development

- **Piaget:** stages of cognitive development; assimilation, accommodation and equilibration.
- **Erikson:** a **psychosocial crisis** at each stage; in adolescence identity versus **identity confusion**.
- **Marcia:** identity statuses: identity achievement, moratorium, foreclosure, identity diffusion.
- **Kohlberg:** preconventional, conventional and postconventional levels of moral reasoning.
- **Bronfenbrenner:** the ecological model placing the person within nested systems (micro, meso, exo, macro, chrono).

## Learning and motivation

- **Classical conditioning (Pavlov):** a neutral stimulus paired with an unconditioned stimulus comes to trigger a response; generalisation, discrimination, extinction.
- **Operant conditioning (Skinner):** behaviour increases or decreases depending on its consequences; positive and negative reinforcement, punishment, schedules of reinforcement.
- **Social learning (Bandura):** modelling, vicarious reinforcement, **self-efficacy**.
- **Maslow:** hierarchy of needs; it is hard to pursue higher levels while basic needs are unmet.
- **Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation:** desire arising from the activity itself or from an external reward.

## Social psychology

- **Cognitive dissonance (Festinger):** discomfort when attitudes and behaviour clash, and the effort to reduce it.
- **Attribution:** explaining behaviour by the person or the situation; attributing others' behaviour to personality while ignoring the situation is the **fundamental attribution error**.
- **Conformity (Asch):** changing one's view under group pressure; **obedience (Milgram):** following an authority figure's orders.
- **Bystander effect:** the more people present, the less likely anyone is to help.
- **Groupthink:** a group stops thinking critically out of concern for harmony.
- **Stereotype, prejudice and discrimination:** a cognitive generalisation, a negative attitude and a behaviour, respectively.

## Commonly confused

- *Negative reinforcement* increases behaviour by removing an unpleasant stimulus; *punishment* decreases behaviour.
- *Conformity* is about peer-group pressure, *obedience* about an explicit order from an authority.
- *Moratorium* is an active search; *foreclosure* adopts others' decisions without exploring.

## Study tip

Build a "school scene" for each concept (a student who does homework only when the teacher is watching: extrinsic motivation). Questions keep describing such scenes in other words.
