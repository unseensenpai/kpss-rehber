## Core idea

Adjustment is a person's ability to balance their needs with the expectations of their environment. Whether a behaviour counts as a problem depends on its age-appropriateness, frequency, duration and whether it disrupts daily functioning. The school counsellor does not diagnose; they observe, assess, prevent, give short-term support and **refer** when needed. The test has 6 questions; they usually give a student example and ask you to recognise the behaviour or choose the right intervention.

## Types of problems

- **Internalising problems:** anxiety, excessive shyness, withdrawal, persistent unhappiness and lack of interest, physical complaints; easily missed because they are quiet.
- **Externalising problems:** aggression, rule-breaking, defiance, difficulties with attention and impulse control; noticed more because they affect classroom order.
- **School refusal:** difficulty going to school because of anxiety; the family usually knows.
- **Truancy:** deliberately skipping school without the family knowing; often together with other rule-breaking.
- **Risk behaviours in adolescence:** substance use, self-harm, marked changes in eating behaviour; with signs of self-harm, safety comes first and referral to a specialist is made without delay.

## Peer bullying

- **Criteria:** intentional, **repeated** and involving a **power imbalance**; a one-off fight is not bullying.
- **Types:** physical, verbal, relational (exclusion, rumours) and **cyberbullying**.
- **Roles:** bully, victim, bully-victim and bystanders; mobilising bystanders is a key part of whole-school programmes.

## Intervention

- **Risk and protective factors:** family conflict, peer rejection and academic failure raise risk; a supportive adult, school connectedness and social skills strengthen **resilience**.
- **Functional behaviour assessment:** examines antecedent, behaviour and consequence (ABC) to find the behaviour's function.
- **Positive behaviour support:** teaching and reinforcing appropriate behaviour instead of punishing; whole-school, classroom and individual tiers.
- **Cooperation:** consultation with teachers and families, and referral to health services or relevant units when needed.

## Commonly confused

- *School refusal* is anxiety-based and the family knows; *truancy* is deliberate and hidden.
- *Bullying* involves repetition and a power imbalance; a *conflict* is a one-off disagreement between equals.
- *Internalising* problems distress the person, *externalising* problems disturb those around them.

## Study tip

Among the options, look for observation, cooperation and appropriate referral rather than labelling or diagnosing; if there is a safety risk, the right answer is almost always to act at once.
