## Core idea

This course studies the written history of Turkish together with its present structure. The Turkish Language and Literature ÖABT has **15 questions** on it. The logic is that Turkish has changed over time in **sounds and suffixes** but kept its **agglutinative structure** and **vowel harmony**. Questions ask you to place a work in its period and script, compare a historical form with today's, or analyse grammar in Turkey Turkish.

## Historical periods

- **Old Turkic:** the **Göktürk** period with the **Orkhon Inscriptions** (the Kül Tigin, Bilge Kağan and Tonyukuk inscriptions) in the Göktürk script; the **Uyghur** period with Buddhist and Manichaean texts in the Uyghur script.
- **Middle Turkic:** **Karakhanid Turkic** and the first Islamic works: Yusuf Has Hacib's *Kutadgu Bilig* (Wisdom of Royal Glory), Kaşgarlı Mahmud's *Divanü Lugati't-Türk* (a dictionary explaining Turkic in Arabic), Edip Ahmet's *Atabetü'l-Hakayık*. Then Khwarezmian, Kipchak and **Chagatai** Turkic; the great name of Chagatai is Ali Şir Nevai.
- **Western Turkic:** **Old Anatolian Turkish** (Oghuz-based; the language of Yunus Emre and *Dede Korkut*), then **Ottoman Turkish** and **Turkey Turkish**.

## Historical sound and structure features

- Word-initial *b-* in Old Turkic became *v-* in many Oghuz words (*bar-* → *var-*).
- The Old Turkic *d* sound developed differently across branches: *adak* (foot) survives in Oghuz as *ayak*.
- In Old Anatolian Turkish, rounding harmony is less regular than today; some suffixes have a single form.
- With the **alphabet reform (1928)** Turkish moved to the Latin script; the Turkish Language Association founded afterwards led the simplification work.

## Modern Turkic

- ***Lehçe*:** branches that split early from the common root and are hard to understand (Chuvash, Yakut).
- ***Şive*:** branches that split later, with sound and form differences (Azerbaijani, Turkmen, Kazakh).
- ***Ağız*:** local speech differences within one written language.
- **Grammar of Turkey Turkish:** sound changes, derivational and inflectional suffixes, parts of speech, verbals, phrases and sentence types. The KPSS-level [Grammar](/kpss-rehber/en/topic/turkce/dil-bilgisi/) topic gives the basics; here linguistic explanations of the terms are expected.

## Commonly confused

- The *Göktürk script* is the writing of the Orkhon Inscriptions; the *Uyghur script* that of Uyghur-period texts.
- A *lehçe* split early and is hard to understand; a *şive* is closer; an *ağız* is local.
- *Kutadgu Bilig* is a work of advice; *Divanü Lugati't-Türk* is a dictionary.

## Study tip

Put the periods on a timeline and link each to one work and one script. Learn historical sound changes through a few sample words.
