## Core idea

Analytical chemistry teaches how to determine reliably **what is in** a sample (qualitative analysis) and **how much** (quantitative analysis). It does this by combining solution equilibria with measurement methods. There are **10** questions in the test. They mostly cover titration and equilibrium calculations, types of error and choosing a method.

## Data and solution equilibria

- **Accuracy** is closeness to the true value; **precision** is closeness of repeated measurements to each other.
- **Systematic error** is one-directional and can be corrected (calibration, blank runs); **random error** goes both ways and is expressed by the standard deviation.
- **Weak acid:** Ka = [H⁺]·[A⁻]/[HA]; unless the concentration is very low, [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka·C).
- **Buffer solution:** a mixture of a weak acid and its conjugate base; adding a little acid or base barely changes the pH. **Henderson-Hasselbalch:** pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]). Buffering works best near pH ≈ pKa.
- **Solubility product:** for AgCl, Ksp = [Ag⁺]·[Cl⁻]. A precipitate forms when the ion product exceeds Ksp.
- **Common ion effect:** solubility drops if one of the precipitate's ions is already present.

## Gravimetry and titrimetry

- **Gravimetry:** the analyte is separated and weighed as a pure, sparingly soluble precipitate of known composition.
- **Titration:** a solution of known concentration (titrant) is added until it reacts completely with the analyte. The **equivalence point** is the theoretical point; the **end point** is where the indicator is seen to change colour.
- **Acid-base titration:** strong acid with strong base gives pH 7 at equivalence; weak acid with strong base gives pH above 7 (phenolphthalein suits it). At half-equivalence pH = pKa.
- **Complexometry:** EDTA forms 1:1 complexes with metal ions; used to measure water hardness.
- **Redox titration:** permanganate (KMnO₄) in acidic medium acts as its own indicator.
- **Precipitation titration:** silver nitrate is used to determine chloride (argentometry).

## Instrumental methods

- **Nernst equation (25 °C):** E = E° − (0.0592/n)·log Q. A pH meter measures potential with a glass electrode (potentiometry).
- **Beer-Lambert law:** A = ε·b·c; absorbance is directly proportional to concentration. Transmittance T = I/I₀, A = −log T.
- **Atomic absorption** is used for metals; **UV-visible** and **IR** spectroscopy for molecules.
- **Chromatography:** components separate because they distribute differently between a stationary and a mobile phase. Gas chromatography (GC) suits volatile substances, liquid chromatography (HPLC) non-volatile ones; identification uses **retention time**.

## Commonly confused

- *Accuracy* (*doğruluk*) is closeness to the true value, *precision* (*kesinlik*) closeness of repeats; precise but inaccurate results point to systematic error.
- The *equivalence point* is calculated, the *end point* is observed.
- *Solubility* (s, mol/L) and the *solubility product* (Ksp) are not the same; for an AB₂ salt Ksp = 4s³.

## Study tip

Think of a titration curve in four regions: start, buffer region, equivalence and excess titrant. If you know which species dominates in each region, the pH calculation follows naturally.
