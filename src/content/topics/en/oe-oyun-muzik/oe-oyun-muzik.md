## Core idea

Play and music (*oyun ve müzik*) support language, social, cognitive and motor development in a **natural and motivating** context. Since many children in special education do not acquire play skills on their own, **play is also a skill to be taught**. There are **2 questions**; they ask about stages of play and the purposes of music.

## Stages of play

- **Social play (Parten):** solitary play → **onlooker** play (watches others) → **parallel** play (side by side with similar toys but no interaction) → **associative** play (shares and talks but no common goal) → **cooperative** play (common goal and roles).
- **Cognitive play:** **functional** (repetitive movement and object use), constructive, **symbolic** (using an object as if it were something else) and **games with rules**.
- Symbolic and cooperative play are often limited in children with ASD.

## Teaching play skills

- Play actions are broken into steps through **task analysis** and taught with modelling and prompts.
- **Video modelling** and play scripts are used to teach symbolic play.
- **Peer-mediated** play increases social interaction.
- Toys are chosen and adapted to the child's interests and motor characteristics.

## Music

- **Rhythm and songs:** ease teaching of words, concepts and sequences; make routine transitions clear.
- **Orff approach:** active participation through rhythm, body percussion and simple instruments.
- With **hearing impairment**, vibration and visual rhythm cues can be used.
- **Music education** is teaching led by the teacher; **music therapy** requires specialist training.

## Commonly confused

- In *parallel play* children play side by side without interaction; in *associative play* there is interaction but no common goal.
- *Functional play* uses an object for its actual function or repetitively; *symbolic play* gives the object a new meaning.

## Study tip

In play-stage questions check two things: are the children interacting, and do they share a goal? Those two answers settle the stage.
