## Core idea

The Turkish economy subject studies economic policy from the Republic to the present in **periods**. Each period has a **growth strategy** (private enterprise, etatism, planned import substitution, opening up) and a **crisis or external shock** that ends it. The test has **4 questions**; they usually ask you to place an institution, law or decision in the right period.

## 1923-1929: a national economy based on private enterprise

- The Republic inherited a war-worn, agricultural economy with weak industry.
- The **Izmir Economic Congress** (*İzmir İktisat Kongresi*, 1923) stressed a national economy and support for private enterprise.
- The **tithe** (*aşar*) was abolished in 1925, easing the burden on farmers.
- The **Law for the Encouragement of Industry** (*Teşvik-i Sanayi Kanunu*, 1927) gave private industry tax exemptions and other incentives.
- Because the **Treaty of Lausanne** limited customs tariffs until 1929, trade policy was not free. Railways and some foreign companies were nationalised.

## Etatism and the war years

- After the 1929 **Great Depression** and given weak private capital, **etatism** (*devletçilik*) was adopted in the 1930s: the state became a direct producer in industry.
- The **First Five-Year Industrial Plan** (1934) was implemented; public enterprises such as **Sümerbank** and **Etibank** were founded. The Central Bank also began operating in this period.
- During the Second World War defence spending rose; the **National Protection Law** (*Milli Korunma Kanunu*, 1940) gave the state wide powers over prices and production; the **Capital Tax** (*Varlık Vergisi*, 1942) was imposed.

## 1950-1960 and the planned period

- The 1950s brought **liberalisation**, farm mechanisation, road investment and **Marshall aid**. At the end of the decade, external payment problems and high inflation led to the 1958 stabilisation programme.
- The **State Planning Organisation** (*DPT*) was set up in 1960; the **First Five-Year Development Plan** began in 1963. Plans were binding for the public sector and indicative for the private sector.
- This period followed **import-substituting industrialisation**. In the 1970s, oil shocks and a foreign-exchange bottleneck pushed the economy into crisis.

## After 1980: opening up and crises

- **24 January 1980 decisions:** exchange-rate adjustment, removal of price controls, **export-oriented** growth and a move to a market economy.
- With **Decree No. 32** (1989), capital movements were liberalised and the lira became convertible.
- After the 1994 crisis the **5 April decisions** were taken. The **customs union** with the EU entered into force in 1996.
- After the **November 2000 and February 2001 crises**, Türkiye moved to a **floating exchange rate**; the **Transition to a Strong Economy Programme** was implemented; banking was restructured, the Central Bank was given **instrument independence** and a price-stability objective, and the BRSA (*BDDK*) became fully operational.
- In 2005 **six zeros were removed** from the currency (the New Turkish Lira); **explicit inflation targeting** began in 2006.

## Commonly confused

- The *Law for the Encouragement of Industry* favours the private sector; *etatism* puts public enterprise first.
- The *First Five-Year Industrial Plan* (1934) is not the *First Five-Year Development Plan* (1963).
- The *import-substitution* period looks to the home market; the period after *24 January* looks to exports.

## Study tip

Lay the periods out on a timeline and give each one a strategy, an institution and a crisis. For current growth, inflation and trade data, follow official sources such as TurkStat (*TÜİK*), the Central Bank (*TCMB*) and the Presidency of Strategy and Budget.
