## Core idea

**Akaid** (creed) is the set of Islam's articles of faith; **kelam** (theology) is the science that explains and defends them with scriptural and rational proofs. The theological schools answered the same questions differently: what is the status of someone who commits a grave sin, are God's attributes distinct from His essence, who creates human acts, can reason know good and evil? Questions usually give a view and ask which school holds it. In DHBT the depth depends on your level: bachelor's candidates follow the İlahiyat faculty curriculum, associate candidates the İlahiyat associate programme, and secondary-level candidates the İmam Hatip curriculum.

## Emergence of kalam

- The first debates grew out of political events and centred on the **grave sinner**.
- The **Kharijites** (*Hariciler*) declared the grave sinner an unbeliever; the **Murji'a** (*Mürcie*) deferred judgement to God.
- The **Mu'tazila** arose when **Wasil b. Ata** left the circle of Hasan al-Basri; it held that the grave sinner is in a position between believer and unbeliever.

## The Mu'tazila and its five principles (*usul-i hamse*)

- **Tevhid (oneness):** does not accept attributes as entities distinct from the essence; holds that the Quran is created.
- **Adl (justice):** God does no wrong; humans create their own acts.
- **El-va'd ve'l-vaîd (promise and threat):** God always fulfils His promise and His threat.
- **El-menzile beyne'l-menzileteyn (the intermediate position):** the grave sinner is between the two positions.
- **Emr bi'l-maruf nehy ani'l-münker:** commanding good and forbidding evil.
- It gives reason wide scope: **hüsün and kubuh** (good and evil) are known by reason and binding.

## Maturidism and Ash'arism

- **Maturidism:** Abu Mansur al-Maturidi (Samarkand); main work *Kitab al-Tawhid*. It spread in Hanafi circles. It gives reason an important role: reason can know some good and evil, and knowing God is required by reason even before revelation. Humans have a **cüz'i irade** (partial will): God creates the act, the human chooses it and is responsible.
- **Ash'arism:** Abu'l-Hasan al-Ash'ari, who was first a Mu'tazilite and then left them. Good and evil are set by **revelation**. To explain human acts it uses **kesb** (acquisition): God creates the act and the human acquires it.
- Both schools are Sunni (*Ehl-i Sünnet*); they affirm the reality of the attributes and that God will be seen in the hereafter (*rü'yetullah*).

## Attributes, prophethood and destiny

- **Essential attributes (*zati*):** existence, pre-eternity, everlastingness, oneness, unlikeness to created things, self-subsistence (*vücud, kıdem, beka, vahdaniyet, muhalefetün li'l-havadis, kıyam bi-nefsihi*).
- **Affirmative attributes (*subuti*):** life, knowledge, hearing, sight, will, power, speech and **tekvin**. Maturidis count tekvin as a separate attribute; Ash'aris relate it to power.
- **Prophethood (*nübüvvet*):** the qualities of prophets are **truthfulness, trustworthiness, infallibility, intelligence and conveying the message** (*sıdk, emanet, ismet, fetanet, tebliğ*). On the common view a **resul** brings a new book or law, while a **nebi** conveys an earlier law.
- **Kader and kaza:** for Maturidis **kader** is God's pre-eternal knowing and determining of everything, and **kaza** its realisation when the time comes; Ash'aris use the two terms the other way round. The **Jabriyya** (*Cebriyye*) deny human will, while the **Qadariyya** (*Kaderiyye*) attribute acts wholly to humans.

## Commonly confused

- *Akaid* is the articles of faith themselves; *kelam* is the science that defends them with proofs.
- *Kesb* (Ash'ari) stresses that humans acquire the act; *cüz'i irade* (Maturidi) stresses their power of choice.
- The *Mu'tazila* treat good and evil as rationally binding, the *Ash'aris* tie them to revelation; the *Maturidis* stand in between.

## Study tip

Put the three schools in a table with rows for the grave sinner, the attributes, human acts, good and evil, and seeing God. When you read a view, ask which cell it falls into; this lets you solve school questions quickly.
