## Core idea

*Tefsir* (tafsir) is the discipline of explaining the meaning of Qur’anic verses as far as human ability allows. To understand a verse, the *müfessir* (exegete) draws on other verses, the Prophet’s explanations, the views of the Companions and Successors, language and reason. Which source dominates determines the type of tafsir. Questions usually ask for the type or author of a work, the name of a method, or principles such as occasions of revelation and abrogation.

## Key concepts

- **Tefsir (tafsir):** literally to open up or reveal; explaining a verse’s meaning, relying mainly on transmitted knowledge.
- **Te’vil (ta’wil):** literally to return something to its origin; preferring, with evidence, one of the possible meanings of a word, relying mainly on reason and insight.
- **Müfessir (exegete):** a scholar who does tafsir.

## The birth of tafsir

- **The Prophet:** the first explainer of the Qur’an; his words and practice explain the verses.
- **Companions (*sahabe*):** they witnessed the revelation. Leading Companion exegetes include Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas’ud, Ubayy ibn Ka’b and Ali. Ibn Abbas is called “Tarjuman al-Qur’an” (interpreter of the Qur’an).
- **Successors (*tabiun*):** the generation that passed on what they learned from the Companions. The schools of Mecca (students of Ibn Abbas), Medina (circle of Ubayy ibn Ka’b) and Kufa (students of Ibn Mas’ud) took shape in this period; Mujahid and Ikrima belong to the Meccan school.

## Tradition-based and reason-based tafsir

- **Rivayet (me’sur) tefsiri (tradition-based tafsir):** explains the Qur’an by the Qur’an, the Sunnah and the words of the Companions and Successors. Al-Tabari’s *Jami’ al-Bayan* is the foundational work of this type; Ibn Kathir’s tafsir also belongs here.
- **Dirayet (re’y) tefsiri (reason-based tafsir):** uses language, rhetoric, reason and the sciences alongside transmission. Al-Zamakhshari’s *al-Kashshaf* (strong in language and rhetoric, with a Mu’tazili outlook), Fakhr al-Din al-Razi’s *Mafatih al-Ghayb* and al-Baydawi’s *Anwar al-Tanzil* are examples.
- **Ahkam tefsiri (legal tafsir):** focuses on verses containing legal rulings; al-Qurtubi’s *al-Jami’ li-Ahkam al-Qur’an* is a well-known example.

## Methods of tafsir

- **Tahlili (analytical) tafsir:** explains verses in detail, one by one, in the order of the mushaf; most classical works follow it.
- **İcmali (concise) tafsir:** explains verses briefly.
- **Mukaren (comparative) tafsir:** compares the views of different exegetes.
- **Mevzui (thematic) tafsir:** gathers the verses on one subject to show the Qur’an’s overall view of it.

## Principles and tafsir in Türkiye

- **Esbab-ı nüzul (occasions of revelation):** the event or question that led to a verse being revealed; it helps with the verse’s context.
- **Nasih-mensuh (abrogating and abrogated):** a later ruling lifting an earlier one; the lifting one is *nasih*, the lifted one *mensuh*.
- **Muhkem-müteşabih (clear and ambiguous):** verses with a clear, single meaning are *muhkem*; those open to several meanings or not fully graspable are *müteşabih* (Al Imran 7).
- **Elmalılı Hamdi Yazır:** author of *Hak Dini Kur’an Dili*, the Turkish tafsir and translation commissioned by the Presidency of Religious Affairs on a decision of the Turkish Grand National Assembly.

## Commonly confused

- *Tefsir* explains mainly on the basis of transmission; *te’vil* chooses between possible meanings with evidence.
- *Tradition-based tafsir* (al-Tabari) rests on transmission; *reason-based tafsir* (al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi) also uses language and reason heavily.
- *Analytical tafsir* follows the verses in order; *thematic tafsir* is organised by subject.

## Study tip

Make a table with columns for work, author and type: al-Tabari tradition-based, al-Zamakhshari and al-Razi reason-based, al-Qurtubi legal, Elmalılı Turkish tafsir. Matching works with authors is the most frequently tested knowledge in this subject.
