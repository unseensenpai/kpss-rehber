## Core idea

The science of hadith studies the words, acts and approvals attributed to the Prophet through two questions: **who transmitted it** (the chain) and **what was transmitted** (the text). A narration's value depends on how many people passed it on, how reliable they were, and where the chain ends. Questions mostly ask for definitions of terms, classifications, and the main collections with their authors. In DHBT the depth depends on your level: bachelor's candidates follow the İlahiyat faculty curriculum, associate candidates the İlahiyat associate programme, and secondary-level candidates the İmam Hatip curriculum.

## Basic terms

- **Hadis (hadith):** words, acts and *takrir* (tacit approvals) attributed to the Prophet.
- **Sünnet (sunnah):** the Prophet's exemplary way; divided into **verbal** (*kavli*), **practical** (*fiili*) and **tacit** (*takriri*).
- **Haber and eser (athar):** usually synonyms of hadith; some scholars reserve *eser* for statements of the Companions and their Successors.
- **Sened / isnad (chain):** the series of **narrators** (*ravi*). **Metin (text):** the words the chain conveys.
- **Hadis-i kudsi (sacred hadith):** its meaning is from God, its wording from the Prophet; it is distinct from the Quran.

## Classification

- **By number of narrators:** **mütevatir**, transmitted by so many people that agreement on a lie is unthinkable; **ahad**, below that level. Ahad is further divided into **meşhur, aziz and garib** (well-known, rare, singular).
- **By reliability:** **sahih**, with an unbroken chain of upright (*adil*) and precise (*zabıt*) narrators, free of irregularity (*şaz*) and hidden defect (*illet*); **hasen**, whose narrators are slightly less precise; **zayıf**, lacking one of these conditions.
- **By where the chain ends:** **merfu** reaches the Prophet, **mevkuf** a Companion (*sahabi*), **maktu** a Successor (*tabii*).

## Compilation and collections

- In the Prophet's time some Companions kept personal written pages (*sahife*).
- Official collection was launched under the Umayyad caliph **Umar b. Abd al-Aziz**; **Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri** is prominent in this work.
- **Types of collection:** a **müsned** arranges hadiths by Companion (Ahmad b. Hanbal's *Musnad*); a **sünen** follows the chapters of fiqh; a **câmi** covers all topics such as faith, ethics and exegesis.
- **Kütüb-i Sitte (the Six Books):** the *Sahih*s of Bukhari and Muslim and the *Sunan*s of Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i and Ibn Majah. Some scholars count Malik's *Muwatta* as the sixth book instead of Ibn Majah.
- **Sahihayn:** the joint name for the works of Bukhari and Muslim.

## Narrator criticism and fabrication

- **Cerh and ta'dil:** the science that assesses narrators' reliability; **cerh** finds a narrator faulty, **ta'dil** finds them trustworthy.
- **Mevzu hadis (fabricated hadith):** a saying falsely attributed to the Prophet. Political and sectarian rivalry, storytellers seeking to sway the public, and well-meant but mistaken efforts to encourage piety are counted among the main causes. A fabricated report is not a hadith; it may be quoted only while stating that it is fabricated.

## Commonly confused

- A *hadis-i kudsi* is worded by the Prophet; the *Quran* is God's speech in both wording and meaning.
- A *mevkuf* report ends with a Companion, a *maktu* report with a Successor.
- A *müsned* is arranged by Companion, a *sünen* by fiqh topics.

## Study tip

Tie the classifications to three questions: "How many transmitted it?", "How reliable is it?", "Where does the chain end?" If you learn each term as the answer to one of these, you will easily separate similar-looking terms in the options.
