## Core idea

*Hitabet* (oratory) is the art of conveying an idea to an audience through speech and **persuading** them. In religious oratory the aim is to deliver religious knowledge correctly and effectively and to guide people towards good. The Qur'an describes this method as inviting "with wisdom and good counsel" (al-Nahl 125). Questions mostly ask you to tell the *hutbe* (sermon) from the *vaaz* (religious talk), or to find which principle a speech example follows. Depth varies by level: bachelor's candidates follow the İlahiyat faculty curriculum, associate candidates the İlahiyat associate programme and secondary-level candidates the İmam Hatip curriculum.

## Vaaz and hutbe

- ***Vaaz*:** a talk giving counsel and knowledge on religious matters. Its time and length are more flexible; it is usually given in the mosque from a lectern (*kürsü*).
- ***Hutbe*:** a speech tied to particular prayers and delivered from the *minber* (pulpit). It includes praise of God, blessings on the Prophet, a verse or hadith, counsel and prayer. It has two parts, with the speaker sitting briefly in between.
- **Friday *hutbe*:** delivered **before** the prayer; for the Hanafis it is a condition for the Friday prayer to be valid.
- **Eid *hutbe*:** delivered **after** the prayer and is *sunnah* (recommended).
- In Türkiye, Friday sermons are generally prepared centrally by the Presidency of Religious Affairs (Diyanet).

## Qualities of a good speaker

- **Sincerity (*ihlas*):** the aim is God's pleasure and the listeners' good, not self-display.
- **Word and deed in harmony:** the *hatip* (speaker) must first live what they preach; otherwise persuasive power is lost.
- **Knowledge:** knowing the subject from sound sources; quoting verses and hadith accurately.
- **Knowing the audience:** choosing topic and language to suit listeners' age, level of knowledge and needs.
- **Voice and body:** clear **diction**, suitable tone and emphasis, eye contact and measured **body language**.

## Preparation and delivery

- **Choosing a topic:** one main idea that is current and meets the congregation's needs.
- **Research:** supporting the topic with verses, authentic hadith and reliable examples.
- **Plan:** an attention-catching **introduction**, a **development** backed by evidence, a **conclusion** summing up the main idea.
- **Delivery:** plain, clear language; avoiding needless repetition and length; keeping to time.

## Persuasion, communication and the Prophet's example

- **Persuasion:** a balance of evidence that speaks to reason and examples and feelings that speak to the heart; using both warning and hope together.
- **Gentle speech (*kavl-i leyyin*):** the Qur'an commands Moses and Aaron to speak gently even to Pharaoh (Ta-Ha 44); a harsh style breaks communication.
- **The Prophet's style:** he expressed much meaning in few words (*cevami'u'l-kelim*). He repeated important sayings so they would be understood, drew attention by asking questions and used comparisons and examples. He spoke according to his listener's situation and advised making things easy. He is known to have kept his sermons short.

## Commonly confused

- The *Friday hutbe* comes before the prayer and is a condition; the *Eid hutbe* comes after the prayer and is *sunnah*.
- The *hutbe* is tied to specific acts of worship and given from the *minber*; the *vaaz* is a counsel talk with a flexible time.
- *Hitabet* is the art of persuading an audience; *tebliğ* is the duty of conveying the religion's message.

## Study tip

Make a small table comparing *hutbe* and *vaaz* by time, place, ruling and content. Match the speaker's qualities with concrete examples; questions usually describe a speaker's behaviour and ask which principle it reflects.
