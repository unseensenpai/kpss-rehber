## Core idea

Fiqh is knowing the religious rulings on the acts of the *mükellef* (a sane adult who is legally responsible), derived from their **detailed proofs**. The logic is simple: every ruling rests on a **proof**, *usul al-fiqh* (legal theory) studies how rulings are drawn from proofs, and the resulting rulings are classed as **defining** (*teklifi*) or **declaratory** (*vaz'i*). Questions usually ask for a definition, the category of a ruling, or which school stresses a given proof. In DHBT the depth depends on your level: bachelor's candidates follow the İlahiyat faculty curriculum, associate candidates the İlahiyat associate programme, and secondary-level candidates the İmam Hatip curriculum.

## Sources (proofs)

- **Kitap:** the Quran; the first and fundamental source.
- **Sunnah:** the Prophet's words, acts and approvals; it explains the Quran and can also establish rulings.
- **İcma (consensus):** agreement of the qualified jurists (*müçtehit*) of a period on a ruling.
- **Kıyas (analogy):** extending a known ruling to a new case because they share the same **illet** (effective cause). Its elements: original case, new case, cause, ruling.
- **Secondary proofs:** **istihsan** (leaving analogy for a stronger reason; especially Hanafis), **maslahat-ı mürsele** (public interest not regulated by the texts; especially Malikis), **örf** (settled custom not contrary to religion), **sedd-i zerai** (blocking the means to a forbidden result).

## Types of ruling

- **Defining rulings** ask the person to do, avoid, or freely choose an act:
  - **Farz:** required, established by definitive proof.
  - **Vacip:** for Hanafis, required but established by probable proof; other schools treat it as a synonym of farz.
  - **Sünnet / mendub:** requested, but omitting it is not punishable.
  - **Mübah:** doing or not doing is equally allowed.
  - **Mekruh:** avoidance is requested; Hanafis split it into *tahrimen* (close to forbidden) and *tenzihen* (mildly disliked).
  - **Haram:** definitively forbidden.
- **Declaratory rulings** make something the **cause, condition or impediment (mani)** of another ruling; **validity, defectiveness and nullity** (*sıhhat, fesad, butlan*) and **rule and concession** (*azimet, ruhsat*) are also studied here.

## Schools

- **Hanafi:** Abu Hanifa (Numan b. Sabit); gives wide room to reasoned opinion and istihsan. The prevalent school in Turkey.
- **Maliki:** Malik b. Anas; values the practice of the people of Medina and public interest.
- **Shafi'i:** Muhammad b. Idris al-Shafi'i; his *al-Risala* is regarded as the first systematic work of legal theory; he criticised istihsan.
- **Hanbali:** Ahmad b. Hanbal; known for close adherence to texts and narrations.

## Divisions and family law

- **İbadat (worship):** purity, prayer, fasting, zakat, pilgrimage.
- **Muamelat (transactions):** contracts such as sale, lease and partnership, and property relations; family law is also studied here.
- **Ukubat (penalties):** **had** (amount fixed by the texts), **kısas** (retaliation in kind), **tazir** (left to the authority's discretion).
- **Family law:** **marriage** (*nikah*) is formed by offer and acceptance and requires witnesses; the wife receives a **mahr** (*mehir*); marriage can end by **divorce** (*talak*) among other ways; after separation the woman observes a **waiting period** (*iddet*).

## Commonly confused

- *Farz* rests on definitive proof, *vacip* (for Hanafis) on probable proof.
- *Istihsan* moves from analogy to a stronger reason; *maslahat-ı mürsele* serves a public interest the texts leave unregulated.
- A *had* penalty is fixed by the texts; a *tazir* penalty is left to the authority's judgement.

## Study tip

First line up the seven defining rulings like a ladder by degree of bindingness, then make a small table matching each proof to the school that uses it most. When you see a school's name in a question, its proofs from that table should come to mind at once.
