## Core idea

Islamic history studies the political, administrative and cultural development of the Muslim community after the Prophet's death (632). The Prophet's own life is a separate topic: [Sirah](/kpss-rehber/en/topic/temel-din-bilgisi/siyer/). The logic of the topic is that every period has a **style of government**, an **internal problem** and a **lasting contribution**. Questions mostly ask you to match an event or institution with the right caliph or state. Depth varies by level: bachelor's candidates follow the İlahiyat (theology) faculty curriculum, associate candidates the İlahiyat associate programme and secondary-level candidates the İmam Hatip school curriculum.

## The Four Caliphs (632–661)

- **Abu Bakr:** the struggle against those refusing *zakat* and against false prophets (the *ridde* wars) preserved unity. After many Qur'an memorisers fell at the Battle of Yamama, the Qur'an was **compiled** (*cem'*) into a single *mushaf* (codex) by a committee led by Zayd ibn Thabit. Campaigns towards Iraq and Syria began.
- **Umar:** the period of the widest conquests; the victories of Yarmuk, Qadisiyya and Nahavand opened Syria, Iraq and Iran, and Jerusalem and Egypt were taken. The *divan* (state registers) was set up, the **Hijri calendar** introduced and judges (*kadı*) appointed. He was the first caliph to use the title *Emîrü'l-mü'minîn* (Commander of the Faithful).
- **Uthman:** because of differences in recitation, the Qur'an was **copied** (*istinsah*) and sent to the main centres. The first navy was built and a campaign was made to Cyprus. He was martyred in the unrest at the end of his rule.
- **Ali:** the capital moved to Kufa. The battles of the **Camel** and **Siffin**, the **arbitration** (*tahkim*) after Siffin and the rise of the **Kharijites**, who rejected it, belong to this period.

## The Umayyads (661–750)

- With **Muawiya**, the caliphate became a **dynastic monarchy** passed from father to son; the capital was Damascus.
- **Karbala** (680): the martyrdom of Husayn left deep and lasting marks on the community.
- **Abd al-Malik:** Arabic became the official language of the registers, Islamic coins were minted and the Dome of the Rock was built in Jerusalem.
- **Walid I:** Tariq ibn Ziyad crossed into Spain (711) and conquests continued in Transoxiana. Sieges of Constantinople were also undertaken.
- **Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz** is remembered for his justice. Discrimination against non-Arab Muslims (*mevali*) caused resentment and contributed to the dynasty's fall.

## The Abbasids (750–1258)

- A dynasty descended from the Prophet's uncle Abbas; **Baghdad** was founded by Abu Ja'far al-Mansur and made the capital.
- **Battle of Talas** (751): won against China with Turkish support; it sped up the rapprochement between Turks and Islam.
- The reigns of **Harun al-Rashid** and **al-Ma'mun** were the golden age. Through the **translation movement** centred on the *Bayt al-Hikma* (House of Wisdom), Greek, Persian and Indian works were translated into Arabic.
- From **al-Mu'tasim** onwards, Turks gained weight in the army and administration.
- Political power passed in time to the Buyids and then the Seljuks; Baghdad fell to the Mongols in 1258.

## Al-Andalus and the Turks

- **Umayyads of al-Andalus** (756–1031): founded by Abd al-Rahman (al-Dakhil), who escaped the Abbasids; the capital **Cordoba** became a centre that passed learning to Europe. Abd al-Rahman III took the title of caliph.
- **Turks:** Islamisation accelerated after Talas. The first Muslim Turkish state was the **Karakhanids**, followed by the Ghaznavids and Seljuks. Details belong to the History topics.
- **Centres of learning:** the *Bayt al-Hikma* in Baghdad, the Seljuk **Nizamiyya madrasas** founded by Nizam al-Mulk, Cordoba and Cairo (al-Azhar).

## Commonly confused

- *Cem'* is the compilation of the Qur'an into one codex under Abu Bakr; *istinsah* is its copying under Uthman.
- The *Umayyads of al-Andalus* were of Umayyad descent but a separate state in Spain, contemporary with the Abbasids; the *Umayyads* proper ended in 750.
- The *Bayt al-Hikma* was the Abbasid translation and research centre in Baghdad; the *Nizamiyya* were Seljuk madrasas.

## Study tip

Make a three-column table for each caliph and ruler: key event, institution founded, conquest made. Most questions ask for this matching; keep only the dates you are sure of and practise putting events in order.
