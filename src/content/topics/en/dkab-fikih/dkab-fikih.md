## Core idea

*Fıkıh* (fiqh) is knowing the religious rulings on human acts from their detailed evidence; *usul-i fıkıh* (legal theory) studies from which sources and by what method those rulings are derived. Because the same evidence can lead to different conclusions, **schools of law** arose; their differences are regarded as legitimate *ictihad* (independent reasoning). The test has 7 questions, making this one of the heaviest subjects in the field. Questions ask about sources and rulings, the conditions and essential parts of worship, and differences between schools.

## Sources and rulings

- **Primary sources:** the **Qur'an**, the **sunnah**, ***icma*** (consensus of the jurists of an era) and ***kıyas*** (analogy: linking an unregulated case to a regulated one through a shared *illet*, effective cause).
- **Secondary sources:** ***istihsan*** (leaving analogy for a more fitting ruling on a specific ground; stressed by Hanafis), ***istislah*** / *mesalih-i mürsele* (public interest; stressed by Malikis), ***örf*** (custom), *sedd-i zerai* (blocking the means), *istishab* (presumption of continuity).
- **Prescriptive rulings:** **fard**, **wajib**, **sunnah/mandub**, **mubah**, **makruh**, **haram**. Only the **Hanafis** distinguish fard from wajib: wajib rests on evidence less certain than fard.
- **Declaratory rulings:** cause, condition, impediment; valid-defective-void; ***azimet*** (the original rule) and ***ruhsat*** (a concession granted because of hardship).

## Schools and history

- **Hanafi:** Abu Hanifa; Kufa; gives wide room to reasoned opinion, analogy and istihsan.
- **Maliki:** Imam Malik; Medina; values the **practice of the people of Medina**.
- **Shafi'i:** Imam al-Shafi'i; his *al-Risala* is among the first systematic works of legal theory.
- **Hanbali:** Ahmad ibn Hanbal; close adherence to texts and hadith.
- **Ja'fari:** the Shi'i legal tradition, attributed to Ja'far al-Sadiq.
- ***Mecelle***: the Ottoman civil code prepared on the basis of Hanafi law by a committee chaired by Ahmed Cevdet Pasha.

## Worship

- **Ablution:** for Hanafis it has four obligatory parts (washing the face, the arms including elbows, wiping a quarter of the head, washing the feet including ankles); Shafi'is add **intention** and **sequence**.
- Example of difference: bleeding breaks ablution for Hanafis but not for Shafi'is; touching the opposite sex breaks it for Shafi'is but not for Hanafis.
- **Prayer:** its **conditions** (purity from ritual impurity and filth, covering the body, facing the qibla, time, intention) come before it; its **essential parts** (opening takbir, standing, recitation, bowing, prostration, final sitting) are within it.
- **Zakat:** due on wealth reaching the *nisap* (minimum threshold) that has been held for a year; one-fortieth for money and trade goods.
- **Fasting and pilgrimage:** acts that break the fast require *kaza* (making up) or *kaza and keffaret* (making up plus expiation); the core pillar of hajj is **standing at Arafat**.

## Family, transactions and crime

- **Contract (*akit*):** formed by offer and acceptance; e.g. marriage, sale, lease.
- **Penalties:** ***had*** (fixed by text), ***kısas*** (retaliation), ***ta'zir*** (discretionary).

## Commonly confused

- A *condition* is outside and before the act of worship; an *essential part* is within it.
- *Fard* rests on certain evidence, *wajib* (for Hanafis) on less certain evidence.
- *Istihsan* leaves analogy for a more fitting ruling; *istislah* considers public interest.

## Study tip

For each topic, first ask the category of the ruling (fard, wajib, sunnah...), then the school difference; studying Hanafi and Shafi'i views side by side in two columns prevents confusion.
