## Core idea

Urbanisation is the flow of population from the countryside to cities and the social, economic and spatial change that comes with it. When this change is unplanned, housing, infrastructure and **environmental** problems follow. The Public Administration test has about **6 questions** on it. Questions mostly ask about the causes of urbanisation in Turkey, squatter housing and zoning policy, metropolitan administration and international environmental documents.

## Causes of urbanisation and the Turkish experience

- **Push factors:** farm mechanisation, land split through inheritance, rural poverty and security problems.
- **Pull factors:** jobs, education, health and social services in the city.
- **After 1950:** farm mechanisation and road building sped up rural-to-urban migration.
- **Over-urbanisation:** urban population grows faster than industry and services can provide jobs; it swells the informal sector.
- ***Gecekondu*** (squatter housing): homes built without permission on someone else's land, usually public land; **zoning amnesties** (*imar affı*) were used at times and criticised for encouraging new unauthorised building.

## Planning, urban regeneration and metropolitan areas

- **Master development plan** (*nazım imar planı*): a large-scale plan showing the general framework of land use.
- **Implementation development plan** (*uygulama imar planı*): a detailed plan at parcel and building level, consistent with the master plan.
- **Urban regeneration** (*kentsel dönüşüm*): rebuilding areas at disaster risk or unhealthy areas; important in Turkey because of earthquake risk.
- **Metropolitan municipality** (*büyükşehir belediyesi*): an upper-tier authority coordinating district municipalities in large cities; a 2012 reform extended metropolitan boundaries to the provincial borders and abolished special provincial administrations in those provinces.

## Environmental problems and international policy

- **Main problems:** air, water and soil pollution, noise, waste management, loss of biodiversity, **climate change**.
- **Stockholm Conference (1972):** the first major UN conference to put the environment on the international agenda.
- **Brundtland Report (1987):** defined **sustainable development** as meeting today's needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs.
- **Rio Summit (1992):** documents such as Agenda 21 and the framework convention on climate change.
- **Kyoto Protocol (1997)** and **Paris Agreement (2015):** cutting greenhouse gases; the Paris Agreement asks every party for nationally determined contributions.

## Environmental law in Turkey

- **Constitution:** everyone has the right to live in a healthy and balanced environment; protecting it is a duty of the state and citizens.
- **Environment Law (No. 2872):** adopts the **polluter pays** principle.
- **EIA** (*ÇED*, environmental impact assessment): examining a project's environmental effects before it starts.

## Commonly confused

- *Urbanisation* is a structural population shift; *over-urbanisation* is when jobs cannot keep up with population growth.
- A *master plan* shows the general framework; an *implementation plan* works at parcel level.
- The *Kyoto Protocol* placed obligations mainly on developed countries; the *Paris Agreement* covers all parties.

## Study tip

Put international environmental documents on a timeline with the year and a one-line contribution. For urbanisation, think of a Turkish example for each concept. Environmental and zoning rules change often, so check the current legislation and recent developments in up-to-date sources.
