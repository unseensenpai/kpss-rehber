## Core idea

Organic chemistry studies the structure and reactions of carbon compounds. The key to the subject is the **functional group**: how a molecule reacts depends largely on its functional group and on where electrons are rich or poor. There are **10** questions in the test. They mostly cover predicting products, choosing mechanisms, naming and isomerism.

## Structure and stereochemistry

- **Functional groups:** alcohol (−OH), aldehyde (−CHO), ketone (C=O), carboxylic acid (−COOH), ester (−COO−), amine (−NH₂), ether (C−O−C).
- **IUPAC:** choose the longest chain containing the functional group; number it so the functional group gets the lowest number.
- **Structural isomers** share a formula but have atoms connected differently; **stereoisomers** have the same connections but a different arrangement in space.
- **Chiral centre:** a carbon bonded to four different groups. A non-superimposable mirror-image pair is a pair of **enantiomers**; they rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions. n chiral centres give at most 2ⁿ stereoisomers.
- **Geometric isomerism:** if each carbon of a double bond carries two different groups, cis-trans (or E/Z) isomers exist.

## Hydrocarbons and mechanisms

- **Alkanes** contain only single bonds and undergo radical substitution with halogens in light.
- **Addition to alkenes:** for HX addition **Markovnikov's rule** applies: H bonds to the carbon that already has more hydrogens, because the more stable carbocation forms (tertiary > secondary > primary).
- **SN2:** one step, backside attack, inversion of configuration; fast for primary substrates.
- **SN1:** two steps, carbocation intermediate, racemisation; dominant for tertiary substrates in polar protic solvents.
- **Elimination (E1, E2):** strong, bulky bases and heat favour elimination; usually the more substituted alkene forms (Zaitsev's rule).
- **Aromaticity:** cyclic, planar, fully conjugated structures with 4n + 2 π electrons are aromatic (benzene has 6 π). Benzene undergoes **electrophilic aromatic substitution** rather than addition (nitration, halogenation, Friedel-Crafts).

## Oxygen and nitrogen compounds

- **Oxidation of alcohols:** a primary alcohol oxidises to an aldehyde, then a carboxylic acid; a secondary alcohol to a ketone; a tertiary alcohol does not oxidise easily.
- **Carbonyl group:** its carbon is partially positive and undergoes **nucleophilic addition**. Aldehydes oxidise more easily than ketones (Tollens' test).
- **Esterification:** carboxylic acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (acid-catalysed); the reverse is hydrolysis, and basic hydrolysis is saponification.
- **Amines** act as bases thanks to the lone pair on nitrogen.
- **Polymers:** addition polymers (polyethylene) form by opening double bonds; condensation polymers (nylon, polyester) form with loss of a small molecule (usually water).
- **Biomolecules:** proteins form from amino acids joined by peptide bonds, polysaccharides from monosaccharides joined by glycosidic bonds; fats are esters of glycerol and fatty acids.

## Commonly confused

- *SN1* goes through a carbocation and gives racemisation; *SN2* is one step and inverts the configuration.
- *Structural isomers* (*yapı izomerleri*) differ in connections, *stereoisomers* have the same connections but a different spatial arrangement.
- An *aldehyde* carries the carbonyl at the end of a chain, a *ketone* within it.

## Study tip

In every reaction first mark the nucleophile (electron-rich) and the electrophile (electron-poor). If you draw arrows from electron-rich to electron-poor sites, the products appear without memorising.
