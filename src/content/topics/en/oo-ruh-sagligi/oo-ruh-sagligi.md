## Core idea

Mental health is not just the absence of problems; it is the child's ability to regulate emotions, form relationships and function in age-appropriate ways. Many behaviours are **developmentally typical** at a certain age; what matters is how often and how long they occur and whether they affect daily life. The teacher does not diagnose; they observe, record, work with the family and refer to a specialist when needed. The test has 4 questions; they usually describe a behaviour and ask for the most suitable adult response.

## Common difficulties

- **Separation anxiety:** difficulty parting from the family when starting school; short, consistent goodbyes and a gradual settling-in period help.
- **Developmental fears:** of the dark, animals, being alone; the child is not mocked or pushed into the feared situation.
- **Temper tantrums:** if attention-seeking, stay calm and do not reward the behaviour (**extinction**); apply rules consistently.
- **Sibling jealousy:** special time for the child and preparing them for the new sibling in advance.
- **Thumb sucking, nail biting:** may signal anxiety or habit; focus on the cause instead of punishing or shaming.
- **Bedwetting:** if it continues past the expected age, a medical cause is checked first; the child is not blamed.
- **Developmental disfluency:** hesitations in speech are common at this age; do not interrupt or tell the child to "speak slowly".
- **Aggression and withdrawal:** observe and record when and in which situations the behaviour appears.

## Developmental differences and risk

- **Specialist assessment:** marked and persistent differences in language, social interaction, attention and activity level call for referral; the teacher does not label.
- **Risk and protective factors:** family conflict, neglect and traumatic events raise risk; secure attachment, a supportive adult and consistent routines build **resilience**.
- **Neglect and abuse:** with signs such as unexplained injuries, sudden behaviour changes or lack of care, the teacher has a legal duty to report; check current legislation and school procedures in official sources.

## Commonly confused

- A *developmentally typical* behaviour is temporary and age-appropriate; a situation *needing specialist support* is persistent and disrupts daily functioning.
- *Extinction* means not reinforcing an unwanted behaviour; *punishment* means giving an unpleasant consequence.

## Study tip

Options involving shaming, mocking, threats or force are almost always wrong. Look for the option with consistency, calm and cooperation with the family.
