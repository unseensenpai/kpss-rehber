## Core idea

Financial management makes the firm's decisions on **raising funds (financing)**, **investing them in assets** and distributing profit. In the modern view the main aim is not short-term profit but **maximising firm value (shareholder wealth)**, because this aim also accounts for the time value of money and risk. The test has **8 questions**; they usually ask you to compute a ratio or a present value, or to make an investment decision with appraisal methods.

## Time value of money

- One lira today is worth more than one lira in the future, because it can be invested to earn interest.
- **Future value:** FV = PV × (1 + r)^n.
- **Present value:** PV = FV / (1 + r)^n. The higher the rate or the longer the period, the lower the present value.
- **Annuities** (equal periodic payments) and perpetuities are discounted with the same logic.

## Ratio analysis

- **Liquidity ratios:** **current ratio** = current assets / short-term liabilities. **Acid-test ratio** = (current assets - inventories) / short-term liabilities. The **cash ratio** uses only cash and marketable securities.
- **Financial structure ratios:** debt ratio = total debt / total assets; equity / total assets.
- **Activity ratios:** **inventory turnover** = cost of goods sold / average inventory; receivables turnover = net sales / average trade receivables; asset turnover = net sales / total assets.
- **Profitability ratios:** net profit margin = net profit / net sales; **return on assets (ROA)** = net profit / total assets; **return on equity (ROE)** = net profit / equity.
- **Du Pont analysis:** ROA = net profit margin × asset turnover; ROE = ROA × (total assets / equity).

## Capital budgeting and cost of capital

- **Net present value (NPV)** (*net bugünkü değer*): present value of future cash inflows minus the initial investment. Accept the project if NPV > 0.
- **Internal rate of return (IRR)** (*iç kârlılık oranı*): the discount rate that makes NPV zero. Accept if IRR exceeds the cost of capital.
- **Payback period:** how many years cash inflows take to recover the initial investment; simple, but ignores the time value of money and cash flows after the payback point.
- **Profitability index:** present value of inflows / initial investment; accept if above 1.
- **Weighted average cost of capital (WACC)** (*AOSM*): the average cost of each financing source weighted by its share in the capital structure; used as the cut-off rate for investments. Because interest is tax-deductible, the after-tax cost of debt is lower.

## Leverage, working capital and risk

- **Operating leverage** arises from fixed operating costs: a change in sales changes operating profit by a larger percentage.
- **Financial leverage** arises from fixed financing costs such as interest: a change in operating profit changes earnings per share by a larger percentage. Their product is **combined leverage**.
- **Break-even point (units):** fixed costs / (unit selling price - unit variable cost).
- **Gross working capital** is total current assets; **net working capital** is current assets minus short-term liabilities.
- **Risk and return:** risk that diversification can remove is **unsystematic**; market risk that cannot be removed is **systematic**. **Beta** measures an asset's sensitivity to the market. **CAPM:** expected return = risk-free rate + beta × (market return - risk-free rate).

## Commonly confused

- The *current ratio* includes inventories; the *acid-test ratio* excludes them.
- *Operating leverage* comes from fixed operating costs; *financial leverage* from fixed interest costs.
- *Systematic risk* cannot be diversified away; *unsystematic risk* can.

## Study tip

Write each ratio with its numerator and denominator on a card and compute them all once on a small balance sheet. For investment questions remember the decision rule: accept when NPV is above zero, IRR above the cost of capital, and the profitability index above one.
