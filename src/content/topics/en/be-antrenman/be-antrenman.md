## Core idea

Training (*antrenman*) is a set of loads applied in a **planned and systematic** way to improve performance. The logic is simple: the body gets a little more load than it is used to, it **adapts** to that load with rest, and performance rises. The test has **3 questions**; they usually ask which principle is applied in an example or which load component has changed.

## Principles

- **Overload:** to adapt, the load must be above the usual level.
- **Progressive overload:** the load is raised gradually over time.
- **Specificity:** the body adapts specifically to the type of load; a sprinter trains sprints.
- **Individuality:** age, level and recovery speed differ; the programme is adjusted to the person.
- **Reversibility:** if training stops, gains are lost over time.
- **Continuity, variety and the balance of load and rest.**

## Load and planning

- **Intensity:** how heavy or fast the load is (heart rate, weight lifted).
- **Volume:** total amount of work (repetitions, sets, distance).
- **Density:** the time relationship between work and rest.
- **Duration and frequency:** length of a stimulus or session, and sessions per week.
- **Supercompensation:** capacity rising above the starting level after enough rest.
- **Periodisation:** preparation, competition and transition periods; macro-, meso- and microcycles from largest to smallest.
- **Methods:** **continuous** (non-stop, moderate intensity, long duration), **interval** (incomplete rest between bouts), **repetition** (high intensity with full rest), **circuit** training.

## Commonly confused

- *Intensity* is how hard the load is; *volume* is how much is done.
- In the *interval* method rest is incomplete; in the *repetition* method it is nearly full.
- *Overload* is a principle; *overtraining* is a drop in performance caused by too little recovery.

## Study tip

Build a school example for each principle (raising repetitions each week is progressive overload). If you look at what changes in a question's example, the principle follows.
