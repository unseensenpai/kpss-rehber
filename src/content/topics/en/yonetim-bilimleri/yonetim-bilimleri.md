## Core idea

Administrative sciences study how organisations are set up, how they work and how people in them are managed; public administration applies this knowledge to the state. The subject rests on understanding how schools of thought followed one another over time. The Public Administration test has about **8 questions** on it. Questions mostly match thinkers with concepts and ask for the core assumptions of each approach.

## Birth of the discipline and the classical approach

- **Woodrow Wilson:** treats public administration as a separate field and argues for the **politics-administration dichotomy**.
- **Taylor (scientific management):** measuring work to find the most efficient method, division of labour and standardisation.
- **Fayol (management functions):** planning, organising, commanding, coordinating and controlling; principles of management.
- **Weber (bureaucracy):** an ideal-type organisation run on written rules, hierarchy, specialisation and impersonal relations.
- **Gulick:** sums up the executive's duties with the acronym **POSDCORB**.

## Neoclassical and modern approaches

- **Hawthorne studies (Mayo):** showed that productivity depends not only on physical conditions but also on social relations and **informal groups**.
- **Simon:** decision-makers have limited information and time, so they choose what is satisfactory rather than optimal (**bounded rationality**).
- **Systems approach:** sees the organisation as an open system of inputs, processes and outputs interacting with its environment.
- **Contingency approach:** there is no single best way to manage; the right structure depends on conditions.

## Motivation and leadership

- **Maslow:** needs follow a hierarchy from physiological needs up to self-actualisation.
- **Herzberg:** **hygiene factors** only prevent dissatisfaction; **motivators** create real motivation.
- **McGregor:** **Theory X** sees people as avoiding work, **Theory Y** as willing to take responsibility.

## New approaches and Turkish public administration

- **New public management:** market mechanisms, performance measurement, treating citizens as customers, privatisation.
- **Governance:** the public sector, private sector and civil society share in decision-making; transparency and accountability.
- **Central administration:** the capital organisation and the provincial organisation (provinces and districts).
- **Deconcentration** (*yetki genişliği*): the centre gives decision-making power to its local representative (such as the governor); it creates no separate legal personality.
- **Decentralisation** (*yerinden yönetim*): **local** (special provincial administration, municipality, village) and **functional** (*hizmet yerinden yönetim*, such as universities); these have separate legal personality.

## Commonly confused

- *Deconcentration* stays within central administration; *decentralisation* creates a separate legal person.
- *Taylor* focuses on the job and the worker, *Fayol* on top management and its functions.
- *Hygiene factors* prevent dissatisfaction; *motivators* increase motivation.

## Study tip

Put the approaches on a timeline (classical, neoclassical, modern, new public management) and write two thinkers and one key concept under each. Most wrong options are built by mixing up these pairings.
