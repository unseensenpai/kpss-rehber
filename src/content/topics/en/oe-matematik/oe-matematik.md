## Core idea

Teaching mathematics in special education (*özel eğitimde matematik öğretimi*) is a tightly **sequenced** process that starts with pre-number concepts and extends to operations and the **functional mathematics** used in daily life. The basic rule: each new skill is taught after its prerequisite is mastered, and **from concrete to abstract**. There are **2 questions**; they ask about counting principles, teaching sequence and error analysis.

## Pre-number and number concept

- **Pre-number concepts:** **matching**, **classification**, **seriation**, pattern, **one-to-one correspondence**.
- **Counting principles:** one-to-one (one number for each object), stable order (numbers always in the same order), **cardinality** (the last number gives the total), abstraction (any objects can be counted), order irrelevance (the result is the same wherever you start).
- **Rote counting** differs from **rational counting**; a child who counts by rote may not know how many objects there are.

## Teaching approaches

- **Concrete-representational-abstract sequence:** real objects or manipulatives → pictures, tallies, dots → numerals and symbols.
- **Direct instruction:** modelling, guided practice, independent practice; clear, systematic language.
- **Schema-based problem solving:** the student learns to recognise the problem type (combine, change, compare) and place it in a schema; relying only on keywords is misleading.
- **Error analysis:** systematic errors (e.g. forgetting to regroup, confusing place values) are identified and teaching is adjusted to them.

## Functional mathematics

- Skills needed for independent living such as **money, time and measurement**; taught in real settings where possible.
- Supports such as a **calculator** can reduce the computation load and let the student focus on problem solving when needed.

## Commonly confused

- *Rote counting* is saying numbers in order; *rational counting* is finding how many objects there are.
- *Matching* puts identical items together; *classification* groups items by a shared feature.

## Study tip

When you see a child's counting error, name which counting principle is broken; questions mostly ask for exactly that match.
