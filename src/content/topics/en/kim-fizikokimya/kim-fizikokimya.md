## Core idea

Physical chemistry explains chemical events with the quantitative tools of physics: thermodynamics tells whether a reaction **will go on its own**, kinetics tells **how fast** it goes. There are **6** questions in the test. They are usually short calculations linking Gibbs energy, the equilibrium constant and kinetic relations.

## Thermodynamics and equilibrium

- **First law:** ΔU = q + w (in chemistry w is work done on the system). Heat absorbed at constant pressure is ΔH.
- **Second law:** the entropy of the universe increases in spontaneous processes. **Third law:** a perfect crystal has zero entropy at 0 K.
- **Gibbs energy:** ΔG = ΔH − T·ΔS; at constant temperature and pressure a process with ΔG < 0 is spontaneous.
- **Link to equilibrium:** ΔG° = −R·T·ln K; ΔG = ΔG° + R·T·ln Q. At equilibrium ΔG = 0.
- **Link to electrochemistry:** ΔG = −n·F·E; F ≈ 96 485 C/mol.

## Phase equilibria and kinetics

- **Gibbs phase rule:** F = C − P + 2. At the triple point of a pure substance there are zero degrees of freedom.
- **Phase diagram:** water's solid-liquid line leans to the left; raising the pressure lowers its melting point.
- **Rate law:** rate = k·[A]^m·[B]^n; orders are found by experiment, not from the coefficients.
- **First order:** ln[A] = ln[A]₀ − k·t; half-life t½ = ln2/k, independent of the starting concentration.
- **Arrhenius:** k = A·e^(−Ea/(R·T)); k grows when temperature rises or activation energy falls.

## Commonly confused

- *ΔG°* depends on standard conditions and K; *ΔG* depends on the current composition (Q) and tells the reaction's direction at that moment.
- *Thermodynamics* tells spontaneity, *kinetics* tells speed; a spontaneous reaction can be very slow.

## Study tip

Draw the three relations between ΔG°, K and E° as a triangle; once you know one, you should find the other two at once.
