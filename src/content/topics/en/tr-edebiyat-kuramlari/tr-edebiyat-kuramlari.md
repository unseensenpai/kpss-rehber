## Core idea

This course teaches the **concepts and methods** used to read a literary text. The Turkish ÖABT has **4 questions** on it. The logic is that every text has a **genre**, a **narrative arrangement** and a **lens** through which it is interpreted. Questions usually ask you to find the narrator or a figure of speech in a passage, or to match an interpretation with the right theory or movement.

## Genres and narrative

- **Mimesis:** the "imitation" concept Aristotle used in the *Poetics*; art re-creating reality.
- **Basic genres:** **lyric** (feeling), **epic** (narrative, heroism), **dramatic** (stage), **didactic** (instruction).
- **Narrative elements:** plot, characters, time, setting and narrator.
- **Point of view:** the **omniscient** narrator (*ilahi*), the **protagonist** narrator (first person), the **observer** narrator (reports only what is visible).

## Poetics

- **Harmony:** metre, rhyme, *redif* (repeated suffix or word after the rhyme), alliteration and assonance.
- **Image:** a mental picture that appeals to the senses.
- **Figures of speech:** ***teşbih*** (simile), ***istiare*** (metaphor built on one element of a simile), *teşhis* (personification), *mecaz-ı mürsel* (metonymy), *kinaye* (allusion with a double sense), *mübalağa* (hyperbole), *tezat* (antithesis), *tecahül-i arif* (feigned ignorance).

## Movements and theories

- **Movements:** classicism (reason, restraint), romanticism (feeling, individuality), realism (objective observation), naturalism (scientific determinism), Parnassianism (formal perfection), symbolism (musicality and suggestion).
- **Theories:** **Russian formalism** and **structuralism** look at the text's inner structure, **reception aesthetics** at the reader building meaning, psychoanalytic criticism at the unconscious, sociological criticism at the social context; **intertextuality** studies how texts relate to one another.

## Commonly confused

- In *teşbih* both elements are present; in *istiare* only one remains.
- *Realism* depicts society through observation; *naturalism* explains it through heredity and environment with a scientific attitude.
- An *omniscient* narrator knows the characters' inner worlds; an *observer* narrator reports only what can be seen.

## Study tip

When given an interpretation, ask "Where does it look for meaning: in the text, the reader, the author or society?" This question usually leads to the right theory.
