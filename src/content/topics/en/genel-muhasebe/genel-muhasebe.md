## Core idea

General accounting is the process of **recording, classifying, summarising and reporting** a business's transactions that can be measured in money. Everything rests on one equation: **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**. Every transaction affects at least two accounts without breaking this equation; this is **double entry**. **28** of the test's 40 questions come from this subject, so it deserves most of your time. Questions usually ask for the journal entry of a transaction, an account balance, a period-end adjusting entry, or an amount on the balance sheet or income statement.

## Basic concepts and the chart of accounts

- **Basic concepts:** social responsibility, business entity, going concern, **periodicity**, monetary measurement, historical cost, objectivity and documentation, **consistency**, full disclosure, **prudence**, materiality, substance over form. Think of an example situation for each.
- **Uniform Chart of Accounts** (*Tekdüzen Hesap Planı*): accounts are grouped into classes: *1* current assets, *2* non-current assets, *3* short-term liabilities, *4* long-term liabilities, *5* equity, *6* income statement accounts, *7* cost accounts, *9* memorandum accounts.
- **Common accounts:** *100 Kasa* (cash), *101 Alınan Çekler* (cheques received), *102 Bankalar* (banks), *120 Alıcılar* (trade receivables), *121 Alacak Senetleri* (notes receivable), *153 Ticari Mallar* (merchandise), *191 İndirilecek KDV* (deductible VAT), *255 Demirbaşlar* (fixtures and equipment), *257 Birikmiş Amortismanlar* (accumulated depreciation), *300 Banka Kredileri* (bank loans), *320 Satıcılar* (trade payables), *321 Borç Senetleri* (notes payable), *391 Hesaplanan KDV* (output VAT), *500 Sermaye* (capital), *600 Yurt İçi Satışlar* (domestic sales), *610 Satıştan İadeler* (sales returns), *621 Satılan Ticari Mallar Maliyeti* (cost of merchandise sold).
- **Contra accounts:** work opposite to the account they adjust; *257* sits among assets but carries a **credit** balance.

## How accounts work

- **Asset accounts** (classes 1 and 2) increase on the **debit** side and decrease on the credit side; they carry a debit balance.
- **Liability and equity accounts** (classes 3, 4, 5) increase on the **credit** side; they carry a credit balance.
- **Revenue accounts** are credited; **expense and cost accounts** are debited.
- **Recording flow:** document → **journal** (*yevmiye defteri*, in date order) → **general ledger** (*büyük defter*, by account) → **trial balance** (*mizan*, checks that debit and credit totals agree). A balanced trial balance does not prove the entries are right; posting to the wrong account does not unbalance it.

## Merchandise and inventory

- **Periodic inventory:** a sale records only revenue; cost is found at period end: **COGS = opening inventory + net purchases − closing inventory**.
- **Perpetual inventory:** every sale records both revenue and a transfer from *153* to *621*.
- **Gross sales − sales deductions = net sales**; net sales − COGS = **gross profit**.
- **Inventory valuation:** first-in first-out (FIFO) and weighted average are asked most. When prices rise, FIFO shows a higher closing inventory and a lower COGS.
- **VAT:** on purchases *191* is debited, on sales *391* is credited; at period end the two are offset.

## Fixed assets and depreciation

- **Cost:** purchase price plus the expenses needed to make the asset ready for use.
- **Depreciation** spreads an asset's cost over its useful life. The **straight-line** (*normal*) method charges the same amount each year; the **declining balance** method charges more in early years.
- **Entry logic:** debit an expense account, credit *257*. On the balance sheet the asset appears at **net book value** (cost minus accumulated depreciation).
- **Disposal:** compare the sale price with net book value; the difference is a gain or loss.

## Period-end procedures and statements

- **Accruals:** revenue earned this period but not yet collected is **accrued revenue** (an asset); expense incurred but not yet paid is an **accrued expense** (a liability).
- **Deferred** revenue and expenses collected or paid in advance are moved into the period they belong to; this applies **periodicity**.
- **Other steps:** inventory count, depreciation, allowance for doubtful receivables, VAT offset, transferring class 7 costs to class 6, and closing the income statement accounts.
- The **balance sheet** shows the position on a given date; the **income statement** shows the result of a period. Its order: gross sales → net sales → gross profit → operating profit → ordinary profit → profit for the period → net profit for the period.

## Commonly confused

- *Accrued revenue* is earned but not yet collected; *deferred revenue* is collected in advance but belongs to a later period.
- In *periodic inventory* cost is recorded at period end; in *perpetual inventory* at each sale.
- *Depreciation expense* is a period expense on the income statement; *accumulated depreciation* is a contra account that reduces the asset on the balance sheet.

## Study tip

Record a few transactions yourself every day and check how each entry keeps the equation balanced. Knowing by heart which class an account is in and on which side it increases lets you solve half the questions without writing an entry.
