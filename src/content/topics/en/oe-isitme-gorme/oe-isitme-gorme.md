## Core idea

Hearing and visual impairment (*işitme ve görme yetersizliği*) affect the sensory channel through which information arrives, so the essence of teaching is to deliver information through **the channels that remain intact**. There are **5 questions**. Questions ask about types of loss, communication approaches and classroom adaptations.

## Hearing impairment

- **Types:** **conductive** (outer or middle ear; often responds to medical treatment), **sensorineural** (inner ear or auditory nerve; permanent), **mixed** (both).
- **Degree:** from mild to profound, measured in decibels (dB) and shown on an **audiogram**.
- **Age of onset:** **prelingual** loss affects language acquisition much more, so early identification and fitting of devices is critical.
- **Technology:** hearing aids, the **cochlear implant** (a device placed in the inner ear that stimulates the auditory nerve), FM systems.
- **Communication approaches:** the **auditory-verbal** approach (speech through residual hearing), **sign language** (Turkish Sign Language in Türkiye), **total communication** (sign, speech, gesture and reading used together), the **bilingual** approach.
- **In class:** seat the student where they can see the teacher and classmates, face them and speak at a natural pace, use visual support.

## Visual impairment

- A student with **low vision** uses remaining sight with magnification and adaptations; a **blind** student relies on touch and hearing for learning.
- **Braille:** raised writing in six-dot cells, written with a slate and stylus or a Braille writer.
- **Adaptations:** large print, contrast, suitable lighting, magnifiers, audio and tactile materials, raised maps.
- **Concept teaching:** with real objects and touch; lack of visual experience affects concept development.
- **Orientation and mobility:** orientation (knowing where you are in a space) and mobility (moving safely); white cane, sighted guide technique.

## Commonly confused

- *Conductive* loss involves the outer and middle ear; *sensorineural* loss the inner ear and nerve.
- *Total communication* uses every channel together; the *auditory-verbal* approach focuses on hearing and speech.
- *Orientation* is knowing position and direction; *mobility* is the movement itself.

## Study tip

For every case ask "which channel is lost and which is intact?"; the right adaptation is always the one that uses the intact channel.
