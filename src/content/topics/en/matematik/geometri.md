**Question types in past exams:** Angles in triangles, area of triangles and quadrilaterals, angles in circles.

## Core idea

The last questions of the General Ability test (57–60) are geometry in every exam. In the past questions examined, **angles in triangles** and **area** dominate; angles in circles also appear. Geometry questions are solved by using a few basic relations in the right place. The real skill is spotting **hidden equalities** in the figure (isosceles sides, parallel lines, a shared height).

## Angles

- A straight angle is 180°. **Complementary angles** sum to 90°, **supplementary angles** to 180°.
- **A line cutting two parallel lines:** corresponding angles and alternate interior angles are equal; co-interior angles sum to 180°.
- With a zigzag between parallel lines, the angles pointing left sum to the angles pointing right.

## Angles in a triangle and special triangles

- **Interior angles sum to 180°.** An **exterior angle** equals the sum of the two interior angles not adjacent to it.
- **Isosceles triangle:** angles opposite equal sides are equal. When you see equal sides in a figure, mark the equal angles at once.
- **Equilateral triangle:** every angle is 60°.
- The larger angle is opposite the longer side.
- **Right triangle and Pythagoras:** a² + b² = c². Common triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17. In a 30°-60°-90° triangle the sides are in the ratio k, k√3, 2k; in a 45°-45°-90° triangle k, k, k√2.

## Area

- **Area of a triangle** = (base × height) / 2.
- Triangles with **the same height** have areas in the ratio of their bases. This rule solves most questions about triangles formed by points on one side.
- A **median** splits a triangle into two equal areas.
- **Quadrilaterals:** rectangle a × b, square a², parallelogram base × height, trapezoid (lower base + upper base) / 2 × height.
- Find the area of a complex shape by splitting it into familiar parts or subtracting empty parts from a larger shape.

## Circle

- A **central angle** equals the measure of its arc.
- An **inscribed angle** is half the measure of its arc. Inscribed angles on the same arc are equal.
- An inscribed angle on a diameter is 90°.
- A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

## Commonly confused

- A *central angle* equals the arc; an *inscribed angle* equals half the arc.
- An *exterior angle* is the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles; with the adjacent interior angle it makes 180°.
- Figures may not be drawn to scale; trust the given information rather than your eye.

## Study tip

In every question, first mark all the given information on the figure, then colour equal angles and equal sides. Most geometry questions are then solved with a single relation.
