## Core idea

The first questions of the mathematics section (around 31–40) open almost every exam with **operations** and **numbers**. In the past questions examined, every booklet has a "what is the result of this operation?" question, along with place value and division, odd/even, equation and inequality questions. They are not hard but need **speed and care**: applying the right rule without mistakes saves time for the rest of the section.

## Numbers and operations

- **Number sets:** natural numbers (0, 1, 2, …), integers (…, −1, 0, 1, …), rational numbers (a/b, b ≠ 0), real numbers.
- **Order of operations:** parentheses → exponents and roots → multiplication and division → addition and subtraction (left to right).
- **Odd and even:** odd + odd = even, odd + even = odd; a product is even if any factor is even. To check whether an expression is "always even", try odd and even values for the variables.
- **Primes:** natural numbers greater than 1 with no divisors other than 1 and themselves (2, 3, 5, 7, 11…). 2 is the only even prime.

## Place value, division and divisibility

- **Place value:** the three-digit number ABC = 100A + 10B + C. This expansion turns most digit questions into an equation.
- **Division:** dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder; the remainder is smaller than the divisor.
- **Divisibility rules:** 2 (last digit even), 3 and 9 (sum of digits), 4 (last two digits), 5 (last digit 0 or 5), 10, 11 (add the digits from right to left with + − + −).
- **GCD and LCM:** the product of two numbers = GCD × LCM. Use the GCD for splitting into equal parts and the LCM for when things coincide again.

## Rational numbers, exponents and roots

- To add fractions, make the denominators equal; to multiply, multiply numerators and denominators separately. Converting decimals to fractions before calculating reduces mistakes.
- **Exponents:** aᵐ · aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ, (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ, a⁰ = 1 (a ≠ 0), a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ.
- **Roots:** √a · √b = √(ab); simplify by taking perfect-square factors outside (√12 = 2√3).
- **Factorising:** the difference of squares a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b) often helps in simplification questions.

## Equations, inequalities, sets, functions

- **Equations:** tie the unknowns to one variable or add the equations side by side. When several equalities are given and "x + y + z" is asked, adding the equalities is often the shortest way.
- **Inequalities:** multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality. In ordering questions, try suitable numbers for the variables.
- **Sets:** s(A ∪ B) = s(A) + s(B) − s(A ∩ B). A Venn diagram makes intersection and difference questions visible.
- **Functions:** f(x) defines a rule; in the composition f(g(x)), evaluate the inner function first.

## Commonly confused

- The *GCD* is about splitting; the *LCM* is about common multiples.
- Multiplying an inequality by a **negative** number reverses it; a positive one does not.
- In digit questions, remember that digits run from 0 to 9 and the first digit cannot be 0.

## Study tip

Solve 10–15 operation questions a day against the clock. The goal here is not solving hard questions but solving easy ones **quickly and without mistakes**; the minutes you save go to word problems and geometry.
