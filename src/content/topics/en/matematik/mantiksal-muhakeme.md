## Core idea

Towards the end of the mathematics section of the KPSS General Ability test (usually **questions 51/52–56**), every exam has two question groups based on the same information and answered independently. These questions need no advanced mathematics. They need you to understand a rule correctly, **try possible cases systematically** and check the result carefully. Think of them as the numerical counterpart of verbal logic.

## Question types

- **Defined operation:** a new operation or rule is defined ("a ⊕ b = a + 2b") and applied to different numbers.
- **Numerical ordering and distribution:** numbers are distributed to people, boxes or days under conditions; "minimum", "maximum" or "certainly" is asked.
- **Patterns:** finding the rule in number or shape sequences and the next step.
- **Table and graph information:** drawing conditional conclusions from data in a table or graph.

## How to solve

1. **Try the rule with an example:** after reading the definition, apply it once with the simplest numbers; that is how you know you understood it.
2. **List the conditions:** write each as a short expression (A ≥ 2, B ≠ C).
3. **Try systematically:** test possible cases in order from small to large; random trials make you miss cases.
4. **In "minimum / maximum" questions,** first make the other quantities **as large or as small as possible**; the value you want then appears.
5. **Extra information** applies only to that question; do not carry it to the rest of the group.

## Commonly confused

- To minimise the quantity asked, you must **maximise the others**, not the reverse.
- More than one rule can fit the first few terms of a pattern; confirm the rule with at least three terms.

## Study tip

Write every trial in a small table; trials done in your head are the biggest time-wasters in the exam. When redoing solved groups, aim to build the same table faster.
