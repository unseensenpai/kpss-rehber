**Question types in past exams:** Word problems based on numbers, fractions, ratios and setting up equations.

## Core idea

Word problems need the skill of **turning what you read into an equation** more than mathematical knowledge. Past exams use everyday situations: balls of different colours in a box, rented cars, a heater's consumption at different settings, the chapters of a book. All are solved with the same steps: **choose the unknown, write the conditions as equations, solve, check**.

## General method

1. **Read the question to the end** and mark what is asked.
2. **Choose the unknown wisely:** if possible, make the smallest or most connected quantity x. With fractions, choosing the LCM of the denominators removes fractions (if 1/3 and 1/4 of a book are mentioned, take the total pages as 12x).
3. **Turn each condition into an equation:** "more than" → +, "times" → ×, "half" → ÷ 2.
4. **Solve** and compare with the quantity asked; often a value derived from x is asked, not x itself.
5. **Check:** put the value back into every condition of the problem.

## Types of problem

- **Number problems:** relations of sum, difference and multiple. *There are 25 more red balls than yellow* → R = Y + 25.
- **Fraction problems:** parts of a whole; separate carefully whether a part is a fraction of "the rest" or of "the whole".
- **Ratio and proportion:** a/b = c/d. **Direct proportion:** one rises as the other rises at the same rate (quantity and price). **Inverse proportion:** one rises as the other falls (number of workers and time).
- **Percentage problems:** x% = x/100. In successive percentage changes, each change is calculated on the **new value**.
- **Age problems:** the age difference between people never changes; after t years, everyone is t years older.
- **Speed problems:** distance = speed × time. When moving towards each other speeds add; in the same direction they subtract.

## Using the options

- If the answer must be a whole number (people, balls, cars), trying the options can be faster than solving the equation.
- **Divisibility** clues, such as a total that must be a multiple of a number, eliminate options quickly.

## Commonly confused

- "Half of the rest" is not "half of the whole".
- A value raised by 20% and then lowered by 20% does not return to the start; it ends 4% lower.
- Always check whether the quantity asked is the same as x.

## Study tip

Before solving, write only the equation and set it aside; for a week, "set up" five problems a day this way. Once setting up equations becomes a habit, the calculation speeds up on its own.
