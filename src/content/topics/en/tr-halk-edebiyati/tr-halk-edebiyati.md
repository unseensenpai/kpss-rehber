## Core idea

Folk literature grows out of the people, lives mostly **orally** and is built on **syllabic metre** (*hece ölçüsü*), the **quatrain** and **half rhyme**. The Turkish ÖABT has **5 questions** on it. It has three branches: **anonymous**, **minstrel** (*âşık*) and **religious-mystical**. Questions usually ask you to identify a poem's form or genre and match a poet with the right tradition.

## Anonymous folk literature

- ***Mani*:** usually a single quatrain of seven-syllable lines rhymed *aaxa*; there is also a pun-based type.
- ***Türkü*:** a sung poem made of verse sections (*bent*) and a refrain (*kavuştak*).
- **Others:** lullaby, lament (*ağıt*), riddle, *tekerleme*, proverb, folk tale, legend and **epic**.
- ***Dede Korkut* stories:** tales of the life of the Oghuz tribes, seen as a bridge from epic to folk romance.

## Minstrel literature

- Sung with the *saz*; the poet uses his **pen name** (*mahlas*) in the last quatrain.
- ***Koşma*:** 11-syllable lines, the most common form; by theme *güzelleme* (praise of love and nature), *koçaklama* (heroism), *taşlama* (satire) and *ağıt* (lament).
- ***Semai*** (8 syllables, its own melody) and ***varsağı*** (8 syllables, bold heroic tone).
- **Poets:** Karacaoğlan (nature and love), Köroğlu (heroism), Dadaloğlu, Âşık Veysel.

## Religious-mystical folk literature

- Developed around the dervish lodges (*tekke*); explains Sufi thought to ordinary people.
- **Genres:** *ilahi* (hymn), **nefes** (poems of Alevi-Bektashi poets), *nutuk*, *devriye*, *şathiye*.
- **Poets:** Ahmet Yesevi with his *hikmet* poems, **Yunus Emre** with his plain Turkish, Pir Sultan Abdal, Kaygusuz Abdal.

## Commonly confused

- A *mani* is a single seven-syllable quatrain; a *koşma* has several quatrains of 11 syllables.
- *Semai* and *varsağı* both use 8 syllables; the *varsağı* stands out with calls like "bre, hey" and a heroic tone.
- *İlahi* is religious poetry in general; *nefes* belongs to the Alevi-Bektashi tradition.

## Study tip

Given a poem, count the syllables first, then check the quatrains and the pen name. The syllable count and the pen name often give the answer on their own.
