**Official scope:** Political, social, economic and cultural developments from the 13th century to the early 20th century.

## Core idea

Ottoman history is a story of **rise and failure to adapt**. The state grew by tying central authority to a military land system (*tımar*). Once conquests stopped and firearms and a cash economy mattered more, that system broke down. From the 18th century the state tried to survive through reforms modelled on Europe. Most questions ask **which period an event belongs to** and **what it caused or resulted from**.

## Foundation period (1299–1453)

- Osman Bey defeated Byzantium at the **Battle of Bapheus (Koyunhisar)** (1302).
- Orhan Bey took **Bursa** (1326); the first regular army (*yaya* and *müsellem*) and the first Ottoman coin date from his reign. The Ottomans crossed into the Balkans.
- Murad I: **Sırpsındığı** (1364) and the **First Battle of Kosovo** (1389); a lasting hold on the Balkans.
- Bayezid I: victory over a crusader army at **Nicopolis** (1396). Defeat by Timur at **Ankara** (1402), followed by the **Interregnum** (1402–1413).
- Murad II secured the Balkans at **Varna** (1444) and the **Second Battle of Kosovo** (1448).

## Rise period (1453–1579)

- **Conquest of Istanbul (1453):** counted among the events that end the Middle Ages. Mehmed II took over the Byzantine legacy and issued law codes (*kanunname*).
- **Otlukbeli (1473):** the Aq Qoyunlu were defeated, strengthening Turkish unity in Anatolia.
- Selim I: **Chaldiran** (1514, Safavids), **Marj Dabiq** (1516) and **Ridaniya** (1517, Mamluks). The caliphate passed to the Ottomans and much of the spice route came under their control.
- Suleiman the Magnificent: **Mohács** (1526), the **siege of Vienna** (1529) and naval supremacy in the Mediterranean after **Preveza** (1538). The **capitulations** granted to France began as a trade privilege and later turned into economic dependence.
- Under Grand Vizier Sokollu Mehmed Pasha, Don-Volga and Suez canal projects were planned.

## Stagnation period (17th century)

- **Treaty of Zsitvatorok (1606):** equality with Austria was accepted.
- **Treaty of Qasr-e Shirin (1639):** the Iranian border took roughly its present shape.
- Celali revolts, the breakdown of the *tımar* system and the spread of tax farming. The reforms of Murad IV and the Köprülü viziers targeted **symptoms, not causes**, so their effect was temporary.

## Decline period (18th century)

- After the failed **second siege of Vienna (1683)**, the **Treaty of Karlowitz (1699)** brought the first major territorial loss.
- **Pruth (1711):** success against Russia. **Passarowitz (1718)** opened the **Tulip Era** (1718–1730), with the first reforms modelled on Europe and İbrahim Müteferrika's printing press.
- **Treaty of Belgrade (1739):** losses were recovered through French mediation. In return, France's capitulations were made permanent in 1740.
- **Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (1774):** Crimea became independent and Russia gained a say over Orthodox subjects. The **Treaty of Jassy (1792)** accepted Crimea as Russian.
- Selim III's **Nizam-ı Cedit** army and reforms.

## Dissolution period (19th century – 1922)

- **Charter of Alliance (Sened-i İttifak, 1808):** signed by the sultan with provincial notables; the first document to limit his power.
- Mahmud II: **abolition of the Janissaries** (1826), replaced by the Asakir-i Mansure-i Muhammediye; the **Treaty of Balta Liman** (1838) gave Britain broad trade rights.
- **Tanzimat Edict (1839):** rule of law and security of life, property and honour.
- **Reform Edict (1856):** new rights for non-Muslims; in the same year the **Treaty of Paris** recognised the empire as a European state.
- **First Constitutional Era (1876):** the first constitution, the **Kanun-i Esasi**, and the Chamber of Deputies. The **Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878** and the **Treaty of Berlin** (1878) brought heavy losses.
- **Public Debt Administration (Duyun-u Umumiye, 1881):** creditor states supervised tax revenues to collect foreign debts.
- **Second Constitutional Era (1908)** and the **31 March Incident** (1909); constitutional amendments reduced the sultan's powers.
- **Italo-Turkish War** (1911–1912), **Balkan Wars** (1912–1913), **First World War** (1914–1918) and the **Armistice of Mudros** (1918).

## Organisation, society and economy

- **Imperial Council (Divan-ı Hümayun):** where state affairs were discussed (grand vizier, military judges, treasurer, chancellor...).
- **Devşirme:** non-Muslim boys were trained for the standing army (*Kapıkulu*) and the administration; it strengthened central authority.
- **Tımar:** a cavalryman (*sipahi*) kept soldiers in return for tax revenue; production, security and the army were solved in one system. The land belonged to the state (**miri land**).
- **Tax farming (iltizam), life-term tax farms (malikane), esham:** as the need for cash grew, tax collection rights were sold first for a term, then for life, and debt instruments followed.
- Society was organised by religious community (**millet system**); craftsmen were organised in **guilds** (*lonca*).

## Culture and civilisation

- **Education:** madrasas and the **Enderun** palace school that trained statesmen. In the 19th century Western-style schools (*rüştiye*, *idadi*, the School of Civil Service) opened.
- **Architecture:** the peak of the classical period is **Mimar Sinan** (Şehzade, Süleymaniye and the **Selimiye**, which he called his masterpiece). Mosque complexes (*külliye*) combined a mosque, madrasa, soup kitchen and bath.
- **Foundations (vakıf):** provided much of education, health care and social services.
- **Science and art:** Piri Reis's maps and *Kitab-ı Bahriye*, Kâtip Çelebi, Evliya Çelebi's *Book of Travels*; divan poetry, miniature painting, calligraphy and tiles.

## Commonly confused

- *Karlowitz* is the first major territorial loss; *Küçük Kaynarca* led to the first loss of a Muslim population (Crimea) and to Russian interference in internal affairs.
- The *Tanzimat* brings rule of law, the *Reform Edict* focuses on non-Muslims, and the *Kanun-i Esasi* is the first constitution.
- The *Charter of Alliance* is the first document to limit the sultan's power, but it is not a constitution.

## Study tip

Summarise each period on one page in the order "situation, events, reforms, treaties". Put the treaties in a table: date, parties, most important result. The exam most often asks for the results of treaties and the aims of reforms.
