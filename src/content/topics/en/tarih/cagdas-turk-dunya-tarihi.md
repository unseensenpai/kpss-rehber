**Official scope:** The world from the early 20th century to today; Turkey from the Second World War to today.

## Core idea

The 20th century was shaped by **two world wars** and the **bipolar world** that followed. Turkey's domestic and foreign policy in this period largely followed that divide: Soviet demands pushed Turkey towards the West, and ties with the West sped up the move to multi-party politics. Most questions ask you to match a world event with its counterpart in Turkey.

## The interwar period (1918–1939)

- After the First World War, the **Treaty of Versailles** (1919) imposed harsh terms on Germany, one of the seeds of the next war.
- The **League of Nations** was founded but lacked the power to bind the great powers.
- The **Great Depression of 1929** increased state intervention worldwide; **totalitarian regimes** grew stronger, such as fascism in Italy, Nazism in Germany and Stalin's rule in the USSR.

## The Second World War (1939–1945)

- It began with Germany's **invasion of Poland** (1939). The Axis (Germany, Italy, Japan) fought the Allies (Britain, France, the USSR, the USA).
- The attack on **Pearl Harbor** (1941) brought the USA into the war. It ended in 1945 with Germany's surrender and the atomic bombs dropped on Japan.
- The **United Nations** was founded in 1945; Turkey is a founding member.
- Turkey stayed out of the war; the war economy brought measures such as the National Protection Law and the Capital Tax (*Varlık Vergisi*).

## The Cold War (1947–1991)

- A rivalry between the US-led Western bloc and the Soviet-led Eastern bloc that never became a direct war between them.
- Soviet demands over the Straits and Kars-Ardahan pushed Turkey towards the West. Military and economic aid came through the **Truman Doctrine** (1947) and the **Marshall Plan** (1948).
- **NATO** was founded in 1949. Turkey sent troops to the **Korean War** (1950–1953) and **joined NATO in 1952**. The Eastern bloc formed the **Warsaw Pact** in 1955.
- **Cuban Missile Crisis** (1962): in the US-Soviet bargain the Jupiter missiles in Turkey were removed. Turkey saw that great powers could bargain over it.
- **Johnson Letter** (1964): the USA warned Turkey against intervening in Cyprus; Turkey began looking for a multi-directional foreign policy.

## Détente and the end of the Cold War

- From the late 1960s, arms-limitation treaties and the **Helsinki Final Act** (1975) marked détente.
- **Cyprus Peace Operation (20 July 1974):** carried out by Turkey as a guarantor power in response to a Greek Cypriot coup. The **Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus** was proclaimed in 1983.
- The **fall of the Berlin Wall** (1989) and the **dissolution of the USSR** (1991) ended the Cold War. The **Turkic republics** of Central Asia and the Caucasus became independent; Turkey was the first country to recognise them.

## Turkey after the Second World War

- **Multi-party politics:** the Democrat Party was founded in 1946 and the first multi-party election was held that year. Power changed hands in the **14 May 1950** election.
- The **27 May 1960** coup and the **1961 Constitution** (Constitutional Court, National Security Council, a two-chamber parliament).
- The **12 March 1971** memorandum.
- The **12 September 1980** coup and the **1982 Constitution**.
- In the 1980s, open, free-market economic policies (the 24 January 1980 decisions).

## Turkey and Europe

- **Ankara Agreement (1963):** association with the European Economic Community.
- The **Customs Union** took effect in 1996. Candidate status came with the **Helsinki Summit (1999)** and accession negotiations began in **2005**.

## Globalisation and today

- The **Maastricht Treaty** (1992) founded the European Union.
- The 11 September 2001 attacks, the fight against terrorism and regional wars; rapid change in communication and technology; global problems such as climate and migration.

## Commonly confused

- The *Truman Doctrine* is military aid; the *Marshall Plan* is economic aid.
- The *Cuban Crisis* is about removing the missiles, the *Johnson Letter* about blocking intervention in Cyprus; both led Turkey to diversify its foreign policy.
- The *1961 Constitution* brought broad freedoms and two chambers; the *1982 Constitution* returned to a single chamber.
