**Official scope:** Political, social, economic and cultural developments.

## Core idea

This topic runs from the first Turkic states in Central Asia to the founding of the Ottoman state. There are two big turning points: **the conversion to Islam** (10th century) and **Anatolia becoming a Turkish homeland** (from Manzikert in 1071 to Myriokephalon in 1176). Questions rarely ask for bare dates; they ask you to see **continuity and change** in the idea of the state: the belief in *kut* survives, while the ruler's title, the army and the land system change.

## Pre-Islamic Turkic states

- **Asian Hun Empire:** grew strong under Mete Han (209–174 BC). He organised the army on the **decimal system**, which later Turkic states and the Ottomans kept. Its wars with China influenced the building of the Great Wall.
- **European Huns:** crossed into Europe with the **Great Migration** that began in 375. Their peak was under Attila (434–453).
- **First Göktürk Khaganate (552):** founded by Bumin Khagan; the first state to use "Türk" as its name. Ruled with a **dual organisation**: east (Bumin) and west (İstemi Yabgu).
- **Second Göktürk (Kutluk) Khaganate (682):** founded by İlteriş Kutluk Khagan. The **Orkhon Inscriptions** (Tonyukuk, Kül Tigin, Bilge Khagan; 8th century) are among the first historical documents written in Turkish and naming the Turks; they describe the khagan's duties to the people.
- **Uyghur Khaganate (744):** the **shift to settled life** became clear: cities, farming, their own alphabet and the influence of Manichaeism.

## State and society before Islam

- **Kut:** the belief that the right to rule is granted by the Sky God. Since *kut* passed to every male member of the dynasty, struggles for the throne were frequent.
- **Töre:** unwritten customary law; the ruler, too, had to obey it.
- **Kurultay (toy, kengeş):** the council where state affairs were discussed; the ruler had the final word.
- **Ülüş system:** the realm was the common property of the dynasty and was shared among its members.
- Nomadic life, horses and herding; army and people were intertwined (the "army-nation").

## The first Turkic-Islamic states

- **Battle of Talas (751):** between the Abbasids and China; the Karluks siding with the Abbasids started the Turkic-Arab rapprochement.
- **Karakhanids:** with Satuk Buğra Khan's conversion, the **first Turkic state** to adopt Islam as the state religion. Works in Turkish: *Kutadgu Bilig* (Yusuf Has Hajib), *Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk* (Mahmud of Kashgar), *Atabetü'l-Hakayık* (Edip Ahmet).
- **Ghaznavids:** Mahmud of Ghazni used the title "sultan"; his campaigns into India spread Islam.

## The Great Seljuk Empire

- **Battle of Dandanakan (1040):** victory over the Ghaznavids turned the Seljuks into a state.
- In 1055 **Tughril Bey** entered Baghdad and freed the Abbasid caliph from the Buyids; political leadership of the Islamic world passed to the Turks.
- **Alp Arslan – Battle of Manzikert (1071):** Byzantium was defeated and Anatolia opened to the Turks.
- Under **Malik Shah**, the vizier **Nizam al-Mulk**; the **Nizamiyya madrasas** and the military **iqta** system. The iqta is the forerunner of the Ottoman *tımar*.
- **Battle of Qatwan (1141):** Sultan Sanjar lost to the Kara Khitai; the empire's breakup accelerated.

## The Seljuk Sultanate of Rum and the first beyliks

- Founded by **Süleyman Shah**, son of Kutalmış; first capital **İznik (Nicaea)**, later Konya.
- After Manzikert, the **first beyliks** were founded in Anatolia: Danishmendids, Saltukids, Mengujekids, Artukids and others.
- **Battle of Myriokephalon (1176):** Kılıç Arslan II defeated Byzantium, which lost hope of recovering Anatolia; Anatolia's status as a Turkish homeland was settled.
- **Battle of Kösedağ (1243):** defeat by the Mongols; the sultanate fell under Ilkhanid control and the **second beyliks** (including the Ottomans) emerged.
- **Caravanserais** along the trade routes and the **Ahi brotherhoods** that organised craftsmen and traders.

## Commonly confused

- The *first Muslim Turkic state* is the Karakhanids; the *first to use the title "sultan"* is Mahmud of Ghazni.
- *Manzikert* opens the gate to Anatolia; *Myriokephalon* settles it as a Turkish homeland.
- The *iqta* (Seljuk) and the *tımar* (Ottoman) share the same logic: tax revenue from land pays for soldiers.

## Study tip

Put the chronology on a single timeline, then answer three questions for each state: What happened for the first time? Which battle was the turning point? Which institution passed on to later states? Most questions rest on one of these three.
