## Core idea

Athletics (*atletizm*) is made up of running, jumping and throwing and is the sport closest to fundamental movement skills, which is why it is called "the mother of sports". At school it is used to develop both fitness and fundamental skills. The test has **2 questions**; they usually ask about classifying the events, technical phases or well-established basic rules.

## Running events

- **Sprints** (100, 200, 400 m) are run in lanes and begin with a **crouch start**; starting blocks are used.
- **Middle- and long-distance** races use a **standing start**.
- **Hurdles** and the **steeplechase** are also track events.
- The **running stride** has support and flight phases; in sprinting, stride length and stride frequency are decisive.
- **Relays** (4×100 m, 4×400 m): the baton must be passed inside the **takeover zone**; an exchange outside the zone breaks the rules.

## Jumps and throws

- **Jumps:** long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault. The phases of the long jump are **approach run, take-off, flight and landing**. In the high jump the take-off is from **one foot**.
- **Throws:** shot put, discus, javelin and hammer. The shot is **pushed** from inside the circle, not thrown with the arm swinging from behind.

## Combined and road events

- The **decathlon** has 10 events for men; the **heptathlon** has 7 for women.
- The **marathon** is a 42.195 km road race; in **race walking** the athlete must keep contact with the ground.

## Commonly confused

- The *crouch start* is used in sprints; the *standing start* in middle and long distances.
- The *triple jump* is a hop-step-jump sequence; the *long jump* is a single jump.
- The *shot* is pushed; the *javelin* and *discus* are thrown.

## Study tip

Make a table for each event with three columns: category, how it starts, technical phases. Learn the stable basic rules, not fine rule details.
