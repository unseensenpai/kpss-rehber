## Core idea

Many early childhood approaches put the child's **active participation**, play and the environment at the centre; they differ in the teacher's role, materials and how the process is organised. The test has 6 questions; they usually describe a classroom scene and ask which approach it belongs to, so you need each approach's "signature concepts".

## Montessori

- **Prepared environment:** an orderly classroom scaled to children, with materials within reach.
- **Sensitive periods** and the **absorbent mind:** children are especially open to certain skills at certain times and absorb their surroundings effortlessly.
- **Self-correcting materials:** error control is built into the material.
- **Areas:** practical life, sensorial education, language, mathematics, culture; mixed-age groups.
- **Teacher:** an observer and guide who does not interrupt the child's independent work.

## Reggio Emilia

- **Origins:** developed in Italy under the leadership of Loris Malaguzzi.
- **"The hundred languages of children":** children express themselves through drawing, clay, body, light and many other means.
- **Atelier** and an art educator (*atelierista*); long-term projects arising from children's interests.
- **Documentation:** making the process visible with photos, notes and children's work.
- **The environment is the "third teacher";** the teacher is a co-researcher.

## HighScope and Waldorf

- **HighScope:** **active learning**; a daily **plan-do-review** cycle (children plan what they will do, carry it out, then talk about it); observation through key developmental indicators. Known for long-term follow-up research (the Perry Preschool Project).
- **Waldorf (Rudolf Steiner):** **rhythm** and a repeating daily routine, **imitation**, imagination, natural materials, tales and art; early academic instruction is avoided.

## Project approach and others

- **Project approach (Katz and Chard):** in-depth study of a topic children are interested in; beginning, development (field visits, investigation) and culmination (sharing) phases.
- **Bank Street:** developmental-interaction approach; integrated learning centred on social studies.
- **Head Start:** a comprehensive (education, health, family) compensatory programme in the USA for disadvantaged children.
- **Learning centres:** learning by free choice in block, dramatic play, art, book and science corners.

## Commonly confused

- In *Montessori* materials are structured and self-correcting; *Reggio Emilia* stresses open-ended materials and documentation.
- *HighScope* is recognised by the plan-do-review cycle, the *project approach* by phased in-depth study of a topic.
- *Waldorf* avoids early academics through imagination and natural materials; *Montessori* works with real-life and sensorial materials.

## Study tip

Memorise three signature concepts per approach and look for them in the question stem ("atelier" and "documentation" mean Reggio Emilia, "planning and recall time" means HighScope).
