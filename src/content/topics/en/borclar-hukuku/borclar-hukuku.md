## Core idea

The law of obligations governs legal relationships in which one person (the creditor) may demand a performance from another (the debtor). The general part of the Turkish Code of Obligations (*TBK*, Law 6098) explains how an obligation arises, how it is performed, what happens if it is not, and how it ends. The three main sources of obligations are **contract**, **tort** (*haksız fiil*) and **unjust enrichment** (*sebepsiz zenginleşme*). The test has 6 questions; they usually ask, in a short case, whether a contract is valid or what type of liability arises.

## Contract

- **Formation:** through mutual and matching declarations of will, that is **offer and acceptance** (*icap ve kabul*).
- **Freedom of form:** as a rule contracts need no particular form; if a form required by law is not followed, the contract is invalid.
- **Absolute invalidity** (*kesin hükümsüzlük*): contracts contrary to mandatory law, morality, public order or personality rights, or whose subject is impossible from the start, are void.
- **Exploitation** (*aşırı yararlanma*): if a clear imbalance between performances was obtained by exploiting one party's distress, thoughtlessness or inexperience, the injured party may declare that they are not bound.
- **Defects of consent:** **fundamental mistake**, **deceit** and **duress**; they do not void the contract automatically but allow the party whose will was impaired to declare they are not bound.
- **Agency** (*temsil*): an act done by an agent within their authority in the principal's name binds the principal directly. With **unauthorised agency** the act binds the principal only if they ratify it.

## Torts and unjust enrichment

- **Elements of a tort:** an act, **unlawfulness**, **damage**, **causal link** and **fault**.
- **Strict liability cases:** such as the liability of an employer, an animal keeper, a building owner, and (on grounds of equity) a person lacking discernment.
- **Unjust enrichment:** whoever is enriched from another's assets without a just cause must give it back.

## Performance and non-performance

- **Performance:** as a rule the debt ends when the debtor fully renders the performance to the creditor at the proper place and time.
- **Debtor's default** (*borçlu temerrüdü*): a debtor who does not perform a due debt is, as a rule, in default upon the creditor's notice; no notice is needed where a fixed date was agreed.
- **Options in bilateral contracts:** the creditor may demand performance plus damages for delay, waive performance and claim positive damages, or withdraw from the contract and claim negative damages.
- **Excessive hardship** (*aşırı ifa güçlüğü*): if an unforeseeable extraordinary change upsets the balance of performances excessively against the debtor, adaptation of the contract may be sought.
- **Penalty clause** (*ceza koşulu*): a performance agreed in advance to be paid if the debt is not performed or not performed properly.

## Extinction and transfer

- **Ways of extinction:** performance, **release** (*ibra*), **novation** (*yenileme*, replacing an old debt with a new one), **set-off** (*takas*, offsetting mutual due claims), merger of creditor and debtor, and impossibility without fault.
- **Limitation** (*zamanaşımı*): does not destroy the debt but makes the claim unenforceable in court; the judge does not apply it of their own motion, the debtor must raise it.
- **Assignment of a claim:** the debtor's consent is not needed but written form is. **Assumption of debt** requires the creditor's participation.
- **Joint and several liability** (*müteselsil borçluluk*): the creditor may claim the whole debt from any one of the debtors.

## Commonly confused

- *Absolute invalidity* voids the contract from the start; a *defect of consent* leaves it standing unless the party raises it.
- *Positive damages* are the loss of the position performance would have produced; *negative damages* are losses that would not have been incurred had one not relied on the contract.
- *Set-off* offsets mutual claims; *novation* ends the old debt and creates a new one in its place.

## Study tip

In every case question, first identify the source of the obligation (contract, tort or unjust enrichment), then check that source's elements one by one. Always check limitation periods and time bars in the current TBK on mevzuat.gov.tr.
