## Core idea

Early childhood special education (*erken çocuklukta özel eğitim*) is support for children with disabilities or at risk, from birth to school age. Its logic is simple: support that starts when development is fastest is **more effective and less costly**. There are **2 questions**; they ask about the rationale for early intervention, working with families and naturalistic teaching approaches.

## Why early?

- **Critical period and plasticity:** in the first years the brain is most open to environmental input.
- **Preventing secondary problems:** early support reduces further delays and behaviour problems.
- **Support for the family:** the family gains the knowledge and skills to support the child's development early on.

## Risk and screening

- **Risk groups:** children with an established diagnosis (e.g. Down syndrome), at **biological risk** (e.g. premature birth, low birth weight) and at **environmental risk** (e.g. poverty, neglect).
- **Screening:** applied to many children with short tools to pick out those needing further assessment; it **does not diagnose**.
- **Diagnosis:** made through a detailed assessment by a specialist team.

## Family and teaching approaches

- **Family-centred services:** the family is a partner in decisions; goals follow the family's priorities. In infancy this takes concrete form in the **individualised family service plan**.
- **Natural environment:** teaching happens in the child's everyday settings such as home, preschool class and play.
- **Embedded instruction:** the target skill is placed inside daily routines and activities.
- **Incidental teaching:** an interaction or interest the child starts is turned into a teaching opportunity.
- **Inclusion:** learning with peers in preschool supports social and language development.

## Commonly confused

- *Screening* identifies children at risk; *diagnosis* establishes the type and level of disability.
- In the *family-centred* approach the family is a decision partner; in the *expert-centred* approach the specialist decides.
- The teacher places *embedded instruction* into a routine; *incidental teaching* uses a moment the child starts.

## Study tip

Options stressing "natural environment", "partnership with the family" and "starting early" usually point to the answer; eliminate options that remove the child from their setting or leave the family out.
