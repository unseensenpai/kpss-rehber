## Core idea

Islamic history studies the political, social and cultural development of the Muslim community after the Prophet's death; its **civilisation** side covers the institutions, science and art of that development. The test has 5 questions; they usually ask in which caliph's or state's time a development took place and what civilisational institutions did.

## The four caliphs

- **Abu Bakr:** fought the ***ridde*** (apostasy) movements; collection of the Qur'an.
- **Umar:** conquests in Syria, Iraq, Iran and Egypt; the ***divan*** (registry) system and the **Hijri calendar**.
- **Uthman:** copying of the Mushaf; growth of naval power; ended in civil strife and his martyrdom.
- **Ali:** the battles of **the Camel** and **Siffin**, the **arbitration** and the departure of the Kharijites.

## Umayyads and Abbasids

- **Umayyads:** capital Damascus; the caliphate became a dynasty. **Karbala** belongs to this period. Discrimination against non-Arab Muslims (***mevali***) caused resentment; under Abd al-Malik Arabic became the official language. Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz is remembered for his justice. North Africa and **al-Andalus** were conquered.
- **Abbasids:** capital Baghdad; Turks and Persians became influential in the state. Turkish support for the Abbasids at the **Battle of Talas** sped up Islamisation. Under the caliph al-Ma'mun the **translation movement** centred on the ***Beytü'l-Hikme*** (House of Wisdom) peaked. The Abbasid caliphate in Baghdad ended when the Mongols took the city.
- **Other states:** the **Umayyads of al-Andalus** (Córdoba), the **Fatimids** (Cairo, al-Azhar), the **Mamluks**.

## The Turks and civilisational institutions

- **Turkish-Muslim states:** the **Karakhanids** are known as the first Muslim Turkish state; the Ghaznavids and Seljuks became leading powers in the Muslim world. The **Nizamiyya madrasas** founded by the Seljuk vizier **Nizam al-Mulk** were a turning point in institutionalising education.
- **Institutions:** mosque, madrasa, library, ***darüşşifa*** (hospital), *ribat* (frontier post and lodge), caravanserai; most were sustained through the ***vakıf*** (waqf, endowment) system.
- **Science:** **al-Khwarizmi** (algebra), **Ibn Sina** (*al-Qanun fi'l-Tibb*), **al-Biruni**, **Ibn al-Haytham** (optics).
- **Art:** calligraphy, illumination, miniature, marbling, tile work and mosque architecture.

## Commonly confused

- The *collection* of the Qur'an was under Abu Bakr, the *copying* under Uthman.
- The *Umayyads* ruled on Arab primacy; the *Abbasids* ran a multi-ethnic, multicultural state.
- A *madrasa* is for education, a *darüşşifa* for health, a *ribat* for defence and lodging.

## Study tip

For each caliph and dynasty, memorise the trio "capital, key event, contribution to civilisation"; questions usually give one of the three and ask for another.
