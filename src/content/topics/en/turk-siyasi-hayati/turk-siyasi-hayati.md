## Core idea

This subject follows how **constitutional and political institutions** developed from the late Ottoman period to today: limits on the sultan's power, the birth of parliament, party politics and the move to democracy. The main thread is a shift of power from persons to institutions and elections, interrupted at times by military interventions. The Public Administration test has about **4 questions** on it. Questions mostly cover chronology, cause and effect, and parties.

## Ottoman modernisation and the constitutional eras

- ***Sened-i İttifak* (1808):** a document between the sultan and provincial notables; seen as the first limit on the sultan's power.
- **Tanzimat Edict (1839):** declared security of life, property and honour and equality before the law.
- **Reform Edict (1856):** extended rights for non-Muslims.
- **Young Ottomans:** intellectuals who argued for constitutional government.
- **First Constitutional Era (1876):** the *Kanun-ı Esasi* and the first Ottoman parliament; parliament was closed in 1878.
- **Second Constitutional Era (1908):** parliament reopened; the **Committee of Union and Progress** came to dominate politics.
- **31 March Incident (1909):** an uprising against the constitutional order; after it was suppressed, Abdülhamid II was deposed.

## Founding and the single-party era

- The **Grand National Assembly (1920)** opened, the **Republic (1923)** was proclaimed and the **caliphate (1924)** was abolished.
- **Progressive Republican Party (1924):** the first opposition party; closed after the Sheikh Said rebellion.
- **Free Republican Party (1930):** a controlled opposition experiment; it dissolved itself shortly after.

## The multi-party era and interventions

- **1946 election:** the first multi-party general election; criticised for open voting and secret counting.
- **1950 election:** the **Democrat Party** came to power; power changed hands through elections.
- **27 May 1960:** military coup; followed by the **1961 Constitution**.
- **12 March 1971:** the government resigned after a military memorandum.
- **12 September 1980:** military coup; the **1982 Constitution** was then adopted by referendum.

## After 1980

- The **1983 election** marked the return to civilian politics.
- **1987 referendum:** political bans on former politicians were lifted.
- **2007 referendum:** election of the president by the people was approved.
- **15 July 2016:** a coup attempt was defeated.
- **2017 referendum:** introduced the presidential system of government, which took effect with the **2018 elections**.

## Commonly confused

- The *Tanzimat Edict* gave general guarantees to all; the *Reform Edict* gave rights specifically to non-Muslims.
- The *Progressive Republican Party* was closed; the *Free Republican Party* dissolved itself.
- *27 May 1960* and *12 September 1980* were direct takeovers; *12 March 1971* was a memorandum.
