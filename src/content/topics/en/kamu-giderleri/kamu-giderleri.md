## Core idea

Public expenditure is spending by public bodies to deliver public services. The key questions are **what** the money is spent on, **why it keeps growing** and **how it affects** the economy. This subject has 6 questions in the test; they usually ask you to classify an expenditure, match approaches to expenditure growth with their authors and interpret effects.

## Classifications

- **Real expenditure** (*reel harcama*): goods or services are received in return; it directly enters production and demand (civil servant salaries, purchases, investment).
- **Transfer expenditure** (*transfer harcaması*): nothing is received in return; purchasing power moves from one group to another (social assistance, subsidies, interest payments).
- **Current expenditure** is made regularly to run public services; **investment expenditure** builds lasting capital.
- **Economic classification** shows the economic nature of spending, **functional classification** shows which service it goes to (education, health, defence), and **institutional classification** shows which body spends it.

## Causes of growth

- **Apparent (nominal) increase** (*görünür artış*): spending rises without public services growing; price increases, a change of currency unit or different accounting methods cause it.
- **Real increase** (*gerçek artış*): the quantity or quality of public services grows; population growth, urbanisation, wars, the social state idea and technological change are the main causes.
- To remove apparent growth, spending is measured at **constant prices** and **per person**.

## Explaining approaches

- **Wagner's law:** as industrialisation and income rise, public spending grows faster than national income.
- **Peacock and Wiseman:** in social upheavals such as wars, spending jumps and does not return to its earlier level afterwards (**displacement effect**).
- **Baumol:** productivity grows more slowly in labour-intensive public services, so their costs rise relatively (**Baumol's cost disease**).
- **Musgrave and Rostow:** the composition of spending changes with the stage of development; infrastructure investment dominates early stages.

## Economic effects and evaluation

- **Multiplier effect:** public spending can raise total demand by more than the initial outlay.
- **Crowding out:** public spending pushes up interest rates and reduces private investment.
- Transfers and social spending affect **income distribution**.
- **Cost-benefit analysis:** social benefits and costs of public projects are discounted to the present and compared, using **net present value** and the **internal rate of return**. The **social discount rate** is used for discounting.

## Commonly confused

- *Real expenditure* buys goods and services; *transfer expenditure* is a one-way payment.
- An *apparent increase* happens without services growing; a *real increase* comes with larger services.
- *Wagner's law* explains steady long-run growth; the *displacement effect* explains jumps in times of crisis.

## Study tip

Link each approach to one keyword: Wagner with industrialisation, Peacock-Wiseman with war, Baumol with productivity gaps. In classification questions, first ask, "Are goods or services received in return?"
