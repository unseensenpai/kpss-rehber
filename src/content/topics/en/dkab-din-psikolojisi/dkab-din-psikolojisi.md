## Core idea

**Psychology of religion** does not examine whether religion is true but studies a person's religious beliefs, feelings and behaviour with psychological methods. The stages of religious development and the link between religiosity and personality are direct classroom knowledge for a religious culture teacher. The test has 2 questions; they usually ask you to match theorists with views or about religious development.

## Founders and theories

- **William James:** *The Varieties of Religious Experience*; studied religious experience as individual experience.
- **Edwin Starbuck:** one of the first systematic, questionnaire-based studies of conversion (*ihtida*).
- **Sigmund Freud:** interpreted religion as an **illusion** rooted in the father figure.
- **Carl Gustav Jung:** the **collective unconscious** and **archetypes**; saw religion as positive for mental health.
- **Gordon Allport:** **intrinsic** religiosity lives religion as an end; **extrinsic** religiosity uses it as a means to other ends.
- **Erich Fromm:** distinguished authoritarian and humanistic religion.

## Religious development

- **Childhood:** the image of God is concrete and human-like; religious thinking develops in parallel with Piaget's cognitive stages (Goldman).
- **Adolescence:** with abstract thinking come **religious awakening**, questioning and **religious doubt**; this is a critical stage for lasting religious attitudes.
- **James Fowler:** stages of **faith development**; faith grows from imitative forms in childhood towards a more universal understanding in adulthood.

## Religiosity and mental health

- **Glock's dimensions:** belief, practice (ritual), experience, knowledge and consequences.
- **Religious coping:** using religious resources to deal with stress (Pargament).
- **Mystical experience:** a sense of direct union with or nearness to the sacred.

## Commonly confused

- *Intrinsic religiosity* treats religion as an end, *extrinsic* as a means.
- *Freud* sees religion as a negative illusion, *Jung* as a positive force for psychological integration.

## Study tip

Match each theorist with one concept, then think about what to do in class at each stage (concrete examples for children, open discussion of questions for adolescents).
