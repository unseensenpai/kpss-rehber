## Core idea

For a student with special educational needs, science and social studies (*fen ve sosyal bilgiler*) are tools for understanding their surroundings and taking part in society. Teaching therefore rests on **concrete experience**, links to daily life and **concept teaching**. There are **2 questions**; they ask about concept teaching and suitable teaching arrangements.

## Concept teaching

- The concept's **critical attributes** (present in every example) and **variable attributes** are identified.
- **Examples and non-examples** are presented together so the student sees the boundary of the concept.
- Teaching moves **from concrete to abstract**: real object → model or picture → word.
- **Direct instruction:** the sequence of modelling, guided practice and independent practice.

## Science teaching

- **Experiment and observation:** students learn by doing; steps are broken down through task analysis.
- **Structured inquiry:** asking questions, predicting and recording results under the teacher's guidance.
- **Safety:** materials and experiment steps are adapted to the student's motor and sensory characteristics.

## Social studies teaching

- **Community-based instruction:** teaching in real settings such as a market, bank or public transport; it eases generalisation.
- **Simulation and role-play:** recreating a real setting in the classroom.
- **Graphic organisers** and **mnemonic strategies** (acronyms, rhymes, keywords) help students retain information.
- Abstract concepts such as time, place and direction are taught with calendars, maps and concrete experiences.

## Commonly confused

- A *critical attribute* is present in every example of a concept; a *variable attribute* changes from example to example.
- *Community-based instruction* happens in the real setting; *simulation* happens in a setting recreated in class.

## Study tip

In concept-teaching questions ask "Were non-examples presented?" and "Did teaching start from the concrete?"; the missing step is usually one of these.
