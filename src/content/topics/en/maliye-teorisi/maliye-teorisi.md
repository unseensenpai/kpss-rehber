## Core idea

Public finance theory asks **why and how far** the state should take part in the economy. The basic logic: in some cases the market cannot allocate resources efficiently (**market failure**), and the state fills the gap through taxes, spending and regulation. **Public choice theory**, however, shows that the state can fail too. This subject has 4 questions in the test; they usually ask you to recognise a type of good, an example of market failure or a public choice concept.

## Types of goods

- **Pure public good** (*tam kamusal mal*): **non-rivalry** (one person's consumption does not reduce another's) and **non-excludability** (keeping non-payers out is impossible or very costly) both hold. Examples: national defence, justice.
- **Quasi-public good** (*yarı kamusal mal*): benefits spread to both the individual and society; education and health are typical.
- **Club good:** excludable but non-rival up to a level of crowding (a toll bridge).
- **Common resource:** non-excludable but rival (open-sea fishing); it tends to be overused.

## Market failures

- **Public goods:** non-excludability creates the **free rider** problem, so the market under-produces them.
- **Externalities** (*dışsallık*): unpriced effects of an activity. Negative externalities lead to over-production, positive ones to under-production.
- **Imperfect competition and natural monopoly:** where average cost keeps falling, a single firm emerges.
- **Asymmetric information:** causes adverse selection and moral hazard.
- **Remedies for externalities:** a **Pigouvian tax** (taxing the negative externality) or a subsidy; the **Coase theorem** says that with clear property rights and low bargaining costs, parties can reach an efficient outcome by bargaining.

## Functions of the state

- **Allocation:** providing public goods and correcting externalities.
- **Distribution:** adjusting income distribution through taxes and transfers.
- **Stabilisation:** securing employment and price stability.
- This threefold division is associated with **Musgrave**.

## Public choice and views of public finance

- **Public choice theory:** assumes politicians, voters and bureaucrats also pursue self-interest; associated with **Buchanan**.
- **Median voter:** under majority voting, the voter in the middle decides the outcome.
- **Logrolling** (*oy ticareti*): legislators trading support for each other's proposals.
- **Rent seeking:** spending resources not on production but on obtaining privileges granted by the state.
- **Voting paradox:** majority voting can produce cyclical results (Condorcet).
- **Classical view:** the state should be limited and **neutral**, with a balanced budget. **Modern (functional) view:** public finance is used as a tool to steer the economy.

## Commonly confused

- *Non-rivalry* concerns consumption; *non-excludability* concerns the ability to keep non-payers out.
- A *Pigouvian tax* relies on state action; the *Coase theorem* relies on bargaining between parties.
- The *distribution function* deals with income shares; the *allocation function* deals with which goods resources go to.

## Study tip

Memorise an everyday example for each type of good and link each public choice concept to a scene. Questions usually give an example and ask for the concept, so studying through examples rather than definitions makes you faster.
