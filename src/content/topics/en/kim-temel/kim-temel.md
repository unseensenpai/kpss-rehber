## Core idea

General chemistry builds the shared language of all chemistry, from the **atomic structure** of matter to **how far, how fast and in which direction** reactions go. **16** of the 50 questions come from this subject, making it the heaviest area. Questions are both conceptual (periodic trends, bond type, geometry) and numerical (moles, gases, pH, equilibrium).

## Atoms, the periodic table and bonds

- **Quantum numbers:** n (energy level), l (orbital type), m_l (orientation), m_s (spin). Electrons fill orbitals by the Aufbau, Pauli and Hund rules.
- **Periodic trends:** across a period, atomic radius shrinks while ionisation energy and electronegativity generally rise; down a group, radius grows and ionisation energy falls.
- **Bond types:** ionic (electron transfer), covalent (electron sharing; nonpolar or polar depending on electronegativity difference), metallic.
- **VSEPR:** the number of electron pairs sets the geometry: 2 linear, 3 trigonal planar, 4 tetrahedral. Lone pairs narrow bond angles (H₂O is bent, NH₃ trigonal pyramidal).
- **Hybridisation:** sp (linear), sp² (trigonal planar), sp³ (tetrahedral).
- **Intermolecular forces:** London forces, dipole-dipole and hydrogen bonds; they control boiling points.

## Moles, gases and solutions

- **Mole:** 1 mol = 6.022·10²³ particles; n = m/M.
- **Stoichiometry:** coefficients in a balanced equation give mole ratios; the **limiting reagent** sets how much product forms.
- **Ideal gas:** PV = nRT, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) = 0.082 L·atm/(mol·K). At 0 °C and 1 atm, 1 mol of ideal gas occupies 22.4 L.
- **Dalton's law of partial pressures:** P_total = ΣP_i; P_i = X_i·P_total.
- **Concentration:** molarity M = n/V(L); molality m = n/kg solvent.
- **Colligative properties:** freezing point depression and boiling point elevation depend on the number of dissolved particles (ΔT = K·m·i).

## Energy, rate and equilibrium

- **Enthalpy:** ΔH < 0 exothermic, ΔH > 0 endothermic. **Hess's law:** the total ΔH does not depend on the path.
- **Reaction rate:** rises with concentration, temperature, surface area and catalysts. A catalyst lowers the activation energy and leaves ΔH unchanged.
- **Equilibrium constant:** for aA + bB ⇌ cC, K = [C]^c/([A]^a·[B]^b); pure solids and liquids are left out.
- **Le Chatelier:** when an equilibrium is disturbed, the system shifts to reduce the disturbance. K changes only with temperature.
- **Acids and bases:** Arrhenius (gives H⁺/OH⁻), Brønsted-Lowry (proton donor/acceptor), Lewis (electron-pair acceptor/donor). pH = −log[H⁺]; at 25 °C pH + pOH = 14.
- **Electrochemistry:** oxidation is loss of electrons, reduction is gain. In a galvanic cell oxidation happens at the anode and reduction at the cathode; E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode.

## Commonly confused

- *Molarity* (*molarite*) is defined per litre of solution, *molality* (*molalite*) per kilogram of solvent.
- A *catalyst* speeds up a reaction but does not move the equilibrium; *temperature* changes both the rate and K.
- *Ionisation energy* is the energy needed to remove an electron from an atom; *electron affinity* concerns the energy released when an atom gains one.

## Study tip

In numerical questions convert everything to moles first; the mole is the common bridge between stoichiometry, gas, solution and equilibrium calculations.
