## Core idea

Ottoman history tells how a frontier principality became a world empire through the **timar, the devshirme and central authority**, and then tried to **adapt through reform** to changing military and economic conditions. The traditional periods (founding, rise, stagnation, decline, dissolution) are still used, while recent scholarship prefers ideas such as a "classical age" and an "age of transformation". The test has 9 questions; they usually ask you to match events with periods, how institutions worked, and the causes and results of reforms.

## Outline of political history

- **Founding:** traditionally **1299**; **Bapheus (1302)**, the taking of **Bursa (1326)** under Orhan and the crossing into Rumelia; **First Kosovo (1389)**, **Nicopolis (1396)**; defeat at **Ankara (1402)** and the **Interregnum**; **Varna (1444)**.
- **Classical age:** **conquest of Constantinople (1453)**; under Selim I, **Chaldiran (1514)**, **Marj Dabiq (1516)** and **Ridaniya (1517)**; under Süleyman, **Mohács (1526)** and **Preveza (1538)**.
- **17th century:** **Zsitvatorok (1606)** brought equal status with Austria; **Qasr-e Shirin (1639)** largely fixed the Iranian border; **Karlowitz (1699)** was the first major loss of territory.
- **18th century:** the **Tulip Era** and the first printing press; **Küçük Kaynarca (1774)** made Crimea independent and gave Russia grounds to claim a say over Orthodox subjects.
- **19th century:** **Tanzimat (1839)**, **Reform Edict (1856)**, **Kanun-i Esasi and the First Constitutional Era (1876)**, **Berlin (1878)**, **Second Constitutional Era (1908)**, the **Tripolitanian** and **Balkan Wars**, **World War I** and **Mudros (1918)**.

## Organisation and economy

- **Imperial Council** (*Divan-ı Hümayun*): the highest decision body, chaired by the grand vizier, with the military judges, the treasurer, the chancellor and viziers.
- **Devshirme and kapıkulu:** children taken from Christian families were trained in the Enderun palace school and the Novice Corps; the central army and ruling cadre came from them.
- **Timar:** the tax income of a district granted to a cavalryman (*sipahi*) for service; it both supported the provincial army and supervised production.
- **Iltizam** leased tax collection for a short term; **malikane (1695)** sold that right for life. Both spread as the timar system broke down.
- **Capitulations** began as one-sided trading privileges granted by the sultan and over time made the economy dependent; the **Public Debt Administration** (*Duyun-u Umumiye*, 1881) managed foreign debt.
- **Millet system:** non-Muslims were organised as religious communities that ran their own religious and legal affairs.

## Reforms

- **Selim III:** the **Nizam-ı Cedid** army and permanent embassies in European capitals.
- **Mahmud II:** the **Charter of Alliance** (*Sened-i İttifak*, 1808) with the provincial notables; **abolition of the Janissaries (1826)**, ministries and the first census.
- **Tanzimat Edict:** security of life, property and honour; the idea of the rule of law.
- **Reform Edict:** broad rights for non-Muslims, issued under European pressure.
- **Kanun-i Esasi:** the first constitution; an elected Chamber of Deputies and an appointed Senate.

## Commonly confused

- *Iltizam* is a short-term tax farm; *malikane* is a life-term one.
- The *Tanzimat Edict* gave guarantees to all subjects; the *Reform Edict* gave rights especially to non-Muslims.
- The *Charter of Alliance* shared the sultan's power with the notables; the *Kanun-i Esasi* set up a constitutional order.

## Study tip

For each century pick one treaty, one reform and one institutional change; this trio is the skeleton of event-period questions.
