## Core idea

**Growth** (*büyüme*) is the rise in real output (income per head); it is a quantitative concept. **Development** (*kalkınma*) adds improvements in education, health, income distribution and economic structure; it is qualitative and broader. This subject asks why countries grow at different speeds and how underdevelopment can be overcome. The test has **4 questions**; they usually ask you to match a model or view with the right economist.

## Indicators and income distribution

- **Income per head** is the most common growth measure, but it says nothing about distribution or quality of life.
- **Human Development Index** (HDI): combines **life expectancy**, **education** and **income**; published by the UN Development Programme.
- The further the **Lorenz curve** is from the line of equality, the greater the inequality. The **Gini coefficient** runs from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality).
- **Kuznets curve:** inequality rises early in development and then falls (inverted U).

## Growth models

- **Harrod-Domar:** growth rate g = s / v, where s is the saving rate and v the **capital-output ratio**. Saving and investment drive growth; the equilibrium is unstable, like a "knife edge".
- **Solow:** capital has **diminishing returns**, so the economy reaches a **steady state**. In the long run only **exogenous technological progress** sustains growth per head. It predicts **conditional convergence** among similar countries. The capital level that maximises consumption is the **golden rule**.
- **Endogenous growth (Romer, Lucas):** technology is explained inside the model; **human capital**, R&D and knowledge spillovers prevent diminishing returns. Policy can affect long-run growth.

## Development theories

- **Rostow:** five stages: traditional society, preconditions for take-off, **take-off**, drive to maturity, age of high mass consumption.
- **Lewis (dual economy):** **disguised unemployment** in traditional agriculture gives modern industry an unlimited labour supply at a fixed wage.
- **Nurkse:** the **vicious circle of poverty** (low income → low saving → low investment → low income); the remedy is **balanced growth**.
- **Rosenstein-Rodan:** a **big push** of simultaneous investment in mutually supporting sectors.
- **Hirschman:** **unbalanced growth**; priority goes to key sectors with strong forward and backward **linkages**.
- **Dependency school and Prebisch-Singer:** the world divides into **core** and **periphery**; the **terms of trade** of raw-material exporters worsen over the long run.
- **Industrialisation strategies:** **import-substituting** (domestic market, protection) and **export-oriented** (foreign markets, competition) industrialisation.

## Commonly confused

- *Growth* is a quantitative increase; *development* is qualitative and structural change; growth without development is possible.
- *Nurkse* argues for balanced growth, *Hirschman* for unbalanced growth.
- Technology is exogenous in the *Solow* model and endogenous in *endogenous growth* models.

## Study tip

Learn one keyword per economist: Rostow "take-off", Lewis "unlimited labour", Nurkse "vicious circle", Hirschman "linkages", Solow "steady state". Questions are often built around one of these words.
