## Core idea

Criminal law decides which conduct is a crime and which sanction follows; the general part of the Turkish Criminal Code (*TCK*, Law 5237) sets rules common to all crimes, and the Code of Criminal Procedure (*CMK*, Law 5271) governs how the material truth is investigated. The test has 6 questions; they usually give a short case and ask whether liability exists or how a procedural institution works.

## Basic principles and elements of a crime

- **Principle of legality** (*kanunilik*): no penalty for an act the law does not expressly define as a crime; criminal provisions cannot be extended by **analogy**, and crimes and penalties cannot be created by administrative regulations.
- **More favourable law** (*lehe kanun*): if the law changes between the act and the judgment, the one more favourable to the offender applies.
- **Elements:** **conformity to the legal definition** (*tipiklik*: act, result, causal link), **unlawfulness** and **culpability**.
- **Intent** (*kast*): knowingly and willingly realising the elements of the offence. With **eventual intent** (*olası kast*) the offender foresees the result and accepts it.
- **Negligence** (*taksir*): an unforeseen result caused by breaching a duty of care. With **conscious negligence** (*bilinçli taksir*) the result is foreseen but not wanted.

## Grounds affecting liability

- **Grounds of justification** (*hukuka uygunluk nedenleri*): performing a legal duty, **self-defence** (*meşru savunma*), exercising a right, and the **consent of the person concerned** for rights they may freely dispose of. The act is lawful from the start.
- **Grounds affecting culpability:** **necessity** (*zorunluluk hali*), force and threat, mistake, mental illness, temporary causes and **minority of age**. A child under twelve has no criminal responsibility; for older children responsibility is excluded or the penalty reduced depending on age and capacity to understand.

## Special forms and sanctions

- **Attempt** (*teşebbüs*): directly starting to commit the crime with suitable acts but failing to complete it for reasons beyond the offender's control. With **voluntary abandonment** (*gönüllü vazgeçme*) there is no penalty for the attempt.
- **Participation** (*iştirak*): **perpetration** (direct, joint, indirect), **incitement** (*azmettirme*, creating another person's decision to commit a crime) and **aiding**.
- **Concurrence** (*içtima*): when one act constitutes several different crimes, **notional concurrence** (*fikri içtima*) means only the most severe penalty is given; committing the same crime several times on one decision is a **chain offence** (*zincirleme suç*).
- **Sanctions:** **imprisonment** (aggravated life, life, fixed-term) and **judicial fines**; plus **security measures**.

## Criminal procedure

- **Stages:** the **investigation** (*soruşturma*) is directed by the public prosecutor and ends when the indictment is accepted; the **prosecution** (*kovuşturma*) stage follows.
- **Suspect and accused:** a person is a suspect (*şüpheli*) during the investigation and an accused (*sanık*) during the prosecution stage.
- **Public action:** if there is sufficient suspicion the prosecutor files an **indictment** (*iddianame*); otherwise they decide not to prosecute.
- **Protective measures:** custody, **detention on remand** (*tutuklama*, requiring strong suspicion and a ground for detention), judicial control, search and seizure.
- **Legal remedies:** the ordinary remedies are **appeal** (*istinaf*, regional courts of justice) and **cassation** (*temyiz*, Court of Cassation); extraordinary remedies include **retrial** and **annulment in the interest of the law**. When only the accused challenges a judgment, the **prohibition of reformatio in peius** applies.

## Commonly confused

- With *eventual intent* the offender foresees and accepts the result; with *conscious negligence* they foresee it but trust it will not happen.
- A *ground of justification* makes the act lawful; a *ground excluding culpability* leaves the act unlawful but the offender unpunished.
- A *suspect* is in the investigation stage; an *accused* is in the stage after the indictment is accepted.

## Study tip

In case questions, move through conformity, unlawfulness and culpability in order; the step where the answer is "no" is your answer. Always check age limits, time limits and penalty ranges in the current TCK and CMK on mevzuat.gov.tr.
