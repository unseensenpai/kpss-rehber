## Core idea

A **hadith** is a saying, act or approval attributed to the Prophet; the **sunnah** is his exemplary practice in religious life. Hadith studies examine, through the *sened* (isnad, chain of narrators) and the *metin* (matn, text), whether a report reached the Prophet by a reliable route. The test has 5 questions; they usually ask about types of hadith, types of collections and methodological terms.

## Basic concepts and compilation

- **Types of sunnah:** *kavli* (verbal), *fiili* (practical), *takriri* (tacit approval).
- The **isnad** is the chain of transmitters, the **matn** the actual wording, the **narrator** (*ravi*) the person who transmits.
- **Writing and compilation (*tedvin*):** personal notebooks were written in the Companions' time. Official compilation began on the order of the Umayyad caliph **Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz**.

## Classification

- **By number of narrators:** ***mütevatir*** (transmitted by so many that agreement on a lie is unthinkable) and ***ahad*** (solitary; subdivided into *meşhur*, *aziz*, *garib*).
- **By reliability:** ***sahih*** (unbroken chain, upright narrators with accurate retention, free of irregularity and hidden defect), ***hasen*** (retention slightly weaker), ***zayıf*** (weak; a condition is missing). A ***mevzu*** report is fabricated and is not counted as hadith.
- **By whom it reaches:** ***merfu*** (the Prophet), ***mevkuf*** (a Companion), ***maktu*** (a Successor).
- ***Kudsi hadis*** (hadith qudsi): its meaning is held to be from God and its wording from the Prophet; it is distinct from the Qur'an.

## Collections and criticism

- A ***câmi'*** covers all topics, a ***sünen*** legal topics; a ***müsned*** is arranged by Companion (e.g. Ahmad ibn Hanbal's *Musnad*).
- **The Six Books (*Kütüb-i Sitte*):** the *Sahih*s of Bukhari and Muslim; the *Sunan*s of Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i and Ibn Majah.
- ***Muwatta***: Imam Malik's work of hadith and law.
- ***Cerh ve ta'dil*** (jarh and ta'dil): assessing narrators' reliability; text criticism checks a report's agreement with the Qur'an and sound sunnah.

## Commonly confused

- *Hadith* is the report itself; *sunnah* is the Prophet's exemplary practice.
- The wording of a *hadith qudsi* is the Prophet's; the *Qur'an* is revelation in wording and meaning.
- *Mawquf* reaches a Companion, *maqtu'* a Successor.

## Study tip

Group hadith types with three questions: how many transmitted it, how reliable is it, whom does it reach. In each question, first decide which criterion is being asked.
