## Core idea

Development is the orderly, continuous change that begins at conception. Early childhood is the fastest period of development, and the areas of development influence each other. The test has 6 questions; they usually describe a child's behaviour and ask which developmental feature or concept explains it. See also the site's developmental psychology topic for the general theories.

## Principles of development

- **Head to toe (cephalocaudal):** head control comes before sitting and walking.
- **Centre to periphery (proximodistal):** trunk and arm control develop before finger skills.
- **General to specific:** whole-arm movement first, then a finger grasp.
- **Individual differences:** development is continuous and its order is the same, but its pace differs from child to child.
- **Critical period:** time windows when some skills are acquired most easily.

## Motor and cognitive development

- **Gross motor:** running, jumping, balance; **fine motor:** using scissors, threading beads, holding a pencil.
- **Sensorimotor stage (Piaget):** object permanence is acquired; the world is known through the senses and movement.
- **Preoperational stage:** symbolic (pretend) play, **egocentrism**, **animism** (attributing life to objects), **centration** (focusing on one dimension), irreversibility and not yet having **conservation**.
- **Vygotsky:** the **zone of proximal development**, the adult's temporary support (**scaffolding**) and the child's self-talk (private speech) guide learning.

## Language development

- **Prelinguistic stage:** cooing and **babbling** (syllable sounds) come before first words.
- **Telegraphic speech:** after the first words come two-word sentences without endings or connectives.
- **Overextension** (calling every four-legged animal "doggy") and **underextension** (using "cat" only for their own cat) are common.
- **Vocabulary spurt:** vocabulary grows fast in the preschool years; grammar rules may be applied to irregular words too.

## Social-emotional development and play

- **Attachment (Bowlby, Ainsworth):** secure, anxious-avoidant, anxious-ambivalent and the later-described disorganised attachment.
- **Erikson:** trust versus mistrust, autonomy versus shame and doubt, initiative versus guilt.
- **Parten's stages of play:** unoccupied behaviour, solitary play, onlooker play, **parallel play** (side by side without interaction), **associative play** (shared materials, loose cooperation), **cooperative play** (shared goal and roles).
- **Moral development:** children follow rules set from outside (Piaget: heteronomous morality; Kohlberg: preconventional level).

## Commonly confused

- In *parallel play* children play side by side but separately; in *associative play* they share materials and interact.
- *Egocentrism* means being unable to take another's viewpoint, not selfishness; *centration* means focusing on one feature of an object.
- *Overextension* uses a word too broadly, *underextension* too narrowly.

## Study tip

Build a little scene for each concept (a child who thinks the water increased when poured into a taller glass: no conservation). Questions almost always arrive as such a scene.
