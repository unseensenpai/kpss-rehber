## Core idea

The history of the Republic of Turkey covers how independence was won through the **National Struggle** (*Millî Mücadele*) that began after Mudros, and how the institutions of a secular republic **based on national sovereignty** were built. The test has 8 questions; they usually ask about the significance of a congress, treaty or reform, matching reforms with principles, and chronology.

## The National Struggle

- **Armistice of Mudros (30 October 1918):** article 7 in particular opened the way to occupations.
- **Mustafa Kemal in Samsun (19 May 1919);** the **Amasya Circular** declared that the nation's will and resolve would save its independence.
- **Erzurum and Sivas congresses (1919):** the idea of a mandate was rejected at regional and national level and the resistance societies were united.
- The **National Pact** (*Misak-ı Millî*, 28 January 1920) was adopted by the last Ottoman parliament; the **Grand National Assembly opened on 23 April 1920**.
- **Fronts:** in the east the **Treaty of Gyumri (1920)**; in the south the **Treaty of Ankara (1921)** with France; in the west the **First and Second İnönü**, **Sakarya** and the **Great Offensive (26 August 1922)**.
- The **Armistice of Mudanya (1922)** and the **Treaty of Lausanne (24 July 1923)** gave international recognition to independence.

## Political and social reforms

- **Abolition of the sultanate (1 November 1922), the Republic (29 October 1923), abolition of the caliphate and the Law on Unification of Education (3 March 1924).**
- The **1921 constitution** (*Teşkilat-ı Esasiye*) set up unity of powers and the **1924 constitution** the republican order; secularism entered the constitution in **1937**.
- **Law:** the **Turkish Civil Code (1926)** created family law based on monogamy and equality of women and men.
- **Education and culture:** the **Alphabet Reform (1928)**, the Nation's Schools, the Turkish Historical and Turkish Language societies, the **1933 university reform**.
- **Society:** the Hat Law and closure of dervish lodges (1925), international calendar and time, the **Surname Law (1934)**; women's right to vote and stand in local (1930) and national (1934) elections.
- **Multi-party attempts:** the Progressive Republican Party (1924) and the Free Republican Party (1930).

## Principles, economy and foreign policy

- **Six principles:** republicanism, nationalism, populism, statism, secularism, reformism.
- **Economy:** the **İzmir Economic Congress (1923)**, **abolition of the tithe (1925)**; after the 1929 crisis, **statism** and the **First Five-Year Industrial Plan**.
- **Foreign policy:** the Mosul question, **joining the League of Nations (1932)**, the **Balkan Pact (1934)**, **Montreux (1936)** restoring Turkish sovereignty over the Straits, the **Saadabad Pact (1937)** and the joining of **Hatay** (1939).

## Commonly confused

- The *Amasya Circular* set out the reason and method of the National Struggle; the *National Pact* set its border and independence goals.
- *Mudanya* is an armistice; *Lausanne* is a peace treaty.
- The *Law on Unification of Education* unified schooling; the *Alphabet Reform* aimed to make literacy easier.
