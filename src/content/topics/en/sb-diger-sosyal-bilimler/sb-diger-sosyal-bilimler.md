## Core idea

Social studies is an interdisciplinary subject that draws not only on history and geography but also on **sociology, psychology, economics, anthropology and philosophy**. In these fields you are expected to know the basic concepts and pioneering thinkers at an introductory level. The test has 8 questions; they usually ask you to match a concept with an example and a thinker with an idea.

## Sociology

- **Comte** named sociology and argued for positivism; **Durkheim** put forward the **social fact**, mechanical and organic solidarity and **anomie**; **Weber** interpretive sociology and the study of bureaucracy; **Marx** class conflict. **Ibn Khaldun** explained societies through nomadic (*bedevi*) and settled (*hadari*) life.
- **Socialisation:** learning society's values; family, school, peer group and media are the main agents.
- **Status and role:** **ascribed** (sex, descent) and **achieved** (occupation) status; a role is the behaviour a status requires.
- **Social stratification** and **social mobility** (vertical and horizontal).
- **Culture:** material and non-material elements; **cultural lag (Ogburn)** is non-material culture falling behind material change.

## Psychology

- **Schools:** **structuralism** (Wundt, the first experimental laboratory in Leipzig), **functionalism** (James), **behaviourism** (Watson, Skinner), **Gestalt** (the whole is more than the sum of its parts), **psychoanalysis** (Freud: id, ego, superego), the **humanistic** approach (Maslow's **hierarchy of needs**, Rogers), the **cognitive** approach.
- **Core topics:** sensation and perception, learning, memory, motivation, personality.

## Economics

- **Scarcity:** limited resources against unlimited wants; every choice has an **opportunity cost**.
- **Supply and demand:** when price rises, quantity demanded falls and quantity supplied rises; where they meet is **market equilibrium**.
- **Macro concepts:** GDP, inflation, unemployment; **monetary policy** is the central bank's tool, **fiscal policy** the government's tax and spending tool.
- **Adam Smith** is known for the free market and the "invisible hand", **Keynes** for state intervention in depressions.

## Anthropology and philosophy

- **Anthropology:** **physical** and **cultural** anthropology; **ethnography** and **participant observation** (Malinowski) are its core methods. **Ethnocentrism** is judging by one's own culture; **cultural relativism** is judging each culture in its own context.
- **Philosophy:** theory of knowledge (**epistemology**), metaphysics of being (ontology), moral philosophy (ethics) and philosophy of art (aesthetics). On the source of knowledge, **rationalism** (Descartes) rests on reason and **empiricism** (Locke, Hume) on experience; **Kant** tried to reconcile the two.

## Commonly confused

- *Ascribed status* comes by birth; *achieved status* by effort.
- *Ethnocentrism* takes one's own culture as the measure; *cultural relativism* judges each culture on its own terms.
- *Rationalism* finds the source of knowledge in reason; *empiricism* in experience.

## Study tip

Make a card for each discipline with five core concepts and two thinkers; questions mostly rely on matching these concepts with an everyday example.
