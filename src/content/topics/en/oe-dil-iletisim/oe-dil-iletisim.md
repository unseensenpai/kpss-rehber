## Core idea

Communication is passing a message from one person to another; **language** (*dil*) is the rule-governed symbol system used for it, and **speech** (*konuşma*) is the spoken production of language. In special education the aim is functional communication; a student who cannot speak can still communicate. There are **2 questions**; they ask about language components, disorders and support techniques.

## Components of language

- **Phonology:** rules for sounds and sound combinations.
- **Morphology:** word structure, suffixes.
- **Syntax:** word order in sentences.
- **Semantics:** the meaning of words and sentences.
- **Pragmatics:** using language in social context (turn-taking, staying on topic).
- **Receptive language** covers understanding; **expressive language** covers expressing oneself.

## Disorders

- **Articulation disorder:** difficulty producing sounds correctly (omitting, substituting or distorting sounds).
- **Fluency disorder:** interruptions in the flow of speech, such as stuttering.
- **Voice disorder:** problems with pitch, loudness or quality of the voice.
- **Language disorder:** difficulty understanding or using the components of language.

## Support techniques

- **Incidental teaching:** a moment when the child shows interest is turned into teaching.
- **Modelling:** the teacher says the target word or structure and waits for the child to repeat it.
- **Time delay:** the teacher looks expectantly and waits for the child to initiate.
- **Expansion:** saying back what the child said in a correct, complete form ("ball" → "Red ball").
- **Augmentative and alternative communication:** signs, picture cards, **PECS**, speech-generating devices; it does not hinder speech development.

## Commonly confused

- *Speech* is spoken production; *language* is the symbol system. A student who cannot speak may still have language.
- *Syntax* is about word order; *morphology* is about word structure.
- An *articulation* disorder concerns individual sounds; a *fluency* disorder concerns the flow of speech.

## Study tip

For a language-error example, ask where the error is: sound, suffix, word order, meaning or social use? The answer names the component directly.
