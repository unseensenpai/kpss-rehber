## Core idea

Plant biology explains how an organism fixed on land moves water, minerals and food, responds to its environment and reproduces. The logic follows a **tissue → organ → function** chain: xylem carries water upward, phloem carries food from source to sink, and hormones direct growth. The Biology test has **6 questions** here, on tissue functions, transport mechanisms, hormones and reproduction in flowering plants.

## Tissues and organs

- **Meristem:** dividing cells; apical meristems give growth in length, lateral meristems (cambium) growth in girth.
- **Dermal tissue:** epidermis, cuticle, stomata and root hairs.
- **Vascular tissue:** **xylem** (tracheids and vessels; dead at maturity) carries water and minerals; **phloem** (sieve tubes and companion cells; living) carries organic matter.
- **Ground tissue:** parenchyma (photosynthesis, storage), collenchyma and sclerenchyma (support).
- **Root:** the **Casparian strip** in the endodermis forces water and minerals moving through cell walls to pass through a cell membrane, giving selectivity.
- **Leaf:** palisade and spongy mesophyll photosynthesise; stomata regulate gas exchange and transpiration.

## Transport

- **Water:** taken up by the root through osmosis; the main force lifting it is **transpiration** from the leaves. **Cohesion-tension theory:** water molecules cling to each other (cohesion) and to xylem walls (adhesion), forming an unbroken column. **Root pressure** and **guttation** are supporting forces.
- **Stomata:** guard cells swell when they take up water and the stoma opens; in drought **abscisic acid** closes stomata.
- **Organic matter:** in the **pressure flow model**, sugar is loaded into the phloem at the source (leaf), water follows, pressure rises and the sap flows to a sink or storage site.

## Hormones and reproduction

- **Auxin:** cell elongation, apical dominance, phototropism; **cytokinin:** cell division, stimulates lateral buds; **gibberellin:** stem elongation, seed germination; **abscisic acid:** dormancy and stomatal closure; **ethylene:** fruit ripening, leaf fall.
- A **tropism** is a growth movement that depends on the direction of the stimulus (photo-, gravi-, thigmotropism); a **nastic movement** is independent of direction (the touch-me-not plant folding its leaves).
- **Reproduction in flowering plants:** after pollination, **double fertilisation** occurs: one sperm fuses with the egg to form the zygote (2n), the other with the two polar nuclei to form the endosperm (3n). The seed develops from the ovule; the fruit mostly from the ovary.
- **Alternation of generations:** plants alternate between gametophyte (n) and sporophyte (2n) stages; in seed plants the sporophyte dominates.

## Commonly confused

- *Xylem* carries water one way, upward, and is made of dead cells at maturity; *phloem* carries organic matter in both directions and is made of living cells.
- A *tropism* depends on the direction of the stimulus; a *nastic movement* does not.
- *Auxin* maintains apical dominance; *cytokinin* works against it by stimulating lateral buds.

## Study tip

Collect the hormones in a table with "promotes / inhibits" columns; for transport questions, trace the path of water and sugar with arrows from root to leaf and from leaf to fruit.
