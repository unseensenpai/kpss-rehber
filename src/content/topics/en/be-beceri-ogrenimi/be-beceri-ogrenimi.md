## Core idea

Motor learning (*beceri öğrenimi*) is a **relatively permanent** change in movement skill resulting from practice and experience; a rise in performance within one lesson does not always mean learning. The teacher classifies the skill and chooses practice and feedback suited to the learner's stage. The test has **3 questions**; they usually ask for the stage, the type of practice or the type of feedback in a scene.

## Classification and stages

- **Open skill:** the environment changes and cannot be predicted (a pass during a match). **Closed skill:** the environment is stable (a free throw, the shot put).
- **Discrete** (clear start and end, a shot), **serial** (a sequence of discrete movements, a gymnastics routine) and **continuous** (running, swimming) skills.
- **Fitts and Posner's stages:** **cognitive** (working out what to do, many errors), **associative** (refining the movement, fewer errors), **autonomous** (fluent, with little attention needed).

## Practice and feedback

- **Massed practice** has little rest; **distributed practice** has spaced rest.
- The **whole method** teaches the movement in one piece; it suits complex skills whose parts are tightly linked. The **part method** breaks the movement into sections.
- **Constant and variable practice:** repeating under the same conditions or under changing conditions; variable practice supports transfer in open skills.
- **Mental practice:** rehearsing the movement in the mind without doing it physically.
- **Intrinsic feedback** comes from the senses; **extrinsic feedback** comes from sources such as the teacher or video.
- **Knowledge of results** reports the outcome (the ball missed the hoop); **knowledge of performance** reports the quality of the movement (your elbow stayed out).

## Transfer

- **Positive transfer:** one skill makes learning another easier (some elements from tennis to badminton).
- **Negative transfer:** an earlier skill makes new learning harder.
- **Bilateral transfer:** what is learned with one limb carries over to the opposite limb.

## Commonly confused

- *Knowledge of results* reports the outcome; *knowledge of performance* reports how the movement was done.
- An *open skill* is done in a changing environment; a *closed skill* in a stable one.
- *Performance* is a momentary change; *learning* is a lasting one.

## Study tip

In scene questions, focus on what the teacher says: if it states the outcome, it is knowledge of results; if it corrects part of the movement, it is knowledge of performance.
