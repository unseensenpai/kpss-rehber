## Core idea

Mechanics studies **how bodies move** (kinematics) and **why they move that way** (dynamics). The whole subject rests on a few big principles: **Newton's laws**, **conservation of energy** and **conservation of momentum**. **15** of the 50 questions come from this subject, making it the heaviest area. Questions usually give a situation and expect you to see which principle leads to the answer fastest.

## Kinematics and dynamics

- **Constant acceleration:** v = v₀ + a·t, x = v₀·t + ½·a·t², v² = v₀² + 2·a·x.
- **Projectile motion:** horizontal velocity is constant, vertical acceleration is g. Range R = v₀²·sin(2θ)/g; maximum range at 45° (no air resistance, landing at launch height).
- **Newton's first law:** if the net force is zero, a body keeps its state of motion (inertia).
- **Second law:** F_net = m·a (general form F = dp/dt).
- **Third law:** action and reaction are equal in size, opposite in direction and act on **different bodies**.
- **Friction:** f = μ·N; static friction is at most μs·N, kinetic friction is usually smaller.
- **Uniform circular motion:** centripetal acceleration a = v²/r = ω²·r, directed toward the centre.

## Energy and momentum

- **Work:** W = F·d·cosθ. **Work-energy theorem:** net work equals the change in kinetic energy.
- **Energies:** KE = ½·m·v², gravitational PE = m·g·h, spring PE = ½·k·x².
- **Conservation of mechanical energy:** if only conservative forces do work, KE + PE is constant. Friction turns energy into heat.
- **Power:** P = W/t = F·v.
- **Momentum:** p = m·v; **impulse** J = F·Δt = Δp. With no external force, total momentum is conserved.
- **Collisions:** in an elastic collision both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved; in an inelastic one only momentum. In a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies stick together.

## Rotation, gravitation and oscillation

- **Torque:** τ = r·F·sinθ; for rotation τ = I·α.
- **Moment of inertia:** I = m·r² for a point mass; rotational kinetic energy ½·I·ω².
- **Angular momentum:** L = I·ω; conserved when there is no external torque (a skater pulling in her arms spins faster).
- **Rolling:** rolling without slipping gives v = ω·r and total KE = ½·m·v² + ½·I·ω².
- **Gravitation:** F = G·m₁·m₂/r². Kepler: orbits are ellipses, areal velocity is constant, T² ∝ a³.
- **Simple harmonic motion:** mass-spring T = 2π·√(m/k); simple pendulum at small angles T = 2π·√(L/g).

## Commonly confused

- *Mass* (*kütle*) is the amount of matter and does not change from place to place; *weight* (*ağırlık*, W = m·g) is a force and changes with g.
- In an *elastic* collision kinetic energy is conserved; in an *inelastic* one only momentum is.
- *Action-reaction* forces act on different bodies; *balanced forces* act on the same body.

## Study tip

In every question first ask "is time involved?": if so, kinematics or impulse; if not, energy conservation is usually the shortest route. For collisions and explosions, write momentum first.
