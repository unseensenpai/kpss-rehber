## Core idea

Anatomy studies the structure of the body; kinesiology studies **how that structure produces movement**: bones act as lever arms, joints as pivots and muscles as the source of force. The test has **4 questions**. They usually describe a sports movement and ask for its plane, axis, the role of a muscle, the type of contraction or the lever class.

## Bones, joints and muscles

- **Bones:** an adult has about 206 bones. Types: **long** (femur), **short** (wrist bones), **flat** (skull, shoulder blade), **irregular** (vertebrae), **sesamoid** (kneecap).
- **Joints:** **immovable** (skull sutures), **slightly movable** (between vertebrae), **freely movable/synovial** (shoulder, knee, hip).
- **Muscle types:** **skeletal** (striated, voluntary), **smooth** (internal organs, involuntary), **cardiac** (striated, involuntary).
- **Muscle roles:** the **agonist** is the prime mover, the **antagonist** produces the opposite movement, the **synergist** assists the agonist, the **stabiliser** holds a body part steady.

## Types of contraction

- **Concentric:** the muscle shortens as it contracts (pulling up in a pull-up).
- **Eccentric:** the muscle lengthens under tension (controlling the way down).
- **Isometric:** muscle length does not change and there is no movement (pushing a wall, plank).
- **Isokinetic:** constant speed throughout the movement; done with special machines.

## Planes, axes and levers

- **Sagittal plane:** flexion and extension (running, push-ups); movement happens around the frontal (side-to-side) axis.
- **Frontal plane:** abduction (away from the body) and adduction (towards it), as in jumping jacks.
- **Transverse plane:** rotation (turning the trunk); movement happens around the vertical (longitudinal) axis.
- **Levers:** **first class** with the fulcrum in the middle (moving the head forwards and back), **second class** with the load in the middle (rising onto the toes), **third class** with the effort in the middle (bending the elbow); third-class levers are the most common in the body.

## Commonly confused

- In *concentric* contraction the muscle shortens; in *eccentric* contraction it produces force while lengthening.
- The *agonist* performs the movement; the *synergist* helps it.
- *Abduction* moves away from the body; *adduction* moves towards it.

## Study tip

Memorise the middle element of each lever class (1: fulcrum, 2: load, 3: effort). In movement questions, find the plane first; the axis and the name of the movement follow from it.
