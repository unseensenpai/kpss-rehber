**Question types in past exams:** Sentences that break the flow of thought, ordering sentences, sentences that should swap places, completing a paragraph.

## Core idea

A paragraph is a set of sentences arranged in a **logical order** around one idea. Structure questions disturb or leave out part of that order and ask you to repair it. In the past questions examined, the most common type is **the sentence that breaks the flow of thought**, followed by ordering, swapping and completion. The key is to see the **clues** that link sentences to each other.

## Parts of a paragraph

- **Opening sentence:** introduces the subject and states a general judgement. It does not start with words that point back to an earlier sentence, such as *bu, böyle, ama, çünkü, ayrıca* (this, such, but, because, also).
- **Development:** explains, illustrates and supports the idea.
- **Concluding sentence:** wraps up the idea; it may begin with *kısacası, bu nedenle, sonuçta* (in short, therefore, in the end).

## Linking clues

- **Reference words:** *bu, bunlar, böyle, o, söz konusu* (this, these, such, it, the said) point to an earlier sentence, so such sentences cannot open the paragraph.
- **Conjunctions:** *ama, ancak, oysa* show contrast; *çünkü* cause; *bu yüzden* result; *ayrıca, üstelik* addition. Each sets the relation between two sentences.
- **Repeated words:** a concept at the end of one sentence often reappears at the start of the next.
- **Time and order words:** *önce, ardından, sonunda, ilk olarak* (first, then, finally, firstly).

## By question type

- **Breaks the flow:** state the paragraph's subject in one sentence. Find the sentence that **changes the paragraph's direction** (moves to another aspect), even if it seems related. Remove it and check whether the rest reads without a gap.
- **Ordering:** find the opening sentence first (no reference word, a general statement). Then build the chain with the linking clues; start eliminating by comparing the first sentences in the options.
- **Swapping:** test which two sentences, once swapped, make every reference word and conjunction fall into place.
- **Splitting in two:** find the sentence where the subject or point of view changes; the second paragraph starts there.
- **Completion:** look at the sentences before and after the blank. The right sentence must follow from the one before and prepare for the one after.

## Commonly confused

- The flow-breaking sentence is often **not off-topic**; it moves to a different aspect of the same subject.
- The opening sentence may not be the longest or most general-looking one; what matters is that it is **not tied** to a previous sentence.

## Study tip

Shuffle the sentences of a newspaper paragraph and put them back in order. The habit of marking linking words and references lets you solve these questions in minutes in the exam.
