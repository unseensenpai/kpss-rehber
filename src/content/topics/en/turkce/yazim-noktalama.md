**Question types in past exams:** Finding the misspelled expression.

## Core idea

Spelling and punctuation rules follow the **Spelling Guide of the Turkish Language Association (TDK)**. Questions usually target the most common mistakes: whether *de* and *ki* are written together or apart, separating suffixes on proper nouns with an apostrophe, and compound words. Past exams have asked which underlined expression is misspelled (such as writing the conjunction *ki* joined to the previous word).

## de, ki and mi

- The **conjunction "de / da"** (too, also) is always written **apart**: *Ben de geldim* ("I came too"). Removing it narrows the meaning but leaves the sentence intact.
- The **locative suffix "-de / -da / -te / -ta"** (in, at) is written **joined**: *Evde kaldım* ("I stayed at home"). Removing it breaks the sentence.
- The **conjunction "ki"** is written **apart**: *Öyle yorgundu ki...* ("He was so tired that..."), *var ki, şu ki*. Some fixed words are joined: *belki, çünkü, sanki, oysaki, mademki, halbuki*.
- The **suffix "-ki"** is written **joined**: *evdeki* (the one at home), *seninki* (yours), *yarınki* (tomorrow's).
- The **question particle "mi"** is written **apart** and follows the vowel of the preceding word: *Geldi mi? Okul mu?* Suffixes after it attach to it: *Geliyor musun?*

## Capitals and the apostrophe

- Capitals start sentences, names of people and places, institutions, books and journals, and names of nations, languages and religions.
- Inflectional suffixes on proper nouns are **separated by an apostrophe**: *Ankara'ya, Ayşe'nin*.
- Suffixes on **names of institutions and organisations** take no apostrophe: *Türk Dil Kurumuna, Millî Eğitim Bakanlığından*.
- Suffixes on numbers are separated by an apostrophe: *1923'te, 3'üncü*.

## Compound words and numbers

- Compounds whose meaning has shifted are written **joined**: *hanımeli* (honeysuckle), *kuşburnu* (rose hip), *aslanağzı* (snapdragon).
- Compound verbs with *etmek, olmak* are **joined** if a sound change occurs (*hissetmek, kaybolmak*) and **apart** otherwise (*yardım etmek, mutlu olmak*).
- Numbers are written **apart**: *on beş* (fifteen), *iki yüz* (two hundred). Ordinal numbers in digits use a full stop or an apostrophe and suffix: *5. sınıf, 5'inci sınıf*.

## Punctuation marks

- **Full stop:** at the end of a complete sentence; in abbreviations.
- **Comma:** between words with the same function; after a subject far from its predicate in long sentences; around parenthetical phrases.
- **Semicolon:** separates groups of equal elements that contain commas, or closely linked sentences.
- **Colon:** at the end of a sentence followed by an explanation, example or list.
- **Ellipsis:** at the end of unfinished sentences, in place of words left unsaid.
- **Question mark, exclamation mark, quotation marks, parentheses, hyphen and dash** may also be asked.

## Commonly confused

- *Sen de mi?* (conjunction, apart) vs *Evde mi?* (suffix, joined): if removing "de" breaks the sentence, it is a suffix.
- *Mademki, sanki, belki* are joined; *var ki, öyle ki* are written apart.
- *Türk Dil Kurumuna* (no apostrophe) vs *İstanbul'a* (apostrophe).

## Study tip

Check short texts you write against the Spelling Guide and keep your three most frequent mistakes on a card. Spelling questions in the exam are largely solved with that short list.
