## Core idea

When writers put an idea across they make two choices: **which mode of writing** to use (narrate, explain, argue, describe) and **how to support** the idea (definition, example, comparison, citing someone, numbers). A common past-exam question is "Which of the following is **not** used in this passage?", so each option has to be looked for in the passage one by one.

## Modes of writing

- **Expository:** aims to inform; explains a subject to the reader. Textbook language, common in instructional texts.
- **Argumentative:** defends an idea and tries to refute the opposing view; aims to persuade. Patterns such as *"Bazıları ... diyor, oysa ..."* ("Some say ..., whereas ...") are clues.
- **Narrative:** tells events in time order, with events, characters, place and time. Verbs are mostly in the past tense.
- **Descriptive:** presents a thing, place or person so that it can be seen; it paints a picture in the reader's mind. Adjectives and sensory details are dense.

## Ways of developing an idea

- **Definition:** answers "what is ...?" *Mizah, ... sanatıdır* ("Humour is the art of ...").
- **Giving examples:** opens up an abstract idea with a concrete example. *Örneğin, ...* ("For example, ...").
- **Comparison:** shows similarities and differences between two or more things or ideas.
- **Citing an authority:** refers to the words of a famous person or expert.
- **Using statistics:** supports the idea with figures, ratios or numbers.
- **Simile:** explains an idea by likening it to something better known.
- Other common ones: explanation, prediction.

## Qualities of expression

- **Clarity:** the idea is easy to understand and cannot be read in different ways.
- **Fluency:** sentences connect without the reader stumbling.
- **Concision:** no unnecessary words or repetition.
- **Plainness:** simple, natural language without ornament.
- **Originality:** the writer expresses the idea in their own way.

## Commonly confused

- *Giving examples* offers an example; *citing an authority* quotes someone.
- *Description* makes a scene visible; *narration* tells a chain of events. If there is movement and the passing of time, it is narration.
- *Comparison* sets two things against each other; a *simile* likens one to the other.

## Study tip

For "which is not used?" questions, look for each option in the passage and underline where you find it. The one option you cannot trace is the answer. Comparison and definition are the ones most often missed.
