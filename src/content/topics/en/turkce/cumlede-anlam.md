## Core idea

Sentence-meaning questions ask not **what** a sentence says but **how** it says it: does it give a reason, make a comparison, state a personal view? So look at two things in every sentence: the **type of judgement** (subjective or objective) and the **meaning relation** (cause, purpose, condition, comparison). Past exams include questions such as finding which numbered sentences contain no simile.

## Meaning relations

- **Cause and effect:** the reason for a judgement is given. *Yağmur yağdığı için maç ertelendi* ("The match was postponed because it rained"). Clues: *çünkü, -dığı için, -den dolayı* (because, due to).
- **Purpose:** the aim of an action. *Sınavı kazanmak için çalışıyor* ("She studies to pass the exam"). Test it by adding "with the aim of".
- **Condition:** a judgement depends on a condition. *Erken gelirsen birlikte çıkarız* ("If you come early, we'll go out together").
- **Comparison:** similarities or differences between two or more things. *Bu kitap öncekinden daha akıcı* ("This book reads more easily than the last").
- **Simile (benzetme):** likening something to another in one respect. *Su gibi berrak bir dil* ("language as clear as water").
- **Exaggeration:** making something seem bigger or smaller than it is. *Bin kere söyledim* ("I've said it a thousand times").

## Types of judgement

- **Objective:** can be proven and does not vary by person. *Kitap üç bölümden oluşuyor* ("The book has three parts").
- **Subjective:** expresses personal opinion or taste and can be disputed. *Kitabın en etkileyici bölümü sonuncusu* ("The most moving part is the last").
- **Certainty / possibility:** *Kesinlikle gelecek* ("He will certainly come") versus *Gelebilir* ("He may come").
- **Assumption:** accepting a situation as if it were real and reasoning from it. *Diyelim ki herkes katıldı...* ("Suppose everyone joined...").
- **Prejudice:** judging without knowing. *Okumadım ama sıkıcı olduğu belli* ("I haven't read it, but it's obviously boring").

## Other meaning features

- **Definition:** explains what a concept is. *Mizah, ... sanatıdır* ("Humour is the art of ...").
- **Criticism:** evaluates the positive or negative sides of a work or situation.
- **Suggestion:** offers a solution or a way forward.
- **Complaint:** expresses discomfort with a situation.
- **Implied meaning:** what is not said outright but follows. *Ali de geldi* ("Ali came too") → others came as well.

## Commonly confused

- A *cause-and-effect* sentence gives the reason for an action; a *purpose* sentence gives its goal. "-mek için" (in order to) usually signals purpose.
- A *comparison* places two things side by side; a *simile* likens one to the other.
- A sentence can be subjective without "I think": evaluative words (*güzel, etkileyici, gereksiz*: beautiful, moving, unnecessary) show subjectivity.

## Study tip

Define the feature asked for in your own words first ("simile: X is like Y"), then look for that pattern in the options. With numbered sentences, give each sentence a small label (cause, subjective, simile...); the answer follows from the labels.
