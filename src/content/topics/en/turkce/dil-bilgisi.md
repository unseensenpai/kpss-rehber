**Question types in past exams:** Sound changes, elements of the sentence.

## Core idea

Grammar questions test **telling things apart**, not memorisation: a word's type depends on its role in the sentence. *Güzel* is an adjective in *güzel ev* (a beautiful house) and an adverb in *güzel konuştu* (spoke well). So always look first at the word's **role in the sentence**. Past exams have asked about sound changes and the elements of the sentence.

## Phonology and sound changes

- **Major vowel harmony:** after a back vowel (a, ı, o, u) comes a back vowel, after a front vowel (e, i, ö, ü) a front vowel.
- **Minor vowel harmony:** unrounded vowels are followed by unrounded ones; rounded vowels by low-unrounded (a, e) or high-rounded (u, ü) ones.
- **Consonant softening:** p, ç, t, k become b, c, d, ğ/g before a suffix starting with a vowel. *Kitap → kitabı, ağaç → ağacı.*
- **Consonant assimilation (hardening):** suffixes beginning with c, d, g become ç, t, k after a voiceless consonant (f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p). *Sokakta, kitapçı.*
- **Vowel elision:** some words with a high vowel in the second syllable drop it before a vowel-initial suffix. *Ağız → ağzı, burun → burnu.*
- **Vowel narrowing:** verbs ending in a or e change it to ı, i, u, ü before -yor. *Başla → başlıyor.*
- **Consonant loss:** *küçük → küçülmek.* **Consonant doubling:** *his → hissetmek.* **Buffer consonants:** y, ş, s, n come between two vowels.

## Word structure and parts of speech

- **Root + derivational suffix** forms a new word (*göz-lük*, glasses); an **inflectional suffix** keeps the meaning and sets the role (*göz-ler-i*, his eyes).
- By structure, words are **simple** (*kitap*), **derived** (*kitaplık*) or **compound** (*hanımeli*).
- **Parts of speech:** noun, adjective, pronoun, adverb, postposition, conjunction, interjection and verb.
- **Verbals (fiilimsi):** words derived from verbs that act as nouns, adjectives or adverbs: verbal noun (*okuma*), participle (*okunan kitap*), converb (*okuyunca*).

## Elements of the sentence

- Find the **predicate** first (the part that makes the statement), then ask questions of it.
- **Subject:** who or what does the action; found with "kim, ne" (who, what).
- **Object:** what is affected. "Neyi, kimi" (what/whom, definite) finds the **definite object**; "ne" (what) the **indefinite object**.
- **Indirect complement (yer tamlayıcısı):** carries -e, -de, -den and answers "kime, nerede, nereden" (to whom, where, from where).
- **Adverbial complement (zarf tümleci):** answers "nasıl, ne zaman, niçin, ne kadar" (how, when, why, how much).
- Word groups are **not split** across elements: *Dünkü toplantıya katılan herkes* ("everyone who attended yesterday's meeting") is one subject.

## Sentence types

- **By predicate:** nominal sentence (*Hava soğuk.*, The weather is cold) and verbal sentence (*Kar yağdı.*, It snowed).
- **By structure:** simple, complex, compound (juxtaposed) and coordinated sentences.
- **By word order:** **regular** when the predicate is last, **inverted** when it is not.

## Commonly confused

- A *definite object* takes the accusative "-ı"; an *indefinite object* takes no suffix and sits next to the verb.
- *Indirect complement* vs *adverbial complement*: a phrase with -e, -de, -den is usually an indirect complement; if it answers "how" or "when", it is an adverbial complement.

## Study tip

Break a few sentences into their elements every day and put sound changes into a table with example words. For sound changes, the fastest way is to write the word's root next to it and spot the difference.
