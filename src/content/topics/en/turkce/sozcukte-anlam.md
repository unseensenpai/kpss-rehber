## Core idea

A word's meaning is set by the **sentence**, not the dictionary. *Ağır* ("heavy") is literal for a load, figurative for words, and an extended meaning for an illness. That is why most word questions give a **context** and ask for the word that fits it best, or what an expression means in that context. In past exams the most common type is fitting a pair of words into a sentence with two blanks.

## Types of meaning

- **Literal meaning (gerçek anlam):** the first meaning that comes to mind. *Kapıyı kapattı* ("He closed the door").
- **Extended meaning (yan anlam):** a new meaning that keeps a link to the literal one. *Masanın ayağı* ("the leg of the table"), *dağın eteği* ("the foot of the mountain").
- **Figurative meaning (mecaz anlam):** a meaning that has moved away from the literal one. *Ağır sözler* ("harsh words"), *soğuk karşılama* ("a cold welcome").
- **Technical meaning (terim anlam):** a meaning specific to a field. *Açı* (an angle in geometry), *perde* (an act in theatre).
- **Concrete / abstract:** perceived by the senses (*taş*, stone) versus existing only in the mind (*sevgi*, love).
- **General / specific:** *ağaç* (tree) is general, *çınar* (plane tree) specific.

## Meaning relations

- **Synonyms:** different spelling, same meaning (*yanıt – cevap*, answer). **Near-synonyms:** similar but not identical (*bakmak – görmek*, look – see).
- **Antonyms:** opposite meanings (*uzun – kısa*, long – short). A negative suffix expresses negation, not opposition (*gel – gelme*, come – don't come).
- **Homonyms:** same spelling, different meanings (*yüz*: hundred, face, to swim).
- **Metonymy (ad aktarması):** using the name of one thing for something related. *Ankara bu kararı onayladı* ("Ankara approved this decision", Ankara = the government).
- **Onomatopoeia:** *şırıl şırıl* (trickling), *hışırtı* (rustle).

## Idioms, proverbs and reduplications

- **Idiom (deyim):** a fixed, usually figurative phrase that describes a situation and gives no advice. *Gözden düşmek* ("to fall out of favour").
- **Proverb (atasözü):** a fixed saying that gives advice or a general judgement. *Damlaya damlaya göl olur* ("Drop by drop, a lake forms").
- **Reduplication (ikileme):** repeating or pairing words to strengthen meaning. *Yavaş yavaş* (slowly), *eğri büğrü* (crooked).

## Fill-in-the-blank questions

- First find the sentence's **overall direction**: positive or negative? Is there a contrast (*ama, oysa*: but, whereas) or a cause and result (*bu yüzden*: therefore)?
- Look at the words just before and after the blank; many options drop out at this first pass.
- With two blanks, **start with the blank you are sure of**, eliminate options, then test the second.
- Put the option in and read the whole sentence: does it flow, and does the word match the tone?

## Commonly confused

- An *extended meaning* keeps a link to the literal meaning; a *figurative meaning* does not.
- An *idiom* describes a situation; a *proverb* states a judgement or gives advice.
- *Homonyms* share spelling; *synonyms* share meaning.

## Study tip

Mark figurative and extended uses in what you read and collect them in a notebook as "word – sentence – type of meaning". In blank questions, placing each option and reading it aloud is the fastest way to eliminate.
