## Core idea

Paragraph questions are the largest part of the Turkish section of the General Ability test. In past exams the most common types are **finding the main idea** and **choosing the statement that cannot be made from the passage**. Questions 20–26 also come every year as groups tied to shared passages. Success depends less on knowledge than on **careful, systematic reading**: what the passage says must not be confused with what you know or think.

## Main idea and subject

- **Subject:** what the passage is about; usually a noun phrase (*the function of humour*).
- **Main idea:** the core message the writer wants to give about that subject; it is a statement (*Humour makes the familiar visible*).
- The main idea is often in the **first or last** sentence, after words such as *kısacası, sonuç olarak, yani, aslında* (in short, in conclusion, that is, in fact).
- The correct option covers the **whole** passage; an option that fits only one sentence is a supporting idea.

## Supporting ideas and inference

- **Supporting ideas** are details that explain and back up the main idea.
- In **"can be said"** questions, the option is either stated outright or follows **necessarily** from the passage.
- In **"cannot be said" / "not mentioned"** questions, four options usually match sentences in the passage; the answer is the one that **contradicts** the passage or is **absent** from it.
- Watch for absolute words in options such as *her zaman, yalnızca, hiçbir, en* (always, only, none, most); if the passage is not that absolute, the option is wrong.

## The writer's purpose and attitude

- The writer may be **criticising**, **suggesting**, **complaining** or **explaining**. The verb in the question (*yakınmaktadır, vurgulamaktadır, eleştirmektedir*: complains, emphasises, criticises) tells you what to look for.
- Adjectives and evaluative words reveal the attitude (*ne yazık ki, maalesef, sevindirici olan*: unfortunately, sadly, what is pleasing).

## Question groups

- Two or three questions follow the same passage. Read the **questions** first, then read the passage once carefully with them in mind.
- One question may ask for the main idea and another for a detail or word meaning; go back to the passage for each.

## Commonly confused

- A *subject* is a phrase; a *main idea* is a statement.
- A detail in the passage can be *true* without being the *main idea*.
- An option with correct information is not the answer to a "can be said" question if the passage does not contain it.

## Study tip

Read a few paragraphs every day and sum up each in one sentence. The closer that summary is to the main idea, the faster paragraph questions become. For questions you get wrong, find which sentence of the passage the misleading option relied on.
