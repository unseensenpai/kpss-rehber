## Core idea

Physical education (*beden eğitimi*) is the part of education that aims at a person's physical, mental, emotional and social development **through movement**; sport is organised physical activity with set rules and **competition**. This subject brings together the field's concepts, aims, history and how it is taught. With **8 questions** it is the heaviest subject in the field. Questions usually ask you to tell concepts apart, match historical figures and events, or identify the teaching style used in a lesson scene.

## Concepts and aims

- **Physical education:** planned and programmed at school, aimed at **everyone**; the goal is development.
- **Sport:** rule-bound, competitive, organised activity; performance and winning come first.
- **Play:** voluntary, fun, with its own rules. **Recreation:** optional activities for using leisure time.
- **Aims:** **psychomotor** (basic movement and sport skills), **cognitive** (rules, strategy, health knowledge) and **affective** (self-confidence, cooperation, fair play) development, plus an active, healthy lifestyle for life.

## Historical development

- **Ancient Greece:** educating body and mind together was the ideal; the ancient Olympic Games were held at Olympia.
- **Modern Olympics:** revived under the leadership of Pierre de **Coubertin**; the first Games were held in Athens in 1896.
- **Gymnastics schools:** **Jahn** in Germany (apparatus-based, nationally minded Turnen movement) and **Ling** in Sweden (health-oriented and corrective, mostly free-exercise gymnastics).
- **Sport among the Turks:** equestrian sports, *cirit* (mounted javelin game), archery and wrestling stand out; the Ottomans had lodges (*tekke*) for archery and wrestling. **Selim Sırrı Tarcan** is known for introducing Swedish gymnastics and promoting school physical education.

## Teaching styles (Mosston)

- Mosston's spectrum orders styles by how far **decisions shift from teacher to learner**.
- **Reproduction cluster:** **command** (all decisions with the teacher), **practice** (learners work at their own pace; the teacher gives feedback), **reciprocal** (an observer partner gives feedback against criteria), **self-check**, **inclusion** (several difficulty levels; each learner chooses their own).
- **Production cluster:** **guided discovery** (the teacher leads with questions to one correct answer), **convergent discovery**, **divergent discovery** (several solutions) and styles in which learners design and initiate their own programme.

## Curriculum and the teacher

- Check the names, learning areas and outcomes of the physical education curricula in MEB's **current official programme texts**.
- The teacher sets up a safe environment, gives correct demonstrations and feedback, chooses activities suited to different levels and keeps learners active.

## Commonly confused

- *Physical education* emphasises everyone's development; *sport* emphasises competition and performance.
- In the *practice* style the teacher gives feedback; in the *reciprocal* style an observing partner does.
- *Guided discovery* leads to one correct answer; *divergent discovery* leads to several solutions.

## Study tip

Put Mosston's styles in a table using two questions: who makes the decisions, and who gives feedback? Asking these two questions about a lesson scene usually points to the right style.
