## Core idea

Political science studies how **power** is gained, used and limited, and the relationship between the state, institutions and citizens. For a social studies teacher the aim is a conceptual base for democratic citizenship education. The test has 8 questions; they usually ask about definitions, systems of government and matching thinkers with ideas.

## Basic concepts and the state

- **Power** is the ability to influence others' behaviour; **authority** is power accepted as legitimate.
- **Legitimacy (Weber):** **traditional**, **charismatic** and **legal-rational** authority.
- **Elements of the state:** a population, a territory, **sovereignty** and the political organisation that runs them.
- **Forms of state:** a **unitary** state has one centre and one constitution; in a **federal** state power is shared by the constitution between the centre and member units; a **confederation** is a treaty-based union of independent states.
- **Forms of rule:** monarchy, republic, oligarchy, theocracy; Aristotle classified regimes by the number of rulers and whose interest they serve.

## Systems of government

- **Separation of powers (Montesquieu):** legislative, executive and judicial power given to separate bodies.
- **Parliamentary system:** a dual executive (head of state and government); the government depends on the confidence of parliament; **interpellation** (motion of censure) and **dissolution** are mutual checks.
- **Presidential system:** a popularly elected president heads both the state and the executive; strict separation between branches (the US example).
- **Semi-presidential system:** a strong popularly elected president alongside a government responsible to parliament (the French example).
- **Turkey:** under the **Presidential Government System**, adopted by the 2017 referendum and in force from 2018, executive power belongs to the president.

## Thinkers and ideologies

- **Machiavelli** separated politics from morality; **Hobbes** argued for an absolute sovereign for security, **Locke** for natural rights and limited government, **Rousseau** for the **general will**.
- **Liberalism** stresses individual freedom and the market, **conservatism** tradition and gradual change, **socialism** social equality and collective control of the means of production, **anarchism** the rejection of the state; **fascism** defends a single party and a totalitarian state.

## Democracy and rights

- **Types of democracy:** direct, representative and participatory.
- **Electoral systems:** **majoritarian** systems favour stability, **proportional representation** fairness of representation; Turkey uses the d'Hondt method of proportional representation in parliamentary elections.
- **Participation:** parties, pressure and interest groups, **civil society** organisations, public opinion.
- **Human rights:** the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)**; first-generation (civil and political), second-generation (economic, social and cultural) and third-generation (solidarity) rights.

## Commonly confused

- *Power* is force; *authority* is force accepted as legitimate.
- In a *federal state* power-sharing is constitutional; in a *unitary state* local governments work within powers delegated by the centre.
- *Hobbes* favours a strong sovereign; *Locke* favours limited government.

## Study tip

Tabulate systems of government by three questions: how many heads does the executive have, who elects it, and to whom is it responsible. When a question describes a country's practice, these three questions reveal the system at once.
