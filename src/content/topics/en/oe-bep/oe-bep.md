## Core idea

An individualised education programme (*bireyselleştirilmiş eğitim programı*, BEP) is a written plan built for a student with special educational needs, starting from their **present performance**. The logic: first establish what the student can do, then write the goals they are expected to reach and the support those goals need. There are **3 questions**; they mostly ask about IEP components and writing objectives.

## Who prepares it?

- The **IEP development team** (*BEP geliştirme birimi*) is formed at the school; it includes school management, the student's teachers, the guidance service, the family and, where appropriate, the student.
- The family is a **mandatory and equal** partner. Check the current regulation for the team's structure and duties.

## Components

- **Present level of performance:** a description, based on observation and measurement, of what the student can and cannot do now.
- **Long-term goals:** broad targets usually expected by the end of a school year.
- **Short-term objectives:** measurable intermediate steps leading to the long-term goal.
- **Support education services** and required **adaptations** (content, method, materials, setting, assessment).
- **Evaluation:** how and when progress towards the goals will be measured.

## Writing objectives

- A good short-term objective has three elements: **behaviour** (observable action), **condition** (in what situation, with what materials) and **criterion** (level of success).
- Example: "Given number cards up to 10, the student will point to the number said in at least 9 trials in three consecutive sessions."
- Unobservable verbs such as "understands" or "knows" are not used in objectives.

## Commonly confused

- An *IEP* is not a separate curriculum but an individualisation of the general programme; an *adaptation* is one tool of that individualisation.
- A *long-term goal* is the yearly target; a *short-term objective* is a measurable step towards it.
- The *condition* states the situation in which the behaviour occurs; the *criterion* states the level of success.

## Study tip

In objective questions split the statement into its three parts and see which is missing; most questions want you to catch a missing criterion or a non-observable verb.
