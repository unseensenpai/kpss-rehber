## Core idea

Rhythm education (*ritim eğitimi*) develops the ability to **organise movement in time** with music or a beat; dance and folk dance (*halk oyunları*) are cultural and creative forms of that ability. Folk dances also carry **cultural heritage**. The test has **2 questions**; they usually ask about the elements of rhythm and matching folk dance types with their regions.

## Rhythm and movement

- **Rhythm:** regular repetition of sound or movement. **Tempo:** speed. **Beat:** the basic unit of time. **Metre:** the grouping of beats (2/4, 3/4, 4/4). **Accent:** a beat played strongly.
- **Asymmetric rhythm** (*aksak*): metres made of unequal units (such as 9/8); common in Turkish music and folk dance.
- **Laban movement analysis:** movement is studied through the elements of **body**, **space**, **effort** (time, weight, flow) and **relationship**.
- **Creative dance:** the student expresses an idea, feeling or image with their own movements; there is no single right answer.

## Folk dances

- **Zeybek:** Aegean; slow and stately, often in asymmetric rhythm.
- **Horon:** Black Sea; fast, with quivering movements, accompanied by the *kemençe* (a small bowed fiddle).
- **Halay:** Eastern, South-eastern and Central Anatolia; hand in hand or shoulder to shoulder, with *davul* and *zurna* (drum and shawm).
- **Bar:** North-eastern Anatolia (around Erzurum); danced as an orderly group.
- **Spoon dances** (*kaşık oyunları*): Central and Southern Anatolia, keeping rhythm with wooden spoons.
- **Karşılama:** Thrace and the Marmara region; danced facing a partner.

## Teaching

- Simple to complex: first let students feel the rhythm by counting and clapping, then teach the basic steps, and only then combine the figures.
- **Mirror method:** a teacher facing the class performs the movement on the opposite side to avoid confusion over direction.

## Commonly confused

- *Rhythm* is regular repetition; *tempo* is its speed.
- *Horon* belongs to the Black Sea; *bar* to North-eastern Anatolia.
- *Creative dance* relies on the student's own expression; *folk dance* relies on traditional figures.

## Study tip

Mark folk dances on a map of Türkiye with their region and accompanying instrument; visual memory helps a lot in matching questions.
