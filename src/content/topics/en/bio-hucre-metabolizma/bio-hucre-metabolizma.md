## Core idea

The cell is the smallest unit of life, and metabolism is the sum of the reactions in which cells capture, store and use energy. The subject rests on **structure and function** and **energy flow**: the membrane chooses what enters, organelles divide the work, enzymes speed up reactions and ATP carries energy. The Biology test has **10 questions** here. They ask where respiration and photosynthesis steps happen and what they produce, about membrane transport, enzyme graphs and division phases.

## Biomolecules, membrane and organelles

- **Carbohydrates** are joined by glycosidic bonds, **proteins** by peptide bonds, **nucleic acids** by phosphodiester bonds; **triglycerides** form from glycerol and three fatty acids through ester bonds. These bonds are made by dehydration and broken by hydrolysis.
- **Fluid mosaic model:** proteins that can move within a phospholipid bilayer; in animal cells cholesterol regulates fluidity.
- **Transport:** simple and facilitated diffusion and osmosis are passive; **active transport** (for example the Na⁺/K⁺ pump: 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in) uses ATP; large molecules move by **endocytosis** and **exocytosis**.
- **Endomembrane system:** rough ER (proteins for membranes and secretion), smooth ER (lipid synthesis, detoxification), Golgi apparatus (modification and packaging), lysosomes and vacuoles.
- **Mitochondria and chloroplasts** have their own DNA and 70S ribosomes, key evidence for the **endosymbiotic theory**.

## The cell cycle

- **Interphase:** G1 (growth), S (DNA replication), G2 (preparation for division). After S the DNA amount doubles but the chromosome number does not change.
- **Mitosis:** prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase, followed by cytokinesis. Plants form a cell plate; animal cells pinch in (cleavage furrow).
- **Meiosis I** separates homologous chromosomes (number halves); **meiosis II** separates sister chromatids. Crossing over happens in prophase I.
- **Checkpoints** (G1, G2, metaphase) control the cycle; when this control fails, uncontrolled division (cancer) can result.

## Enzymes and energy metabolism

- **Enzymes** lower activation energy and do not change a reaction's ΔG. Activity falls outside the optimum temperature and pH; high temperature denatures them.
- **Inhibition:** a competitive inhibitor binds the active site (overcome by adding more substrate); a non-competitive inhibitor binds elsewhere.
- **Glycolysis** (cytoplasm): 1 glucose → 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP and 2 NADH; no oxygen needed.
- **Krebs cycle** (mitochondrial matrix): pyruvate becomes acetyl-CoA; the cycle releases CO₂ and produces NADH and FADH₂.
- **Electron transport chain** (inner mitochondrial membrane): the final electron acceptor is O₂, forming water; the proton gradient drives ATP synthase (**chemiosmosis**). Most ATP is made here.
- **Fermentation:** regenerates NAD⁺ without oxygen; only the 2 ATP from glycolysis are gained.
- **Photosynthesis:** the light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membrane; water is split, O₂ is released, ATP and NADPH are made. The **Calvin cycle** in the stroma fixes CO₂ with the enzyme RuBisCO and uses ATP and NADPH to make sugar.
- **C4 and CAM plants** show adaptations that reduce photorespiration and water loss in hot, dry habitats.

## Commonly confused

- *Chemiosmosis* is the mechanism of ATP synthesis via a proton gradient; *substrate-level phosphorylation* (glycolysis, Krebs) makes ATP directly from a substrate.
- The *light-dependent reactions* release O₂; the *Calvin cycle* fixes CO₂ and does not use light directly.
- *Meiosis I* separates homologues; *meiosis II* and *mitosis* separate sister chromatids.

## Study tip

Make a single table for respiration and photosynthesis: for each step write the location, inputs, outputs and energy carriers gained. Questions usually ask for one cell of that table.
