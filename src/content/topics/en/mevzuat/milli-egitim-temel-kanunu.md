## Core idea

The Basic Law of National Education No. 1739 (adopted **14 June 1973**) is like the **constitution** of Turkish national education: it sets the aims education pursues, the principles it rests on and the general structure of the system. The **basic principles** are asked most. A typical question describes a practice and asks which principle it rests on.

## General aims (Art. 2)

The general aim of Turkish national education is to raise all members of the Turkish nation:

1. as **citizens** loyal to Atatürk's reforms and principles and to Atatürk's nationalism as expressed in the Constitution; who adopt, protect and develop national, moral, human, spiritual and cultural values; and who know their duties and responsibilities towards the Republic of Turkey, a democratic, secular and social state governed by the rule of law;
2. as individuals with a **personality** developed in a balanced and healthy way in body, mind, morals, spirit and emotion, able to think freely and scientifically;
3. ready for life, with their interests, aptitudes and abilities developed, and able to have a **profession**.

In short: **a good citizen, a balanced personality, a person with a profession.** Specific aims are set for each level in line with these general aims (Art. 3).

## Basic principles (Arts. 4–17)

- **Universality and equality:** educational institutions are open to everyone without distinction of language, race, sex, disability or religion; no person, family, group or class may be privileged.
- **Needs of the individual and society:** education is arranged around citizens' wishes and abilities and the needs of society.
- **Guidance (orientation):** individuals are directed to programmes and schools according to their interests, aptitudes and abilities.
- **Right to education:** primary education is the right of every Turkish citizen; later levels are open according to ability.
- **Equality of opportunity:** equal opportunity is provided to women and men alike; successful students without means are supported through free boarding, scholarships and loans; measures are taken for those who need special education.
- **Continuity:** education continues throughout life.
- **Atatürk's reforms, principles and nationalism**, **education for democracy**, **secularism**, **scientific approach**, **planning**.
- **Co-education:** girls and boys are educated together; some schools may be reserved for one sex where necessary.
- **Education campuses and school-family cooperation (Art. 16):** where several institutions share an area, an education campus and campus management can be set up. The article's title was changed in 2009 from "School-family cooperation" to its current form.
- **Education everywhere:** the aims of national education are pursued not only in schools but at home, in the community, at work and everywhere.

## General structure of the system

- The system has two main parts: **formal** and **non-formal** education (Art. 18).
- Formal education covers pre-school (optional), primary education (ages 6–14, compulsory), upper secondary education (four years, compulsory) and higher education.
- The old Articles 43 and 45 on teachers' qualifications and selection were repealed in 2022; the subject is now governed by the **Teaching Profession Law No. 7528**.

## Commonly confused

- The *general aims* (citizen, personality, profession) and the *basic principles* (universality and equality, guidance, etc.) are separate headings.
- *Universality and equality* is about non-discrimination; *equality of opportunity* is about supporting disadvantaged students.
- *Guidance* directs a person to a programme suited to their ability; the *right to education* guarantees everyone primary education.

## Study tip

List the principles and put a school example beside each (a scholarship scheme is equality of opportunity, a public education course is continuity and education everywhere). Principle questions are then solved quickly from the examples.
