## Core idea

The Teaching Profession Law No. 7528 (adopted **10 October 2024**, Official Gazette **18 October 2024**) regulates teaching in a separate law for the first time. Becoming a teacher no longer means direct appointment: candidates take **preparatory training** at the **National Education Academy**, those who succeed start as contracted teachers, then move to permanent posts and advance through **three career steps**. MEB-AGS is the entrance exam for this preparatory training.

## Purpose, scope and basic principles

- The law governs the selection, training, appointment, rights, duties and responsibilities, rewards and penalties and career advancement of teachers, and the establishment of the **National Education Academy** (Art. 1).
- **Basic principles (Art. 4):** duties and responsibilities set according to the needs of the profession; **merit and career** in appointment and advancement; encouraging participation in decisions on education; training before entering the profession and **continuous development** within it.

## Rights, duties and responsibilities

- Teachers raise students in line with the aims of Law No. 1739, work for their physical, mental, emotional, social and moral development, take their learning styles and personal rights into account, choose methods and materials in line with the curricula, cooperate with colleagues and parents, and develop themselves professionally (Art. 5).
- Teaching lessons, duty supervision, invigilating exams, attending boards and ceremonies, and running guidance services are among a teacher's duties.
- Except in states of emergency, general disasters and epidemics, teachers **cannot be assigned work unrelated to their profession against their will**.

## Qualifications, preparatory training and the Academy

- Teaching is a **specialised profession** requiring preparation in general culture, subject knowledge and pedagogical knowledge (Art. 7).
- Teachers are selected from graduates of at least **bachelor's** programmes who **succeed in preparatory training**.
- **Preparatory training** is given by the Academy and lasts **four terms**; depending on the graduate's programme it can be **three terms** (Art. 8). A term lasts **no less than 10 and no more than 14 weeks**.
- **Success (Art. 10):** at least two written exams per theoretical course; a course average of **60 out of 100** or higher is a pass. One **make-up exam** is allowed for each failed course; those who still fail are dismissed from the Academy. Practical performance is assessed **three times**.
- **National Education Academy (Arts. 26–27):** attached to the ministry; prepares and runs preparatory training, school leader training, and career and promotion programmes.

## Contracted and permanent posts

- Those who complete preparatory training are employed as **contracted teachers** (Civil Servants Law No. 657, Art. 4/B); placement follows the **placement score** and their preferences (Art. 15).
- Contracted teachers who complete **three years** of service are, on request, appointed to **permanent teaching posts** where they serve. Those appointed cannot transfer for **one year**, except for excused reasons (Art. 16).

## Career steps (Art. 20)

- The profession has three career steps: **teacher, expert teacher and head teacher**.
- **Expert teacher:** at least **10 years** as a teacher, no penalty halting step progression, and completion of the Academy's expert-teacher training.
- **Head teacher:** at least **10 years** as an expert teacher, no such penalty, and completion of the Academy's head-teacher training.
- Each title also brings **one grade** of promotion.

## Protection from violence (Art. 33)

- For **intentional injury, threats, insults** and **resisting to prevent the performance of duty** committed against teachers and school leaders because of their duties, penalties are **increased by half** and **suspension of the prison sentence does not apply**.

## Commonly confused

- Preparatory training is **four terms** (or three), contracted service before a permanent post is **three years**, and each career step requires **ten years**.
- *Contracted* teaching falls under Article 4/B of Law No. 657; a *permanent* post comes on request after the contracted period.
- There are three career steps: teacher, expert teacher, head teacher.

## Study tip

Put the path to teaching on a timeline: bachelor's degree → AGS → preparatory training (terms) → contracted teacher (3 years) → permanent post → expert teacher (10 years) → head teacher (10 years). Number questions come straight from this timeline. Laws and regulations can change, so check the current text before the exam.
