## Core idea

The legislation section of AGS asks about the Constitution (No. 2709, adopted **18 October 1982**) from a teacher's point of view: alongside the basic structure of the state, the **right to education** and the protection of fundamental rights stand out. The overall structure, the legislature, executive and judiciary are covered in detail in the Citizenship topic "Constitution, Human Rights Law". This page gathers the articles that matter most to a teacher candidate.

## General principles

- The **first three articles** (the Republic, its characteristics, the language, flag, national anthem and capital) cannot be amended or even proposed for amendment (Art. 4).
- **Characteristics of the Republic (Art. 2):** respecting human rights, loyal to Atatürk's nationalism, a **democratic, secular and social state governed by the rule of law**.
- The language of the state is **Turkish** (Art. 3).

## Right and duty of training and education (Art. 42)

- **No one shall be deprived of the right to education.** The scope of the right is set by law.
- Training and education are carried out **in line with Atatürk's principles and reforms**, on the basis of contemporary science and education, **under the supervision and control of the state**. Institutions contrary to these principles cannot be opened.
- Freedom of education does not remove the **duty of loyalty to the Constitution**.
- **Primary education is compulsory for all citizens, girls and boys, and free in state schools.**
- The state helps successful students without means through **scholarships** and other ways, and takes measures for **those who need special education** so that they can contribute to society.
- Only education, teaching, research and study take place in educational institutions; these activities cannot be obstructed.
- **No language other than Turkish** may be taught as a **mother tongue** to Turkish citizens in educational institutions.

## Religious culture and ethics instruction (Art. 24)

- Religious and moral education is carried out under the supervision and control of the state. **Religious culture and ethics** is among the **compulsory courses** in primary and secondary schools.

## Restriction and suspension of fundamental rights

- **Restriction (Art. 13):** fundamental rights and freedoms may be restricted only **by law**, for reasons stated in the Constitution and without touching their essence; restrictions must respect **proportionality**.
- **Suspension (Art. 15):** in war, mobilisation and states of emergency the exercise of rights may be suspended; even then the right to life, freedom of religion and conscience, non-retroactivity of offences and penalties and the presumption of innocence are untouchable.

## Human rights law and remedies

- The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)** and the **European Convention on Human Rights** are the key documents; Turkey accepts individual applications to the **European Court of Human Rights**.
- At home, **individual application to the Constitutional Court**, the **Ombudsman Institution** and the **Human Rights and Equality Institution of Turkey** are the remedies.
- The **Convention on the Rights of the Child** is the international instrument that secures the right to education from the child's perspective.

## Commonly confused

- What is *compulsory and free* under the Constitution is **primary education**; the Constitution does not list upper secondary education, whose compulsory status is set by law.
- *Religious culture and ethics* is a compulsory course; other religious education depends on the person's own wish, or for minors on the request of their legal representative.
- *Restriction* applies in normal times, *suspension* in extraordinary times.

## Study tip

Read Article 42 sentence by sentence and tag each sentence with a keyword (right, supervision, loyalty, compulsory and free, scholarship, special education, mother tongue). Most legislation questions rest on one of its sentences.
