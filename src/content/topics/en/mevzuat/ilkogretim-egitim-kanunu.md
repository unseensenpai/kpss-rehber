## Core idea

The Primary Education and Education Law No. 222 (adopted **5 January 1961**) sets out how **compulsory** primary education is ensured: which children attend school, how schools are set up, what parents and public officials must do, and what sanction applies to a parent who does not send a child to school. Questions mostly ask about these duties and time limits.

## Nature of primary education

- Primary education is the basic education that ensures the physical, mental and moral development of all Turkish girls and boys in line with national aims.
- It is **compulsory** for girls and boys of school age and **free in state schools** (Art. 2).
- Turkish children must complete primary education in public or private Turkish primary schools (Art. 4).

## School age and starting school

- Compulsory primary school age covers children aged **6–14** (Art. 3).
- Children who are **72 months** old by **31 December** of that year enrol in the first grade of primary school. Early starting or deferral depending on development is set by regulation.

## Setting up schools

- Primary education institutions are set up in principle as **independent primary and lower secondary schools** (2012 amendment). Depending on conditions, lower secondary schools may share a building with primary schools or high schools (Art. 9).
- Where population is small or scattered, villages are grouped and **regional primary schools** with boarding houses are opened; where that is not possible, **regional boarding schools** or **mobile schools**.
- When a village moves to summer pastures or similar places, the village school is organised to move with it (Art. 10).

## Duties and attendance

- The **parent** (guardian or head of family) must ensure the child **attends regularly** and must notify the school of an excused absence **within three days** (Art. 52).
- District governors and the police help parents and schools ensure attendance.
- For reasons such as a death, wedding, military service or farm work in the family, the school may grant up to **15 days of leave a year** (Art. 54). Students who cannot come because of illness, floods, snow, earthquakes or fires count as on leave (Art. 55).
- A parent who does not send the child to school despite notice is given an **administrative fine** for each day of absence; a parent who still does not comply faces a higher administrative fine (Art. 56).

## Ban on child labour and funding

- Children of school age who do not attend compulsory primary education **may not be employed**, paid or unpaid, anywhere. Those who prove attendance may work only outside school hours, subject to the laws on child labour (Art. 59).
- The law also lists the funding sources of primary education, such as contributions from the state budget and appropriations in provincial and village budgets (Art. 76).

## Commonly confused

- Notice of an excused absence is due within **three days**; family-related leave is up to **15 days a year**.
- *Primary education* covers primary and lower secondary school; upper secondary education is governed by Law No. 1739.
- *Free of charge* applies to state schools; private schools charge fees.

## Study tip

Collect the numbers in the law (ages 6–14, 72 months, 31 December, 3 days, 15 days) on one card. Points in legislation questions often come from these numbers.
