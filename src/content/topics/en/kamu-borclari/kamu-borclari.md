## Core idea

Public debt is the resources the state and public bodies obtain to repay later with interest. Unlike tax, borrowing is **voluntary** and **repaid**, so it spreads the cost of today's spending over time. The central debate is **who bears the burden and when**. This subject has 6 questions in the test; they measure debt types, views on the burden of debt and debt management techniques.

## Classification and instruments

- **Domestic debt** (*iç borç*) comes from sources inside the country, **external debt** (*dış borç*) from foreign sources. With domestic debt the resources stay at home; external debt sends resources abroad when it is repaid.
- **By maturity:** **floating debt** (*dalgalı borç*) is short-term, often taken to cover cash gaps; **consolidated debt** (*konsolide borç*) is long-term and often for investment.
- **Voluntary debt** relies on willing lenders; **forced debt** (*cebri borç*) is compulsory and comes close to a tax.
- **Instruments:** a **Treasury bill** (*Hazine bonosu*) is a short-term and a **government bond** (*devlet tahvili*) a longer-term domestic borrowing security. In Türkiye, borrowing is framed by **Law No. 4749**.

## The burden of debt

- **Classical view:** debt finances unproductive spending and passes the burden to future generations.
- **Keynesian view:** domestic debt is "money we owe to ourselves"; it creates no net burden for the country as a whole and boosts demand in a recession.
- **Buchanan:** the burden appears in the future, when debt is repaid through taxes.
- **Ricardian equivalence:** rational people know today's debt means tomorrow's taxes and save more, so debt and tax financing are equivalent. Its modern form is linked to **Barro**.

## Economic effects

- **Crowding out:** public borrowing can raise interest rates and reduce private investment.
- Interest payments transfer income to bondholders and affect **income distribution**.
- External debt creates a need for foreign currency at repayment and raises exchange-rate risk.
- Borrowing from the central bank increases the money supply and can be **inflationary**.

## Debt management and sustainability

- **Conversion** (*konversiyon*): replacing high-interest debt with lower-interest debt.
- **Consolidation** (*konsolidasyon*): turning short-term debt into long-term debt.
- **Moratorium:** temporarily suspending debt payments.
- **Repudiation** (*borcun reddi*): the state unilaterally refusing to pay its debt.
- **Redemption** (*itfa*): ending the debt by paying principal and interest.
- **Sustainability:** the debt-to-national-income ratio is monitored. If the interest rate exceeds the growth rate, a **primary surplus** is needed to keep the ratio stable.

## Commonly confused

- *Conversion* changes the interest rate; *consolidation* changes the maturity.
- *Floating debt* is short-term; *consolidated debt* is long-term.
- A *moratorium* postpones payment; *repudiation* refuses it entirely.

## Study tip

Sort debt management terms by asking "What does it change?": interest, maturity or payment. For burden-of-debt questions, sum up each view in one sentence; for Ricardo the key phrase is "today's debt, tomorrow's tax".
