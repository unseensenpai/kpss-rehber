## Core idea

Classical Turkish literature is the written literature that grew within Islamic civilisation and shares a common **aesthetic language** with Arabic and Persian literature. The Turkish Language and Literature ÖABT has **10 questions** on it. The tradition is built on **conventional images (*mazmun*)**; a poet's mastery shows less in finding new subjects than in saying a known image in a new way. Questions test verse forms, poet-work-century matches and prose genres.

## Works of the transition period

- ***Kutadgu Bilig*:** a mesnevi on statecraft written by Yusuf Has Hacib in Karakhanid Turkic and in aruz.
- ***Atabetü'l-Hakayık*:** Edip Ahmet's book of morals and advice.
- ***Divan-ı Hikmet*:** Ahmet Yesevi's mystical *hikmet* poems.
- ***Divanü Lugati't-Türk*:** Kaşgarlı Mahmud's dictionary; an important source for the poems and proverbs it contains.

## Verse forms

- ***Gazel*** (*aa ba ca*, lyrical; *matla*, *makta*, *beyt-i gazel* the finest couplet), ***kaside*** (praise; sections *nesip*, *girizgâh*, *methiye*, *fahriye*, *dua*), ***mesnevi*** (*aa bb cc*, long narratives; five mesnevis form a **hamse**).
- ***Rubai*** (*aaxa*), ***tuyuğ*** (*aaba*, Turkish), ***kıta***, ***şarkı***, ***murabba***.
- **Stanzaic forms:** ***terkib-i bent*** (linking couplets differ) and ***terci-i bent*** (the linking couplet repeats unchanged).

## Major poets

- **13th-15th centuries:** Ali Şir Nevai (Chagatai area), Süleyman Çelebi (*Mevlid*), Ahmedi.
- **16th century:** **Fuzuli** (*Leylâ vü Mecnûn*, *Şikâyetnâme*), **Baki** (gazels, elegy for Suleiman the Magnificent).
- **17th century:** **Nef'i** (kaside and satire), **Nabi** (didactic-philosophical poetry, *Hayriyye*).
- **18th century:** **Nedim** (şarkı, localisation), **Şeyh Galip** (*Hüsn ü Aşk*, Indian style).

## Style and prose

- **Localisation (*mahallîleşme*):** bringing local life, Istanbul and spoken language into poetry.
- **Indian style (*sebk-i Hindî*):** a style built on subtle fancies and obscure meaning.
- **Prose genres:** ***tezkire*** (biographies of poets), ***siyasetname*** (advice on government), ***seyahatname*** (travel book; Evliya Çelebi), ***münşeat*** (model letters), ***surname*** (accounts of festivities) and histories. Prose is divided into plain, middle and **ornate**.

## Commonly confused

- In a *terkib-i bent* the linking couplets change; in a *terci-i bent* the same couplet repeats.
- *Localisation* is a local, plain stance; the *Indian style* is abstract and obscure.
- A *tezkire* tells poets' lives; a *seyahatname* records travel observations.

## Study tip

Line the poets up by century and link each to one genre and one work. In verse-form questions, look first at the rhyme scheme, then at the number of couplets or quatrains.
