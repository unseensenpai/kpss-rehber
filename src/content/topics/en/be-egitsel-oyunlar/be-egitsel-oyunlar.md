## Core idea

Play is a **voluntary**, enjoyable activity with its own rules; an educational game (*eğitsel oyun*) is this natural activity planned towards **specific learning outcomes**. In play, children learn movement skills, following rules and cooperation all at once. The test has **2 questions**; they usually ask about theories of play, stages of play and choosing a suitable game.

## Theories of play

- **Surplus energy theory (Spencer):** children use up unspent extra energy through play.
- **Preparation theory (Groos):** play prepares children for skills they will need as adults.
- **Recapitulation theory (Hall):** in play, children relive earlier stages of human history.
- **Relaxation theory:** play is a means of recovering from fatigue.

## Stages

- **Piaget:** **practice (functional) play** (repeated movements), **symbolic play** (using an object as if it were something else), **games with rules**.
- **Parten's stages of social play:** unoccupied behaviour, solitary play, onlooker behaviour, **parallel play** (side by side without interaction), **associative play**, **cooperative play** (a shared goal and divided roles).

## Types and teaching

- **Types:** tag and chasing games, ball games, relay games, rhythmic and singing games, traditional children's games (*yakan top* dodgeball, hide-and-seek, *mendil kapmaca* "grab the handkerchief"), cooperative games.
- **Teaching:** explain the game briefly and clearly, demonstrate if needed, mark out a safe area and keep everyone active.
- **Inclusion:** games in which eliminated students wait outside for a long time should be adapted (for example, letting an eliminated player return quickly).

## Commonly confused

- In *parallel play* children play side by side but separately; in *cooperative play* there is a shared goal.
- *Surplus energy theory* explains play as using up energy; *preparation theory* as getting ready for adulthood.

## Study tip

Remember each theory by one keyword: Spencer "energy", Groos "preparation", Hall "the past". In game-choice questions, prefer the option that keeps the most students active for the longest time.
