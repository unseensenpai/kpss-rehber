## Core idea

Because a primary teacher teaches first reading and writing and the Turkish course, they must know Turkish **sounds, word structure and sentences** well. The Primary School Teaching ÖABT has **3 questions** on this section. The details overlap with the KPSS-level [Grammar](/kpss-rehber/en/topic/turkce/dil-bilgisi/) topic. Questions usually ask for a short analysis or a spelling-punctuation correction.

## Sounds and structure

- **Structure of Turkish:** an **agglutinative** language; suffixes are added after the root, which usually stays unchanged.
- **Vowels and harmony:** major and minor vowel harmony; consonant assimilation.
- **Sound changes:** consonant softening, vowel loss, vowel narrowing, buffer consonants.
- **Suffixes:** **derivational suffixes** form new words, **inflectional suffixes** add grammatical roles.
- **Parts of speech:** noun, adjective, pronoun, adverb, postposition, conjunction, interjection, verb; plus **verbals** (verbal nouns, participles, converbs).

## The sentence

- **Elements:** predicate, subject, object, indirect complement, adverbial complement. Analysis always starts from the predicate.
- **Sentence types:** by structure (simple, complex, compound), predicate type (nominal or verbal), word order (regular or inverted) and meaning (affirmative, negative, interrogative).

## Spelling and punctuation

- **Spelling:** the conjunction *de/da*, *ki* and the question particle *mi* are written separately; compound words, capital letters and writing numbers.
- **Punctuation:** full stop, comma, semicolon, colon, question and exclamation marks, the apostrophe (before suffixes on proper names).

## Commonly confused

- The conjunction *de* is written separately; the case suffix *-de* is attached.
- A *derivational suffix* forms a new word; an *inflectional suffix* does not change the word's meaning.

## Study tip

In spelling questions, try removing the *de*: if the sentence still makes sense, it is the conjunction and is written separately.
