## Core idea

This section tests the **conceptual basis** of the mathematics a primary teacher will teach. The Primary School Teaching ÖABT has **3 questions** on it. Being able to do an operation is not enough; a teacher must explain **why** it works. Questions usually check number and operation properties, fractions, and basic geometry and measurement through short problems.

## Numbers and operations

- **Place value:** the value a digit takes from its position (in 345, the place value of 4 is 40); **face value** is the digit itself.
- **Properties:** **commutative** and **associative** properties of addition and multiplication; the **distributive** property of multiplication over addition; 0 is the identity for addition and 1 for multiplication; 0 is the absorbing element in multiplication. Subtraction and division are not commutative.
- **Divisibility:** rules for 2, 3, 5, 9 and 10; primes, factors and multiples.

## Fractions and decimals

- **Fraction:** shows equal parts of a whole; numerator and denominator. **Equivalent fractions** show the same amount (1/2 = 2/4).
- **Comparing:** with equal denominators, the larger numerator is bigger; with equal numerators, the smaller denominator is bigger.
- **Decimals and percentages:** other ways of writing fractions with denominators 10, 100, 1000 (0.25 = 25/100 = 25%).

## Geometry and measurement

- **Shapes and solids:** triangle, square, rectangle, circle; cube, prism, cylinder, cone, sphere. A line of **symmetry** splits a shape into two matching halves.
- **Perimeter and area:** perimeter is the sum of side lengths, area the size of the surface covered; a rectangle's area is length × width.
- **Measurement:** first **non-standard** units (hand spans, steps), then standard units; conversion between units of length, mass, liquid volume, time and money.
- **Data:** frequency tables, pictographs and bar charts.

## Commonly confused

- *Place value* depends on position; *face value* is the digit itself.
- *Perimeter* is measured in units of length, *area* in square units.

## Study tip

For every rule, think of a model: "How would I show this to a primary pupil?" A rule you can model concretely will not trip you up in a question.
