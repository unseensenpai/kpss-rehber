## Core idea

Earth science studies the planet's structure and the processes that shape it: **internal forces** (plate motion, earthquakes, volcanism) build landforms, while **external forces** (rivers, wind, glaciers, waves) wear them down and deposit material. The Science test has **2 questions** here, usually on rock types, plate boundaries and basic earthquake concepts.

## Structure and rocks

- **Earth's layers:** crust (continental and oceanic), mantle, outer core (liquid) and inner core (solid).
- A **mineral** is a natural, inorganic crystalline solid with a definite chemical composition; rocks are made of minerals.
- **Igneous rock:** forms as magma cools; slow cooling at depth gives coarse crystals (granite), fast cooling at the surface gives fine crystals (basalt).
- **Sedimentary rock:** forms by deposition and compaction (sandstone, limestone); most fossils are found here.
- **Metamorphic rock:** altered by high temperature and pressure (limestone → marble).
- **Rock cycle:** under the right conditions any rock type can turn into another.

## Tectonics and external processes

- **Plate boundaries:** divergent boundaries form mid-ocean ridges; convergent boundaries form mountain belts, trenches and volcanoes; transform boundaries form strike-slip faults.
- **Earthquake:** the sudden release of energy built up along a fault; the **focus** is the starting point underground, the **epicentre** is the point on the surface directly above it.
- **External processes:** weathering breaks rock, erosion carries it away, deposition builds new landforms.

## Commonly confused

- The *focus* is underground; the *epicentre* is on the surface.
- A *mineral* is a single natural compound; a *rock* is an aggregate of minerals.

## Study tip

Draw the rock cycle as a diagram and label each arrow with its process (cooling, compaction, heat and pressure, melting); most questions can be solved from that diagram.
