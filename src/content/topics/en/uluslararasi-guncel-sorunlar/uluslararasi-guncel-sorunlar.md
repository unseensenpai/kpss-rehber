## Core idea

This subject covers the **transnational problems** that came to the fore after the Cold War and that no single state can solve. Security is no longer only military; it also has economic, environmental and **human** dimensions. The test has **4 questions** on this subject; they usually ask you to match a concept or convention with the issue it concerns. Developments change fast, so complete this framework with current sources.

## Globalisation and wider security

- **Globalisation:** growing movement of trade, capital, information and people; stronger non-state actors.
- **Human security:** shifts the focus of security from the state to the individual; stresses freedom from fear and freedom from want.
- **Responsibility to protect (R2P):** if a state fails to protect its people from genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing and crimes against humanity, the international community takes on responsibility; endorsed at the 2005 UN World Summit.
- **Failed state:** a state that cannot provide basic public services and security across its territory.

## Security threats

- **Terrorism:** there is no single universally accepted definition; it moved to the centre of the global agenda after the attacks of 11 September 2001.
- **Nuclear proliferation:** the **NPT (1968)** rests on a distinction between nuclear-weapon and non-nuclear-weapon states; its three pillars are disarmament, non-proliferation and peaceful use.
- **Cyber security and hybrid warfare:** combined use of conventional and unconventional methods.

## Migration, environment and energy

- **Refugee law:** the **1951 Geneva Convention** and its **1967 Protocol**. The **non-refoulement** principle bars returning a person to a country where they face persecution.
- **Climate change:** the **UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (1992)**, the **Kyoto Protocol (1997)** and the **Paris Agreement (2015)**. Kyoto set binding targets only for developed countries; under Paris every party sets its own nationally determined contribution.
- **Sustainable development:** the **Sustainable Development Goals** adopted by the UN in 2015.
- **Energy security:** security of supply, diversification of sources and pipeline routes.

## Regional conflicts

- **Middle East:** the Israeli-Palestinian question, the Syrian civil war from 2011 and the migration wave it caused.
- **Russia-Ukraine:** Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014 and the war that began in 2022 deeply affected the European security order.
- Always follow the latest state of these conflicts in current sources.

## Commonly confused

- A *refugee* flees their country for fear of persecution; an *irregular migrant* crosses a border without legal permission.
- *Kyoto* set binding targets only for developed countries; *Paris* relies on every party's national contribution.
- *Humanitarian intervention* is a contested practice; the *responsibility to protect* is the norm that ties it to the UN framework.

## Study tip

Match each issue with a concept, a convention and an example (migration: non-refoulement, the 1951 Convention, Syrian refugees). Current events may change before the exam, so follow the news and official announcements regularly.
