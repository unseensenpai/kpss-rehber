## Core idea

Human and animal biology explains how body systems work together to maintain **homeostasis** (a stable internal environment). Most control works by **negative feedback**: when a value drifts from normal, a response starts that corrects the drift. This is the heaviest subject in the Biology test, with **14 questions**. Questions focus on matching hormones and structures to functions, nerve conduction, muscle contraction, how the nephron works, and immunity.

## Control systems

- **Neurons and the action potential:** at rest the inside of the membrane is negative relative to the outside; on stimulation Na⁺ channels open and Na⁺ flows in (depolarisation), then K⁺ flows out (repolarisation). **Myelin** speeds conduction (saltatory conduction).
- **Synapse:** a neurotransmitter (for example acetylcholine) released from the axon terminal binds receptors on the next cell; transmission is one-way.
- **Reflex arc:** receptor → sensory neuron → interneuron (spinal cord) → motor neuron → effector.
- **Autonomic nervous system:** the sympathetic division drives the "fight or flight" response; the parasympathetic division supports rest and digestion.
- **Endocrine system:** the hypothalamus controls the pituitary; the anterior pituitary secretes TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, growth hormone and prolactin; the posterior pituitary stores and releases ADH and oxytocin.
- **Blood sugar:** insulin lowers blood glucose, glucagon raises it (pancreas). **ADH** increases water reabsorption in the kidney.

## Movement, digestion, circulation and respiration

- **Muscle contraction (sliding filament model):** when Ca²⁺ is released, myosin heads bind actin and, using ATP, pull the actin filaments toward the centre of the sarcomere; the sarcomere shortens while the filaments keep their length.
- **Digestion:** amylase starts breaking down starch in the mouth, pepsin starts on proteins in the stomach; fats are broken into small droplets by bile in the small intestine (emulsification) and digested by lipase. Most absorption happens across the villi of the small intestine.
- **Circulation:** the right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs, the left ventricle to the body. The heartbeat starts at the sinoatrial (SA) node.
- **Respiration:** O₂ is carried in blood mostly bound to haemoglobin; CO₂ is carried mostly as bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻). Inhalation happens as the diaphragm and intercostal muscles contract.
- **Comparison across animals:** fish have gills and single circulation, birds have air sacs and a four-chambered heart, insects have a tracheal system and open circulation.

## Excretion, immunity and reproduction

- **Nephron:** filtration in the glomerulus, reabsorption and secretion in the tubules; the loop of Henle helps concentrate urine.
- **Innate immunity** is non-specific (skin, phagocytosis, inflammation); **adaptive immunity** is specific: B lymphocytes make antibodies (humoral), T lymphocytes target infected cells (cell-mediated). A **vaccine** gives active immunity by creating memory cells.
- **Menstrual cycle:** FSH stimulates follicle development; an LH surge triggers ovulation; the corpus luteum secretes progesterone.
- **Embryonic development:** zygote → cleavage (morula, blastula) → **gastrulation** forming three germ layers: ectoderm (nervous system, skin), mesoderm (muscle, skeleton, circulation), endoderm (lining of the gut, lungs).

## Commonly confused

- The *sympathetic* division speeds the heart and slows digestion; the *parasympathetic* division does the opposite.
- *Insulin* lowers blood sugar and *glucagon* raises it; both come from the pancreas.
- *Active immunity* is the body making its own antibodies (vaccine, illness); *passive immunity* is receiving ready-made antibodies (breast milk, serum).

## Study tip

Study every system as a "stimulus → sensor → control centre → response" feedback diagram; for hormones, keep a three-column table of gland, target organ and effect.
