## Core idea

Enforcement and bankruptcy law governs how an unpaid claim is collected with the power of the state. The Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (*İİK*, Law 2004) has two main routes: **attachment** (*haciz yolu*) turns specific assets of the debtor into money for an individual creditor; **bankruptcy** (*iflas yolu*) liquidates the debtor's entire estate for all creditors. The test has 6 questions; they usually ask about the right enforcement route, the effects of objections and complaints, and the duties of the enforcement court.

## Organs and complaint

- **Enforcement office** (*icra dairesi*): runs the proceedings, sends the payment order, carries out attachment and sale.
- **Enforcement court** (*icra mahkemesi*): hears complaints, decides requests to remove objections and handles certain actions specific to enforcement law.
- **Complaint** (*şikâyet*): an application to the enforcement court against an act of the enforcement or bankruptcy office that is unlawful or unsuited to the facts, or against its unjustified failure to act. Where public order is breached there is no time limit.

## Enforcement routes

- **Enforcement without judgment** (*ilamsız takip*, general attachment): a creditor without a court judgment starts proceedings and a **payment order** (*ödeme emri*) is sent to the debtor. If the debtor **objects** (*itiraz*) in time, the proceedings stop.
- **Action to annul the objection** (*itirazın iptali*): the creditor sues in a general court; the existence of the claim is fully examined.
- **Removal of the objection** (*itirazın kaldırılması*): the creditor applies to the enforcement court on the basis of a document; review is limited to the document. A judgment or equivalent document leads to **final** removal; a document whose signature is acknowledged or notarised leads to **provisional** removal.
- **Enforcement based on judgment** (*ilamlı takip*): based on a court judgment; an **enforcement order** (*icra emri*) is sent. The debtor cannot object on the merits but may ask for a stay on limited grounds (for example that the debt was paid).
- **Special attachment for bills:** a fast route based on a bill of exchange, promissory note or cheque, with shorter time limits and objections made to the enforcement court.
- **Realisation of a pledge** (*rehnin paraya çevrilmesi*): for a secured claim, the pledged asset is sold first.

## Attachment and distribution

- **Attachment** (*haciz*): seizing the debtor's assets. Certain assets needed for the essential needs of the debtor and their family are **exempt from attachment**.
- **Sale:** attached assets are sold by auction.
- **Distribution:** with several creditors, the proceeds are distributed according to a **ranking table** (*sıra cetveli*).
- **Precautionary attachment** (*ihtiyati haciz*): a provisional attachment ordered by a court for a due, unsecured claim to safeguard later enforcement.
- **Avoidance action** (*tasarrufun iptali*): an action that makes gifts and similar dispositions made by the debtor to harm creditors ineffective against the creditor.

## Bankruptcy and composition

- **Who can go bankrupt:** as a rule **merchants**; non-merchants can be pursued only by attachment.
- **Bankruptcy routes:** ordinary bankruptcy, bankruptcy based on bills and, in certain cases, **direct bankruptcy**.
- **Bankruptcy estate** (*iflas masası*): once bankruptcy opens, all of the debtor's attachable assets form the estate, which is shared among the creditors.
- **Composition** (*konkordato*): a route by which a debtor in financial difficulty restructures debts with the approval of a set majority of creditors and confirmation by the court.

## Commonly confused

- A *complaint* is against an act of the enforcement office; an *objection* is against the debt itself or the signature.
- An *action to annul the objection* is filed in a general court and examines the claim; *removal of the objection* is a limited, document-based review in the enforcement court.
- A *payment order* is sent in enforcement without judgment; an *enforcement order* in enforcement based on judgment.

## Study tip

In every question, first look at what the creditor holds (a judgment, a bill, a pledge or nothing) and match it to the right route. Always check the time limits for objections and complaints in the current İİK on mevzuat.gov.tr.
