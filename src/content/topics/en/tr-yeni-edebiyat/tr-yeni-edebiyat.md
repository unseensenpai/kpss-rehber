## Core idea

Modern Turkish literature is the **Western-influenced** literature that began with the Tanzimat reforms. The Turkish ÖABT has **5 questions** on it. The logic of the topic is a chain of periods: each period reacts to the one before and takes a clear stance on language, themes and art. Questions usually ask you to place a writer or work in the right period.

## From Tanzimat to National Literature

- **Tanzimat (first generation):** "art for society"; new genres such as the newspaper, drama and the novel. Şinasi's *Şair Evlenmesi* (The Poet's Marriage) is the first Turkish play in the Western style; Namık Kemal wrote *İntibah* (Awakening) and *Vatan yahut Silistre* (Homeland or Silistra); Ziya Paşa also belongs here.
- **Tanzimat (second generation):** "art for art's sake"; Recaizade Mahmut Ekrem (*Araba Sevdası*, The Carriage Affair), Abdülhak Hâmit, Sami Paşazade Sezai (*Sergüzeşt*, The Adventure).
- ***Servet-i Fünun*:** heavy language loaded with Arabic and Persian, individual and pessimistic themes. Tevfik Fikret leads in poetry, Halit Ziya (*Mai ve Siyah*, Blue and Black; *Aşk-ı Memnu*, Forbidden Love) in the novel.
- ***Fecr-i Âti*:** "art is personal and to be respected"; its key name is Ahmet Haşim.
- **National Literature:** plain Turkish, syllabic metre, national themes. Ömer Seyfettin in the short story, Ziya Gökalp in ideas, Mehmet Âkif with *Safahat* in poetry.

## The Republican period

- **Poetry:** the **Five Syllabists** keep syllabic metre; Yahya Kemal creates a new voice in aruz; Nazım Hikmet establishes **free verse**.
- ***Garip*:** Orhan Veli, Oktay Rifat and Melih Cevdet; poetry against metre, rhyme and figures of speech, about ordinary people.
- **Second New (*İkinci Yeni*):** Cemal Süreya, Edip Cansever, Turgut Uyar; image-dense, hermetic poetry.
- **Novel and story:** Yakup Kadri (*Yaban*, The Stranger), Reşat Nuri (*Çalıkuşu*, The Wren), Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar (*Huzur*, A Mind at Peace), Sait Faik in the short story.

## Commonly confused

- The *first Tanzimat generation* writes for society, the *second* for art.
- *Garip* stresses plainness and ordinary people; the *Second New* stresses images and hermetic meaning.

## Study tip

For each period, write "language, metre, themes, representatives" on one line. When you see a title, think first of its period, then of that period's features.
