## Core idea

This subject covers students who struggle in specific areas despite normal or above-normal intelligence (learning disability, *öğrenme güçlüğü*) and students who are clearly ahead of their peers (giftedness, *özel yetenek*). There are **3 questions**. Questions mostly ask you to identify the type of difficulty from a case or to choose a suitable educational arrangement.

## Learning disability

- **Specific learning disability:** performing well below expectations in reading, writing or mathematics although intelligence, educational opportunity and sensory functions are adequate.
- **Types:** **dyslexia** (reading, word recognition), **dysgraphia** (writing), **dyscalculia** (mathematics).
- **Exclusion criterion:** the difficulty must not be explained by vision or hearing loss, intellectual disability or inadequate teaching.
- **Identification:** the **ability-achievement discrepancy** model looks at the gap between IQ and achievement and is criticised as "waiting to fail"; **response to intervention** gives tiered, increasingly intensive support and monitors the student's response to it.
- **Teaching:** explicit and systematic instruction, **strategy instruction**, phonological awareness work, multisensory teaching.

## Giftedness

- **Renzulli's three-ring model:** the overlap of above-average ability, **task commitment** and **creativity**.
- **Characteristics:** fast learning, deep curiosity, strong verbal skills; sometimes asynchronous development and perfectionism.
- **Enrichment:** deepening and broadening the curriculum (projects, independent study).
- **Acceleration:** moving through the curriculum faster (early school entry, grade skipping, taking one subject with a higher grade).
- In Türkiye, out-of-school support for gifted students is given at **Science and Art Centres** (*Bilim ve Sanat Merkezleri*, BİLSEM).

## Commonly confused

- A student with a *learning disability* has normal intelligence; in *intellectual disability* intellectual functioning is limited.
- *Enrichment* deepens content; *acceleration* increases the pace.
- A *twice-exceptional* student is both gifted and has a disability; one can mask the other.

## Study tip

In a case question, check the intelligence level first: if it is normal and one area is clearly low, think learning disability; if the student is ahead overall, think giftedness.
