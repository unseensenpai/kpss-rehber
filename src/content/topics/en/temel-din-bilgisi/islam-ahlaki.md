## Core idea

The Turkish word *ahlak* (ethics, morals) is the Arabic plural of *hulk* (character, disposition) and refers to settled tendencies that show themselves easily, without deliberation. Islamic ethics sees these tendencies as **bound up with faith and worship**: in the Qur'an, faith is very often mentioned together with righteous deeds. The aim is for a person to develop a good, balanced character pleasing to God. Questions usually give a short example or the meaning of a verse and ask which concept it relates to.

## Sources and aims

- The **Qur'an** is the primary source of ethics; the **Sunnah** shows it in practice. Reason and the human nature that recognises the good (*fıtrat*) are seen as supporting sources.
- The **Prophet Muhammad** is the model. Sura al-Qalam, verse 4, states that he has a sublime character, and Sura al-Ahzab, verse 21, calls him a fine example (*üsve-i hasene*) for believers. In a well-known hadith he says he was sent to perfect good character.
- **Classical works on ethics** explain the main virtues as the middle way between excess and deficiency: *hikmet* (wisdom), *şecaat* (courage), *iffet* (chastity, self-restraint) and *adalet* (justice).

## Key moral concepts

- ***Takva*** (God-consciousness): the awareness that leads one to follow God's commands and prohibitions with reverence and to guard against wrong.
- ***İhsan*** (excellence): doing things in the best way; the Gabriel hadith defines it as worshipping God as if you see Him. It also means doing good to people.
- ***Adalet*** (justice): giving everyone their due, favouring no one, even relatives.
- ***Emanet*** (trust): being trustworthy; property, words, duties and secrets are all trusts.
- ***Sıdk*** (truthfulness): honesty in word, intention and action. ***İhlas*** (sincerity): seeking only God's pleasure; its opposite is ***riya*** (showing off).

## Virtues and vices

- **Virtues:** *sabır* (patience), *şükür* (gratitude), *tevazu* (humility), *haya* (modesty, a sense of shame), *iffet*, generosity, mercy, forgiveness.
- **Vices:** *kibir* (arrogance), *haset* (envy), lying, *gıybet* (backbiting), *suizan* (assuming the worst of others), *israf* (wastefulness), miserliness.
- Sura al-Hujurat, verse 12, forbids suspicion, spying on others' faults and backbiting.

## Duties

- **To oneself:** protecting the body and health, keeping the mind away from what harms it, seeking knowledge. Life is a trust from God.
- **To the family:** kindness to parents; Sura al-Isra, verse 23, asks that one not say even "uff" to them in their old age. Love and mercy between spouses, and raising children well.
- **To society:** the rights of neighbours, mutual help, keeping one's word, respecting the **rights of others** (*kul hakkı*). Such a right is not cancelled by repentance alone unless its owner forgives it.
- **To the environment:** humans are trustees on earth; avoiding waste, showing mercy to living things and protecting nature are moral duties.

## Commonly confused

- *İhlas* is doing something for God alone; *ihsan* is doing it in the best possible way.
- *Haset* is wishing that someone else lose a blessing; *gıpta* is wishing to have the same blessing without wanting them to lose it.
- *Gıybet* is mentioning a fault a person really has behind their back; *iftira* (slander) is attributing a fault they do not have.

## Study tip

Think of an everyday example for each concept: keeping a secret for emanet, doing your work carefully for ihsan, not boasting about success for tevazu. Linking the scene in a question to a concept gets you to the answer quickly.
