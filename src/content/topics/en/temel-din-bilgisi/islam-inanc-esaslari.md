## Core idea

The heart of Islamic belief is faith in the six pillars listed in the *Amentü* (creed): God, angels, books, prophets, the Last Day, and destiny (*kader* and *kaza*). For Sunni theology, *iman* (faith) is **affirming** these pillars with the heart (*tasdik*) and **declaring** them with the tongue (*ikrar*). Questions usually give a definition and ask for the term (especially for God's attributes, prophets' attributes and the stages of the hereafter) or compare the *Maturidi* and *Eş'ari* (Ash'ari) schools.

## Belief in God and the attributes

- **Essential attributes** (*zati* or *selbi sıfatlar*) belong to God alone and have no likeness in creation: *vücud* (existence), *kıdem* (having no beginning), *beka* (having no end), *vahdaniyet* (oneness), *muhalefetün li'l-havadis* (being unlike created things), *kıyam bi-nefsihi* (self-subsistence).
- **Affirmative attributes** (*subuti sıfatlar*) have a limited reflection in humans: *hayat* (life), *ilim* (knowledge), *semi* (hearing), *basar* (sight), *irade* (will), *kudret* (power), *kelam* (speech) and *tekvin* (bringing into being).
- **Tekvin:** the Maturidis count it as a separate affirmative attribute (eight in total); the Eş'aris treat it as an outcome of power and will, so they count seven.
- **Attributes of action** (*fiili sıfatlar*) are expressions of tekvin: creating, providing, giving life, causing death.

## Angels, books and prophets

- **Angels** are beings created from light who never disobey God. The four great angels: *Cebrail* (Gabriel, revelation), *Mikail* (Michael, natural events), *İsrafil* (blowing the trumpet), *Azrail* (taking souls). The *kiramen katibin* record deeds; *Münker and Nekir* question the dead in the grave.
- **Books:** the *Tevrat* (Torah) to Moses, the *Zebur* (Psalms) to David, the *İncil* (Gospel) to Jesus, the *Qur'an* to Muhammad; some prophets received shorter scrolls (*suhuf*).
- **Attributes of prophets:** *sıdk* (truthfulness), *emanet* (trustworthiness), *ismet* (protection from sin), *fetanet* (superior intelligence), *tebliğ* (conveying revelation in full).
- A **miracle** (*mucize*) is an extraordinary event at a prophet's hands confirming prophethood; the Prophet Muhammad's greatest miracle is the Qur'an.

## The hereafter and destiny

- **Stages of the hereafter:** death, life in the *grave* (*berzah*, barzakh), the *kıyamet* (end of the world), *ba's* (resurrection), *haşir* (gathering), *hesap* (reckoning), *mizan* (weighing of deeds), *sırat* (the bridge), then paradise or hell. Sunni theology also accepts *şefaat* (intercession).
- **Kader** is God's eternal knowledge and determination of all that will happen; **kaza** is its realisation, when the time comes, in line with that knowledge.
- **Cüz'i irade** (partial, human will) is the person's power to choose and the basis of responsibility; **külli irade** is God's universal will. Destiny does not compel anyone, because God's knowledge is not the cause of an event.

## Faith and deeds; shirk, kufr, nifaq

- In the **Hanefi-Maturidi** view, deeds are not part of faith and faith does not increase or decrease; a believer who commits a major sin does not leave the faith.
- For the **hadith scholars and many Eş'aris**, deeds belong to the perfection of faith, and faith increases and decreases. In this view too, a major sin does not take a person out of faith.
- **Şirk** (shirk) is associating partners with God, **küfür** (kufr) is denying the pillars of faith, **nifak** (nifaq) is appearing to believe without believing (the *münafık*, hypocrite).

## Commonly confused

- *Kader* is the eternal knowledge and decree; *kaza* is that decree taking place when its time comes.
- *Essential* attributes have no likeness at all in creation; *affirmative* attributes have a limited counterpart in humans.
- *Kıdem* means having no beginning; *beka* means having no end.

## Study tip

Memorise the six essential attributes and the five prophetic attributes as short lists; questions often give a definition and ask "which attribute is this?". Write the stages of the hereafter in order, like a timeline.
