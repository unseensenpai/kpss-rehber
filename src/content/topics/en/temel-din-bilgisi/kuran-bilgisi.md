**Official scope:** History of the Qur’an, tajwid, understanding the Qur’an and translation.

## Core idea

Qur’anic Studies answers three questions: how the Qur’an was revealed (*vahiy* and *nüzul*), how it was preserved and gathered (*cem* and *istinsah*), and how it is recited and understood correctly (*tecvid* and *meal*). Questions usually ask you to match an event with the right caliph, to tell Meccan from Medinan verses, or to identify the tajwid rule in a recitation example. Verses and translations in the exam come from the Türkiye Diyanet Vakfı translation.

## Revelation and descent

- **Vahiy (revelation):** God conveying His message to a prophet in a special way. The Qur’an is revelation brought by the angel Gabriel (*Cebrail*).
- **Forms:** true dreams, the angel inspiring the heart unseen, the angel coming in human form, revelation arriving like the ringing of a bell, and speech from behind a veil.
- **First revelation:** began in the cave of Hira, in the month of Ramadan, with the first five verses of Sura al-Alaq.
- **Nüzul (descent):** the Qur’an was revealed in parts over about 23 years, in response to events and needs.
- **Meccan and Medinan:** by the most common criterion, verses revealed before the Hijra are Meccan and those after are Medinan. Meccan verses mostly treat faith, God’s oneness (*tevhid*), the hereafter and stories of prophets; Medinan verses treat details of worship, law, social order and relations with the People of the Book and the hypocrites.

## Compilation and copying

- **The Prophet’s time:** verses were both memorised and written down by scribes of revelation; the order within suras was set by the Prophet.
- **Abu Bakr (*cem*, compilation):** after many memorisers were martyred at the Battle of Yamama, on Umar’s suggestion a committee led by Zayd ibn Thabit gathered the Qur’an into a single **mushaf** (written copy).
- **Uthman (*istinsah*, copying):** when differences in recitation appeared in the expanding lands, copies made from this mushaf were sent to major centres to unify recitation.

## Suras and verses

- **Sura (chapter):** a section with a clear beginning and end; the Qur’an has 114 suras. The first is al-Fatiha, the longest is al-Baqara.
- **Ayah (verse):** the smallest unit within a sura. Sura al-Tawba is the only sura that does not begin with the *besmele* (basmala).

## Basic tajwid rules

- **Med (lengthening):** prolonging the sound with the letters of lengthening (alif, waw, ya). With no extra cause it is *med-i tabii* (one alif’s length); when a hamza or sukun follows, it is the longer *med-i fer’i*.
- **İzhar (clear pronunciation):** when a throat letter follows a silent nun or tanwin, the nun is pronounced clearly, without nasalisation.
- **İdgam (merging):** when one of the *yermelun* letters follows, the nun merges into the next letter; with ya, nun, mim and waw it is nasalised (*maa’l-ğunne*), with lam and ra it is not (*bila ğunne*).
- **İklab (conversion):** when ba follows, the nun is turned into a mim.
- **İhfa (concealment):** when any remaining letter follows, the nun is concealed and nasalised.
- **Kalkale (echoing):** the letters qaf, ta, ba, jim and dal are pronounced with a bounce when they carry sukun.

## Translation and inimitability

- **Meal (translation):** conveying the meaning of the Qur’an into another language; it is an effort to understand, not the Qur’an itself.
- **Principles of understanding:** read a verse with its context (*siyak-sibak*), related verses and the occasion of revelation.
- **İ’caz (inimitability):** nothing like the Qur’an can be produced; the Qur’an itself issues this challenge (al-Isra 88).

## Commonly confused

- *Cem* is gathering the Qur’an into one mushaf under Abu Bakr; *istinsah* is copying that mushaf under Uthman.
- *İhfa* conceals the nun with nasalisation; *iklab* turns the nun into a mim before ba.
- The *Meccan*/*Medinan* distinction is based, in the most common view, on the Hijra, not on place.

## Study tip

Build a table by the letter that follows a silent nun or tanwin: throat letter is izhar, yermelun is idgam, ba is iklab, everything else is ihfa. For Qur’anic history, write the caliph’s name next to each event; questions often test exactly that match.
