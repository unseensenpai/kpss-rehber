## Core idea

International economics studies **why countries trade**, how the gains from trade are shared, and how exchange rates and the balance of payments affect the economy. The core idea is that countries can all gain by specialising in the good with the **lower opportunity cost**. The test has **4 questions**; they usually cover comparative advantage calculations, the content of theorems and the effects of exchange-rate policy.

## Trade theories

- **Absolute advantage (Smith):** a country specialises in a good it produces **with less labour** than another country.
- **Comparative advantage (Ricardo):** even if a country is better at both goods, it should specialise where it is **relatively better**; **opportunity cost** decides.
- **Heckscher-Ohlin:** a country exports the good that uses its **abundant factor** intensively (labour-abundant countries export labour-intensive goods).
- **Leontief paradox:** US exports turned out to be labour-intensive, contrary to expectations.
- **Stolper-Samuelson:** when a good's relative price rises, the real return of the factor used intensively in it rises.
- **Factor price equalisation:** free trade pushes factor prices across countries towards equality.
- **Rybczynski:** when the amount of one factor rises, output of the good that uses it intensively rises and output of the other falls.
- **New theories:** **economies of scale** and product differentiation explain **intra-industry trade**; **Linder** stresses similar demand structures, **Vernon** the product life cycle.

## Trade policy and integration

- **Tariff:** raises the domestic price; producers gain, consumers lose, the government earns revenue; for a small country the net effect is a **welfare loss**.
- A **quota** limits quantity; an **export subsidy** supports exports; **dumping** is selling abroad below the domestic price.
- **Stages of integration:** **free trade area** (tariffs among members removed) → **customs union** (+ common external tariff) → **common market** (+ free factor movement) → **economic union** (+ policy harmonisation).
- **Viner:** a customs union **creates trade** if it shifts purchases to an efficient member and **diverts trade** if it shifts them away from an efficient third country.
- The customs union between Türkiye and the EU entered into force in 1996.

## Balance of payments and exchange rates

- **Balance of payments** (*ödemeler dengesi*): the **current account** (goods, services, primary and secondary income), the capital account, the **financial account** and net errors and omissions.
- Under a **fixed rate** the central bank defends the rate; under a **floating rate** the market sets it.
- **Marshall-Lerner condition:** if the sum of export and import demand elasticities is above 1, a devaluation improves the external balance.
- **J-curve:** after a devaluation the external balance first worsens, then improves.
- **Purchasing power parity:** in the long run exchange rates reflect price-level differences; **interest parity** says the interest gap equals the expected change in the exchange rate.
- **Mundell-Fleming:** with free capital movement, **fiscal** policy is effective under fixed rates and **monetary** policy under floating rates.
- **Impossible trinity:** a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy cannot all hold **at the same time**.

## Commonly confused

- *Absolute advantage* looks at absolute labour cost; *comparative advantage* at opportunity cost.
- A *customs union* has a common external tariff; a *free trade area* does not.
- *Trade creation* raises welfare; *trade diversion* may lower it.

## Study tip

In comparative advantage questions, write each country's opportunity cost for both goods in a small table and mark the lower one. Study the theorems by pairing each author's name with a one-sentence result.
