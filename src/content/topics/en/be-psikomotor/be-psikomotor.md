## Core idea

Psychomotor development (*psikomotor gelişim*) is the orderly development of movement skills from birth through the interaction of **maturation and experience**. The order is universal, but the pace **differs from person to person**. The test has **3 questions**; they usually ask for a movement's category, a principle of development or the phase a child is in.

## Concepts and principles

- **Growth** is quantitative increase (height, weight), **development** is the whole of qualitative and quantitative change, and **maturation** is biological readiness set by heredity.
- **Cephalocaudal:** development runs from head to feet (head control first, walking later).
- **Proximodistal:** development runs from the centre outwards (arm and shoulder first, fingers later).
- **General to specific:** gross (large-muscle) movements develop before fine (small-muscle) movements.

## Gallahue's phases

- **Reflexive movement phase:** involuntary responses; primitive reflexes such as sucking, palmar grasp and Moro.
- **Rudimentary movement phase:** first voluntary movements; sitting, crawling, walking, reaching and grasping.
- **Fundamental movement phase:** preschool and early primary years; it has **initial, elementary and mature** stages.
- **Specialised (sport-related) movement phase:** fundamental movements applied to sports and daily activities.

## Fundamental movement skills

- **Locomotor:** walking, running, jumping, hopping, sliding, galloping.
- **Object control (manipulative):** throwing, catching, kicking, striking, dribbling.
- **Stability (non-locomotor):** bending, turning, twisting, standing on one foot.
- A child who lacks fundamental skills struggles to move on to sport skills, so primary physical education should focus on fundamental skills.

## Commonly confused

- *Growth* is quantitative increase; *development* is the whole, including qualitative change.
- *Cephalocaudal* means head to feet; *proximodistal* means centre to extremities.
- In *stability* movements the body stays in place; in *locomotor* movements it travels from one point to another.

## Study tip

To classify a movement, ask "does the body travel, and is there an object?" For phase questions, look not at age but at whether the movement is voluntary and how mature it is.
