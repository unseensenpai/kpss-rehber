## Core idea

This subject traces the move from the **feudal, church-centred** order of the Middle Ages to the 19th-century world of **industrialised nation states**. Each major break (discoveries, Reformation, revolutions, industrialisation) results from the one before and prepares the next. The test has 7 questions; they usually ask about the results of developments, matching thinkers with ideas, and the effects of revolutions.

## Medieval Europe

- **Feudalism:** an order with weak central authority, where land was run through the relationship of **lord**, **vassal** and **serf** bound to the land.
- The **Church** was strong in religion, politics and the economy; popes and kings struggled over authority.
- The **Crusades** (from 1096) revived trade with the East and the towns, and weakened feudal nobles.
- **Magna Carta (1215):** a document limiting the power of the English king.
- The **Black Death** (14th-century plague) reduced the workforce and shook the feudal order.

## Discovery, Renaissance and Reformation

- **Age of Discovery:** **Columbus (1492)** reached the Americas; **Vasco da Gama** sailed round the Cape of Good Hope to India; **Magellan**'s expedition showed the world could be circumnavigated. Results: trade routes shifted to the Atlantic, colonialism, flows of precious metals.
- **Renaissance:** an art and thought movement starting in Italy that put people at the centre through **humanism**.
- **Reformation:** **Luther (1517)** criticised the Catholic Church; Calvinism and the Anglican Church followed. The religious wars ended with the **Peace of Westphalia (1648)**, which strengthened the system of sovereign states.

## From absolutism to revolution

- **Absolutism:** the example of **Louis XIV** in France; a central army and bureaucracy.
- **Scientific Revolution:** Copernicus, Galileo, Newton; a method based on observation and experiment.
- **Enlightenment:** **Locke** natural rights, **Montesquieu** separation of powers, **Rousseau** the social contract and general will, **Voltaire** freedom of thought.
- **England:** the **Glorious Revolution (1688)** and the **Bill of Rights (1689)** created a constitutional monarchy.
- The **American Declaration of Independence (1776)** and the **French Revolution (1789):** the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen; ideas of nationalism, equality and liberty spread. After the **Napoleonic** wars the **Congress of Vienna (1815)** tried to preserve the old order; the revolutions of **1830** and **1848** shook it.

## Industry and imperialism

- **Industrial Revolution:** steam power and factory production in Britain; a working class, urbanisation and the search for raw materials and markets.
- **Ideologies:** liberalism, nationalism, socialism.
- **Nation states:** **Italy** and **Germany (1871)** completed their unification.
- **Imperialism:** the partition of Africa and Asia; **Meiji** modernisation in Japan; rivalry among the great powers prepared World War I.

## Commonly confused

- The *Renaissance* renewed art and thought; the *Reformation* renewed religion.
- *Montesquieu* argued for separation of powers; *Rousseau* for the general will.
- The *Congress of Vienna* stood for preserving old monarchies; the *1848 revolutions* for liberal and nationalist demands.

## Study tip

Build a "cause, event, result" trio for each break and link each result to the cause of the next event. This chain answers almost every results question.
