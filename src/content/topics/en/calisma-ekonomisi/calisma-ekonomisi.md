## Core idea

Labour economics studies the **labour market**: how labour is supplied and demanded, how wages are set and why unemployment arises. Labour is not an ordinary good; it cannot be separated from the person, cannot be stored and is surrounded by institutions (unions, minimum wage, law). The test has 12 questions on it. Questions usually describe a situation and ask which effect, type of unemployment or wage theory applies, or how an indicator is calculated.

## Basic indicators

- **Labour force (*işgücü*):** the employed plus the unemployed within the working-age population. People not seeking work are **outside the labour force**.
- **Participation rate** = labour force / working-age population. **Unemployment rate** = unemployed / labour force. **Employment rate** = employed / working-age population.
- **Discouraged workers** stop searching and are counted outside the labour force, which can make the unemployment rate look lower than it is.

## Labour supply and labour demand

- **Labour supply:** a person divides time between work and leisure. When the wage rises, the **substitution effect** pushes towards more work and the **income effect** (leisure being a normal good) towards less. If the income effect dominates at high wages, the result is a **backward-bending labour supply curve**.
- **Labour demand** is a **derived demand (*türev talep*)**: labour is wanted because the goods it produces are wanted. A competitive firm hires until labour's **marginal revenue product** equals the wage.
- **Elasticity of labour demand** (Hicks-Marshall rules): it is higher when product demand is elastic, labour is easy to substitute, the supply of other inputs is elastic and labour's share of total cost is large.
- **Monopsony:** a labour market with a single buyer; wages and employment stay below the competitive level. Here a suitably set minimum wage can raise employment rather than reduce it.

## Wage theories and wage differentials

- **Subsistence wage / iron law of wages** (classical economists, Ricardo; Lassalle): in the long run wages fall to the workers' subsistence level.
- **Wages fund** (J. S. Mill): wages equal the capital fund set aside for workers divided by the number of workers.
- **Marginal productivity theory** (J. B. Clark): the wage equals labour's marginal product.
- **Surplus value** (Marx): the difference between the value a worker produces and the wage paid goes to capital.
- **Bargaining theory:** the wage is set within limits according to the parties' bargaining power.
- **Efficiency wage:** a firm pays above the market wage to raise productivity and reduce turnover and shirking.
- **Compensating wage differential:** extra pay for dangerous or unpleasant jobs.

## Unemployment

- **Frictional:** from job changes and search; short-lived. **Structural:** from mismatch between skills and jobs or technological change. **Cyclical:** from insufficient demand during recessions. **Seasonal:** from seasonality in sectors such as farming and tourism. **Disguised unemployment (*gizli işsizlik*):** surplus workers whose marginal productivity is zero.
- **Phillips curve:** an inverse relation between unemployment and inflation. Friedman and Phelps argued that this trade-off disappears in the long run and the economy returns to the **natural rate of unemployment**.
## Human capital and market structure

- **Human capital** (Schultz, Becker): education and on-the-job training are investments that raise future productivity and wages. The worker usually bears the cost of **general training**; the firm and worker share the cost of **firm-specific training**.
- **Segmented (dual) labour market** (Doeringer and Piore): the **primary market** offers high pay and security, the **secondary market** low pay and insecurity; moving between them is hard.

## Commonly confused

- The *substitution effect* of a wage rise leads to more work, the *income effect* to less.
- *Frictional unemployment* comes from search time, *structural unemployment* from skill mismatch.
- The *wages fund* rests on a capital fund, *marginal productivity theory* on the contribution of the last unit of labour.

## Study tip

Put each wage theory on a card with its author and a one-sentence main idea. In indicator questions read the denominator carefully: the unemployment rate divides by the labour force, the participation rate by the working-age population.
