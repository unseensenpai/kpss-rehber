## Core idea

Children's literature is artistic writing suited to a child's development and interests; for a primary teacher it is both a **reading source** and a **teaching tool**. The Primary School Teaching ÖABT has **2 questions** on it. The key criterion is **child-appropriateness** (*çocuğa görelik*). Questions usually ask you to judge a book for a given grade or choose a practice that supports a reading habit.

## Genres and books

- **Genres from oral tradition:** lullabies, ***tekerleme*** (rhythmic nonsense rhymes), riddles and folk tales; they build rhythm, phonological awareness and listening skills.
- **Fable:** a short tale with talking animals that teaches a lesson.
- **Picture book:** a book where pictures and text build meaning together; a core source in preschool and early reading.
- **Early reading books:** short sentences, large print, many pictures and words made from sounds the pupil has learned.

## Criteria for choosing books

- **Content:** subject suited to age; free of violence, fear and **stereotypes** (gender, origin); a message that is implied, not preached (avoiding heavy **didacticism**).
- **Language:** clear words, short and correct sentences.
- **Format:** legible type, suitable line spacing, durable binding, pictures that match the text.

## Building a reading habit

- The teacher's regular **reading aloud** serves as a model.
- **Interactive reading:** asking for predictions, questions and comments while reading.
- A class library, free reading time and book talks make reading enjoyable.

## Commonly confused

- In a *picture book* the pictures help tell the story; in an *illustrated book* they only accompany the text.
- A *folk tale* builds an extraordinary world; a *fable* aims to teach an explicit lesson.

## Study tip

Mark the pupil's grade in the question stem and screen the options by content, language and format in turn. A single detail that does not suit the child's age makes an option wrong.
