## Core idea

Nutrition provides the **energy** exercise needs and supports **recovery**. The basic rule is adequate, balanced eating; any special "athlete's diet" is built on that base. The test has **2 questions**; they usually ask about the functions of nutrients and correct eating and drinking around exercise.

## Nutrients

- **Carbohydrate:** the main fuel for exercise; stored as **glycogen** in muscle and liver. About 4 kcal per gram.
- **Fat:** an important fuel in long, low-to-moderate intensity exercise; about 9 kcal per gram.
- **Protein:** builds and repairs tissue; not a priority energy source. About 4 kcal per gram.
- **Vitamins and minerals:** regulate energy production; iron helps carry oxygen, calcium supports bone health.
- **Water:** regulates body temperature; losing it quickly lowers performance.

## Eating around exercise

- **Energy balance:** if energy in equals energy out, weight is maintained.
- **Before:** an easily digested meal rich in carbohydrate and low in fat and fibre, eaten a few hours before.
- **After:** carbohydrate to refill glycogen stores and protein for repair.
- **Fluids:** drink regularly before, during and after exercise; **thirst is a late signal**. In long efforts, electrolyte loss also matters.
- **Carbohydrate loading:** a practice to increase glycogen stores before long endurance events.
- **Doping:** banned substances and methods that raise performance unfairly; harmful to health and against fair play.

## Commonly confused

- *Glycogen* is stored carbohydrate; *glucose* is the sugar in the blood.
- *Protein* is the priority for building and repair; *carbohydrate* is the priority for energy.
- An *ergogenic aid* is any aid meant to improve performance; *doping* is the banned kind.

## Study tip

Read each question by asking "how long and how hard is the exercise?": short, intense efforts point to carbohydrate, long, light efforts to fat, and long efforts in the heat to fluids and electrolytes.
