## Core idea

The geography of continents and countries **applies physical and human geography to specific places**: a country's location, landforms and climate explain its population and economy. The test has 10 questions; they usually ask you to identify a country from its features and to link economic activities with natural conditions.

## The continents

- **Asia:** the largest continent by area and population; the **Himalayas**, great river plains and the **monsoon** climate. The two most populous countries, **India** and **China**, are here; the Gulf region holds large oil reserves.
- **Europe:** indented coasts, the **Alps**, navigable rivers such as the Rhine and Danube; early industrialisation and high urbanisation.
- **Africa:** climate belts symmetrical on both sides of the Equator (equatorial, savanna, desert, Mediterranean); the **Sahara**, the **Nile** and the Congo basin; raw-material exports weigh heavily in the economy.
- **North America:** the **Rocky Mountains** in the west, the Appalachians in the east, the **prairies** and Mississippi basin in the centre; industry around the **Great Lakes**.
- **South America:** the **Andes** in the west, the **Amazon** basin and rainforest in the north, the **pampas** in the south.
- **Oceania:** the interior of **Australia** is dry and people live on the coasts; mining and livestock farming matter.
- **Antarctica:** covered by ice with no permanent population; scientific research is governed by an international treaty.

## Country examples

- **Russia** is the largest country by area; **Canada** and Russia stand out for taiga forest and sparse population.
- **Japan:** in an earthquake and volcano belt, poor in raw materials, with advanced industry based on imports.
- The **Netherlands** is known for **polders** reclaimed from the sea, **Norway** for **fjords**, **Iceland** for geothermal energy.
- Most of **Egypt**'s population lives in the Nile valley and delta; the **Suez Canal** is a major source of income.
- **Brazil** stands out for the Amazon forest and farm exports, **Saudi Arabia** for oil.

## Passages and organisations

- **Canals and straits:** **Suez** (Mediterranean-Red Sea), **Panama** (Atlantic-Pacific), and the straits of **Gibraltar**, **Hormuz** and **Malacca**.
- **Organisations:** the **United Nations**, **European Union**, **NATO**, **OPEC** (oil-exporting countries), **ASEAN** (Southeast Asia), the **African Union** and the **Organization of Turkic States**.

## Commonly confused

- *Prairies* are North America's grasslands; the *pampas* are South America's.
- A *polder* is low land reclaimed from the sea; a *fjord* is a deep inlet cut by a glacier.
- *Suez* links the Mediterranean to the Red Sea; *Panama* links the Atlantic to the Pacific.

## Study tip

On a blank world map, mark three landforms, three countries and one passage for each continent. For "identify the country" questions, map memory is your fastest helper.
