## Core idea

Work psychology studies a person's behaviour, motivation and satisfaction at work; the sociology of work studies the social organisation of work, the division of labour and the worker's place in society. The shared question is: why and how do people work, and how does work affect them? The test has 6 questions on it. Questions mostly ask you to match a theory or concept with its author, or to link an example situation to the right theory.

## Classical and neoclassical approaches

- **Scientific management** (Taylor): work is broken into its most efficient parts through time-and-motion study; the worker is motivated mainly by money.
- **Hawthorne studies** (Elton Mayo and colleagues): productivity depends less on physical conditions than on **attention**, group norms and social relations. They revealed the importance of **informal groups** and gave rise to the **human relations approach**.

## Motivation theories

- **Maslow, hierarchy of needs:** physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualisation; once a lower need is met, the next one becomes dominant.
- **Alderfer, ERG:** existence, relatedness, growth; several needs can act at once, and frustration can lead back to a lower level.
- **Herzberg, two-factor theory:** without **hygiene factors** (pay, working conditions, supervision) dissatisfaction arises, but their presence does not motivate; **motivators** (achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work itself) create satisfaction and motivation.
- **McClelland:** needs for achievement, power and affiliation are learned.
- **McGregor, Theory X and Y:** Theory X assumes people avoid work and must be controlled; Theory Y assumes they seek responsibility under the right conditions.
- **Process theories:** **Vroom's expectancy theory** (expectation that effort leads to performance and performance to reward, plus the value of the reward), **Adams's equity theory** (people compare their input-output ratio with others'), **Locke's goal-setting theory** (specific, challenging goals raise performance).

## Problems at work

- **Job satisfaction (*iş doyumu*):** a person's positive emotional attitude towards their job.
- **Burnout (*tükenmişlik*)** (Maslach): emotional exhaustion, depersonalisation and a reduced sense of personal accomplishment; common in jobs involving intense work with people.
- **Mobbing (psychological harassment)** (pioneering work by Leymann): systematic, persistent hostile behaviour towards a person at work.

## Sociology of work

- **Durkheim:** traditional societies have **mechanical solidarity** based on similarity; modern societies have **organic solidarity** based on the division of labour and interdependence.
- **Marx:** in capitalist production the worker is **alienated** from the product, the production process, their human essence and other people.
- **Weber:** **bureaucracy** (rules, hierarchy, specialisation, impersonality) and the link between the **Protestant ethic** and the spirit of capitalism.
- **Fordism:** assembly line, standard products, mass production and consumption. **Post-Fordism:** flexible production, multi-skilled workers, production to demand.
- **Braverman:** **deskilling** in the labour process; separating design from execution takes control away from the worker.

## Commonly confused

- *Hygiene factors* only prevent dissatisfaction; *motivators* create motivation.
- *Mechanical solidarity* rests on similarity, *organic solidarity* on the division of labour.
- *Expectancy theory* explains the effort-reward link; *equity theory* explains comparison with others.
