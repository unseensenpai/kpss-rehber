## Core idea

Turkey's physical geography is explained by three basic facts: it lies **in the mid-latitudes**, its land is **young and high**, and it is **surrounded by sea on three sides**. Most differences in temperature, rainfall, river regimes and vegetation come from combining these three. Questions usually ask "why?", so linking causes and effects pays off more than memorising.

## Location

- **Absolute location:** between latitudes 36° and 42° north and longitudes 26° and 45° east. It is in the Northern Hemisphere and the mid-latitudes, with four distinct seasons.
- The east and west ends are **19 degrees of longitude** apart, which means a **76-minute difference in local time**; the sun rises earlier in the east.
- The whole country uses **UTC+3, based on the 45° east meridian**.
- **Relative location:** a bridge between Asia and Europe; the Straits; a peninsula (Anatolia) surrounded by sea on three sides; on energy and trade routes; a wide variety of natural resources.
- The average elevation is about **1,132 m** and rises from west to east. The highest point is **Mount Ağrı (Ararat)**, 5,137 m.

## Landforms

- Turkey lies on the **Alpine-Himalayan fold belt**; its mountains are young and the earthquake risk is high.
- **Fold mountains:** the North Anatolian Mountains in the north and the Taurus Mountains in the south. They run parallel to the coast, which makes travel from the coast inland difficult.
- **Horst and graben structure:** in the Aegean, mountains run perpendicular to the coast. The Gediz, Little and Great Menderes grabens form plains, with the Bozdağlar and Aydın Mountains as horsts between them. That is why sea influence reaches far inland in the Aegean and the coast is deeply indented.
- **Fault lines:** the North Anatolian, East Anatolian and West Anatolian fault zones.
- **Volcanic mountains:** Ağrı, Süphan and Nemrut (Eastern Anatolia); Erciyes and Hasandağı (Central Anatolia); Karacadağ (Southeast); Kula (Aegean).
- **Karst landforms:** formed where water dissolves limestone: lapies, dolines, poljes, caves and travertines (Pamukkale). They are most common around the Mediterranean (Taurus Mountains).
- **Delta plains:** Çukurova (Seyhan-Ceyhan), Bafra (Kızılırmak), Çarşamba (Yeşilırmak).

## Climate

- **Mediterranean climate:** hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters. Vegetation: **maquis**.
- **Black Sea climate:** rain in every season and a small summer-winter temperature difference. Vegetation: **forest**.
- **Continental climate:** hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters. Vegetation: **steppe**; meadows at high elevations.
- The most rainfall is on the **eastern Black Sea coast** (Rize); the least is **around Lake Tuz**.
- **Types of rainfall:** **orographic**, when moist winds rise against mountains (Black Sea and Mediterranean coasts); **frontal**, where air masses meet (winter rain); **convectional**, when warm air rises (spring showers in Central Anatolia).
- Because mountains run parallel to the coast, sea influence cannot reach the interior, so the interior is continental.

## Rivers and lakes

- Turkish rivers usually have **irregular regimes** and **steep gradients**: high hydroelectric potential, poor for navigation.
- The longest river is the **Kızılırmak**. The **Euphrates and Tigris** flow into the Persian Gulf. **Closed basins** such as Konya, Lake Tuz and Van do not drain to the sea.
- **Lake types:** Lake Tuz (tectonic), **Lake Van** (volcanic dam), **Nemrut** (crater), **Tortum** (landslide dam), **Bafa** (alluvial dam), karst lakes around the Mediterranean region.

## Vegetation and soils

- Vegetation mirrors climate: maquis on the Mediterranean coast, forest on the Black Sea, steppe in Central Anatolia, meadows in the high parts of Eastern Anatolia.
- **Terra rossa** (red Mediterranean soil) on limestone; **alluvial soils** in delta and river plains, which are the most fertile.
- **Erosion** increases where slopes are steep and vegetation is thin; it is most visible on the bare slopes of Central and Southeastern Anatolia.

## Natural hazards

- **Earthquakes** are the biggest risk along fault lines. **Floods and landslides** are most common on the Black Sea, **avalanches** in the high parts of Eastern Anatolia and the eastern Black Sea. **Forest fires** increase in summer on the Mediterranean and Aegean coasts.

## Commonly confused

- *Fold mountains* run parallel to the coast (Black Sea, Mediterranean); the *horst and graben* structure runs perpendicular to it (Aegean). Coast types and sea influence follow from this difference.
- *Lake Van* is a volcanic dam lake; *Nemrut* is a crater lake. Both are in Eastern Anatolia.
- *Orographic rainfall* falls on coastal mountain slopes; *convectional rainfall* falls inland in spring.
