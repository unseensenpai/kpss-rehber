## Core idea

Human geography asks **where and why** people live. In Turkey, the main factors behind population distribution are climate, landforms, water, industry and transport. Questions on this topic often give a chart or table and ask you to interpret it, so reading data matters as much as knowing facts.

## Censuses

- The first general census of the Republic was held in **1927**. Between 1935 and 1990 censuses were mostly held every five years.
- **Since 2007** the population has been determined every year through the **Address-Based Population Registration System**, calculated from records instead of a door-to-door count.
- Turkey's population has passed 85 million. Population growth and fertility have long been falling while the average age rises: the **population is ageing**.

## Distribution and density

- **Arithmetic population density** = population / area.
- **Dense areas:** the Marmara region (especially Istanbul and Kocaeli), the Aegean and Mediterranean coastal plains, the Black Sea coast. Industry, trade, a mild climate and fertile plains attract people.
- **Sparse areas:** the high, rugged parts of Eastern Anatolia, the dry parts of Central Anatolia (around Lake Tuz) and the high Taurus. Harsh climate, rough terrain and water shortage limit population.
- There is a clear contrast between the coast and the interior: people gather on the coasts and in large cities.

## Population structure

- **Population pyramid:** a wide base shows a young, fast-growing population; a narrowing base shows falling fertility. Turkey's pyramid is narrowing at the base.
- **Sex ratio:** generally balanced. Industrial cities that receive migrants can have relatively more men, rural places that send migrants relatively more women.
- **Working population:** the share of agriculture has fallen while services and industry have grown, together with urbanisation.
- Literacy and average years of schooling rose steadily during the Republic.

## Migration

- **Internal migration:** from the 1950s, farm mechanisation, a lack of rural jobs and urban job opportunities sped up **rural-to-urban** migration. The results included unplanned urbanisation and shanty towns (*gecekondu*).
- **Seasonal migration:** farm workers moving for harvests (hazelnuts, cotton, apricots), summer moves to tourist areas, herders moving to summer pastures.
- **International migration:** the 1961 labour agreement with Germany started worker migration to Europe. **Brain drain** is the departure of skilled people. In recent years Turkey has also received heavy migration from neighbouring countries.

## Settlement

- **Dispersed settlement:** on the Black Sea, rough terrain and water available everywhere keep houses far apart.
- **Nucleated settlement:** in Central Anatolia and the Southeast, limited water sources gather houses around the water.
- **Temporary rural settlements:** **yayla** (summer pasture for livestock; Black Sea and Taurus), **kom** (small livestock-based settlement in Eastern Anatolia), **mezra** (small settlement attached to a village, used for farming or herding), **oba** (tent settlement of nomads).
- **Urban functions:** administrative (Ankara), industrial (Kocaeli, Bursa), port and trade (İzmir, Mersin), tourism (Antalya, Nevşehir), education and culture; large cities have several functions.

## Commonly confused

- *Arithmetic density* is calculated over the whole area; a province with low density can still have a crowded city centre.
- *Kom* is typical of Eastern Anatolia; *yayla* is found more on the Black Sea and in the Taurus.
- *Dispersed settlement* is caused by rough terrain and plentiful water; *nucleated settlement* by water shortage.

## Study tip

For every chart or table, ask "why is it like this in this region?" Climate, landforms, water and jobs appear in the answer to almost every population and settlement question.
