## Core idea

In economic geography every activity depends on a **cause**: crops follow climate and soil; industry follows raw materials, energy, markets and transport; tourism follows natural and cultural attractions. Questions are usually "why does this crop grow here?" or "why was this plant built here?". Linking products to these causes is faster than memorising them.

## Agriculture

- Farmland is limited by climate, landforms and water; irrigated plains have higher yields and more variety.
- **Cereals:** wheat mostly in Central Anatolia (drought-tolerant); barley in similar conditions. Maize on the Black Sea; rice on well-watered plains (Marmara, Black Sea).
- **Industrial crops:** cotton in Çukurova, the Aegean and the Southeast (Şanlıurfa-Harran, irrigated by GAP); sugar beet in Central Anatolia (Konya); tobacco in the Aegean and Black Sea; sunflowers in Thrace; poppies around Afyon.
- **Typical of the Black Sea:** tea (Rize, the rainiest part) and hazelnuts (Ordu, Giresun, Samsun).
- **Typical of the Mediterranean and Aegean:** olives (Aegean and southern Marmara), citrus (Mediterranean coast), bananas (Anamur–Alanya), figs (Aydın), grapes (Manisa and the Aegean; also widespread in the Southeast).
- Greenhouse farming is concentrated around Antalya and Mersin; mild winters allow early-season produce.

## Livestock, forestry and fishing

- **Cattle:** the lush summer pastures of Eastern Anatolia (Erzurum-Kars), the Black Sea and Marmara. **Sheep and goats:** Central and Eastern Anatolia, where steppe is widespread. **Hair goats** in the Taurus, **Angora goats** (mohair) in Central Anatolia.
- **Silkworm farming** is common around Bursa; **beekeeping** where flowering plants are diverse.
- **Forestry** is concentrated in the mountainous parts of the Black Sea and Mediterranean.
- **Fishing** is mostly done in the Black Sea, led by anchovies.

## Minerals and energy

- **Boron:** most of the world's reserves are in Turkey (Eskişehir, Kütahya, Balıkesir, Bursa).
- **Chromium** (Elazığ-Guleman, Muğla), **iron** (Sivas-Divriği, Malatya-Hekimhan), **copper** (Artvin-Murgul, Kastamonu-Küre, Diyarbakır-Ergani), **bauxite** (Konya-Seydişehir, aluminium plant), **hard coal** (Zonguldak), **lignite** (Kahramanmaraş-Afşin-Elbistan, Manisa-Soma, Kütahya-Tunçbilek), **oil** (Batman, Adıyaman and the Southeast).
- **Hydroelectric:** above all the **Atatürk, Keban and Karakaya** dams on the Euphrates, and rivers in the East and Southeast with steep gradients and high flow.
- **Thermal power plants** are built next to lignite deposits (Afşin-Elbistan, Soma).
- **Geothermal:** around fault lines, especially Denizli and Aydın. **Wind:** the Aegean and Marmara coasts. **Solar:** the Mediterranean and Central Anatolia (Konya), with long sunshine hours. **Nuclear:** Mersin-Akkuyu.
- Turkey **depends heavily on imports** for oil and natural gas, which makes renewables and pipelines important.

## Industry

- Industrial location depends on raw materials, energy, labour, capital, markets and transport.
- **Marmara**, especially the Istanbul–Kocaeli–Bursa belt, has the densest industry.
- **Iron and steel:** Karabük (the first plant, 1939), Zonguldak-Ereğli and Hatay-İskenderun; proximity to hard coal and ports is decisive.
- **Agro-industry:** sugar factories in beet-growing areas (the first in 1926 at Uşak and Alpullu), tea factories in Rize, cotton textiles in Çukurova and the Aegean.

## Transport, trade and tourism

- **Road** transport dominates; high-speed rail lines have expanded in recent years. The **Straits** and the major ports (Istanbul-Ambarlı, Mersin, İzmir, Kocaeli) are the gateways of foreign trade.
- **Tourism:** coastal (Antalya, Muğla), cultural (Cappadocia, Ephesus, Istanbul), thermal (Afyon, Denizli, Bursa), winter (Uludağ, Erciyes, Palandöken, Sarıkamış) and faith tourism.

## Regional development projects

- **GAP (Southeastern Anatolia Project):** dams, irrigation (Harran Plain) and energy on the Euphrates and Tigris; it increased cotton production and agro-industry in the region.
- **DAP** (Eastern Anatolia), **DOKAP** (Eastern Black Sea), **KOP** (Konya Plain): aim to reduce development gaps between regions.

## Commonly confused

- *Hard coal* is in Zonguldak; *lignite* is in Afşin-Elbistan and Soma. Thermal plants mostly burn lignite.
- *Tea* needs heavy rain and acidic soil (Rize); *hazelnuts* grow along a wider Black Sea belt (Ordu-Giresun).
- *Hydroelectric* potential is high in the east; *geothermal* potential is high in the west (Aegean fault lines).

## Study tip

Mark every crop and mineral on a map and write a one-word reason beside it (rain, heat, plain, fault, limestone...). You can then solve a new matching question in the exam from these reasons.
