## Core idea

Teaching reading and writing in special education (*özel eğitimde okuma-yazma öğretimi*) applies the methods of the general classroom in **smaller steps, with more repetition and more systematically**. The aim is not only decoding but **comprehension** and using reading in daily life. There are **2 questions**; they ask about readiness skills, teaching techniques and reading assessment.

## Readiness and early literacy

- **Readiness skills:** **phonological awareness** (noticing sounds, syllabifying, blending sounds), auditory and visual discrimination, attention, **fine motor** skills.
- **Sound-based sentence method** (*ses temelli cümle yöntemi*): the method used for early literacy in Türkiye; it moves from sounds to syllables, words and sentences.
- **Functional words:** for students with severe disabilities, everyday words such as "exit", "danger" or "men/women" are taught as whole words.
- **Errorless teaching:** constant time delay and simultaneous prompting are often used for word recognition.

## Fluency and comprehension

- **Fluency:** accuracy, speed and appropriate expression; developed through **repeated reading** and modelled reading.
- **Reading comprehension:** predicting before reading, asking questions while reading, summarising afterwards; graphic organisers such as the **story map** (character, place, time, problem, solution).
- **Writing:** starts with holding a pencil and forming letters and moves on to words, sentences and short texts; adapted pencils and lined pages can be used.

## Assessment

- **Reading error analysis:** errors such as omission, addition, reversal, repetition and misreading are recorded; teaching is planned around the pattern.
- **Reading levels:** **independent** (reads without help), **instructional** (reads with support), **frustration** (the text is too hard); teaching uses texts at the instructional level.

## Commonly confused

- *Phonological awareness* is noticing sounds by ear; *letter knowledge* is matching a sound to a letter.
- *Fluency* concerns speed and accuracy, *comprehension* concerns meaning; not every fluent reader understands.

## Study tip

If a question describes a student's error, first name the error type, then choose the technique that fits it; error analysis and teaching technique always go together.
