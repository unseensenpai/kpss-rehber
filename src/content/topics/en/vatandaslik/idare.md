## Core idea

The administration is the organisation through which the state provides public services. Under the Constitution, the administration **forms a whole in its structure and functions** and is **regulated by law**. This whole has two legs: the **central administration**, where decisions are made at the centre, and **decentralised administration**, where certain services are run by bodies with their own legal personality. Questions usually ask which group an institution belongs to and whether the link between them is hierarchy or tutelage.

## Organising principles

- **Centralisation** and **decentralisation** are used together.
- **Administration by law:** the structure and functions of the administration are set by law.
- **Unity of the administration:** all administrative units are parts of a single structure; the central administration holds **administrative tutelage** over decentralised bodies.

## Central administration

- **Capital organisation:** the **President**, the vice presidents, the **ministries**, and auxiliary bodies such as the councils and offices attached to the Presidency.
- **Provincial organisation:** Turkey is divided into **provinces** on the basis of geography, economic conditions and the needs of public services, and provinces into **districts**.
  - **Governor (vali):** represents the state and the President in the province and heads the provincial general administration.
  - **District governor (kaymakam):** heads the general administration of the district.
- **Deconcentration (yetki genişliği):** the centre leaves some decision-making powers to its representatives in the provinces (governors, district governors). It creates no separate legal person; acts are still done on behalf of the state.

## Decentralised administration

- **Territorial (local administrations):** set up to meet the common local needs of the people of a province, municipality or village; their decision-making organs are **elected**. Local elections are held **every five years**.
  - **Special provincial administration:** provincial council, provincial executive committee, governor.
  - **Municipality:** **municipal council**, **municipal executive committee**, **mayor**. Large cities have a **metropolitan municipality** with district municipalities inside it; provinces with a metropolitan municipality have no special provincial administration.
  - **Village:** the **village assembly** (all voters), the **council of elders**, the **headman (muhtar)**.
- **Service-based:** bodies with their own legal personality that run a specific public service: **universities**, TRT, professional organisations with public-institution status (bar associations, medical chambers).
- **Regulatory and supervisory authorities (independent administrative authorities):** regulate and supervise a specific sector: RTÜK (broadcasting), the Competition Authority, BDDK (banking), the Capital Markets Board, the Energy Market Regulatory Authority.

## Hierarchy and tutelage

- **Hierarchy:** the power of a superior over subordinates within the same legal person. The superior can approve, change or annul the subordinate's act. Example: a minister and ministry staff, a governor and a provincial director.
- **Administrative tutelage:** the supervision by the central administration of decentralised bodies with their own legal personality. It can only be used **in the cases and within the limits set by law**; it cannot change an act, only approve or refuse it. Example: the Ministry of the Interior's oversight of municipalities.

## Public servants

- The core and permanent duties that the state and public legal persons carry out under the general principles of administration are performed by **civil servants and other public employees**.
- The rights and duties of civil servants are mainly set by **Civil Servants Law No. 657**. Civil servants are appointed **by examination** (KPSS); merit and career are the guiding principles.
- Disciplinary penalties can be challenged in court.

## Judicial review of the administration

- **Recourse to the courts is open** against every act and action of the administration.
- **Administrative courts:** administrative and tax courts, regional administrative courts and, at the top, the **Council of State** (*Danıştay*).
- **Action for annulment:** brought to set aside an unlawful administrative act.
- **Full remedy action:** brought for compensation of damage caused by an act or action of the administration.
- The administration must pay for the damage it causes; courts review the **lawfulness** of administrative acts but cannot review their **expediency**.

## Commonly confused

- *Deconcentration* stays inside the central administration; *decentralisation* means a separate legal person.
- *Hierarchy* is within one legal person and broad; *tutelage* is between different legal persons and limited by law.
- The *governor* both represents the central administration and heads the special provincial administration.
