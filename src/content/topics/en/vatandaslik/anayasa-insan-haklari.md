## Core idea

The constitution is the **highest norm**, setting the basic structure of the state and the relationship between the individual and the state. It answers two questions: **Who uses state power, and how?** (legislature, executive, judiciary) and **Where does that power stop in front of the individual?** (fundamental rights and freedoms). Questions are usually based on the 1982 Constitution; the **presidential system of government** introduced by the 2017 amendments must be known in its current form.

## Basic concepts

- **Rigid constitution:** harder to amend than ordinary laws; the 1982 Constitution is rigid.
- **Separation of powers:** legislative, executive and judicial power are given to different organs. The judiciary is independent.
- **State governed by the rule of law:** every act and action of the state is bound by law and open to judicial review.

## Turkish constitutional history

- The **Charter of Alliance (1808)**, the **Tanzimat Edict (1839)** and the **Reform Edict (1856)** were the first steps of constitutional development, but none was a constitution.
- **Kanun-i Esasi (1876):** the first constitution.
- **Teşkilat-ı Esasiye (1921):** the principle of national sovereignty, government by assembly.
- **1924 Constitution:** the first constitution of the Republic; the six principles were added in 1937.
- **1961 Constitution:** the Constitutional Court, a two-chamber parliament, broad rights and freedoms.
- **1982 Constitution:** adopted by referendum; one of its most extensive amendments came in 2017.

## General principles of the 1982 Constitution

- The **first three articles** (the form of the state is a Republic; the characteristics of the Republic; language, flag, national anthem and capital) cannot be amended. **Article 4** sets out this ban, and its own amendment cannot even be proposed.
- **Characteristics of the Republic (Art. 2):** a **democratic, secular and social state governed by the rule of law**, respecting human rights within the notions of public peace, national solidarity and justice, loyal to Atatürk's nationalism and based on the fundamental principles in the Preamble.
- **Sovereignty** belongs to the nation without condition; the nation exercises it through the authorised organs.
- **Amending the Constitution:** at least **one third** of the members of the Assembly (200) must propose it. Adoption needs at least **three fifths** (360). An amendment adopted with 360–399 votes goes to a referendum unless the President sends it back to the Assembly; one adopted with **two thirds** (400) or more may be put to a referendum by the President.

## Fundamental rights and duties

- **Rights and duties of the individual (protective rights):** the right to life, liberty and security of person, privacy, inviolability of the home, freedom of religion and conscience, freedom of thought and expression.
- **Social and economic rights (rights to claim):** education, work, social security, health and housing. They require the state to act and are fulfilled as far as financial resources allow.
- **Political rights and duties (participation rights):** voting, standing for election, forming political parties, entering public service, the right of petition; national service and the duty to pay taxes.
- **Restriction (Art. 13):** only **by law**, for the reasons set out in the Constitution and **without touching their essence**; restrictions may not conflict with the requirements of a democratic society or the principle of **proportionality**.
- **Suspension (Art. 15):** the exercise of rights can be suspended in war, mobilisation and states of emergency. Even then, the **right to life** (except deaths from lawful acts of war), physical and mental integrity, **freedom of religion and conscience**, **non-retroactivity of offences and penalties** and the **presumption of innocence** are untouchable.

## The legislature

- Legislative power belongs to the **Grand National Assembly of Turkey (TBMM)** and cannot be delegated. It has **600 members**.
- **Parliamentary and presidential elections are held on the same day every five years.** Every Turkish citizen **aged 18 or over** can be elected (other conditions apply).
- Its duties include making, amending and repealing laws; adopting the **budget**; deciding on printing money, declaring war and ratifying international treaties; and declaring general and special amnesties.
- **Means of oversight:** parliamentary inquiry, general debate, parliamentary investigation and written questions.

## The executive

- Executive power belongs to the **President** (2017 amendment, in effect since 2018).
- The President is elected directly by the people and must be **at least 40**, have higher education and be eligible to be a member of parliament. A person can be elected **twice** at most.
- The President can issue **Presidential decrees** on executive matters; fundamental rights, individual rights and political rights cannot be regulated by decree, and no decree may be issued on a matter explicitly regulated by law.
- The President appoints and dismisses the vice presidents and ministers, and has the power to declare a **state of emergency**.

## The judiciary

- Judicial power is exercised on behalf of the Turkish nation by **independent and impartial courts**.
- **Constitutional Court** (15 members): reviews the constitutionality of laws and Presidential decrees, examines individual applications and sits as the Supreme Court (*Yüce Divan*).
- The **Court of Cassation** (*Yargıtay*) is the highest court of the ordinary courts, the **Council of State** (*Danıştay*) of the administrative courts. The **Court of Jurisdictional Disputes** settles disputes between the branches of the judiciary.
- The **Council of Judges and Prosecutors (HSK)** handles the appointment, promotion and discipline of judges and prosecutors. The **Supreme Election Council** runs and supervises elections; its decisions cannot be appealed.

## Human rights law

- The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)** and the **European Convention on Human Rights** (ECHR, 1950) are the key documents. Turkey is a party to the ECHR and accepts individual applications to the **European Court of Human Rights**.
- **Generations of rights:** first generation, civil and political rights; second, economic, social and cultural rights; third, solidarity rights (peace, a healthy environment, development).
- **Protection in domestic law:** **individual application to the Constitutional Court** (introduced by the 2010 amendment, in use since 2012), the **Ombudsman Institution** (*Kamu Denetçiliği Kurumu*) and the **Human Rights and Equality Institution of Turkey**.

## Commonly confused

- *Restriction* (Art. 13) applies in normal times; *suspension* (Art. 15) in war, mobilisation and states of emergency.
- In a constitutional amendment, *360* votes trigger a referendum; *400 or more* give the President a choice about holding one.
- The *Court of Cassation* tops the ordinary courts and the *Council of State* the administrative courts; the *Court of Accounts* (*Sayıştay*) audits public spending on behalf of the Assembly and judges public accounts.

## Study tip

Put the rules with numbers (600, 18, 40, two terms, 1/3, 3/5, 2/3, 15 members) in one table. In citizenship questions, points often come from matching these numbers and organs correctly. The Constitution can change, so check the current text in an official source.
