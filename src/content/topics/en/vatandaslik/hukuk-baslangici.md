## Core idea

Law is the part of the rules ordering society that is **backed by state-enforced sanctions**. Religious, moral and etiquette rules also keep social order, but breaking them leads to social disapproval or a guilty conscience. Most questions on this topic ask you to match a situation with the right concept: Which branch of law is this? Which sanction? Does this person have capacity?

## Rules of social order

- **Religious rules:** spiritual sanction (sin, punishment in the afterlife).
- **Moral rules:** conscience and social condemnation.
- **Rules of etiquette:** disapproval and exclusion.
- **Legal rules:** enforced by the power of the state. This is the key feature that sets them apart. Legal rules are general, abstract and lasting.

## Sources of law

- **Primary (binding) sources:**
  - **Written:** the Constitution, laws, Presidential decrees, regulations and other regulatory acts; international treaties duly put into effect have the force of law.
  - **Unwritten:** customary law.
- **Auxiliary sources:** court decisions (**case law**) and scholarly opinion (**doctrine**). Judges draw on them when deciding.
- **Hierarchy of norms:** a lower norm cannot contradict a higher one. The Constitution is at the top.
- Under Article 1 of the Turkish Civil Code, if no statutory provision applies, the judge decides by custom, and failing that by the rule the judge would make as legislator.

## Branches of law

- **Public law** (relations where the state is a party with superior power): constitutional, administrative, criminal, tax and procedural law, public international law.
- **Private law** (relations between equals): civil law, law of obligations, commercial law, private international law.
- Some branches have features of both; for example, labour law and enforcement and bankruptcy law are mixed.

## Types of sanction

- **Punishment:** imprisonment and judicial fines for offenders; administrative sanctions such as administrative fines.
- **Compulsory enforcement:** making a debt be performed by state power.
- **Compensation:** making good a loss with money (material and non-material damages).
- **Invalidity:** an unlawful act produces no effect; it can take the form of **non-existence**, **absolute nullity** or **voidability**.

## Rights, persons and capacity

- **Right:** a power the law grants a person. Divided into public rights (e.g. voting and standing for election) and private rights (e.g. ownership).
- **Person:** an entity that can hold rights and obligations.
  - **Natural person:** a human being. Personality begins at **complete and live birth** and ends at death. A child has legal capacity from conception, provided it is born alive.
  - **Legal person:** private-law legal persons such as associations, foundations and companies; public-law legal persons such as the state, municipalities and universities.
- **Legal capacity:** the ability to hold rights and obligations. Every human being has it, equally.
- **Capacity to act:** the ability to acquire rights and take on obligations through one's own acts. Requirements: **power of discernment**, **majority** and **not being under guardianship**.
- **Majority:** begins at 18. Marriage confers majority; a minor aged 15 can be declared of age by a court at their own request with their guardian's consent.

## Kinship and domicile

- **Blood kinship:** between people descended from one another (ascendants and descendants) or from a common ancestor.
- **Kinship by marriage:** with the blood relatives of one's spouse.
- **Domicile:** the place where a person lives with the intention of staying. No one can have more than one domicile at the same time.

## Commonly confused

- Everyone has *legal capacity*; *capacity to act* depends on conditions.
- *Case law* and *doctrine* are auxiliary sources; *custom* is an unwritten primary source.
- *Compensation* repairs a loss; *punishment* answers an offence.

## Study tip

Find an everyday example for each concept: a rental contract (law of obligations), a traffic fine (administrative sanction), an inheritance (civil law). Exam questions arrive as exactly these kinds of cases.
