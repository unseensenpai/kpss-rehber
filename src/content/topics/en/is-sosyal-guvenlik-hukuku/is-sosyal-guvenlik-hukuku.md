## Core idea

Labour law arose to **protect the worker** by balancing the **unequal bargaining power** between worker and employer; most of its rules are therefore mandatory and cannot be changed to the worker's detriment. Social security law protects people against **social risks** such as illness, accidents, old age and death. With 22 questions it is the heaviest subject in this test. Questions usually describe a case and ask which type of termination, time limit, insurance branch or duty applies.

## Individual labour law (Labour Law No. 4857)

- **Worker (*işçi*):** a natural person working under an employment contract. **Employer (*işveren*):** a natural or legal person who employs workers. **Employer's representative (*işveren vekili*):** a person who manages the work on the employer's behalf.
- **Subcontractor (*alt işveren*):** an employer taking on auxiliary work, or a part of the main work that requires expertise for technological reasons. The **principal employer** is **jointly and severally liable** with the subcontractor towards the subcontractor's workers.
- **Contract types:** fixed-term and open-ended, part-time, on-call work, probationary, team contract, remote work. A fixed-term contract needs an **objective reason**; if renewed in a chain without a substantial reason it is treated as open-ended.
- **Probation period (*deneme süresi*):** at most two months, extendable to four by collective agreement. Either party may terminate without notice or compensation.
- **Working time:** at most **45 hours a week**; daily work may not exceed 11 hours. **Overtime (*fazla çalışma*)** is paid with a fifty per cent increase, up to 270 hours a year.
- **Weekly rest:** at least 24 uninterrupted hours in each seven-day period. **Annual paid leave:** for those with at least one year of service, 14, 20 or 26 days depending on seniority; at least 20 days for workers under 18 or over 50.

## Termination and job security

- **Termination with notice:** open-ended contracts have **notice periods** of two, four, six or eight weeks depending on seniority.
- **Termination for just cause (*haklı neden*, immediate):** health reasons, conduct against morality and good faith, and force majeure; for the employer also detention or arrest of the worker exceeding the notice period. Termination for misconduct must be used within **six working days** of learning of it and in any case within **one year** of the event.
- **Job security (*iş güvencesi*):** in a workplace with thirty or more workers, a worker with at least six months' seniority on an open-ended contract may be dismissed only for a **valid reason (*geçerli neden*)** related to capability, conduct or operational needs. The worker first applies to **mandatory mediation**, then may file a **reinstatement (*işe iade*) lawsuit**.
- **Severance pay (*kıdem tazminatı*):** still based on the retained provision of the old Labour Law No. 1475; it requires at least one year of service and one of the termination cases listed in the law.

## Collective labour law (Law No. 6356)

- **Trade unions** are organised by **branch of activity (*işkolu*)**; workplace unions cannot be founded. Joining and leaving a union is free (**positive and negative freedom of association**).
- A **collective labour agreement (*toplu iş sözleşmesi*)** is made at workplace or enterprise level for one to three years. Competence requires the branch threshold plus membership of **more than half** of the workers at the workplace, or forty per cent at enterprise level.
- **Disputes:** a *dispute of rights* concerns the interpretation or application of an existing right and goes to court. A *dispute of interests* arises while making a new agreement; after mediation it may lead to a **strike** or **lockout**.
- A **lawful strike** is possible only in a collective agreement dispute and following the legal procedure; political, general and solidarity strikes are prohibited. Where strikes are banned, the **High Arbitration Board (*Yüksek Hakem Kurulu*)** settles the dispute.

## Social security (Law No. 5510)

- **Insured status:** **4/1-a** employees under a contract of service, **4/1-b** self-employed, **4/1-c** public servants.
- **Short-term insurance branches:** work accident and occupational disease, sickness, maternity. **Long-term branches:** invalidity, old age, death. There is also **general health insurance**.
- A **work accident** occurs at the workplace or because of work carried out by the employer; cases such as transport by employer-provided shuttle and breastfeeding time are also covered. An **occupational disease** arises from a recurring cause linked to the nature of the work.

## Occupational health and safety (Law No. 6331)

- It applies to **all work and workplaces** in the public and private sectors regardless of the number of employees, apart from the exceptions listed in the law.
- The employer must carry out a **risk assessment**, appoint an occupational safety specialist and a workplace physician, and train employees. Buying these services from outside **does not remove the employer's responsibility**.
- Workplaces are classed as **less hazardous, hazardous and very hazardous**.
- Facing serious and imminent danger, the employee has a **right to refuse work**. Work with fifty or more employees lasting more than six months must set up an **occupational health and safety board**.

## Commonly confused

- *Termination for just cause* is immediate, without notice; *termination for a valid reason* follows the notice period and is subject to job-security review.
- A *dispute of rights* goes to court; a *dispute of interests* can lead to a strike or lockout.
- *Short-term branches* cover temporary loss of income; *long-term branches* cover permanent risks such as invalidity, old age and death.
