## Core idea

Career guidance helps people make realistic career decisions by getting to know themselves and occupations. Career development is seen as a lifelong process. The test has 6 questions; they usually give a student's situation or statement and ask which theory it belongs to.

## Matching and personality theories

- **Trait-factor (Parsons):** knowing oneself, knowing occupations and **true reasoning** between the two; matching through tests and occupational information.
- **Holland:** six personality types and work environments (**RIASEC**: realistic, investigative, artistic, social, enterprising, conventional) arranged on a hexagon; **congruence** (person-environment fit), consistency and **differentiation** are key concepts.
- **Roe:** the view that early parent-child relationships and needs steer people toward occupations oriented to people or not oriented to people.

## Developmental theories

- **Ginzberg:** fantasy, tentative and realistic periods.
- **Super:** occupational choice is the implementation of the **self-concept**; growth, exploration, establishment, maintenance and disengagement stages; **career maturity** and the **life-career rainbow** showing different life roles.
- **Gottfredson:** children first **circumscribe** occupational options by gender role and prestige, then **compromise** with reality.

## Learning and cognitive theories

- **Krumboltz (social learning):** genetic endowment, environmental conditions, learning experiences and task approach skills shape decisions; making use of unexpected opportunities (**planned happenstance**).
- **Social cognitive career theory (Lent, Brown and Hackett):** **self-efficacy**, **outcome expectations** and goals shape interests and choices.
- **Cognitive information processing approach:** treats career choice as a problem-solving and decision-making process.
- **Career construction (Savickas):** people make sense of their careers through their life stories; **career adaptability**.

## Practice at school

- **Activities:** assessing interests and abilities, introducing occupations, workplace visits, preparing for choices of field and school type, career portfolios.
- **Sources:** check the official sources of the Ministry of National Education and related institutions for current occupational information and school placement procedures.

## Commonly confused

- *Holland* focuses on person-environment fit, *Super* on the lifelong development of the self-concept.
- *Ginzberg* deals with the choice process from childhood to young adulthood, *Super* with the whole life span.
- *Krumboltz* stresses learning experiences, *social cognitive theory* self-efficacy and outcome expectations.

## Study tip

Link the keyword in the question stem to a theory: "self-concept" is Super, "personality type" is Holland, "learning experience" is Krumboltz, "self-efficacy" is social cognitive theory.
