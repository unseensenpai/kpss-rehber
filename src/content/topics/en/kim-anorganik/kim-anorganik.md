## Core idea

Inorganic chemistry studies the bonding, structure and reactions of all the elements beyond carbon chains. Two big themes sit at its centre: **bonding theories** (molecular orbital, crystal field) and **coordination compounds**. **8** questions in the test come from this subject. They mostly ask you to work out results such as magnetism, colour or the number of isomers from the structure of a molecule or complex.

## Bonding and solids

- **Molecular orbital theory:** atomic orbitals combine into bonding and antibonding orbitals. **Bond order** = (bonding electrons − antibonding electrons)/2.
- **Examples:** N₂ has bond order 3; O₂ has bond order 2 and two unpaired electrons, so it is **paramagnetic** (something the Lewis structure cannot explain).
- **Lattice energy:** grows with larger ion charges and smaller ions (MgO > NaCl). It cannot be measured directly; it is calculated with the **Born-Haber cycle** using Hess's law.
- **Crystal structures:** simple cubic, body-centred cubic and face-centred cubic unit cells; in the NaCl structure each ion has coordination number 6.

## Acids, bases and the main group

- A **Lewis acid** accepts an electron pair (BF₃, metal cations); a **Lewis base** donates one (NH₃, H₂O).
- **Hard-soft acid-base principle:** hard acids (small, highly charged: H⁺, Al³⁺) form more stable compounds with hard bases (F⁻, oxygen donors); soft acids (Ag⁺, Hg²⁺) with soft bases (I⁻, sulfur donors).
- **Main group trends:** alkali metals react with water to give hydroxides and H₂; the oxidising power of the halogens falls down the group (F₂ is strongest). In heavy main group elements the lower oxidation state becomes more stable (inert pair effect).

## Coordination compounds

- **Structure:** **ligands** bond to a central metal ion by donating electron pairs. The number of donor atoms bonded is the **coordination number**: 4 commonly gives tetrahedral or square planar geometry, 6 gives octahedral.
- **Chelate:** a ligand bonding through several donor atoms (ethylenediamine, EDTA) gives a more stable complex (chelate effect).
- **Isomerism:** square planar MA₂B₂ and octahedral MA₄B₂ complexes show cis-trans isomerism; complexes not superimposable on their mirror image show optical isomerism.
- **Crystal field theory:** in an octahedral field the d orbitals split into lower t₂g and upper e_g sets; the gap is Δo. In a tetrahedral field the splitting is reversed and smaller.
- **Spectrochemical series (short form):** I⁻ < Br⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < CN⁻ < CO. Strong-field ligands give a large Δo and lead to **low-spin** complexes.
- **Colour and magnetism:** colour is the complement of the light absorbed in a d-d transition; a complex with unpaired electrons is paramagnetic.

## Commonly confused

- The *oxidation state* is the metal's formal charge; the *coordination number* is the number of donor atoms bonded to it.
- *High spin* arises with weak-field ligands (many unpaired electrons), *low spin* with strong-field ligands (few unpaired electrons).
- A *Lewis acid* accepts an electron pair, a *Brønsted acid* donates a proton; every Brønsted acid gives up H⁺, which is itself a Lewis acid.

## Study tip

When you see a complex, find in order: the metal's oxidation state, its d-electron count, the geometry and the ligand field strength. These four steps solve most magnetism and colour questions.
