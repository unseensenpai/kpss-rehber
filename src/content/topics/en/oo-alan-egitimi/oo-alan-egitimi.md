## Core idea

The field teaching test measures whether a preschool teacher can plan and run language, science, mathematics, music, art, movement and play activities in a way that is **developmentally appropriate**. In early childhood, learning happens through concrete experience, play and discovery, and activities are often planned by **integrating** areas. The test has 20 questions, the heaviest section; they usually describe an activity and ask which skill it supports or whether the teacher's approach is appropriate. Current programmes are based on the **Türkiye Century Education Model** (*Türkiye Yüzyılı Maarif Modeli*); see the site's [Maarif Model](/kpss-rehber/en/topic/egitim-bilimleri/maarif-modeli/) topic for its general framework and check programme details in the Ministry's current official text.

## Language and early literacy

- **Language activities:** conversation, storytelling and story completion, poems, finger plays, rhymes, puppets and picture-book reading.
- **Phonological awareness:** finding rhymes, splitting words into syllables, noticing the first sound.
- **Print awareness:** noticing that print is read left to right and top to bottom and carries meaning.
- **Readiness for reading and writing:** fine motor, visual discrimination and listening skills are supported; formal letter teaching is not the aim of this period.

## Science and mathematics

- **Science process skills:** observing, comparing, classifying, predicting, measuring, drawing conclusions and communicating; experiments with everyday materials, nature walks.
- **Teacher's role:** sparks curiosity with **open-ended questions** instead of giving ready answers.
- **Mathematical foundations:** matching, **one-to-one correspondence**, **classification**, **seriation** (ordering from largest to smallest), **patterns**.
- **Counting principles:** one-to-one, stable order, cardinality (the last number counted gives the amount), abstraction and order irrelevance.
- **Other concepts:** geometric shapes, spatial concepts (under, on top of), **measuring with non-standard units** (steps, hand spans), simple graphs.

## Music, art, movement and drama

- **Music:** songs within the child's vocal range, rhythm work, simple percussion instruments, listening and moving to music.
- **Art:** in drawing development the **scribbling stage** comes first, followed by the preschematic and schematic stages (Lowenfeld); the **process** matters, not the product. Colouring books and ready-made templates limit creativity; the child's drawing is not corrected.
- **Movement and play:** gross motor (running, balance, throwing and catching), creative movement, games with rules; a balance of free play and teacher-supported play.
- **Creative drama:** warm-up, enactment (improvisation) and evaluation stages; there is no right or wrong.

## Assessment

- **Methods:** observation, anecdotal records, checklists, portfolios and children's work.
- **Purpose:** assessment is done to follow development and adjust planning, not to compare children.

## Commonly confused

- *Phonological awareness* is about spoken sounds, *print awareness* about the functions of written text.
- *Classification* groups by a shared feature, *seriation* orders by a feature.
- In *process-focused* art the child experiments freely; in a *product-focused* activity everyone copies the same model.

## Study tip

For every question ask: "does this activity make the child active, is it concrete, does it suit their development?" Options involving rote learning, copying or forcing a single right answer are usually wrong.
