## Core idea

This section tests the main lines of **Turkish history and culture** that a primary teacher passes on in life sciences and social studies. The Primary School Teaching ÖABT has **2 questions** on it. The logic is continuity and change: some elements of the Turkish state tradition carry over from period to period, while each period adds new institutions. Questions usually ask you to place an institution or event in the right period.

## Pre-Islamic and Turkish-Islamic periods

- **Idea of the state:** **kut**, the belief that the right to rule is granted by God; the **kurultay**, where state affairs were discussed; **töre**, unwritten customary law.
- **Culture:** nomadic life, horses and herding; the **Orkhon Inscriptions** in the Göktürk script are among the earliest major written records of Turkish.
- **Turkish-Islamic states:** the Karakhanids, Ghaznavids, Great Seljuks. The **Battle of Manzikert (1071)** opens the way for Anatolia to become Turkish.

## Ottoman and Republican periods

- **Ottoman:** from principality to empire; the **conquest of Istanbul (1453)**; institutions such as the imperial council (*divan*), the land-grant system (*tımar*) and the madrasa.
- **National Struggle:** the national forces (*Kuvâ-yı Millîye*), the congresses, the **opening of the Grand National Assembly (23 April 1920)**, Sakarya and the Great Offensive.
- **Republic:** **proclamation of the Republic (29 October 1923)**; the alphabet reform and reforms in law and education.
- **Atatürk's principles:** republicanism, nationalism, populism, statism, secularism and reformism.

## Commonly confused

- The *kurultay* is a council; *töre* is unwritten law.
- The *Grand National Assembly* opened in 1920; the *Republic* was proclaimed in 1923.

## Study tip

Show the periods on a timeline and link each to one institution and one cultural element. Thinking about how you would explain it to a primary pupil makes it stick.
