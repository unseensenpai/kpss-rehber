## Core idea

Genetics studies how hereditary information is **stored, copied, expressed and passed on**. The chain of logic: DNA replicates, genes are turned into RNA and protein (**DNA → RNA → protein**), and alleles are distributed to gametes in meiosis, either independently or linked. The Biology test has **10 questions** here, on cross and pedigree probabilities, protein synthesis, mutations and population genetics calculations.

## Classical inheritance

- **Mendel's laws:** the law of segregation (each individual carries two alleles for a gene, and only one passes to a gamete) and the law of independent assortment (genes on different chromosomes are distributed independently). An AaBb × AaBb cross gives a 9:3:3:1 phenotype ratio.
- **Test cross:** an individual with the dominant phenotype is crossed with a homozygous recessive to reveal its genotype.
- **Incomplete dominance:** the heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype (red × white → pink; 1:2:1 in F2). **Codominance:** both alleles are expressed together (blood group AB).
- **Multiple alleles:** the ABO blood group is set by the alleles Iᴬ, Iᴮ and i.
- **X-linked recessive inheritance** (colour blindness, haemophilia): males have only one X, so such traits are more common in males; an affected male passes the allele to all his daughters.
- **Linked genes:** genes on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together; **crossing over** creates new combinations, and its likelihood increases with the distance between genes.

## Molecular genetics

- **DNA structure:** a double helix with antiparallel strands; two hydrogen bonds between A and T, three between G and C.
- **Replication is semi-conservative:** each new molecule has one old and one new strand. DNA polymerase synthesises only in the 5'→3' direction, so the lagging strand is made as Okazaki fragments joined by ligase.
- **Transcription:** RNA polymerase makes mRNA from DNA. In eukaryotes introns are cut out and exons joined (splicing).
- **Translation:** at the ribosome, mRNA codons pair with tRNA anticodons; AUG is the start codon, and UAA, UAG and UGA are stop codons. The genetic code is a triplet code and degenerate (several codons can specify one amino acid).
- **Gene regulation:** in prokaryotes the **lac operon** is switched on when lactose is present; when it is absent, a repressor protein binds the operator and blocks transcription.

## Mutation, populations and biotechnology

- **Gene mutations:** a point mutation can be silent, missense or nonsense (an early stop codon); inserting or deleting a single base causes a **frameshift**.
- **Chromosomal abnormalities:** non-disjunction changes chromosome number: Down syndrome (trisomy 21), Turner syndrome (45,X), Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY).
- **Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium:** p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1. Equilibrium requires a large population, random mating, and no migration, mutation or selection.
- **Biotechnology:** restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific sequences; ligase joins fragments; plasmids serve as vectors; PCR amplifies a chosen DNA region.

## Commonly confused

- In *incomplete dominance* an intermediate phenotype appears; in *codominance* both traits appear together and distinctly.
- *Transcription* makes RNA from DNA; *translation* makes protein from RNA.
- *Independent assortment* applies to genes on different chromosomes; *linked genes* travel together unless crossing over occurs.

## Study tip

In cross problems, solve each gene pair separately and multiply the probabilities; this is faster and less error-prone than drawing a large Punnett square.
