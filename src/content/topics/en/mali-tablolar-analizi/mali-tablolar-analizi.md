## Core idea

Financial statement analysis evaluates a business's liquidity, debt structure, operating efficiency and profitability by **comparing figures with each other or over time**. A single figure says little on its own; meaning comes from comparison. The test has **8 questions** from this subject. Most ask you to calculate a ratio from given amounts or to say whether a transaction (such as buying inventory on credit) raises or lowers a ratio.

## Methods of analysis

- **Horizontal analysis** (*yatay analiz*, comparative statements): the same item is compared across two or more periods; you find the **absolute change** and the **percentage change**.
- **Vertical analysis** (*dikey analiz*, percentage method): within one period each item is expressed as a share of a total; **total assets** on the balance sheet and **net sales** on the income statement are set to 100. It shows structure.
- **Trend analysis:** a **base year** is set to 100 and later years are indexed to it; it shows long-run direction.
- **Ratio analysis:** two items with a meaningful relationship are divided.

## Liquidity ratios

- **Current ratio** = current assets / short-term liabilities.
- **Acid-test (quick) ratio** = (current assets − inventories) / short-term liabilities. Inventory is removed because it is the slowest item to turn into cash.
- **Cash ratio** = (cash and cash equivalents + marketable securities) / short-term liabilities.
- **Net working capital** = current assets − short-term liabilities.

## Financial structure ratios

- **Leverage ratio** = total liabilities / total assets; shows how much of the assets is financed by debt.
- **Equity to total assets** and **debt to equity** measure financial risk.
- **Non-current assets to equity** shows how far long-lived assets are covered by permanent funding.

## Activity and profitability ratios

- **Inventory turnover** = cost of sales / average inventory. A higher figure means stock sells faster.
- **Receivables turnover** = net sales / average trade receivables; 365 divided by it gives the **average collection period**.
- **Asset turnover** = net sales / total assets.
- **Gross profit margin** = gross profit / net sales; **net profit margin** = net profit / net sales.
- **Return on equity** = net profit / equity; **return on assets** = net profit / total assets.

## Commonly confused

- *Horizontal analysis* compares the same item across periods; *vertical analysis* compares items within one period against a total.
- The *current ratio* includes inventories; the *acid-test ratio* leaves them out.
- *Inventory turnover* uses cost of sales; *receivables turnover* uses net sales.

## Study tip

In ratio questions write the numerator and denominator first, then see how the transaction changes each. For example, paying a debt lowers both current assets and short-term liabilities by the same amount; if the current ratio is above 1, the ratio rises.
