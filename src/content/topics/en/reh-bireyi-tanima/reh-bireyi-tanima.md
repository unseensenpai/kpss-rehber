## Core idea

Getting to know the individual (*bireyi tanıma*) means understanding a student **holistically** through information gathered from different sources and techniques; no single technique is enough for a decision. Information is kept confidential and used for the student's benefit. The test has 7 questions; they usually describe a situation and ask which technique fits or what a technique's limitation is.

## Observation and recording

- **Types of observation:** structured or unstructured, participant or non-participant, in a natural or a controlled setting.
- **Anecdotal record:** a factual record of a significant behaviour without interpretation; comments go in a separate section.
- **Checklist:** marks whether a behaviour is present; a **rating scale** records its degree.
- **Rating errors:** **halo effect** (a general impression colouring every item), leniency or severity error, central tendency.

## Non-test techniques

- **Interview and questionnaire:** gathering information face to face or in writing.
- **Autobiography:** the student writes about their own life; free or guided (on a topic).
- **Time schedule** (*zaman cetveli*): shows how the student uses their time.
- **Who am I** (*ben kimim*): a short text in which the student introduces themselves.
- **Problem checklists:** lists on which students mark the problems they experience.
- **Home visit:** getting to know the family environment on site.

## Peer-based techniques

- **Sociometry:** reveals group relationships through choices made on a given criterion; results are shown in a **sociogram**. A much-chosen student is a **star**, one never chosen is **isolated (neglected)**, one often rejected is **rejected**, and mutual choices appear as **pairs** and **cliques**.
- **Guess who** (*kimdir bu*): students write the classmates who fit given behaviour descriptions; shows how the group sees the individual.

## Test techniques

- **Psychometric properties:** **validity** (measuring what it intends to measure), **reliability** (consistency), standardisation and norms.
- **Types:** intelligence tests, aptitude tests, achievement tests, interest inventories, personality inventories.
- **Projective tests:** try to understand personality through responses to ambiguous stimuli (inkblot and picture-story tests); administering and interpreting them requires special training.
- **Case study:** a detailed, holistic examination of all data on one student; records are kept orderly and confidential in student record systems.

## Commonly confused

- *Sociometry* involves choices for a specific activity; *guess who* involves naming peers by behaviour traits.
- A *checklist* gives present-or-absent information, a *rating scale* gives degree.
- *Validity* means measuring the right thing, *reliability* means measuring consistently; a reliable measure may not be valid.

## Study tip

For each technique ask "who provides the information?": the teacher's observation, the student, peers or a test. That distinction alone solves most questions.
