## Core idea

Public revenue is the resources the state and other public bodies obtain to carry out their duties. The most important is **tax**: unrequited, compulsory and imposed by law. Other revenues are either payment for a service or come from state property and borrowing. This subject has 6 questions in the test; they ask you to tell revenue types apart, recognise the elements of a tax and interpret tax equity and shifting.

## Types of revenue

- **Tax** (*vergi*): money taken **without direct return** and **compulsorily** to finance public spending.
- **Fee** (*harç*): charged to a person who uses a specific public service, **in return** for that service (land registry or passport procedures).
- **Levy** (*resim*): a charge for permission to carry out an activity (a licence).
- **Betterment levy** (*şerefiye*): taken from owners of property whose value rises because of a public investment.
- **Contribution** (*katılma payı*): those who gain special benefit from a public investment share its cost.
- **Parafiscal revenue:** collected by certain bodies for their own purposes; social security contributions are the typical example.
- **Others:** property and enterprise income, borrowing, money creation (*emisyon*), donations and grants.

## Elements of a tax and rate structures

- **Tax object:** what is taxed (income, wealth, spending).
- The **taxpayer** (*mükellef*) is legally liable for the tax; the **tax withholder** (*vergi sorumlusu*) must pay it on the taxpayer's behalf.
- **Tax base** (*matrah*): the value or quantity on which tax is calculated.
- An **exemption** (*muafiyet*) takes a person out of tax; an **exclusion** (*istisna*) takes an object out.
- **Rate structures:** **proportional (flat)**, **progressive** (by bracket or by class), **regressive**, and **specific** (*maktu*) taxes set per quantity.

## Principles of taxation and equity

- **Adam Smith's canons:** equity, certainty, convenience and economy in collection.
- **Benefit principle:** people pay according to the benefit they receive from public services.
- **Ability-to-pay principle:** tax follows a person's financial capacity.
- **Horizontal equity** means equal burden for people in equal positions; **vertical equity** means different burdens for different positions.

## Tax burden and effects

- **Shifting:** passing the burden to others; **forward** to buyers through prices, or **backward** to sellers or factors of production. The person who finally bears it carries the **incidence**.
- Shifting depends on the **elasticity** of supply and demand: the burden stays with the less elastic side.
- **Income effect** and **substitution effect:** a tax reduces disposable income and changes relative prices.
- **Excess burden (deadweight loss):** the efficiency loss beyond the revenue collected.
- **Laffer curve:** beyond a certain point, raising the rate can reduce tax revenue.

## Commonly confused

- A *tax* has no direct return; a *fee* is paid for a specific service.
- An *exemption* applies to a person; an *exclusion* applies to the object of tax.
- *Tax avoidance* uses legal gaps; *tax evasion* means not paying tax through illegal means.

## Study tip

Find an example for each revenue type and sort them with two questions: "Is there a direct return?" and "Is it compulsory?" In shifting questions, first check which side is less elastic; the burden usually falls there.
