## Core idea

This subject looks at what money is, **who creates it and how**, and how the central bank manages the money supply. The main logic: the central bank creates the **monetary base**, while banks create **deposit money** (*kaydi para*) by lending. The test has **4 questions**; they usually ask about the money multiplier, the effect of policy tools and theories of money demand.

## Money and the money supply

- **Functions:** **medium of exchange**, **unit of account**, **store of value** and standard of deferred payment.
- **Types:** commodity money, metal coins, paper (fiat) money, deposit (bank) money, electronic money.
- **Gresham's law:** bad money drives good money out of circulation.
- **Money supply measures:** **M1** = currency in circulation + demand deposits; **M2** = M1 + time deposits; **M3** = M2 + less liquid instruments. The wider the measure, the lower the liquidity.
- **Monetary base** (reserve money): currency in circulation + banks' reserves at the central bank.

## Deposit money and the money multiplier

- Banks keep the **required reserve** share of deposits and lend the rest; loans return as deposits and the process continues.
- **Simple deposit multiplier:** 1 / rr. Example: if rr = 20%, an initial deposit of 1,000 TL can become at most 5,000 TL of total deposits.
- **Extended money multiplier:** m = (1 + c) / (c + rr), where c is the public's currency-to-deposit ratio. If the public holds more cash or banks hold **excess reserves**, the multiplier shrinks.

## The central bank and monetary policy

- **Open market operations:** if the central bank **buys** bonds the money supply rises; if it **sells**, it falls. This is the most flexible tool.
- **Rediscount rate:** the rate for short-term lending to banks; cutting it is expansionary.
- **Reserve requirement ratio:** raising it lowers the multiplier and tightens the money supply.
- The **policy rate** and expectations management are the main tools today. A rate change spreads through credit, exchange-rate, asset-price and expectations channels (the **monetary transmission mechanism**).
- In Türkiye, the main objective of the **CBRT** (*TCMB*) has been **price stability** since the 2001 amendment to its law; banking supervision is carried out by the **BRSA** (*BDDK*).

## Theories of money demand

- **Fisher (quantity theory):** MV = PT; if velocity and output are taken as fixed, money supply determines prices proportionally.
- **Cambridge approach:** M = kPY; k is the share of income people wish to hold as money.
- **Keynes (liquidity preference):** **transactions**, **precautionary** and **speculative** motives. Speculative demand is inversely related to the interest rate.
- **Friedman:** money demand is a stable function of **permanent income**.

## Banking and credit

- **Commercial banks** take deposits and lend; **participation banks** (*katılım bankaları*) work on profit-and-loss sharing instead of interest; development and investment banks do not take deposits.
- **Basel rules** require banks to hold **capital adequacy** in line with their risks.
- **Real interest rate** ≈ nominal rate − expected inflation (the **Fisher effect**).

## Commonly confused

- The *monetary base* is created by the central bank; *deposit money* by banks.
- The *simple deposit multiplier* uses only rr; the *extended money multiplier* also includes the public's cash preference.
- The *Fisher equation* stresses velocity; the *Cambridge equation* stresses the share of money held (k), with k = 1 / V.

## Study tip

Match each central bank tool with the question "does money supply rise or fall?" and work the multiplier formula through an example twice. Numerical questions are solved with these two steps.
