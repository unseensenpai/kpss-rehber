## Core idea

A *mezhep* (school) is a tradition of thought formed around a shared interpretation and method of understanding religion. Schools arose from political events, encounters with other cultures, differing readings of the texts and the weight given to reason. The test has 3 questions; they usually give a view and ask which school holds it. Each school should be presented through its own sources and self-understanding.

## Political-theological schools

- **Kharijites (*Hariciler*):** after Siffin they rejected the ***tahkim*** (arbitration) and left Ali's ranks. They regard a grave sinner as outside the faith and do not tie the caliphate to any lineage. Their moderate surviving branch is the **Ibadiyya**.
- **Shi'a:** treats the ***imamet*** (imamate) as a fundamental matter of religion; holds that the imam comes from Ali and his descendants by designation.
  - **Twelvers (*İsnaaşeriyye*, Ja'faris):** twelve imams; believe the twelfth imam is in occultation.
  - **Zaydiyya:** the Shi'i branch closest to Sunnism; widespread in Yemen.
  - **Isma'iliyya:** continues the imamate through Isma'il, son of Ja'far al-Sadiq; stresses the inner (*batın*) meaning alongside the outward meaning of texts.
- **Murji'a:** left the status of the grave sinner to God (***irca***, deferral) and separated faith from deeds.

## Theological schools

- **Mu'tazila:** gives reason wide scope; has five principles (*usul-i hamse*).
- **Ahl al-Sunnah:** treated in three branches: **Maturidism**, **Ash'arism** and the **Salafiyya** (people of hadith). The Salafiyya accepts texts in their apparent sense and avoids figurative interpretation; Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Ibn Taymiyya are prominent.

## Anatolia and the modern era

- **Alevism-Bektashism:** a faith and cultural tradition shaped in Anatolia around the legacy of Hacı Bektaş Veli, centred on love for the **Ahl al-Bayt** (the Prophet's family), the ***cem*** ceremony and institutions such as *musahiplik* (spiritual companionship).
- **Modern movements:** **reformist** (Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, Muhammad Abduh), **modernist** and **Islamist** currents responding to Western influence and debates on decline; and **Wahhabism**, begun in Arabia by Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, which sees itself as a movement to purify *tevhid* (monotheism).

## Commonly confused

- The *Kharijites* consider the grave sinner outside the faith, the *Murji'a* leave the judgement to God, the *Mu'tazila* place him in an intermediate position.
- *Twelvers* follow twelve imams, the *Isma'iliyya* the line of Isma'il; the *Zaydiyya* is closest to Sunnism.

## Study tip

For each school, put its founding event, core view and key figure on one line; cue words in questions (arbitration, deferral, imamate, occultation) lead you straight to the answer.
