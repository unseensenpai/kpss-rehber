## Core idea

Classical Turkish literature (divan literature) is a written poetic tradition shaped by Islamic culture and Arabic-Persian literature and centred on the palace and the madrasa. The Turkish ÖABT has **5 questions** on it. The tradition seeks the **perfect couplet**, uses conventional images called **mazmun**, and is written in **aruz**, a quantitative metre. Questions mostly ask you to recognise a verse form from its rhyme scheme and match poets with their works.

## Verse forms

- ***Gazel*:** usually 5–15 couplets rhymed *aa ba ca*; love, wine and beauty. The first couplet is the ***matla***, the last the ***makta*** (where the pen name appears).
- ***Kaside*:** longer than a gazel with the same rhyme scheme; written to praise God, the Prophet or rulers.
- ***Mesnevi*:** each couplet rhymes within itself (*aa bb cc*); suited to long love and moral stories. A series of five is a ***hamse***.
- ***Rubai*:** a single quatrain rhymed *aaxa* in its own aruz patterns; philosophical and mystical thought.
- ***Tuyuğ*:** a single quatrain rhymed *aaba*, a Turkish invention; usually with punning rhymes.
- ***Şarkı*:** a form written to be set to music, made of stanzas with a refrain.

## Major poets

- **Fuzuli:** lyrical love poetry; the mesnevi *Leylâ vü Mecnûn* and the *Su Kasidesi* (Water Kaside).
- **Baki:** known for his gazels and his elegy for Suleiman the Magnificent.
- **Nef'i:** master of the kaside; also wrote satires.
- **Nedim:** poet of the Tulip Era; the şarkı and **localisation** (*mahallîleşme*, bringing local life into poetry).
- **Şeyh Galip:** the mesnevi *Hüsn ü Aşk* (Beauty and Love); representative of the **Indian style** (*sebk-i Hindî*).

## Divan prose

- Prose comes in three registers: plain, middle and **ornate**.
- Evliya Çelebi's *Seyahatnâme* (Book of Travels) is the best-known travel work.

## Commonly confused

- The *rubai* rhymes *aaxa*, the *tuyuğ* *aaba*; the rubai comes from Persian, the tuyuğ is Turkish.
- The *gazel* and *kaside* share a rhyme scheme; the gazel is short and lyrical, the kaside long and written in praise.

## Study tip

Make a table of the verse forms with their rhyme schemes. One keyword per poet (Fuzuli-love, Nef'i-kaside, Nedim-şarkı) solves most questions.
