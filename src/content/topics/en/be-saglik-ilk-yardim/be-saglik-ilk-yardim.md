## Core idea

The World Health Organization defines health not merely as the absence of disease or infirmity but as **complete physical, mental and social well-being**. First aid is what is done, without medicines and with what is at hand, until the health team arrives, to **protect life and stop the situation getting worse**. The test has **3 questions**; they usually ask for the correct order of priorities in a sports scene or for a wrong practice.

## Basic principles

- **Protect-Report-Rescue** (*Koruma-Bildirme-Kurtarma*): first make the scene and yourself safe, then call **112** (Türkiye's emergency number), then help the casualty.
- **Scene safety:** a first aider who puts themselves in danger becomes a second casualty.
- **Assessment:** check consciousness, then follow the **ABC** logic (airway, breathing, circulation). Check the steps of basic life support in current first aid guidelines.
- An unconscious person who is breathing is placed in the **recovery position** unless a spinal injury is suspected; nothing is given by mouth to an unconscious person.

## Common situations in sport

- **External bleeding:** **direct pressure** with a clean cloth and, if possible, raising the injured part.
- **Nosebleed:** tilt the head slightly **forward** and pinch the soft part of the nose; do not tilt the head back.
- **Sprains and strains:** rest, cold application, compression bandage and elevation.
- **Suspected fracture:** do not try to set the bone; **immobilise** it including the joints above and below.
- **Heat stroke and heat exhaustion:** move the person somewhere cool and cool the body; if conscious, they may be given fluids.
- **Burns:** cool the area with clean, cool water; do not put oil, paste or similar substances on it.

## Commonly confused

- *First aid* is temporary, life-protecting help; *emergency treatment* is given by health professionals.
- Tilting the head *forward* is right for a nosebleed; tilting it *back* is wrong.
- The *recovery position* is for an unconscious person who is breathing; a person who is not breathing needs *basic life support*.

## Study tip

In every scenario, first ask: "Am I safe, and has 112 been called?" Options such as giving medicine or setting a fracture go beyond a first aider's role and are usually wrong.
