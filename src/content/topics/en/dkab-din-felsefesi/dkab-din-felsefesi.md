## Core idea

**Philosophy of religion** is the branch of philosophy that examines religion's core claims (God's existence, attributes, evil, immortality) by reason, without committing to a particular religion; it analyses **coherence and justification** rather than defending a position. The test has 2 questions; they usually ask about types of argument and conceptions of God.

## Arguments for God's existence

- **Ontological argument:** derives God's existence from the concept of God (a being than which nothing greater can be conceived); **Anselm** and **Descartes**. **Kant** objected that existence is not a predicate.
- **Cosmological argument:** existing things depend on a first cause or necessary being; **Thomas Aquinas**, and the theologians' temporality argument and Ibn Sina's contingency argument belong to this family.
- **Teleological (design) argument:** order and purpose in the universe point to a designer; **Paley**'s watch analogy. **Hume** criticised it.
- **Moral argument:** the moral law and the highest good require God; **Kant** accepted God as a postulate of practical reason.

## Conceptions of God and faith

- **Theism:** a personal God who creates and acts in the world. **Deism:** a God who creates but does not intervene. **Pantheism:** God and the universe are identical.
- **Agnosticism:** God's existence cannot be known; **atheism:** denial of God's existence.
- **Fideism:** faith neither rests on nor needs rational proof (Kierkegaard); **Pascal's wager** argues that believing is a rational choice.

## Evil, pluralism and language

- **Problem of evil:** how an all-powerful, good God fits with the existence of evil; **moral** and **natural** evil are distinguished. **Theodicy** is the attempt to defend God against this objection; the free-will defence is the best-known reply.
- **Religious pluralism:** views on religions and truth are classed as **exclusivism**, **inclusivism** and **pluralism** (John Hick).
- **Religious language:** the meaning of statements about God; analogical (Aquinas) and symbolic (Tillich) approaches.

## Commonly confused

- The *ontological* argument starts from a concept, the *cosmological* from existence itself, the *teleological* from order.
- *Deism* holds a non-intervening God, *pantheism* a God identical with the universe.

## Study tip

Learn what each argument "starts from" together with one critic; questions usually ask which argument the reasoning in a paragraph belongs to.
