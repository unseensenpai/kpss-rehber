## Core idea

Administrative law governs the organisation and activities of the **administration** that exercises public power, and the **judicial review** of those activities. The core logic: the administration has superior powers for the public interest, but every act is bound by law and open to judicial review. The Public Administration test has about **8 questions** on it. Questions mostly ask about the elements of an administrative act, administrative tutelage, types of liability and types of action.

## Administrative organisation

- **Unity of the administration** (*idarenin bütünlüğü*): central and decentralised bodies form one whole, held together by hierarchy and tutelage.
- **Hierarchy:** within central administration, a superior's power to instruct a subordinate and to approve, amend or annul their acts.
- **Administrative tutelage** (*idari vesayet*): the centre's **exceptional, law-limited** control over decentralised bodies; as a rule it cannot amend their acts.
- **Public legal persons:** the state, local governments and functional decentralised bodies; they own property and can sue and be sued.

## Administrative acts

- **Administrative act:** a unilateral act of the administration, using public power, that produces legal effects.
- **Elements:** **competence, form, cause, subject and purpose.** Illegality in any one makes the act open to annulment.
- **Purpose element:** an act must always serve the **public interest**; otherwise it is a misuse of power.
- **Features:** an administrative act enjoys a **presumption of legality** and as a rule can be enforced without a court decision (it is **executory**).
- **Ending an act:** withdrawal (retroactive) and revocation (for the future).

## Public service, police powers and officials

- **Public service:** a continuous activity provided by or under the control of the administration for the public interest; governed by **continuity, equality and adaptability**.
- **Administrative police** (*idari kolluk*): preventive action to protect public order (security, tranquillity, health); **judicial police** assist the courts after a crime.
- **Public officials:** under the Constitution, the essential and permanent duties of public service are carried out by **civil servants and other public officials**.
- **Public property:** property set aside for public service or common use; as a rule it cannot be transferred or seized.

## Liability and administrative courts

- **Access to courts:** under the Constitution, recourse to the courts is open against all acts and actions of the administration.
- **Service fault** (*hizmet kusuru*): the service runs badly, late or not at all; the main basis of the administration's liability.
- **No-fault liability:** based on **risk** and **equality before public burdens**; no fault is required.
- **Action for annulment** (*iptal davası*): brought by a person whose interest is harmed, to set aside an unlawful act.
- **Full remedy action** (*tam yargı davası*): brought by a person whose right is violated, to obtain compensation.
- **Time limit:** unless a law says otherwise, the general limit is 60 days before the Council of State and administrative courts, and 30 days before tax courts.

## Commonly confused

- *Hierarchy* is broad and works inside central administration; *tutelage* is exceptional and limited by law.
- An *action for annulment* removes the act; a *full remedy action* secures compensation.
- *Administrative police* is preventive; *judicial police* acts after a crime.

## Study tip

Memorise the five elements of an act and invent an illegality example for each (an act by an unauthorised official: competence; a decision made out of personal grudge: purpose). Time limits and powers change from time to time, so always check the current legislation.
