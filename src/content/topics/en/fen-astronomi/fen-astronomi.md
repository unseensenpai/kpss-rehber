## Core idea

Astronomy studies the motions and structure of celestial bodies. The core logic: Earth's **rotation** causes day and night, its **revolution** together with its **axial tilt** causes the seasons, and the Moon's motion around Earth causes phases and eclipses. The Science test has **2 questions** here, mostly probing these cause-and-effect links and basic features of the Solar System.

## Earth and Moon

- **Rotation:** Earth turns on its axis in about 24 hours, giving day and night.
- **Revolution:** one orbit of the Sun takes about 365.25 days. Together with the axial tilt (about 23.5°), it causes the seasons; seasons are not caused by distance from the Sun.
- At an **equinox** (around 21 March and 23 September) day and night are equal; at a **solstice** (around 21 June and 21 December) the longest or shortest day occurs.
- **Lunar phases:** the change in how much of the Moon's lit side is visible from Earth; one cycle takes about 29.5 days.
- A **solar eclipse** happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth (at new moon); a **lunar eclipse** happens when Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon (at full moon).

## The Solar System and beyond

- **Terrestrial planets:** Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars; small, rocky and dense. **Gas and ice giants:** Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune; large, ringed and with many moons.
- **Kepler's laws:** planets move in elliptical orbits around the Sun; they speed up as they get closer to it; the orbital period grows with distance.
- **Stars** produce their own energy by nuclear fusion in their cores; the Sun is a star. A **light-year** is the distance light travels in one year, a unit of distance.
- The Solar System lies in the **Milky Way** galaxy.

## Commonly confused

- *Rotation* explains day and night; *revolution* plus axial tilt explains the seasons.
- A *light-year* is a unit of distance, not of time.

## Study tip

Sketch the Sun-Earth-Moon line-up on paper and mark the phases and eclipses on it; which phase an eclipse can occur in follows directly from the sketch.
