## Core idea

Children's literature is the body of artistic texts written to suit a child's **developmental level**, interests and language. The Turkish ÖABT has **3 questions** on it. The key criterion is **child-appropriateness** (*çocuğa görelik*): a text should speak to the child's world without talking down or preaching. Questions usually ask you to judge a book feature by this principle or to recognise genres.

## Criteria for children's books

- **Content:** the subject and message should suit the age; avoid fear, violence and discrimination; the message should be **implied**, not imposed (heavy **didacticism** is a flaw).
- **Hero:** believable characters free of stereotypes that the child can identify with.
- **Language:** vocabulary and sentence length suited to the age, fluency, correct Turkish.
- **Format:** letter size, line spacing, paper quality, binding and harmony of text and pictures.
- **Picture book:** pictures do not just decorate the text; they help tell the story, especially before children can read.

## Genres

- **Genres from oral tradition:** lullabies, *tekerleme* (rhythmic nonsense rhymes), riddles, folk tales; they build a sense of language and rhythm.
- **Fable:** a short tale that teaches a lesson through talking animals; La Fontaine is its best-known name.
- **Stories and novels:** realistic or **fantasy** fiction; adventure, family and school themes.
- **Poetry:** texts driven by sound and rhythm with concrete images.
- **Informational books:** science, nature and biography.

## Key names

- **World:** the **Brothers Grimm** and Charles Perrault for collected tales, **Andersen** for original tales, Lewis Carroll for *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*, Antoine de Saint-Exupéry for *The Little Prince*.
- **Turkish:** Tevfik Fikret for *Şermin*, poems for children; Ziya Gökalp for *Altın Işık* (Golden Light), tales and poems.

## Commonly confused

- A *fable* teaches an explicit lesson with animals; a *folk tale* tells extraordinary events in an unspecified time and place.
- *Child-appropriateness* means fitting the age; *didacticism* means the lesson overpowering the art.

## Study tip

When judging a book, check content, language, character and format in turn. Watch the reader's age in the question: what suits a lower-secondary student may not suit a preschool child.
