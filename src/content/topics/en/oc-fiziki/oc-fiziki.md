## Core idea

Physical geography studies the **internal and external forces** that shape the surface, the **climate processes** of the atmosphere and the resulting patterns of water, soil and vegetation. Every feature has a **cause** (energy, slope, rock, latitude); questions ask you to find it. The test has 20 questions; they usually ask about reading maps and graphs, how landforms form and climate factors.

## The Earth and maps

- **Shape:** the Earth is a **geoid**, flattened at the poles and bulging at the Equator; gravity therefore increases towards the poles.
- **Daily rotation:** day and night, local time differences (4 minutes per meridian), and the Coriolis effect deflecting winds and currents.
- **Yearly revolution and axial tilt:** seasons; solstices on **21 June** and **21 December**, equinoxes on **21 March** and **23 September**; differences in shadow length and day length.
- **Maps:** as the **scale** gets smaller the area shown grows and detail falls. Denser **contour lines** mean steeper slopes; valleys, ridges, saddles and cliffs are read from contour maps.

## Internal forces and rocks

- **Layers of the Earth:** crust, mantle and core; continental crust is thicker and lighter, oceanic crust thinner and denser.
- **Rocks:** **igneous** (granite, basalt), **sedimentary** (limestone, sandstone, coal), **metamorphic** (marble, schist).
- **Plate tectonics:** plates move apart (mid-ocean ridges), together (mountain belts, trenches) or slide past each other (strike-slip faults).
- **Orogeny** is mountain building by folding and faulting; **epeirogeny** is the slow rise and fall of large areas. **Volcanism** and **earthquakes** concentrate at plate boundaries.

## External forces

- **Rivers:** erosion forms valleys and potholes; deposition forms **meanders**, **deltas** and alluvial fans.
- **Karst:** solution of limestone forms **karren, dolines, uvalas, poljes and caves**; precipitation forms **stalactites, stalagmites and travertine**.
- **Wind:** **mushroom rocks** and **barchan** dunes in dry areas.
- **Glaciers:** **cirques**, **moraines**, U-shaped valleys and **fjords**.
- **Waves and currents:** **cliffs**, **spits**, **tombolos** and **lagoons**.

## Climate, water, soil and vegetation

- **Temperature** depends on latitude, altitude, continentality, aspect and ocean currents.
- **Pressure:** a thermal low at the Equator, dynamic highs near 30°, dynamic lows near 60° and thermal highs at the poles; these belts produce the **trade winds**, **westerlies** and **polar easterlies**. **Monsoons**, **land and sea breezes** and the **föhn** are seasonal and local winds.
- **Types of rainfall:** **convectional** (rising warm air), **orographic** (moist air forced over mountains), **frontal** (different air masses meeting).
- **Climate types:** equatorial, savanna, monsoon, desert, Mediterranean, temperate oceanic, continental, tundra and polar; classifications such as Köppen rest on temperature and precipitation.
- **Hydrography:** a river's **regime** (regular or irregular) depends on its source of water; lakes are **tectonic, volcanic, karstic, dammed or glacial**; ocean currents affect coastal climates.
- **Soils:** **zonal** (formed by climate: laterite, podzol, chernozem), **intrazonal** (parent rock dominant: terra rossa), **azonal** (transported: alluvium).
- **Vegetation:** forests, savanna, steppe, **maquis** and tundra match their climates.

## Commonly confused

- *Orogeny* is mountain building; *epeirogeny* is slow continental-scale uplift or subsidence.
- A *doline* is a small solution hollow; a *polje* is a large flat-floored karst plain.
- *Zonal* soil is a product of climate; *azonal* soil a product of transport.

## Study tip

Next to each landform write the force and climate that made it. For questions showing a landform, first ask "which force, which setting"; the answer usually follows.
