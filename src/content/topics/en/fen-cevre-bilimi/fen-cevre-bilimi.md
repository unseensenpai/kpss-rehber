## Core idea

Environmental science studies how living things relate to their surroundings and how human activity upsets that balance. The key principle: in an ecosystem **energy flows one way** while **matter is recycled**; pollution and overuse disrupt these cycles. The Science test has **2 questions** here, usually asking for the cause and effect of an environmental problem or a step in a nutrient cycle.

## Ecosystems and cycles

- **Producers** (plants, algae) make food, **consumers** take in ready-made food, and **decomposers** (bacteria, fungi) turn dead matter into inorganic substances.
- **Energy flow:** only a small part of the energy (typically about 10%) passes from one level to the next, which is why food chains are short.
- **Carbon cycle:** photosynthesis fixes CO₂; respiration, decomposition and burning fossil fuels return CO₂ to the atmosphere.
- **Nitrogen cycle:** nitrogen-fixing bacteria convert atmospheric N₂ into compounds organisms can use; denitrifying bacteria return nitrogen to the atmosphere.

## Environmental problems

- **Greenhouse effect and global warming:** gases such as CO₂ and methane trap heat; a rise in these gases raises the average temperature.
- **Ozone depletion:** CFCs destroy ozone in the stratosphere, letting more harmful UV radiation through.
- **Acid rain:** sulphur and nitrogen oxides form acids with rainwater.
- **Eutrophication:** excess nutrients from fertiliser cause algal blooms and oxygen loss in water.
- **Biomagnification:** heavy metals and some pesticides reach higher concentrations at higher levels of the food chain.
- **Sustainability:** using resources without endangering the needs of future generations; renewable energy, recycling and habitat protection serve this goal.

## Commonly confused

- The *greenhouse effect* is natural and necessary for life; *global warming* is its strengthening as greenhouse gases increase.
- *Ozone depletion* concerns UV radiation; *global warming* concerns greenhouse gases. They are separate problems.

## Study tip

For each environmental problem, build a "cause → process → effect → solution" chain; questions usually leave one link of that chain blank.
